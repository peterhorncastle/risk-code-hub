Occurrence vs Claims-Made — The Core Policy Basis Decision

The single most important underwriting decision for non-US GL is whether to write the risk on an occurrence or claims-made basis. This choice determines when coverage is triggered — at the time of the injury/damage event (occurrence) or when the claim is first made against the insured (claims-made). The distinction is critical for long-tail risks such as products and contractors, where claims can emerge years or decades after the policy period.

The Aggregate Limit — Critical Underwriting Control for Products (NR)

For products liability (NR), the products/completed operations aggregate is the maximum the policy will pay for all products claims in the policy year combined — regardless of how many individual occurrences. This is a fundamental underwriting control because a mass-product defect (e.g. a contaminated batch) can generate thousands of individual claims simultaneously, each separately triggering the any-one-occurrence limit.

  • Standard structure: Limit of Indemnity £Xm any one occurrence AND £Ym in the aggregate (products). The aggregate is typically set at 2–3× the AOO limit for most sectors.
  • Pharmaceutical / food: Aggregate limits are particularly critical — a single contaminated batch can affect millions of consumers. Aggregates for high-volume food or pharma products may be set at 5–10× AOO, or the risk declined if adequate aggregate cannot be agreed.
  • Reinstatement: Some policies offer aggregate reinstatement (one or more) — the aggregate is refilled to full limit after exhaustion. This is expensive and requires careful aggregate loss modelling.
  • Defence costs: Non-US GL policies commonly write defence costs inside the limit of indemnity (DCIL) — costs erode the aggregate. This significantly reduces effective coverage for claims-heavy mass-tort scenarios. Outside-the-limit (DCOL) is available but at additional premium.
