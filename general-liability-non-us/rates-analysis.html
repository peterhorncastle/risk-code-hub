Market Cycle Narrative

2001–2003: Post-9/11 Hard Market

Across-board Lloyd's repricing following 9/11. Non-US GL rates rise 15–25% in 2002–2003. EST Terrorism exclusions mandated. Asbestos reserve strengthening adds pressure. Market disciplines improve; loss-making syndicates merge or close.

2004–2011: Soft Cycle — Rate Erosion

New capital enters Lloyd's; non-US GL rates fall steadily 2004–2011. Combined ratios deteriorate for long-tail risks as prior-year reserves develop adversely. Capacity for pharmaceutical and products risks compresses, but pricing remains competitive for standard risks. EST

2012–2015: Sideways — Structural Change

Lloyd's performance management actions (2012 Franchise Board reviews) stabilise non-US GL pricing. Claims-made conversion accelerated for long-tail products risks. PIP breast implant and other European mass-tort events begin developing; reserve additions required. EST

2016–2019: Modest Firming

Grenfell (2017), Dieselgate civil litigation (2015–), and emerging social inflation in Australia and Brazil trigger selective rate increases. Products liability (NR) hardens ahead of public liability (N). Lloyd's syndicate-level underwriting guidance tightens aggregate deployment. EST

2020–2022: Significant Hard Market

COVID communicable disease exclusion debates, EU Representative Actions Directive (2019), and social inflation reaching non-US markets drive hardest cycle since 2002. NR rates +20–35%; NC contractors +10–20%; N public liability +8–15%. EST Revised EU PLD in development — uncertainty loading applied.

2023–2024: Moderating but Selective Discipline

Rate increases moderate but remain positive for most segments. GPSR (December 2024) and revised EU PLD create new exposure vectors — underwriters applying forward-looking loading for online marketplace and AI/software products liability. Long-tail reserving uncertainty remains elevated. EST

ROE Scenarios — £500m Non-US GL Portfolio

Three return-on-equity scenarios on a hypothetical £500m GWP Lloyd's non-US GL portfolio (mix: 40% products NR, 35% public N, 15% contractors NC, 10% miscellaneous NS). Long-tail basis; 40% expense ratio; investment income at 3.8% on float (long-tail float benefit). ILLUS

Bull Scenario

+16%

Loss ratio 58%; combined 98%; investment income £19m (long float); profit £80m on £500m equity. Favourable: claims-made conversion limiting IBNR, social inflation contained, no major mass-tort year. ILLUS

Base Scenario

+8%

Loss ratio 68%; combined 108%; investment income £19m; profit £40m. Long-tail IBNR development broadly stable; moderate prior-year adverse development offset by current-year rate. ILLUS

Bear Scenario

−12%

Loss ratio 86%; combined 126%; large mass-tort event (pharmaceutical or food), adverse reserve development on long-tail prior years, EU class action crystallisation. Loss £60m. Demonstrates long-tail volatility. ILLUS

Note: Non-US GL ROE is materially enhanced by investment income on long-tail float compared to short-tail classes. A 5-year average payment pattern on a £500m GWP portfolio generates approximately £2.5bn of invested reserves at any time — investment income at 3.8% = ~£95m p.a. in the base scenario. Long-tail benefits disproportionately disadvantage underwriters in low-interest-rate environments (2010–2022) and benefit them in rising-rate environments (2023–2024). ILLUS
