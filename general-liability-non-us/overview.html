Lloyd's Risk Codes — N, NC, NR, NS

Four Lloyd's risk codes cover non-US general liability, broadly segmented by type of liability trigger and risk nature. Together they represent one of the largest specialty casualty classes written at Lloyd's, and historically among the most technically complex — liability claims frequently develop over years or decades, requiring careful long-tail reserving and jurisdiction-aware policy drafting.

Coverage Overview

Non-US general liability policies protect insureds against legal liability for bodily injury (BI) and property damage (PD) suffered by third parties. The core insuring agreement is broadly consistent across jurisdictions, but the perils covered, exclusions applied and limits required vary significantly by territory, industry sector and applicable law.

Public / Premises Liability (N)

  • Third-party BI and PD from occupation of premises
  • Occupier's duty of care (Occupiers' Liability Acts 1957/1984 — UK)
  • Slip, trip and fall on premises
  • Third-party access areas, car parks, common areas
  • Visitor and trespasser claims
  • Advertising and personal injury liability (selected)

Contractors & Operations (NC)

  • Works in progress — BI/PD from construction activities
  • Vibration, weakening of support, removal of support (VWS)
  • Subsidence and collapse arising from works
  • Adjacent property damage
  • Completed operations (post-handover defects causing loss)
  • Subcontractor liability extension

Products Liability (NR)

  • Bodily injury from defective products (manufacturing, design, warning defects)
  • Property damage caused by defective products
  • Food contamination / adulteration
  • Pharmaceutical adverse events
  • Medical device malfunction
  • Recall costs (where endorsed; otherwise separate PB code)

Miscellaneous & Special Risks (NS)

  • Event and exhibition liability — public injury at venue
  • Amusement and leisure — fairground, theme park, adventure activity
  • Sports and motorsport liability
  • Hospitality — food service, alcohol service (where not excluded)
  • Umbrella/excess liability above primary layers
  • Non-standard risks not fitting N, NC or NR

Standard Exclusions (All Codes)

  • US/Canada jurisdiction (redirected to UA/UC codes)
  • Employer's liability (separate EL policy required in UK)
  • Professional indemnity / advice errors (separate PI codes)
  • Pollution (sudden & accidental sometimes covered; gradual excluded)
  • War, nuclear, cyber (standard market exclusions LMA5400)
  • Asbestos (blanket exclusion — dedicated asbestos market)
  • Workers' compensation (territorial statutory schemes)

Extensions & Optional Covers

  • Worldwide jurisdiction (excluding US/Canada) — critical for exporters
  • Cross-liability (each insured treated separately)
  • Tenants' liability (damage to rented premises)
  • Employers' liability (jurisdictions permitting combined)
  • Contractual liability (liability assumed under contract)
  • Defence costs — inside or outside the limit of indemnity

Key Legislative Framework by Territory

Unlike US liability, where federal and state law create a relatively uniform litigation environment, non-US GL spans wildly different legal systems — common law, civil law (Code Napoléon derivatives), Germanic law, Sharia-influenced systems — with very different approaches to fault, damages, limitation periods and class action rights. Underwriters must be alert to the governing law of the policy and the jurisdiction of likely claims.

United Kingdom

Common law tort. Occupiers' Liability Acts 1957/1984. Consumer Protection Act 1987 (strict liability for defective products — implements EU PLD). Limitation Act 1980 (3-year BI; 6-year PD). No US-style class actions; representative actions available under CPR 19. VER

European Union

EU Product Liability Directive 1985 (amended 2024) — strict liability for defective products. General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) 2024 replaces GPSD. Representative Actions Directive 2020 enables EU-wide class actions. Limitation: typically 3 years from discovery, 10-year longstop. VER

Australia

Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Schedule 2: Australian Consumer Law) — strict product liability, safety standards, mandatory recalls. Trade Practices Act replaced 2011. State tort law varies; proportionate liability common. Class actions well-established (Federal Court, Victorian regime). VER

Middle East / GCC

Civil law codified systems (UAE Civil Code, Saudi Arabia). Product liability emerging — UAE Federal Law No. 15/2020 on Consumer Protection. Public liability relatively underdeveloped judicially but growing with infrastructure expansion. Sharia law principles influence damages. EST

Asia-Pacific

Highly variable: Singapore/HK common law (English influence); Japan civil law; China Product Quality Law 2009; India Consumer Protection Act 2019. Class actions limited in most jurisdictions. Damages generally lower than Western markets but trend rising. EST

Latin America

Civil law dominant (Civil Codes). Brazil: Consumer Defence Code (CDC) 1990 — strict product liability, class action well-developed, award inflation. Argentina, Mexico: civil law tort. Political and currency risk affects claims settlement. EST

The US/Canada Exclusion — Critical Boundary

Why Non-US GL Always Excludes the USA and Canada

The USA and Canada are excluded from all N/NC/NR/NS policies as a fundamental underwriting boundary. This is not a minor endorsement — it is a core structural feature of the Lloyd's market segmentation. Reasons include:

  • Litigation environment: US litigation is uniquely aggressive — jury trials for liability, punitive damages (absent in most non-US jurisdictions), contingency fee plaintiff attorneys, and no proportionate liability in many states. US products liability awards regularly exceed $100m; equivalent awards are exceptional in non-US courts.
  • Discovery/disclosure: US pre-trial discovery is vastly more extensive and costly than non-US disclosure processes. Even defending a US claim generates massive legal costs regardless of outcome.
  • Class actions: Rule 23 federal class actions and state equivalents enable aggregation of millions of claims. Non-US jurisdictions have limited or no equivalent mechanism (though EU Representative Actions Directive is a developing parallel).
  • Separate market: US and Canadian liability is covered by UA, UC, UR, US Lloyd's codes — written by dedicated casualty underwriters with US-specific expertise, US-qualified claims staff and US-appropriate rating.
  • Territorial trigger: The exclusion is triggered by (a) the jurisdiction of the injury/damage occurring in the US/Canada, or (b) products intended for US/Canadian market, or (c) claims brought in US/Canadian courts. Wording precision is critical — US courts may accept jurisdiction even for non-US incidents if the defendant has a US presence.
