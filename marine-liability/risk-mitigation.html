Marine Liability Risk Control Framework

Effective marine liability risk management spans flag state regulation, class society surveys, port state control (PSC) inspections, vetting systems (SIRE/CDI/RightShip), ISM Code compliance, STCW crew certification, cyber resilience (BIMCO/COMPIT), and environmental management systems. Lloyd's underwriters expect to see documentary evidence of these controls at placement and renewal. Deficiencies in any tier are material to pricing and coverage terms. VER

Prevent — Reduce Probability & Severity
ISM Code / DOC & SMC

International Safety Management System

Mandatory for ships >500 GT in international trade since 1998 (SOLAS Chapter IX). Shipowner must hold a Document of Compliance (DOC); each vessel a Safety Management Certificate (SMC). ISM audits by flag state or RO every 2.5 years. Absence of valid ISM certification is an automatic referral trigger and may void P&I cover under club rules. VER

SIRE / CDI / RightShip Vetting

Third-Party Tanker & Bulk Vetting

Oil majors require SIRE (tanker) or CDI (chemical tanker) inspection before loading — typically valid 6 months. RightShip provides bulk carrier vetting. A vessel with no recent vetting or a SIRE-flagged report is effectively commercially disabled. Lloyd's underwriters treat an adverse vetting report as equivalent to a PSC deficiency for rating purposes. VER

Class Society Survey

Continuous Machinery Survey & Special Survey

Classification by an IACS member (Lloyd's Register, DNV, BV, ABS, Class NK, etc.) is required for most marine liability placements. Special surveys at 5-year intervals check structural, mechanical, and safety systems. Vessels under-classed or with outstanding conditions are excluded from standard market terms. Class notation for ice, DP, chemical or gas tankers affects underwriting approach significantly. VER

MARPOL Pollution Prevention

International Convention for Prevention of Pollution

MARPOL Annexes I–VI regulate oil, bulk liquid, packaged goods, sewage, garbage, and air emissions. Double-hull construction (MARPOL 13G, since 2010 single-hull tankers phased out) materially reduces accidental oil pollution risk. Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan (SOPEP) required >400 GT. Compliance documentation reviewed at underwriting; deficiencies indicate elevated pollution exposure. VER

STCW Crew Certification

Standards of Training, Certification & Watchkeeping

Manila Amendments (2010) updated STCW competence requirements. Officers must hold flag-approved STCW certificates for the vessel type and route. Masters, chief officers, and engineers on tankers require additional advanced STCW endorsements (ARPA, GMDSS, chemical/oil tanker). Crew manning below STCW minimum is grounds for PSC detention and voids most marine liability policies. VER

BMP6 & HOA Security

Best Management Practices — High Risk Areas

BMP6 (2023 edition) updated guidance for Gulf of Aden, Red Sea, Arabian Sea, Indian Ocean. Measures include: citadel construction, razor wire, self-activating fog signals, night-vision equipment, 24/7 bridge watch, UKMTO reporting, convoy participation. Vessels transiting HOA without BMP6 compliance receive a surcharge of 15–40% on GX excess layers and may face coverage sub-limits on crew injury arising from piracy/attack. VER

Detect — Early Warning & Monitoring
Port State Control (PSC) Inspection Tracking

Paris/Tokyo MOU Real-Time Monitoring

PSC inspections are conducted by flag states in 9 regional MOUs (Paris, Tokyo, Indian Ocean, etc.). Deficiencies are public via Paris MOU / Tokyo MOU online databases. A vessel with a detention in the prior 12 months triggers immediate referral. Underwriters should conduct pre-bind PSC checks using equasismou.org and THETIS. Three or more deficiencies in 24 months = material derating or exclusion. VER

AIS / Dark Vessel Monitoring

Automatic Identification System & Satellite Tracking

AIS transponders required >300 GT international voyages (SOLAS V). AIS blackouts — intentional signal suppression — are a red flag for sanctions-evasion or dark fleet operations. Satellite AIS (S-AIS) via providers such as Spire or exactEarth enables detection of AIS manipulation. A vessel found to have engaged in ship-to-ship (STS) transfers with AIS switched off is presumptive evidence of sanctions violation and grounds for immediate coverage avoidance. VER

Cyber Threat Monitoring — BIMCO COMPIT

Onboard Cyber Risk Management

IMO resolution MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3 requires cyber risk management in ISM SMS from January 2021. BIMCO's COMPIT (Computer & IT Security for Ships) framework provides practical shipboard guidance: network segmentation between OT (navigation/propulsion/ECDIS) and IT (crew/admin) systems, regular penetration testing for voyage management systems, update patching schedules, crew cyber awareness training. NotPetya (2017, $250–300m marine exposure) demonstrated consequences of unpatched systems. VER

Incident Reporting & Near-Miss Systems

ISM Non-Conformity Reporting & FNOL Protocols

ISM Chapter 9 requires ships to report accidents and hazardous situations to the DPA (Designated Person Ashore). Near-miss reporting culture — encouraged by IMO GISIS database — reduces accident frequency by identifying precursors before incidents occur. From an underwriting perspective, a company with a robust near-miss reporting record demonstrates safety culture maturity. Absence of any near-miss reports is a red flag (under-reporting, not absence of incidents). EST

Respond — Claims Handling & Limitation
Immediate FNOL & Lloyd's Broker Notification

First Notice of Loss — Timing is Critical

Most marine liability policies require notification within 24–72 hours of an incident likely to give rise to a claim. Pollution incidents under OPA 90 (US) require FNOL to the USCG National Response Center within one hour of discovery — failure attracts civil penalties. For crew injury, notification under MLC 2006 must include immediate medical care provision. Brokers should have a 24/7 FNOL contact protocol in their service agreement. VER

Limitation of Liability — Filing & Fund

LLMC 1976/1996 Protocol — Limitation Action

Shipowners seeking to limit liability under LLMC 1996 must file in the admiralty court of the relevant jurisdiction and constitute a limitation fund (SDR 3.02m for ships <500 GT, scaling upward). US shipowners in US courts may seek limitation under the US Limitation of Liability Act 1851 (46 USC §30501) — though OPA 90 eliminates limitation for pollution. P&I clubs and Lloyd's GX underwriters should be informed immediately of any limitation action, as the fund size affects priority of claims. VER

Salvage & Wreck Removal Coordination

LOF / SCOPIC & Nairobi Wreck Removal

Following a casualty, salvage under Lloyds Open Form (LOF) with SCOPIC clause is the most common commercial arrangement. SCOPIC protects marine environmental salvage contractors when cargo/hull value is insufficient to remunerate under Lloyd's standard. Wreck removal liability under the Nairobi Convention (2007, 55+ states) can be separate from and in addition to hull and P&I claims. Underwriters should engage specialist P&I correspondents and salvage arbitrators at earliest opportunity. VER

Pollution Response — SOPEP & SMFF Activation

Spill Response Resources — OPA 90 & CLC Compliance

OPA 90 requires US-flagged and US-trading vessels to pre-contract with a Salvage and Marine Firefighting (SMFF) provider and file a Vessel Response Plan (VRP) with the USCG. CLC states require similar arrangements via IOPC Fund mechanisms. Activation of pre-contracted SMFF is a condition precedent to OPA 90 liability limitation. Pollution response costs (booming, skimming, shoreline cleanup) are often the largest component of a pollution liability loss, exceeding third-party property damage. VER

Recover — Post-Loss & Subrogation
Subrogation — Collision / RDC Recovery

Recovery Against Negligent Third Vessel

The RDC (Running Down Clause) covers collision liability to the other vessel — typically 3/4ths under the standard collision clause, with the remaining 1/4 falling on P&I. Full RDC coverage (4/4ths) is available on Lloyd's G placements. Post-collision, underwriters should pursue subrogation against the other vessel's hull and P&I insurers. Cross-liability clauses apply when both vessels are insured by the same club or syndicate. VER

Post-Incident ISM Review

Non-Conformity Investigation & Corrective Action

ISM Chapter 9 requires a formal investigation of every accident with corrective action planning. Underwriters should request the ISM non-conformity investigation report at next renewal and treat the quality of the corrective action as a material rating factor. A company that conducts a superficial investigation or fails to identify systemic causes is at significantly higher risk of recurrence. EST

Structured Settlement & IOPC Fund Interface

CLC / IOPC Fund Coordination for Pollution Losses

For oil pollution losses in CLC states, the IOPC Fund 1992 provides top-up compensation above the shipowner's CLC limit (up to SDR 203m first tier; SDR 750m supplementary fund for major incidents). Lloyd's GX underwriters writing excess layers should understand the IOPC Fund intervention procedure to avoid double-payment or coverage gaps. Fund claims are filed with the IOPC Fund Secretariat in London; claims committees assess admissibility. VER

Reputation & Crew Welfare Management

MLC 2006 — Abandonment & Repatriation Obligations

MLC 2006 Standard A4.2 requires financial security for seafarer abandonment (repatriation + 4 months' unpaid wages). The ITF-operated Seafarer Abandonment database recorded 900+ abandonment cases 2012–2022 (many COVID-related). Flags state financial security certificates (from Lloyd's or P&I clubs) are now required by 95+ states. Underwriters writing MLC-related liability must verify current financial security certificates are in force before binding. VER

Claim Guidance — Before, During & After

Marine liability claims require rapid multi-jurisdictional response. Jurisdictional choices (admiralty court, limitation fund state, IOPC Fund) can be determinative of outcome. EST

Before a Claim — Underwriting Pre-Bind

  1. Verify ISM DOC and SMC validity dates; request class certificates and special survey schedule.
  2. Check PSC detention history via THETIS (EU) and Paris/Tokyo MOU online portals — last 36 months.
  3. Confirm SIRE/CDI/RightShip vetting status for tankers and bulk carriers — last inspection date and any adverse findings.
  4. Obtain loss runs for all marine liability classes — minimum 5 years; check for frequency pattern or large single event.
  5. Verify OPA 90 COFR (US-trading vessels), CLC certificate, Bunkers Convention certificate, Nairobi WRC certificate as applicable.
  6. Check MLC financial security certificate — issued by P&I club or Lloyd's syndicate; current date of validity.
  7. For GX excess: obtain full underlying programme schedule (club certificate, fixed-premium primary, intermediate layers) and attach as endorsement.
  8. For vessels trading HOA/Red Sea: confirm BMP6 compliance, citadel arrangements, UKMTO registration.

During a Claim — Active Management

  1. FNOL within policy notification period — typically 24–72 hours. US pollution: USCG NRC notification within 1 hour statutory obligation.
  2. Appoint specialist marine lawyer in jurisdiction of incident — admiralty counsel (US) or specialist P&I solicitor (UK/EU).
  3. Preserve all voyage data recorder (VDR) evidence — flag state, AAIB/MAIB, US NTSB/USCG investigators will seek VDR under port state authority.
  4. File for limitation of liability at earliest opportunity if loss likely to exceed SIR — constituting a fund prevents arrest of other vessels in fleet.
  5. Activate Vessel Response Plan and SMFF contractor (US OPA 90 obligation) or SOPEP (MARPOL); notify IOPC Fund if CLC-covered incident.
  6. Manage crew welfare — MLC 2006 medical care obligation; repatriation arrangements; avoid coercive crew statement-taking without legal advice.
  7. Coordinate with club (if dual P&I and fixed-premium placement) to avoid gaps and double-notification obligations.
  8. For GX excess: notify immediately if underlying layers are likely to be exhausted — excess layer underwriters have independent notification rights.
⚠ OPA 90 Strict Liability Warning: In US waters, the Oil Pollution Act 1990 eliminates the ability of a responsible party to limit liability for oil pollution removal costs and damages. Limitation under LLMC 1996 does not apply to OPA 90 claims. This means a VLCC oil spill in US waters can result in unlimited removal cost liability, natural resource damages assessed by NOAA and USFWS, and third-party economic damages (fishing, tourism). The Exxon Valdez ultimately cost ~$4.3bn in removal, litigation, and NRD — adjusted to today's values well in excess of $8bn. No GX excess programme can be adequately structured without separate analysis of OPA 90 worst-case discharge scenarios. VER
⚠ Dark Fleet / Sanctions Risk Warning: Vessels suspected of transporting Russian, Iranian, or Venezuelan oil outside the IG P&I system present compounding risks for adjacent operators: uninsured bunker spills, unreported casualty risk, and potential OFAC/SDN sanctions secondary liability. Lloyd's market participants must conduct enhanced due diligence on any vessel that has engaged in STS transfers in AIS-dark zones, called at sanctioned ports (Feodosiya, Bandar Abbas, Puerto Cabello), or changed flag/ownership in the prior 12 months. The LMA war and sanctions exclusions (LMA3100) apply across all marine classes. VER
