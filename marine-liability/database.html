2024Dali — Francis Scott Key Bridge Collapse, Baltimore COLLCAT $2–4bn (est.)G / GXEST
CauseContainer ship Dali lost power/propulsion, drifted into Francis Scott Key Bridge, Baltimore. Bridge collapsed; 6 construction workers killed. Port of Baltimore closed 6 weeks.
Liability ExposureBridge reconstruction ($1.7–2bn), wrongful death (6 claims), port disruption/cargo delay, wreck/bridge debris removal, salvage. Britannia P&I (vessel club) filed limitation of liability in Maryland federal court.
Coverage CodeG/GX — collision/FFO liability. OPA 90 unlimited pollution rules potentially overlay if bunkers spilled. LLMC 1976 limitation ($44m) dwarfed by claims — US courts likely to apply OPA 90 unlimited for pollution element.
Market ImpactLargest-ever US marine liability event. Raised industry-wide questions about adequacy of IG Pool limit ($3.1bn) for mega-vessel catastrophic collisions. GX excess capacity immediately reviewed.
2012Costa Concordia — 32 Deaths, Giglio Island, Italy CREWCAT €1.5bn (~$1.7bn)G / GXVER
CauseCaptain Schettino ordered unauthorized route deviation too close to Giglio. Grounded, capsized. 32 deaths; master convicted, sentenced to 16 years imprisonment.
Liability ComponentsPassenger/crew death/injury (Athens Convention SDR 250,000/passenger — not yet in force in Italy at time); wreck removal (€600m — largest single wreck removal operation ever); hull write-off €450m.
Coverage CodeG/GX — passenger liability; crew liability; wreck removal. Costa Cruises (Carnival subsidiary) insured via Gard P&I Club. Lloyd's GX participated in excess layers.
Market ImpactAccelerated mandatory Athens Convention implementation in EU. Established wreck removal as multi-hundred-million dollar liability category. Triggered redesign of cruise ship mustering and evacuation procedures.
1991M/T Haven — 144,000 Tonnes, Gulf of Genoa OILCAT $5–6bn (est.)G / GXEST
CauseExplosion and fire on Iranian tanker after internal explosion during cargo operations. Sank; 144,000 tonnes crude oil contaminated Ligurian/French Riviera coast.
Liability ComponentsCLC and Fund claims exhausted; Italian government provided supplementary compensation. Total liability exceeded international fund limits at time. Became reference event for IG Pool limit adequacy discussions.
Coverage CodeG/GX — oil pollution CLC; wreck removal. Demonstrated catastrophic accumulation when pollution + wreck removal + cargo + crew liabilities combined in single event.
Market ImpactDrove significant increase in IG Pool limit. Led to 1992 Protocol increasing CLC/Fund limits. Key reference case for GX excess underwriters sizing catastrophe scenarios.
2002Prestige — 63,000 Tonnes, Galicia, Spain OILCAT €4bn+ ($4.5bn)G / GXVER
CauseSingle-hull tanker structural failure in Atlantic storm; Spanish authorities refused harbour of refuge. Vessel towed offshore, broke in two. 63,000 tonnes heavy fuel oil contaminated Galicia and French Atlantic coast.
LiabilitySpanish government sought €4bn+ including state clean-up, fisheries compensation, tourism losses. Criminal prosecution of master (initially guilty, later acquitted on appeal), Spanish maritime director. AXA Corporate Solutions (P&I club reinsurer) disputed liability.
Coverage CodeG/GX — CLC pollution; criminal indemnity (where insured). Significant dispute over whether P&I coverage extends to criminal fines/penalties.
Market ImpactTriggered EU ban on single-hull tankers by 2010 (accelerated from IMO 2015 schedule). Established criminal prosecution of seafarers for pollution as real exposure — personal criminal indemnity cover became key P&I feature.
2021Ever Given — Suez Canal; SCA $916m Claim WRECKMAJ Confidential settlementG / GXVER
CauseULCS Ever Given (220,000 DWT) grounded in Suez Canal during sandstorm. Canal blocked 6 days. Salvage took 10 tugs and dredgers. SCA initially demanded $916m including salvage, compensation, and "reputation damage".
LiabilityUK P&I Club filed limitation of liability action. Settlement reached June 2021 (terms undisclosed; reported ~$200m). No cargo liability claims accepted — BoL force majeure/deviation defences upheld. Salvage award ~$150m.
Coverage CodeG (P&I — FFO/wreck removal); GX (excess layers for large ULCS). Demonstrated that canal wreck removal is a distinct major liability category not adequately modelled pre-2021.
Market ImpactHighlighted adequacy concerns for ultra-large vessel P&I limits. GX excess pricing for ULCS/mega-vessel class increased 15–25% post-incident.
1999Erika — 15,000 Tonnes, Brittany; Total SA Liable OILMAJ €193m ($215m)G / GXVER
CauseSingle-hull tanker broke apart in storm south of Brittany. 15,000 tonnes heavy fuel contaminated 400km coast. Total SA (charterer) found civilly liable by French Court of Cassation — groundbreaking charterer liability ruling.
LiabilityFrench courts: Total SA fined €375,000 (maximum criminal), ordered to pay €171m civil compensation. IOPC Fund compensated remaining victims. Total cost €193m+.
Coverage CodeG — CLC (tanker owner); G/GX — charterer liability (new category established by Erika ruling). Charterer liability now explicitly addressed in Lloyd's marine liability wordings.
Market ImpactCreated charterer liability as discrete insurable exposure. Led to charterer's P&I becoming standard insurance requirement in time-charter parties.
2011MV Rena — NZ Wreck Removal NZ$700m+ WRECKMAJ NZ$700m (~$450m)G / GXVER
CauseContainership grounded on Ōtāiti/Astrolabe Reef. 11,500 tonnes fuel oil. Wreck removal complex — reef location, vessel split in heavy swells. 5-year removal operation.
LiabilitySteamship Mutual (P&I club) met all wreck removal costs: NZ$700m+. First major test of Nairobi Wreck Removal Convention. New Zealand government cooperated with P&I club — model for international wreck removal response.
Coverage CodeG — wreck removal under Nairobi Convention; pollution CLC. Demonstrated that reef/coral wreck removals involve long-tail environmental monitoring costs beyond initial removal.
Market ImpactEstablished Nairobi Convention wreck removal as substantial insurable liability category. Lloyd's GX began specifically modelling reef wreck removal scenarios for Pacific routes.
2022MV X-Press Pearl — Sri Lanka; Nurdles + Nitric Acid OILMAJ $1.5bn+ (disputed)G / GXEST
CauseContainer ship fire off Colombo. Cargo included 25 tonnes nitric acid, 78 tonnes plastic pellets (nurdles). Sank in Sri Lankan EEZ. Environmental damage from nurdles to beaches/fisheries still ongoing (2024).
LiabilitySri Lanka government: $40m initial claim, escalating. Wreck removal ordered by Sri Lanka courts. X-Press Feeders and North P&I Club contest quantum. Litigation ongoing in multiple jurisdictions.
Coverage CodeG — pollution/hazmat; wreck removal. Novel issue: are nurdles/microplastics "oil" under CLC? If not, CLC strict liability does not apply — liability falls under general tort law with limitation rights.
Market ImpactOpened regulatory/legal debate about microplastic liability. Lloyd's underwriters added hazardous cargo questionnaires specifically asking about plastic resin/nurdle cargoes.
2017NotPetya — Maersk $250–300m; Marine Cyber Liability Defined CYBERMAJ $250–300m (Maersk)G / GXVER
CauseNotPetya malware destroyed all Maersk IT. 17 container terminals offline. 45,000 PCs / 4,000 servers reimaged. Maersk losses $250–300m; global shipping disrupted 10 days.
Liability DimensionThird-party cargo delay claims by shippers against Maersk — cargo owners sought compensation for delayed/damaged goods during system outage. Maersk ultimately relied on Hague-Visby Rules exception clauses to limit cargo liability.
Coverage CodeG — cargo liability (cyber event trigger). Insurance dispute whether cyber event was "act of war" (exclusion) or peril. Led to "silent cyber" remediation: NMA 2914 exclusion, then cyber affirmative cover options.
Market ImpactTriggered cyber risk management mandatory under ISM from 2021. Marine cyber liability now explicit underwriting consideration. GC binders required to clarify cyber position on each risk.
2023Collision — VLCC & Chemical Tanker, Strait of Hormuz COLLMAJ ~$150m (est.)G / GXEST
CauseIllustrative composite of high-traffic Strait of Hormuz collision — VLCC and laden chemical tanker. One vessel total loss; 300,000 tonnes crude cargo at risk; chemical cargo release.
Liability ComponentsRDC — hull collision liability (¾ covered by hull; ¼ + FFO by P&I); cargo liability; bunker/chemical pollution (CLC and CDEM); crew injury/fatality.
Coverage CodeG (P&I — cargo, crew, pollution); Hull policy (RDC ¾). Illustrates interface between hull and marine liability policies in collision scenario.
Market ImpactHormuz/Gulf collision frequency remains high. Lloyd's marine liability underwriters maintain specific Strait of Hormuz accumulation monitoring for VLCC concentrations.
2020Cargo Misdelivery — Grain Consignment, Black Sea Port CARGOMAJ ~$60m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative composite: 60,000-tonne bulk grain cargo misdelivered against presentation of fraudulent Bills of Lading. Carrier held liable by English Commercial Court for full cargo value plus interest.
LiabilityCarrier P&I liability for misdelivery: established English law — carrier must only deliver against original BoL. Fraud does not relieve carrier. P&I club indemnified carrier under BoL indemnity provision.
Coverage CodeG — cargo liability (misdelivery). High-frequency, material-severity category for bulk commodity carriers. P&I coverage includes misdelivery but subject to policy conditions on BoL compliance.
Market ImpactDrives P&I club guidance on BoL fraud prevention and switch BoL procedures. GC binders for freight forwarders include cargo liability for misdelivery on FIATA terms.
2019SRL — Floating Drydock Collapse, Major European Shipyard PORTMAJ ~$80m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative: floating drydock structural failure during vessel refit. 15,000 GT cruise ferry sank while in drydock. Damage to vessel plus consequential loss of income for 90 days refit delay.
LiabilityShip Repairer's Liability (SRL): damage to vessel in care/custody/control. SRL policy covered hull damage ($45m). Consequential loss (lost revenue) typically sub-limited or excluded — major gap in SRL coverage.
Coverage CodeG/GC — SRL. Illustrates largest SRL exposure: major vessel loss while in drydock. SRL limits must match vessel value in yard — often under-insured at smaller yards.
Market ImpactSRL is a growing Lloyd's specialty. Cruise ship SRL requires bespoke assessment — vessel values of $300m+ require SRL limits rarely written by smaller yards.
2020Beirut Port Explosion — Terminal Operators Liability Exposure PORTMAJ $3bn+ (total); PTOL portion uncertainGEST
Cause2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate stored unsafely at Beirut port for 6 years. Explosion 4 August 2020: 218 deaths, 6,500 injured, half of Beirut damaged. Total insured loss $3bn+.
PTOL/Marine Liability DimensionPort authority/terminal operator negligence in storage of hazardous cargo. Lebanese government has sought to limit liability; criminal investigations ongoing. PTOL exposure for port authorities holding dangerous goods: major uninsured risk in developing world ports.
Coverage CodeG — PTOL; cargo damage. Most of insured loss fell to property (port infrastructure) rather than marine liability. PTOL carriers assessed whether port authority's liability could be established and insured.
Market ImpactHeightened dangerous cargo storage screening in PTOL underwriting. Ammonium nitrate / explosive cargo storage questionnaire added to PTOL submissions.
2021Crew Abandonment — MV Aman, UAE (MLC Test Case) CREWMOD ~$2m (crew entitlements)G / GCVER
CauseMV Aman abandoned in Dubai with 14-crew crew unpaid for 14 months. Crew filed under UAE/ITF abandonment procedures. MLC 2006 financial certificate (issued by insurer) required to pay crew repatriation, wages, social insurance.
MLC CoverageP&I clubs/Lloyd's G underwriters issue MLC financial security certificates. Abandonment trigger: owner insolvent/unreachable. Certificate must cover minimum 4 months wages + repatriation + medical care.
Coverage CodeG — crew liability (MLC abandonment). GC binders increasingly required to specify MLC certificate issuance capability. Ships without valid MLC certificates detained by port state control.
Market ImpactMLC abandonment claims growing: ITF recorded 103 abandonment cases in 2021. Financial certificate underwriters monitor flag state and class status to predict abandonment risk.
2022RDC — Passenger Ferry & Cargo Vessel, Baltic Sea COLLMOD ~$35m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative Baltic ferry collision: cargo vessel crossed restricted ferry lane in poor visibility. Ferry struck cargo vessel; passenger injuries (12 hospitalised); ferry bow damage; cargo vessel sank.
LiabilityRunning Down Clause (RDC) — hull policy covered ¾ of damage to other vessel; P&I (G) covered: ¼ RDC, passenger injury claims (Athens Convention), cargo vessel wreck removal, oil pollution from cargo vessel.
Coverage CodeG — P&I: ¼ RDC, passenger liability, FFO. Hull policy: ¾ RDC. Illustrates typical split between hull and P&I in a multi-party collision scenario.
Market ImpactBaltic Sea collision frequency driven by traffic density and severe weather. Passenger ferry operators require Athens Convention passenger liability endorsement on G policy.
2023Freight Forwarder Liability — Dangerous Goods Misdeclaration CARGOMOD ~$12m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseFreight forwarder misdeclared lithium battery shipment as "machinery spares." Battery ignition caused container fire mid-voyage. Four containers destroyed; $12m cargo loss. Carrier sought indemnity from forwarder for deck damage.
LiabilityFreight forwarder E&O liability under FIATA Standard T&Cs. FF covered under GC binder (freight forwarder liability scheme). IMDG dangerous goods documentation requirement breached.
Coverage CodeGC — freight forwarder liability; dangerous goods misdeclaration. Growing exposure: lithium battery fires now a significant cargo liability category for FF underwriters.
Market ImpactLithium battery misdeclaration now systematic risk for GC freight forwarder binders. IMDG Class 9 declaration auditing required for FF risks with Asia-Europe trade involvement.
2022Bunker Spill — Chemical Tanker, Singapore Strait OILMOD ~$8m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative: bunker overflow during fuel transfer operation in Singapore Strait. 30 tonnes marine fuel oil spilled; 3km oil slick; MPA Singapore cleanup activation; fishing community compensation.
LiabilityBunker oil pollution under Bunkers Convention 2001 (in force 2008). Vessel owner strictly liable. P&I G policy covers bunker spillage from operational accidents. Singapore MPA recovery costs plus fishing community livelihood claims.
Coverage CodeG — bunker pollution liability (Bunkers Convention — not CLC, which applies only to persistent oil from laden tankers). All ships >1,000 GT require Bunkers Convention financial certificate.
Market ImpactSingapore Strait is highest maritime traffic density globally. Bunker spill frequency elevated. P&I clubs track port agent contact lists for immediate response capability.
2021PTOL — Crane Collapse, Container Terminal (Illustrative) PORTMOD ~$20m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative: ship-to-shore (STS) crane structural failure during container discharge. Crane arm fell onto vessel superstructure. Vessel damage $8m; crane repair $4m; terminal shutdown 3 days; cargo delay claims $8m.
LiabilityPort and Terminal Operators Liability (PTOL). Terminal operator's care/custody/control of vessel during berth operations. Physical damage to vessel covered; consequential loss (freight revenue) sublimited or excluded.
Coverage CodeG/GC — PTOL. Crane operations, gangway incidents, and mooring line failures are primary PTOL frequency events. Vessel damage during berth operations is the highest-value PTOL category.
Market ImpactPort automation increasing crane frequency claims (sensor/LIDAR failures). PTOL underwriters assess terminal QA processes for automated crane operations.
2023Crew Illness — Vessel Quarantine, US Port (Post-COVID) CREWMOD ~$3m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative: crew gastrointestinal illness outbreak on cruise vessel. US Coast Guard ordered quarantine for 5 days in Miami. Cost: medical treatment, replacement crew, passenger refunds/rebooking, reputational management.
LiabilityG — crew medical (P&I). Passenger compensation: Athens Convention (if personal injury); contract law (refund/rebooking). Quarantine costs (medical, port fees during quarantine) covered under P&I.
Coverage CodeG — crew illness/quarantine. Cruise sector retains a separate shore-side quarantine cost exposure not always clearly covered by P&I: medical onboard vs. ashore during quarantine.
Market ImpactCOVID demonstrated that crew illness quarantine is material liability. Cruise sector G premium increased 40–60% 2020–2022 to reflect COVID-era crew and passenger welfare obligations.
2022GPS Spoofing — Black Sea; Vessel Deviation & Cargo Dispute CYBERMOD ~$5m (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseGPS spoofing in Black Sea (Iranian or Russian state-attributed) caused bulk carrier to deviate course and enter territorial waters. Vessel detained 2 weeks; cargo delayed; charterer claimed lost market.
LiabilityMarine cyber event — cargo deviation/delay liability. Carrier argued cyber event = force majeure/peril of the sea. Charterer argued operational failure. P&I club disputed whether GPS spoofing creates insured cargo liability or falls under cyber exclusion.
Coverage CodeG — cargo liability (cyber trigger). Illustrates cyber/marine liability interface. BIMCO COMPIT cyber resilience management clause increasingly required by charterers.
Market ImpactGPS spoofing events in Black Sea, Persian Gulf, and Mediterranean confirmed as systematic threat. Lloyd's marine cyber questionnaire now asks about bridge/navigation redundancy systems.
2021MV X-Press Pearl Nurdles — Sri Lanka Beach Contamination OILMOD $40–100m (disputed)G / GXEST
Cause78 tonnes plastic pellets (nurdles) released from burning/sinking X-Press Pearl. Sri Lanka beaches contaminated. 200km coastline affected; fisheries closed. Novel: are nurdles "oil" under CLC? If not, unlimited liability may apply under general tort.
LiabilitySri Lanka government: $40m initial IOPC claim; actual claim may be $100m+. North P&I Club and X-Press Feeders contesting all quantum. Environmental damage ongoing (microplastics in marine food chain).
Coverage CodeG — pollution (contested whether CLC or general tort). Demonstrates legal uncertainty around non-oil pollutant liabilities for carriers.
Market ImpactNurdle/microplastic cargo now flagged in GC binder submissions. Environmental monitoring costs (ongoing) create long-tail liability uncertainty beyond standard P&I annual renewal.
2023Yacht — Collision with Cargo Vessel, Mediterranean COLLMIN ~$800k (illustrative)GCILLUS
CauseIllustrative: superyacht (45m LOA) under autopilot struck anchored cargo vessel at 0200. Cargo vessel bow damage $400k; crew injury on cargo vessel; superyacht damage $200k; oil spill cleanup $50k.
LiabilityGC binder (yacht P&I/third-party liability). Collision liability (RDC-equivalent for yachts); third-party BI (cargo vessel crew); bunker spill. Yacht owner's policy covered collision liability.
Coverage CodeGC — yacht third-party/P&I. Large superyacht GC binders (>24m LOA) carry significant per-occurrence limits ($50m+). Collision with commercial vessel is highest-severity yacht liability scenario.
Market ImpactSuperyacht fleet growth (5,000+ vessels >24m) expanding GC premium volume. Autonomous navigation systems on superyachts creating new liability questions around watchkeeping failures.
2022Stevedore Injury — Container Terminal, UK PORTMIN ~$350k (illustrative)G / GCILLUS
CauseStevedore crushed between container and stanchion during vessel discharge operation. Serious leg injury; 18-month rehabilitation; employer liability (Employers' Liability Act) and possible vessel/terminal liability claims.
LiabilityPTOL — stevedore personal injury. UK terminal operator's PTOL policy. EL insurer also involved (employer's liability). Interface between PTOL and EL common in UK port injury claims.
Coverage CodeG/GC — PTOL (third-party BI for terminal operations). High-frequency attritional category for UK/European terminal operators.
Market ImpactStevedore injury is highest-frequency PTOL claim type. Terminal automation (AGVs, automated stacking cranes) reducing frequency but increasing severity when incidents do occur.
2023MTO Liability — Temperature Excursion, Pharma Cold Chain CARGOMIN ~$2m (illustrative)GCILLUS
CauseMulti-modal transport operator (MTO) failed to maintain reefer container temperature during transshipment delay at hub port. Pharmaceutical cargo ($1.8m value) temperature-compromised and destroyed. Shipper claimed full cargo value plus consequential losses ($200k).
LiabilityMTO/freight forwarder liability (FIATA Standard T&Cs + GC binder). Temperature excursion during MTO custody — MTO liable. CMR sub-limit applied to road segment. Total claim $2m vs. FIATA per-kg limit dispute.
Coverage CodeGC — freight forwarder/MTO liability. Pharmaceutical cold chain is highest-severity FF liability category — per-kg values far exceed standard commodity rates.
Market ImpactPharma cold chain FF liability: growing GC specialty. Separate pharma cold chain freight forwarder facility with higher per-kg limits than standard GC binder now common.
2023Houthi Attack — Crew Injury & War Risk/P&I Interface CREWMOD ~$15m per incident (est.)G / GXEST
CauseHouthi missile/drone strike on commercial vessel in Red Sea. Crew injuries; vessel hull damage. Interface question: does crew injury in a war risk event fall to: (a) P&I club (G — crew liability), (b) war risk underwriters (W/WX), or (c) both?
LiabilityP&I clubs generally cover crew injury/death regardless of war risk cause (crew welfare obligation). War risk hull policy covers vessel damage. Interface: crew personal accident cover (KA/KB) vs P&I crew liability (G).
Coverage CodeG — crew liability. War risk hull (W) — vessel damage. Demonstrates importance of coordinated P&I + war risk placement for high-risk route trading.
Market ImpactHouthi campaign materially increased crew risk premiums for Red Sea transits. Many seafarers refused Red Sea assignments — crew abandonment and hardship pay claims tested G policy limits.
2020MV Wakashio — Mauritius Oil Spill; 1,000t Bunker Oil OILMOD ~$30mGVER
CauseBulk carrier grounded and broke apart on reef off Mauritius; ~1,000 tonnes bunker oil spilled. Significant damage to coral reef ecosystems and Blue Bay Marine Park. Japan P&I Club (Mitsui Sumitomo) — vessel P&I insurer — met cleanup and compensation costs.
LiabilityBunkers Convention 2001 — strict liability for bunker spill. Wreck removal costs separate. Japanese government sent rapid response team. Mauritius fishing community and tourism claims ongoing. Total insured cost ~$30m.
Coverage CodeG — bunker pollution; wreck removal. Reef location significantly increased wreck removal complexity and cost. Coral damage claims (long-tail) may extend beyond initial P&I settlement.
Market ImpactDemonstrated reef-adjacent wreck removal cost escalation. Pacific/Indian Ocean routing risks: reef proximity now a specific underwriting consideration for vessel age/class combinations.
2019MV Solomon Trader — Oil Spill, Solomon Islands EEZ WRECKMOD ~$10mGEST
CauseBulk carrier grounded on reef, Solomon Islands. ~75 tonnes oil spilled. Vessel not entered in IG P&I club — uninsured or underinsured. Solomon Islands government sought compensation; Australian government assisted in cleanup.
LiabilityDemonstrates risk when vessel is outside IG P&I system. Flag state (Tuvalu) limited resources. Environmental cleanup costs partially unmet. Local fishing community compensation insufficient. OAE (Old, Abandoned, Equivalent) vessels outside formal insurance: growing liability gap.
Coverage CodeG — bunker/oil pollution; wreck removal. Vessel not insured = no P&I response. ITF/IMO dark fleet concern. Lloyd's market not involved; illustrates why IG P&I entry is critical to market functioning.
Market ImpactDark fleet and ageing uninsured vessels a growing environmental liability gap. Lloyd's market actively supporting IMO regulation of "non-club" vessels.
2018SS El Faro — Wrongful Death; 33 Crew Lost CARGOMOD ~$100m (wrongful death)GVER
CauseUS-flagged RoRo cargo vessel El Faro lost all 33 crew in Hurricane Joaquin, October 2015. NTSB investigation found inadequate weather routing, poor risk culture, ISM failures. Sank in 4,600m water; voyage data recorder (VDR) recovered by deep-sea ROV.
LiabilityUS Death on High Seas Act (DOHSA) — families of 33 crew. TOTE Services (operator) settled wrongful death claims for reported $110m+. ISM Code failures central to liability finding.
Coverage CodeG — crew wrongful death (US DOHSA). US-flag vessels subject to US admiralty law limits different from international LLMC. DOHSA claims for non-pecuniary damages: loss of companionship etc.
Market ImpactEl Faro demonstrated ISM failure creates massive crew liability. ISM SMS audit now required annually by Lloyd's marine liability underwriters for US-flag risks.
2017USS John S. McCain / USS Fitzgerald — USS Collisions with Commercial Vessels COLLMOD $300m+ (US Navy)GVER
CauseUSS Fitzgerald struck ACX Crystal (container vessel) off Japan, June 2017: 7 US Navy deaths. USS John S. McCain struck Alnic MC (tanker) off Singapore, August 2017: 10 US Navy deaths. Both attributed to US Navy watchkeeping/navigation failures.
LiabilityCommercial vessel owners (Crystal/Alnic) incurred P&I liability for hull repairs to warships — unusual scenario. US Navy paid $162m for John McCain crew injuries/deaths. Commercial vessels' P&I clubs engaged for hull damage allocation.
Coverage CodeG — RDC/collision; military vessel collision (unusual category). Demonstrates that commercial vessels can be partially at fault in military collisions. COLREGS compliance critical for P&I defence.
Market ImpactUS Navy traffic density in Western Pacific/Strait of Malacca flagged as elevated collision risk for commercial vessels transiting these routes.
2024Dark Fleet Vessel — Bunker Spill, Denmark (Uninsured) OILMIN ~$2m (Danish state cost)G (not placed)EST
CauseAged Russian shadow fleet tanker suffered structural failure, spilled bunker oil in Danish EEZ. Vessel uninsured (non-IG P&I). No financial certificate. Denmark DEMA (emergency management) responded; costs unrecoverable from vessel owner.
LiabilityUninsured — no P&I club response. Bunkers Convention requires financial certificate but enforcement at sea is difficult. Danish state bears unrecoverable cleanup costs estimated €2m.
Coverage CodeN/A — vessel not insured. Illustrates the dark fleet liability gap growing as 600–1,400 vessels operate outside IG P&I system following Russia sanctions.
Market ImpactLloyd's market tracking dark fleet incident frequency. Increasing political pressure for IMO enforcement of financial responsibility requirements on non-IG vessels.
