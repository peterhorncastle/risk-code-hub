Marine Liability Programme Structures

Marine liability insurance for ocean-going vessels typically involves a layered structure combining mutual P&I club cover with Lloyd's fixed-premium placements. The precise architecture depends on vessel type, trading area, flag state certificate requirements, and the shipowner's appetite for mutual governance versus fixed-premium certainty. Lloyd's codes G, GC, and GX each play a distinct role within a complete marine liability programme. EST

Structure 1 — Standard

IG P&I Club — Full Mutual Entry

Shipowner enters vessel into an International Group member club. Club provides P&I (injury, pollution, collision 1/4th) up to the pool/reinsurance limit. Appropriate for standard ocean-going commercial tonnage >500 GT seeking mutual governance, pooling, and unlimited (in practice) cover. No Lloyd's G involvement except as GX reinsurer to the pool.

Structure 2 — Fixed Premium Primary

Lloyd's G Fixed-Premium — Full P&I Equivalent

Shipowner places full P&I-equivalent cover directly in Lloyd's market under code G. Common for: smaller fleets that prefer fixed renewal certainty; vessels ineligible for IG club entry (age, flag, class); yachts; fishing vessels; inland waterway craft; specialist workboats. Limit typically $100m–$500m. No supplementary calls.

Structure 3 — Hybrid

Club Primary + Lloyd's GX Excess Layer

Club provides primary P&I up to pool limit; Lloyd's GX provides excess above the pool for VLCC/ULCC tankers, cruise lines, and large containerships where exposures exceed the $3.1bn IG pool. Also used for specific perils excluded or sub-limited by the club (e.g., US Gulf pollution excess, Athens Convention passenger liability top-up).

Structure 4 — Coverholder / Binder

Lloyd's GC Coverholder — SRL / PTOL / Yacht Schemes

A Lloyd's approved coverholder (marine broker or MGA) binds Shiprepairers' Liability (SRL), Port/Terminal/Operator Liability (PTOL), Freight Forwarder (FF), or Yacht schemes under an annual binder. Coverholder underwrites individual risks within agreed parameters; Lloyd's syndicates provide capacity. Provides market access for smaller operators and specialist sub-classes.

8-Step Placement Workflow

Standard Lloyd's marine liability placement process for a fleet account (G primary + GX excess). EST

1

Fleet & Risk ProfilingCompile vessel schedule (name, IMO, flag, GT, type, build year, class society, trading area). Obtain loss runs (5 years, all classes). Confirm IG club status and existing certificates.

2

Certificate & Compliance AuditVerify ISM DOC/SMC, COFR (if US trading), CLC, Bunkers, Nairobi WRC, MLC financial security certificates — all current and correctly issued. Note any expirations within programme period.

3

Vetting & PSC ReviewRun PSC detention checks (THETIS, Paris/Tokyo MOU portals) for last 36 months. Request SIRE/CDI/RightShip inspection reports for tankers and bulk carriers. Flag any adverse findings to lead underwriter.

4

Slip PreparationDraft Lloyd's slip with full risk details: vessel schedule, limits, deductibles, trading warranties, sanctions clause (LMA3100), ISM warranty, class warranty, COFR confirmation, war exclusion (LMA3030).

5

Lead Underwriter EngagementPresent to chosen lead syndicate (typically a marine specialist). Negotiate rate, terms, deductibles, and any bespoke endorsements (OPA 90 COFR guaranty, HOA BMP6 warranty, dark fleet AIS warranty).

6

Following Market PlacementWith lead scratching, approach following market to complete line. GX excess layers placed simultaneously or sequentially. Lloyd's China and international markets may participate as following market.

7

Certificate IssuanceOn binding, issue CLC, Bunkers, Nairobi WRC, MLC, and Athens Convention certificates via Lloyd's wordings team or specialist certificate-issuing bureau. COFR guaranty submitted to USCG where applicable.

8

Renewal Management & Mid-Term MonitoringTrack PSC inspections, SIRE/CDI updates, and any mid-term incidents monthly. Prepare renewal submission 90 days before expiry. Flag any sanctions-screening changes in fleet ownership or management.

Case Study: Adriatica Shipping SRL — P&I Restructuring

Illustrative Case Study — Fixed-Premium Migration

Adriatica Shipping SRL: Migrating from P&I Club to Lloyd's Fixed-Premium (G)

A Greek-managed fleet of six general cargo vessels (aggregate 42,000 GT) previously entered with a mid-tier IG P&I club. The club issued a supplementary call of 22.5% following a heavy claims year, and subsequently declined renewal for three vessels over 22 years of age. The fleet operator approached the Lloyd's market through a specialist marine broker seeking fixed-premium certainty.

Underwriting Approach: Lloyd's lead syndicate accepted the fleet under code G with the following conditions: (i) vessels over 20 years subject to class survey condition within 90 days; (ii) Black Sea trading warranty (no Azov Sea or Ukrainian port calls without prior approval post-2022); (iii) ISM warranty with DOC/SMC copies attached; (iv) $50,000 deductible per claim; (v) annual PSC inspection report to be provided at first request. Age factors applied: three youngest vessels 1.0×, two mid-age 1.35×, one oldest vessel 2.0×. MLC financial security certificates issued by the Lloyd's syndicate.

Outcome: Lloyd's fixed-premium G placement: €$527,000 p.a. (vs prior club advance call ~€480k before supplementary call). Fleet operator gained fixed-cost certainty, avoided supplementary call exposure, and retained coverage for the three older vessels declined by the club. Age-loading on the oldest vessel was accepted as commercially preferable to non-renewal. ISM class condition fulfilled at 60 days.
⚠ DIC/DIL Gap Warning: Where a fleet is entered part in a P&I club and part in Lloyd's fixed-premium (G), differences in conditions (DIC) and differences in limits (DIL) must be carefully reviewed. P&I club rules contain mutual governance provisions — supplementary calls, cesser of cover for non-payment, discretionary cover for war risks — that may not align with Lloyd's fixed-premium policy terms. A claim arising from a peril that is discretionary under club rules but excluded under the Lloyd's policy (or vice versa) can create an uncovered gap. Programme architects must obtain and reconcile full copies of both the club rules and the Lloyd's policy wording before finalising the programme.
⚠ Sanctions & Dark Fleet Compliance: All Lloyd's marine liability programmes must include the LMA Sanctions Limitation and Exclusion Clause (LMA3100). Underwriters must conduct enhanced due diligence before binding or renewing any risk with connections to: vessels previously flagged by OFAC, EU, or UK sanctions lists; fleets managed by entities with Russian, Iranian, or Venezuelan ownership links; vessels that have conducted ship-to-ship (STS) transfers in AIS-dark zones in the past 12 months. The Lloyd's Market Association Sanctions Advisory (updated 2024) provides current guidance. Failure to conduct adequate sanctions screening exposes syndicates to regulatory action from the FCA and US OFAC. VER
