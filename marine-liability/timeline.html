20-Year+ Timeline of Major Marine Liability Events

Key incidents and regulatory milestones that shaped marine liability insurance from 1967 to 2024. VER for court-verified events; EST for estimated loss figures. Filter by loss type.

1967OIL
Torrey Canyon — World's First Major Supertanker Casualty
119,000 tonnes crude oil spilled off Cornwall and Brittany. UK government bombed the wreck. Total cost ~£8m (£120m+ 2024 values). Triggered 1969 CLC and IOPC Fund conventions. Established principle of strict liability for tanker pollution. VER
1978OIL
Amoco Cadiz — 227,000 Tonnes Oil, Brittany
Total mechanical failure off Portsall, France. 227,000 tonnes crude contaminated 360km of Breton coastline. French courts awarded Ffr 1.8bn in compensation. Exposed inadequacy of then-CLC Fund limits. Led to major revision of the IOPC Fund system. VER
1989REG
Exxon Valdez & the Road to OPA 90
37,000 tonnes crude spilled in Prince William Sound, Alaska. Total cost $3.8bn+ including punitive damages of $5bn (reduced to $507m by Supreme Court 2008). Catastrophic ecological damage. Directly triggered Oil Pollution Act 1990 (OPA 90) which eliminated US liability limitation for oil pollution. VER
1990REG
OPA 90 — Oil Pollution Act Signed into US Law
OPA 90 eliminated limitation of liability for oil pollution in US waters. Required Certificate of Financial Responsibility (COFR) — mandating P&I club or equivalent insurance. Established $1bn+ Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund. Fundamentally changed US marine liability market structure. VER
1991OIL
M/T Haven — Largest Post-War Marine Liability Loss
Italian tanker exploded and sank off Genoa, releasing approximately 144,000 tonnes of crude. Total insured liability estimated at $5–6bn (multiple claims; CLC/Fund limits exceeded). Required supplementary Italian government funding. Remains reference case for extreme marine liability accumulation. EST
1998REG
ISM Code Mandatory for All Commercial Vessels >500 GT
International Safety Management Code (ISM Code) under SOLAS Chapter IX became mandatory. Safety Management System (SMS) and Document of Compliance (DOC) now required. ISM certification became prerequisite for P&I club entry and Lloyd's marine liability cover. Non-ISM vessels effectively uninsurable in mainstream market. VER
1999OIL
Erika — 15,000 Tonnes, Brittany; EU Legislative Tsunami
Maltese-flagged tanker broke apart south of Brittany. 15,000 tonnes heavy fuel oil contaminated 400km of coastline. French criminal prosecution of Total SA (charterer) — groundbreaking finding that charterers are civilly liable. Total fines and compensation €193m. Triggered EU Erika I/II/III packages including accelerated single-hull phase-out and EMSA creation. VER
2002OIL
Prestige — 63,000 Tonnes, Galicia; Criminal Prosecution of Master
Single-hull Bahamas-flagged tanker broke in two off Galicia, Spain. 63,000 tonnes heavy fuel oil. Total cost €4bn+; Spanish state compensation programme. Criminal prosecution of master, chief officer, and maritime director resulted in convictions (initially) — creating chilling precedent for personal criminal liability of seafarers for pollution events. VER
2006CREW
Maritime Labour Convention 2006 — Global Crew Welfare Standard
MLC 2006 adopted at ILO Geneva. Consolidated 65 maritime labour conventions into single instrument. Mandatory provisions: work/rest hours, leave, repatriation, medical care, financial security for abandonment. P&I clubs became MLC financial certificate issuers. MLC entered into force August 2013; ratified by nations covering >80% of world tonnage. VER
2012CREW
Costa Concordia — 32 Deaths; Largest Marine Liability Loss
Cruise ship grounded Giglio Island, Italy. 32 deaths; 4,229 people evacuated. Master sentenced to 16 years. Total insured loss ~€1.5bn: hull write-off €450m, liability €470m (passenger/crew), wreck removal €600m. Largest ever marine insured loss. Triggered mandatory implementation of Athens Convention passenger liability in EU. VER
2011WRECK
MV Rena — New Zealand's Worst Maritime Environmental Disaster
Containership grounded Ōtāiti/Astrolabe Reef, New Zealand. 11,500 tonnes fuel oil spilled; 900 containers lost. Wreck removal cost NZ$700m+. First major test of Nairobi Wreck Removal Convention in a State Party. P&I club (Steamship Mutual) met all wreck removal costs. New Zealand declared most expensive wreck removal in Southern Hemisphere at time. VER
2017CYBER
NotPetya Cyberattack — Maersk Loses Navigation & Operations 10 Days
NotPetya malware destroyed Maersk's entire IT infrastructure. 17 terminals worldwide offline; 45,000 PCs, 4,000 servers reimaged. Maersk losses $250–300m. Lloyd's/insurers disputed whether this was covered under marine liability policies — "acts of war" exclusion arguments were ultimately unsuccessful in most courts. Led to NMA 2914 cyber exclusion and the "silent cyber" remediation across marine classes. VER
2019COLL
Viking Sky Near-Sinking — Cruise Passenger Liability Focus
Cruise ship Viking Sky suffered engine failures in severe storm off Norway. 479 passengers airlifted. Ship narrowly avoided grounding. Passenger claim exposures estimated at several hundred million USD if grounding had occurred. Heightened focus on cruise passenger Athens Convention compliance and adequacy of per-passenger limits at SDR 250,000. EST
2021WRECK
Ever Given — Suez Canal Grounding; $916m Claim by SCA
Ultra-large containership blocked Suez Canal for 6 days. Suez Canal Authority initially claimed $916m: $300m for salvage, $300m compensation, $300m for reputation damage, and $16m for canal clean-up. P&I club (UK Club) settled on confidential terms. Economic disruption estimated $9–10bn/day. Demonstrated catastrophic wreck removal and consequential liability potential. VER
2021CARGO
Ever Given — Cargo Delay Claims (60,000+ Containers)
The six-day blockage affected approximately 400 vessels and 60,000+ containers. Cargo interests filed delay claims against the vessel owner/P&I club, but most cargo delay claims were excluded under standard Bills of Lading (force majeure and deviation clauses). Market estimated total cargo liability claim under $50m despite economic disruption far exceeding this. EST
2022OIL
MV X-Press Pearl — Sri Lanka Burning Cargo Container Ship
Container ship caught fire and sank off Colombo, Sri Lanka. Cargo included 25 tonnes nitric acid and 78 tonnes plastic pellets (nurdles). Sri Lankan government claimed $40m initially. Wreck removal ordered; total liability estimate $1.5bn+ including environmental damage. Vessel owners (X-Press Feeders) and P&I club (North P&I) contest liability; litigation ongoing. EST
2023REG
Lloyd's Sanctions & Dark Fleet Underwriting Restrictions
Russia-Ukraine conflict created a "dark fleet" of 600–1,400 vessels transporting Russian oil outside IG P&I Club coverage. These vessels lack: P&I insurance, ISM certificates, class surveys. Lloyd's market issued guidance on sanctions compliance. Several dark fleet vessel incidents (oil spills, groundings) went uninsured. Estimated dark fleet environmental liability exposure: uninsurable / unquantified. EST
2023CREW
Houthi Red Sea Campaign — Crew Abandonment Claims Begin
Houthi attacks on commercial shipping in Red Sea/Gulf of Aden from November 2023. Galaxy Leader seized; crew held captive. Multiple vessels struck by drones/missiles. Crew anxiety claims, hardship pay disputes, and vessel abandonment scenarios tested MLC financial certificate coverage. War risk and P&I interface — who covers crew if vessel is hit — became acute underwriting question. EST
2024COLL
Francis Scott Key Bridge — Baltimore; Vessel Collision with Bridge
Container ship Dali lost power and struck Francis Scott Key Bridge, Baltimore, collapsing it. 6 deaths. Total liability estimate: $2–4bn (bridge reconstruction, workers' compensation, port disruption, cargo delay). Largest-ever marine liability event in US waters. Vessel P&I club (Britannia P&I) filed limitation of liability action in Maryland. Global limitation (LLMC 1976) in tension with US OPA 90 unlimited pollution rules. EST
2024REG
IMO MASS Code — Non-Mandatory Framework for Autonomous Vessels
IMO adopted non-mandatory MASS (Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships) Code at MSC 108 (May 2024). Addresses regulatory gaps for automated and remotely operated vessels. Mandatory MASS Code targeted for 2028. Liability allocation between: owner, operator, software developer, sensor manufacturer creates entirely new marine liability frontier with no settled legal framework. VER
