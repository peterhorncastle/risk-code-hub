Marine Liability Underwriting Framework

Marine liability underwriting at Lloyd's requires a working knowledge of international maritime law, the P&I club system, vessel classification, and the regulatory framework that determines minimum insurance levels. Unlike most other lines, marine liability is heavily convention-driven: MARPOL, CLC, OPA 90, Nairobi, Bunkers, MLC, and Athens Convention each impose compulsory coverage levels that determine minimum underwriting parameters. Lloyd's writers price the risk above these floors and provide coverage for the specialist segments the club system does not serve. EST

P&I (Fixed Premium) — Rating Formula

G — P&I Fixed Premium Annual Rate Premium = Fleet GT × Base Rate per GT × Vessel Type Factor × Age Factor × Flag/Class Factor × Trading Area Factor × Claims Loading × Limit Factor × Deductible Credit

G P&I Rating Factor Cards

Vessel Type Factor

Different vessel types have materially different marine liability profiles. Tankers carry highest pollution exposure; cruise ships carry passenger liability; bulk carriers carry crew and cargo risk.

Yacht: 0.6×  |  Bulk carrier: 1.0× (base)  |  Chemical tanker: 1.8×  |  Cruise: 2.2×  |  VLCC: 2.5×

Flag / Class Factor

Flag state determines enforcement of ISM Code and STCW compliance. Class society determines technical standards. Paris/Tokyo MOU detention rates used as proxy for flag quality.

Top-tier flag (Marshall Islands, Liberia, Panama): 1.0×  |  Mid-tier: 1.1–1.3×  |  Black-listed flag or unclassed: decline or 2.5×+

Age Factor

Vessel age correlates strongly with machinery failure, structural weakness, and crew competence. Age >20 years significantly increases both frequency and severity of marine liability events.

<5 years: 0.85×  |  5–10yr: 1.0×  |  10–15yr: 1.2×  |  15–20yr: 1.5×  |  >20yr: 1.8–2.5×

Trading Area / Route Risk

Route determines: pollution jurisdiction (OPA 90 for US waters; CLC for most others), port state control inspection frequency, piracy exposure (Red Sea/Gulf of Aden), collision density (Malacca, Dover, Hormuz).

Worldwide incl. US: 1.3×  |  European trades: 1.0×  |  Inland/restricted: 0.8×  |  US Gulf: 1.4× (OPA 90)

Claims History Loading

5-year loss run analysis on current and prior club entries. Large loss development (occurrence basis) reviewed. Club call history reviewed where mutual — indicates actual claims spend vs. premium.

Clean 5yr: 0.85×  |  1 moderate loss: 1.15×  |  1 major loss: 1.4–1.8×  |  5yr LR >80%: referral mandatory

Crew Nationality / Manning

Crew competence and language proficiency are significant loss drivers. ITF/STCW compliance; officer/rating ratio; shore-based support quality all assessed.

ITF-approved, STCW compliant, vetting-passed: standard  |  Mixed nationality without STCW alignment: +10–25%

Limit of Liability

Convention minimum limits (CLC, OPA 90, Nairobi, Bunkers) set the floor. Additional limit above floor requires actuarial pricing for severity distribution above conventional limits.

CLC minimum: base rate  |  $100m: ILF ×1.4  |  $500m: ILF ×1.9  |  $1bn+: ILF ×2.5+ (GX layer pricing)

Deductible / SIR Credit

Many P&I-equivalent fixed premium placements include a per-occurrence deductible especially for cargo claims (matching club Member's deductible). Higher SIR for large fleet operators.

No deductible: 0% credit  |  $10k ded: −8%  |  $50k ded: −18%  |  $100k SIR: −28%

Ship Repairer's Liability — Rating Formula

G/GC — Ship Repairer's Liability (SRL) Premium = Maximum Vessel Value in Yard (MVIY) × Rate on Line (RoL) × Yard Quality Factor × Work Type Factor × Limit Factor × Deductible Credit

Pre-Bind Underwriting Checklist

RFleet list: vessel name, IMO number, GT, flag, class society, year built, vessel type for all vessels in scope
RISM Document of Compliance (DOC) and Safety Management Certificate (SMC) for all vessels — must be current and issued by approved IACS classification society
R5-year loss runs from current P&I club or insurer — all claims including nil claims, closed and open with current reserve
RCurrent P&I club certificate of entry (if leaving club — reason for change must be explained; mid-term departures are significant red flag)
RPort state control inspection history — last 3 years, all detentions and deficiencies (Paris/Tokyo MOU public database cross-check)
RPollution record — any oil spill, MARPOL violation, or COFR non-compliance in last 5 years
RFor US waters trading: COFR (Certificate of Financial Responsibility) confirmation; OPA 90 exposure analysis
PSIRE/CDI vetting inspection reports (last 12 months) for tanker fleets — Rightship rating for bulk carriers/containerships
PManning agent details and STCW compliance confirmation — officer certificate copies for senior officers (master/chief officer/chief engineer)
PTrading routes and cargo types — hazardous/dangerous cargoes to be specifically identified (chemicals, LNG, LPG, explosives)
PSRL: yard survey/inspection report within 3 years; details of maximum single vessel value accepted; list of work types performed
PPTOL: port plan/layout; annual throughput (TEU/tonnes); vessel size range at berth; crane operation details; ISPS Code compliance
ICyber security posture — BIMCO COMPIT completion; network segmentation between bridge and commercial IT; crew cyber awareness training
IGX: IG Pool details — which club, entry level, club supplementary call history; prior GX excess arrangements
ICruise vessels: Athens Convention compliance; passenger manifest/emergency muster drill records; medical facility certification
IMLC 2006 financial security certificate — confirmation of current crew abandonment/repatriation financial security

Automatic Referral Triggers

Vessels Without Valid ISM CertificationNo ISM = no mainstream marine liability placement. Only specialist high-risk market (if any) will consider; senior authority required.
Port State Control Detention HistoryAny vessel detained by Paris/Tokyo/US Coast Guard MOU in last 12 months: automatic referral. 2+ detentions in 3 years: likely decline.
5-Year Loss Ratio >80%Developed LR exceeding 80% on total marine liability: root cause analysis required before quotation. Frequency pattern vs. severity spike — different treatment.
US Waters — VLCC/Tanker FleetVLCC or large tanker trading US Gulf/East Coast: OPA 90 unlimited pollution exposure. Senior authority; dedicated US pollution model required.
Cruise Passenger LiabilityAny cruise vessel with passenger liability component: Athens Convention compliance check; per-passenger limit adequacy vs. head count; specialist referral.
Vessel Age >20 Years Without Enhanced SurveySub-standard or aged tonnage without Enhanced Survey Programme (ESP) results and structural assessment: referral to senior underwriter or decline.
Red Sea / HOA / High-Risk Route OperationsVessels regularly trading Red Sea/Horn of Africa (Houthi threat): war risk/P&I interface review required; BMP6 compliance mandatory; crew hardship pay protocol needed.
Limit >$500m (GX Layer)Any single risk GX excess layer above $500m attachment requires accumulation modelling. IG Pool sits at $3.1bn — clash reinsurance position above this analysed at syndicate level.
⚠ Dark Fleet / Sanctions Screening: All marine liability submissions must be screened against OFAC, EU, and OFSI sanctions lists at vessel, owner, manager, and flag state level. Dark fleet vessels (typically aged, trading Russian oil outside IG Club system) present: (a) sanctions exposure for Lloyd's, (b) absent/fraudulent insurance certificates, and (c) catastrophic uninsured pollution risk for coastal states. Vessels with Statutes of Liberty, Maritime Administration, or known Russian beneficial ownership must be declined without senior compliance sign-off.
