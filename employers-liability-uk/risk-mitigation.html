PREVENT Controls (6)
PREVENT

H&S Management System (ISO 45001)

ISO 45001:2018 (replacing OHSAS 18001) provides the systematic framework for identifying hazards, assessing risks, implementing controls and reviewing performance. Certification requires third-party audit. Employers with ISO 45001 demonstrate to underwriters a proactive safety culture — typically correlating with 20–35% lower claim frequency than unaccredited peers. [L3]

PREVENT

Manual Handling Training

Manual handling injuries (back strain, musculoskeletal disorders) are the highest-frequency EL peril across most sectors. Regulation 4 of the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 requires employers to avoid manual handling where reasonably practicable, assess remaining manual handling tasks, and reduce injury risk. Annual refresher training and task-specific risk assessments are best practice. [I1]

PREVENT

PPE Programme

Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 2022 (updating 1992 Regulations) require employers to provide appropriate PPE where risks cannot be eliminated by other means. Key requirements: PPE assessment, selection, provision (free of charge), instruction, maintenance and storage. PPE is the last line of defence — process controls and engineering solutions should be preferred. COSHH RPE programmes for respiratory hazards are critical for disease prevention. [L3]

PREVENT

Young Worker / Apprentice Controls

Workers aged 16–24 suffer injury rates 2–3× higher than experienced workers. The Working Time Regulations 1998 apply specific restrictions for workers under 18. Apprentice risk assessments, buddy/mentor systems, restricted equipment access (scaffolding erection, powered plant) and competency-staged progression are essential controls for construction, manufacturing and logistics employers. [I8]

PREVENT

Contractor Management

Principal contractors owe a duty of care to sub-contractor employees under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM 2015) and HSWA 1974. Contractor pre-qualification (CHAS, Constructionline, SafeContractor), site inductions, permit-to-work systems and joint H&S meetings reduce multi-employer site accident frequency. Failure in contractor management is a common factor in fatal accident prosecutions. [I8]

PREVENT

Noise / Vibration Monitoring

Occupational noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL) and HAVS are preventable occupational diseases that generate EL claims with 10–20 year latency. Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 and Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 require exposure monitoring, action levels (80/85 dBA; 2.5/5.0 m/s²), health surveillance and engineering controls. Audiometric testing records are key evidence in NIHL claims management. [I4]

DETECT Controls (4)
DETECT

Near-Miss Reporting System

Near-miss reporting enables employers to identify precursor events before accidents occur. HSE research shows near-misses outnumber reportable accidents by approximately 300:1 in construction. A functional near-miss reporting culture requires no-blame reporting, management visibility and documented corrective action. RIDDOR-reportable incidents must be reported to HSE within defined timescales. [I8]

DETECT

Occupational Health Surveillance

Statutory health surveillance is required for COSHH-regulated exposures (respiratory sensitisers, lead, asbestos, ionising radiation), noise above 85 dBA action level, vibration above 5.0 m/s² trigger, and in some other regulated activities. Periodic health checks detect early occupational disease allowing intervention before permanent impairment — and create medical records that defend or establish causation in later EL claims. [I4]

DETECT

Absence Management Triggers

Early intervention in sickness absence reduces claim duration and costs significantly. Bradford Factor-type absence monitoring identifies patterns of short-term absence (potential indicator of stress, musculoskeletal problems or unreported injuries). Return-to-work interviews for all absences — however brief — have strong evidence of reducing both absence frequency and the probability of an absence converting to an EL claim. [I6]

DETECT

Employee Assistance Programme (EAP)

EAPs provide confidential counselling, mental health support and financial/legal advice to employees. Well-designed EAPs detect emerging mental health problems before they result in long-term absence and EL stress claims. Duty of care for psychiatric harm is triggered when the employer is aware (or ought to be) of an individual employee's vulnerability — EAP records are sensitive but provide evidence of employer responsiveness. [I5]

Mental Health & Stress Management Checklist

8-Point Employer Duty of Care Checklist — Stress & Psychiatric Injury

✓
Stress Risk Assessment conducted: HSE Management Standards stress risk assessment completed for all work groups; results documented and reviewed annually. Identifies demands, control, support, relationships, role clarity and change management as the six primary stress factors. [I5]
✓
Line Manager Training: All managers trained in mental health awareness, recognising warning signs of distress, and having supportive conversations. Mental Health First Aiders (MHFA) designated across the organisation at a minimum of 1 per 50 employees.
✓
EAP Access Communicated: Employee Assistance Programme actively promoted — not just on intranet. Employees know how to access confidential support. Utilisation rate monitored; low utilisation may indicate culture barriers to disclosure. [I5]
✓
Workload Monitoring: Regular one-to-one meetings between employees and line managers. Workload formally reviewed against capacity. Overtime hours monitored and controlled. Pressure event recording (unexpected project demands, team absence) documented.
✓
Flexible Working Policy: Formal flexible working policy in place and actively applied. Homeworking risk assessments completed (DSE, lone working). Right to request flexible working respected; refusals documented with legitimate business reason.
✓
Return-to-Work Interviews: Structured return-to-work (RTW) interviews conducted for all absences — including short ones. RTW plan documented for absences over 4 weeks. Occupational health referral considered at 4–6 weeks' absence. Fit Note ("may be fit for work") provisions actively considered. [I6]
✓
Reasonable Adjustments: Equality Act 2010 reasonable adjustments for disabled employees (including mental health conditions that are long-term and substantially limiting) actively considered and documented. Failure to make reasonable adjustments is both an EL and discrimination claim risk.
✓
Whistleblower / Speak-Up Protection: Protected disclosure policy in place (Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998). Employees aware they can raise H&S and mental health concerns without fear of detriment. Culture of non-retaliation evidenced — a bullying culture is a significant stress claim amplifier. [I5]

HSE Enforcement — Powers and Consequences: The Health and Safety Executive has extensive enforcement powers under HSWA 1974: [L3]

Improvement Notices — require the duty holder to remedy a contravention within a specified period (minimum 21 days). Breach of an improvement notice is a criminal offence (unlimited fine).

Prohibition Notices — stop an activity immediately where there is risk of serious personal injury. Breach carries unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.

Crown Court Prosecution — serious breaches can result in unlimited fines for organisations and individuals, and up to 2 years' imprisonment for individuals. Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007 applies to gross breaches causing death — unlimited fine, publicity order and remediation order.

Outstanding HSE enforcement actions are a material fact that must be disclosed at EL renewal under the Insurance Act 2015. [I9] Underwriters should specifically request confirmation of any outstanding notices in the proposal form.
Top 7 Actions for Insureds

Risk Management Priorities for EL Policyholders

1

Conduct and record task-specific risk assessments for every significant workplace activity — generic assessments do not satisfy the MHSWR 1999 requirement and will not support an EL defence. Review and update following any incident, process change or new equipment introduction.

2

Implement and document a return-to-work programme. Early RTW reduces claim duration and total cost significantly. Engage occupational health at 4 weeks' absence. Consider modified duties as a bridge before full return. Document everything — RTW records are key evidence in EL claim defence.

3

Maintain statutory health surveillance records. Records of occupational health monitoring (audiometry, spirometry, skin surveillance) are critical for defence of occupational disease claims. Surveillance must be conducted by a competent person (occupational health nurse or physician). Records must be kept for 40 years for COSHH Schedule 6 substances. [I4]

4

Conduct annual stress risk assessments using the HSE Management Standards framework. [I5] Document the results, action plans and review dates. Stress risk assessments are now expected evidence in mental health EL claim defence — their absence is a significant aggravating factor in negligence claims.

5

Comply with RIDDOR and report all qualifying incidents to HSE. [I8] Failure to report RIDDOR-qualifying incidents is a criminal offence and creates an adverse inference in EL civil claims. Ensure first aid officers and H&S managers know the reporting thresholds (over-7-day injuries, specified injuries, dangerous occurrences, occupational diseases).

6

Pre-qualify and document all contractor arrangements. Use CHAS, SafeContractor, Constructionline or equivalent pre-qualification. Issue site induction records. Implement permit-to-work for hot work, confined space entry, work at height and electrical work. Joint H&S meetings for large or long-running projects. Principal contractor CDM duties must be understood and fulfilled. [I8]

7

Register all EL policies with ELTO. [L1] All EL policies issued by Lloyd's syndicates must be submitted to the ELTO database within 90 days of inception. Failure to register creates regulatory exposure and means that mesothelioma victims (potentially 30–50 years post-exposure) cannot trace the insurer — resulting in DMPS levy liability.
