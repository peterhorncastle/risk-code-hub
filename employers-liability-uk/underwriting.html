Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Industry / Trade Classification

The dominant rating factor for UK EL. Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) codes drive an industry hazard modifier that reflects the physical risk profile of the work. Underground mining and heavy construction attract the highest loadings; clerical office-based businesses the lowest. Underwriters verify SIC codes against actual operations — misclassification risk is significant. EST

Industry modifier: 0.4× (clerical/admin) → 4.0× (underground mining, demolition)
High Impact

Payroll / Employee Count

EL premium is fundamentally a rate-on-payroll system: the more payroll, the more exposure (employees × time at risk × wage multiplier for bodily injury severity). Payroll is also used as a proxy for the employer's revenue and size. Employee count is cross-checked against payroll for plausibility — part-time and seasonal workers require careful treatment. EST

Base rate: £0.50–£6.00 per £100 payroll depending on trade class and claims record
High Impact

Claims Experience (3–5 Year Experience Modification)

Prior claims history is the most powerful predictive factor after trade class. The experience modification factor (EMF) compares actual to expected losses over 3–5 years. A single catastrophic claim may reflect exceptional circumstances; a pattern of moderate claims indicates systemic H&S failure. Unverified claims above £5k threshold require loss runs from prior insurer. EST

EMF range: 0.65× (5-year clean, proactive H&S) → 2.5× (adverse, multiple claims)
High Impact

Hazard Profile — Manual vs Clerical

The proportion of employees performing manual, physical or hazardous duties versus sedentary clerical or office duties materially changes the expected loss frequency and severity distribution. A single employer may have both — e.g. a retailer with warehouse operatives and head office staff requires split-payroll rating across trade classes. EST

Manual uplift vs clerical: typically 3–8× for equivalent payroll depending on hazard intensity
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Limit ILF — Above £5m Compulsory Minimum

The EL compulsory minimum is £5m per occurrence. Commercial limits above £5m (£10m, £25m, unlimited) are arranged by layering. The increased limit factor (ILF) above £5m reflects the frequency of large claims reaching excess layers — lower for office-based, higher for high-hazard. Unlimited indemnity is common in London market EL packages. VER

ILF £10m vs £5m: approx. 1.2–1.5× depending on sector; unlimited: 1.4–2.0×
Medium Impact

Disease Exposure — Asbestos, Dust, Chemicals

Any employer with current or historic disease exposure — asbestos, coal dust, silica, organic solvents, isocyanates — requires disease loading. Historic exposure (pre-2000 asbestos removal, mining, shipbuilding) attracts IBNR disease reserve consideration even for current risks. Underwriters must ask specifically about historic occupancies and trades.

Disease loading: +15 to +100% depending on substance, duration and prior exposure
Medium Impact

Geographic Concentration

Risks concentrated in hazardous geographic environments — offshore, tunnelling, North Sea, underground — attract additional loading. Overseas employees working for UK employers may fall under UK EL policy if employed under UK contracts (subject to employee habitual place of work tests). Geographic scope must be clearly defined. VER

Offshore loading: +30 to +80%; overseas extension to UK policy: +15 to +40%
Medium Impact

Contractual Liability Assumed

Where an employer assumes greater liability than would arise at common law (e.g., indemnity to principal contractor under a sub-contract, or hold-harmless agreements), this extends the EL exposure beyond the standard statutory framework. Contractual liability assumed from third parties is typically excluded unless expressly included. Underwriters review key contracts at submission.

Contractual liability loading: +10 to +25% if significant hold-harmless assumed
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

RTW Programme Quality

Documented return-to-work (RTW) programmes reduce claim duration and total claim cost significantly. Employers with structured RTW — phased return, modified duties, occupational health involvement — achieve 20–40% reductions in average claim cost. Evidence of active RTW management supports rate credit.

Lower Impact

H&S Management System (ISO 45001)

ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety management system) certification or equivalent (OHSAS 18001 legacy) demonstrates systematic management of workplace safety risks. Accredited employers have statistically lower injury frequencies. ROSPA Gold/Silver Awards and SafeContractor accreditation are also positive indicators.

Lower Impact

Occupational Health Provision

Access to occupational health (OH) physicians, nurses and physiotherapists — either in-house or through contracted OH service — enables early intervention, supports mental health claims management and reduces PTSD/stress claim duration. OH engagement is increasingly expected by Lloyd's underwriters for complex risks. VER

Lower Impact

Apprentice / Trainee Ratio

Young workers (16–24) and new starters have significantly higher injury frequency than experienced workers — they represent disproportionate EL claim frequency, particularly in construction, manufacturing and hospitality. Apprentice ratio above 15% of workforce warrants upward loading; structured training programmes with competency assessment partially mitigate.

Premium Rating Formula
// UK EL (W3) — indicative payroll-based rating formula
Base Premium = Payroll (£) × Base Rate per £100 payroll

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Trade Class / Industry Modifier // 0.4× (clerical) → 4.0× (mining/demolition)
  × Experience Modification Factor // 0.65× (clean 5yr) → 2.5× (adverse)
  × Disease Exposure Loading // +0 to +100% for asbestos/dust/chemical
  × Manual/Clerical Split Adjustment // payroll split across SIC sub-classes
  × Contractual Liability Loading // +0 to +25% if hold-harmless assumed
  × Limit Factor (ILF above £5m) // 1.0× at £5m → up to 2.0× for unlimited
  × Geographic Scope Factor // 1.0× (UK only) → 1.8× (offshore/overseas)
  − H&S / RTW / OH Credit // −5% to −20% for best practice programmes

Ogden Discount Rate Impact on Large Claims: At the current discount rate of +0.25%, EST lump sum multipliers for serious injuries with long life expectancy are significantly higher than under the former +2.5% rate. A 30-year-old claimant with total earnings loss for life will attract an Ogden multiplier approximately 30–40% higher than under +2.5%. Underwriters must ensure large-loss reserves reflect +0.25% — not historical rates. Any discount rate change under the 5-year review cycle (next due approximately 2024) will require immediate reserve reassessment.
ELTO — Employers' Liability Tracing Office: All UK EL insurers (including Lloyd's syndicates) must submit EL policy data to the ELTO database — enabling injured workers to trace their former employer's insurer even when the employer has ceased trading or is insolvent. ELTO covers policies issued since 1 April 2011. For pre-2011 policies, insurers must respond to "Tracing Agreement" enquiries. Non-compliance with ELTO data submission obligations attracts FCA regulatory action. VER
