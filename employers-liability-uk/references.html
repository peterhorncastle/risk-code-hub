Legal & Statutory Sources [L1]–[L9]
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 (c.57)

    The foundational statute requiring every UK employer to hold approved employers' liability insurance. Came into force 1 January 1972. The Act imposes a criminal penalty (originally £200 per day, later revised) for failure to hold cover. Certificate of insurance must be displayed or made available for inspection. Applies to employers carrying on business in Great Britain. The Act does not specify the minimum limit — this is done by secondary legislation (SI 1998/2573). Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Regulations 1998 — SI 1998/2573

    Secondary legislation made under the EL (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. Raised the minimum approved EL insurance limit to £5 million per occurrence with effect from 1 January 1999. Also amended the certificate of insurance requirements, increased the HSE enforcement fine to £2,500 per day of failure to hold cover, and introduced requirements for certificates to be kept for 40 years (later amended). Employers' Liability Tracing Office (ELTO) requirements introduced by subsequent amendment (SI 2008/1765). Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (c.37)

    Primary health and safety legislation in Great Britain. Created the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) and the Health and Safety Commission. Section 2: general duty on employers to ensure health, safety and welfare of employees so far as is reasonably practicable. Section 3: duty to persons other than employees. Powers: improvement notices (s.21), prohibition notices (s.22), criminal prosecution (s.33 — unlimited fine / up to 2 years' imprisonment). Regulations made under HSWA 1974 include MHSWR 1999, Manual Handling Operations 1992, COSHH 2002, RIDDOR 2013, Noise at Work 2005, Vibration at Work 2005. Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL Fairchild v Glenhaven Funeral Services Ltd [2002] UKHL 22

    House of Lords judgment (20 June 2002). Established the "Fairchild exception" to the normal causation rules in mesothelioma claims: where a claimant has been exposed to asbestos by multiple employers in circumstances where scientific uncertainty prevents attribution of the cause of mesothelioma to a specific employer's asbestos, any defendant who materially contributed to the risk of exposure is liable. This "material contribution to risk" test — departing from the orthodox "but for" causation test — dramatically extended the pool of potential defendants in mesothelioma litigation. Lords Bingham, Nicholls, Hoffmann, Hutton and Rodger. [2002] 3 WLR 89. Later modified by Barker v Corus [2006] UKHL 20 (proportionate liability) and restored to joint and several liability by Compensation Act 2006, s.3.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL Civil Liability Act 2018 (c.29)

    Reformed the personal injury discount rate (Ogden rate) setting process and whiplash injury compensation. Part 1 (discount rate): the Lord Chancellor must set the rate following a review conducted every 5 years, using a "low risk" investment assumption (rather than the "very low risk" ILGS-only basis used in 2017). Part 2 (whiplash): introduced a tariff system for soft tissue road traffic injuries under £5,000 and the OIC portal for claims up to the tariff threshold. Under Part 1, the discount rate was set at +0.25% in August 2019. Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 (LASPO) (c.10)

    Implemented the Jackson reforms to civil litigation costs. Key EL-relevant provisions: (1) success fees under conditional fee arrangements (CFAs) no longer recoverable from defendants; (2) after-the-event insurance (ATE) premiums no longer recoverable from defendants; (3) Qualified One Way Costs Shifting (QOCS) introduced for personal injury claims — claimants protected from adverse costs orders in most circumstances; (4) Part 2 (ss.56–60) reformed CFA provisions. LASPO also affected legal aid availability for personal injury claims. Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL Barker v Corus UK Ltd [2006] UKHL 20

    House of Lords judgment (3 May 2006). Held that in Fairchild-type mesothelioma cases (material contribution to risk), defendants were only liable in proportion to their share of the total asbestos exposure risk — not jointly and severally for the full loss. Claimants therefore bore the risk of insolvent or uninsured defendants. This decision was almost immediately reversed by Parliament: Compensation Act 2006, Section 3 (in force 25 July 2006) restored full joint and several liability for mesothelioma, so that any one defendant can be pursued for the full amount, leaving that defendant to seek contribution from others. [2006] 2 WLR 1027.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL Courts Act 2003 (c.39) — Ogden Tables Statutory Basis

    Section 100 of the Courts Act 2003 inserted Section 10 into the Damages Act 1996, giving the Government Actuary's Department (GAD) Actuarial Tables (Ogden Tables) statutory status. Courts must take account of the tables when making or confirming consent orders for periodical payments or lump sums for future pecuniary loss in personal injury and fatal accident claims. The 8th Edition of the Ogden Tables (2020) is the current version, incorporating gender-neutral life tables and revised mortality projections. Available at legislation.gov.uk and actuaries.org.uk.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL Compensation Act 2006 (c.29) — Mesothelioma Joint and Several Liability

    Section 3 of the Compensation Act 2006 reversed the House of Lords decision in Barker v Corus [2006] UKHL 20. Section 3 provides that in mesothelioma cases involving multiple defendants who materially increased the risk of the claimant developing mesothelioma, each defendant is liable for the whole of the damages (joint and several liability) notwithstanding that the defendant's exposure was only part of the total exposure. The defendant who pays can seek contribution from others under the Civil Liability (Contribution) Act 1978. In force: 25 July 2006. Available at legislation.gov.uk.

Industry & Market Sources [I1]–[I10]
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY HSE Annual Workplace Injury and Disease Statistics 2023/24

    Health and Safety Executive, Health and Safety at Work: Summary Statistics for Great Britain 2024. Annual statistics report published by HSE based on RIDDOR reporting and Labour Force Survey (LFS) data. Key 2023/24 figures: 138 fatal injuries to workers; approximately 561,000 self-reported non-fatal workplace injuries (LFS); 1.8 million workers suffering work-related ill health; 34.3 million working days lost. Available at hse.gov.uk/statistics. Updated annually in October/November.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY ABI UK Employers' Liability Market Statistics 2023

    Association of British Insurers (ABI), UK Insurance and Long-Term Savings Key Facts 2023 and associated liability market data. ABI publishes annual data on UK EL GWP, claims frequency and average claim cost. Total UK EL market GWP estimated at approximately £1.8–2.0bn in 2022/23 (est. — ABI data not fully disaggregated at EL sub-class level). ABI also publishes Ogden rate impact analysis and lobbies Government on discount rate reviews. Available at abi.org.uk.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY Ogden Tables — 8th Edition, Government Actuary's Department 2020

    Government Actuary's Department (GAD), Actuarial Tables with Explanatory Notes for Use in Personal Injury and Fatal Accident Cases, 8th Edition (2020). The Ogden Tables provide multipliers for calculating lump sums representing future loss of earnings, pension loss, care costs and other pecuniary losses in personal injury and fatal accident claims. Key change in 8th edition: gender-neutral tables following ECtHR ruling on discrimination. Used with the statutory discount rate (+0.25% as at publication, per Civil Liability Act 2018). Available at judiciary.gov.uk and gov.uk/government/publications. Courts take the tables into account under the Courts Act 2003, s.100.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY IOSH / Zurich Occupational Disease Claims Analysis

    Institution of Occupational Safety and Health (IOSH) and Zurich Insurance research on occupational disease claims in the UK EL market, including British Coal HAVS/VWF group settlement data (approximately £2bn total, 1999), NIHL claims trends, and musculoskeletal disease incidence by sector. Reference to: British Coal Corporation v Miley & others — vibration white finger group litigation. HSE HAVS statistics published at hse.gov.uk/vibration. IOSH publications available at iosh.com. The British Coal HAVS settlement is referenced widely in EL actuarial literature as a benchmark group disease outcome.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY ACAS Mental Health in the Workplace Report 2023

    Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (ACAS), Managing Mental Health in the Workplace (2023 update). Guidance on employer duty of care for mental health; reasonable adjustments under Equality Act 2010; EAP implementation; stress risk assessment under the HSE Management Standards. Also references: HSE Management Standards for Work-Related Stress (hse.gov.uk/stress); MHFA England mental health first aider programme statistics. Mental health-related absences account for approximately 17% of all work-related ill health cases (HSE 2023/24). Available at acas.org.uk and hse.gov.uk/stress.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY DWP / BEIS Taylor Review of Modern Working Practices 2017

    Matthew Taylor, Good Work: The Taylor Review of Modern Working Practices (2017), commissioned by BEIS/DWP. Reviewed employment status (employed, worker, self-employed) and its implications for workplace rights and employer obligations — directly relevant to EL coverage boundaries for gig economy workers following Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5. Recommended alignment of employment status for tax and employment rights. Available at gov.uk. Also references: DWP Fit for Work occupational health guidance and Fit Note framework.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY Mesothelioma UK Charity Statistics 2023

    Mesothelioma UK, Mesothelioma Statistics (2023). Approximately 2,700 mesothelioma deaths annually in Great Britain — a peak/plateau after decades of increase driven by asbestos exposures in the 1940s–1970s in shipbuilding, construction, manufacturing and other industries. Mesothelioma accounts for approximately 20% of UK EL claims cost (est.). Mesothelioma UK supports patients and funds research; charity also publishes data on the Diffuse Mesothelioma Payment Scheme (DMPS). DMPS paid approximately £46m in 2022/23 to mesothelioma victims unable to trace liable insurers. Available at mesothelioma.uk.com and gov.uk/mesothelioma-payment.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY HSE RIDDOR Statistics 2023/24

    Health and Safety Executive, RIDDOR Statistics: Injuries, Ill Health and Dangerous Occurrences, 2023/24 Annual Report. RIDDOR = Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/1471). Key reportable events: fatalities (immediate); specified injuries (immediate); over-7-day incapacitating injuries (15 days); occupational diseases; dangerous occurrences. RIDDOR data used as underwriting intelligence — RIDDOR rate by SIC code is a key actuarial input to EL rate modelling. Available at hse.gov.uk/statistics/riddor.htm. Employers must report via HSE website; non-compliance is a criminal offence under HSWA 1974.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's EL Market Bulletin — LMA5234

    Lloyd's Market Association (LMA), Market Bulletin LMA5234: Employers' Liability Insurance — UK Compulsory Requirements and Lloyd's Syndicate Obligations. Covers ELTO data submission requirements, Mesothelioma Payment Scheme levy calculation for Lloyd's syndicates, guidance on W3 risk code scope, and liaison with the Association of British Insurers on EL reform. Also relevant: Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y4763 on disease aggregate reinsurance for long-tail EL portfolios. LMA publications available to market participants via lma.org.uk. Lloyd's W3 GWP estimated at £600–800m (est. — not publicly disaggregated).

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY CMS Law Firm — EL Claims Trends Report 2024

    CMS Cameron McKenna Nabarro Olswang LLP, UK Employers' Liability Claims Trends and Legal Developments 2024. Annual review of EL litigation trends including: mental health and stress claim statistics; discount rate impact analysis; QOCS reform proposals; CDM 2015 enforcement trends; COVID-related occupational disease claims development. Also references: DWP mesothelioma claims pipeline data; ELTO tracing statistics; HSE enforcement statistics. CMS is a leading UK defendant EL firm. Available at cms.law. Also: Kennedys LLP and DAC Beachcroft LLP publish equivalent annual EL trend analyses.
