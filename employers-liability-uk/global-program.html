Jurisdiction Profiles
🇬🇧
UK — Primary W3 Hub

United Kingdom

EL (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — minimum £5m per occurrence. VER FCA-authorised insurer or Lloyd's required. Certificate must be available for inspection. ELTO registration mandatory. HSE enforcement. Disease latency tail requires long-tail reserving. Scotland/NI: same EL Act; different procedural law.

🇮🇪
Republic of Ireland

Ireland

Separate compulsory EL regime under the Liability for Defective Products Act and common law employer duty. Insurers must be authorised by Central Bank of Ireland. Minimum limit not prescribed by statute (unlike UK) but typically €13m+. English and Irish law diverge on discount rate and claims quantum. Separate admitted policy required — UK W3 does not extend. VER

🇪🇺
EU Member States (Post-Brexit)

European Union

Post-Brexit passporting rights lost — Lloyd's syndicates cannot write EL direct in EU member states without a locally admitted carrier or fronting arrangement. Each EU member state has different compulsory EL/WC requirements (e.g. Germany: BG Bau statutory accident insurance; France: CPAM statutory scheme; Netherlands: separate WA-Werkgevers policy). Fronting via EU admitted insurer (e.g. Lloyd's Brussels) essential. VER

🇦🇺
Australia

Australia

Workers' compensation is a state/territory matter in Australia — each of the 8 states/territories has its own compulsory workers' compensation scheme (e.g. WorkCover NSW, WorkSafe Victoria, ReturnToWorkSA). No national scheme. Employers with Australian employees must hold locally admitted workers' comp for each state in which they employ. Return-to-work obligations are strictly regulated. VER

🇺🇸
USA — W5/W6 Hub

United States

US Workers' Compensation is covered separately under Lloyd's risk codes W5 (WC Standard) and W6 (WC Excess/Surplus). Each US state has its own WC system — a no-fault statutory benefit scheme paying medical, wage replacement and disability benefits. UK EL is fundamentally different — fault-based, common law, Ogden multipliers — see the W5/W6 hub for US detail. EST

🌍
Emerging Markets

Emerging Markets / MENA / Africa

Many emerging markets require locally admitted employer liability or occupational accident policies — fronting structures are essential where Lloyd's cannot write direct. UAE: DESS/DHA health insurance mandatory; employer liability typically written by local insurer. Saudi Arabia: GOSI statutory occupational injury scheme. Sub-Saharan Africa: highly variable by jurisdiction; local legal advice essential before placing. Political risk of local insurer solvency must be considered. EST

RIDDOR Compliance Alert — Reporting Injuries Overseas: Where UK employees are injured overseas (construction project in UAE, offshore platform in Gulf of Mexico), RIDDOR (Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013) still applies to UK employers in respect of UK employees. VER Fatal accidents and specified injuries must be reported to HSE within the statutory timeframes even when the incident occurs internationally. Failure to report is a criminal offence. Claims handlers must build RIDDOR compliance into their international EL notifications process. Local incident reporting requirements in the country of occurrence will additionally apply independently.
Case Study — UK Construction Group: International Trigger Event

UK Construction Group — 800 UK Employees + 300 Overseas (Poland, UAE, Australia)

Background: A mid-sized UK civil engineering and construction group (HQ London) employs 800 UK-based workers and has permanent employee presences in Poland (150 employees, infrastructure contract), UAE (100 employees, commercial build), and Australia (50 employees, tunnelling project). Programme structure: UK EL (W3) with Lloyd's — £5m compulsory + £10m excess; local admitted policies in Poland (PZU), UAE (AXA Gulf) and Australia (QBE WorkCover). Reinsurance via London market XL programme at syndicate level.

Trigger Event — UK Scaffolding Fall Fatality (Yorkshire Site): A 28-year-old scaffolder falls 9 metres from an insufficiently braced scaffold on a UK construction site, suffering fatal injuries. The employee was employed directly (not sub-contracted). HSE is notified immediately (RIDDOR — fatal accident — immediate report required EST). HSE issues a Prohibition Notice stopping all work at height on the site and begins investigation.

1

EL Policy Response: UK W3 EL policy notified immediately. Lloyd's lead syndicate appoints specialist fatal accident solicitors. HSE criminal investigation runs parallel to civil EL claim by the deceased's family. Fatal accident claim (spouse + 2 children) estimated at £1.6m in lump sum damages using Ogden +0.25% tables — plus dependency claim for wife's loss of earnings support, past and future.

2

Criminal Prosecution: Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) charges the employer with Corporate Manslaughter (Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007). HSE separately prosecutes under HSWA 1974 for failure to ensure a safe system of work. Criminal fines (unlimited) and remediation order sought. Directors personally prosecuted for H&S offences.

3

Programme Review Triggered: Following the UK fatality, the insured reviews its entire programme. The overseas operations are audited: Poland — scaffolding and working at height controls found inadequate; UAE — local admitted EL found to have inadequate limits for UK engineering expat employees. Programme restructured with: global EL standards adopted across all jurisdictions, UK W3 extended for UK expats temporarily in UAE (added overseas extension endorsement), local limits increased in Australia and Poland, and a group-wide safety leadership programme implemented. EST

4

Claims Resolution: UK fatal EL claim settled out of court at £1.45m (3 years post-incident). Criminal prosecution: £480,000 fine (corporate) + £120,000 individual H&S fines + remediation order. Total EL programme cost (UK W3): £1.45m + defence costs £210,000 = £1.66m. EMF for renewal: +85% loading applied. ISO 45001 certification required as renewal condition.

Overseas Employee Coverage Under UK EL Policies: The EL (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 VER applies to employers carrying on business in Great Britain. UK employees temporarily working overseas (short-term assignments, project work) may be covered by the UK EL policy depending on: (1) whether the employee is habitually based in the UK; (2) whether the contract is governed by English/Welsh/Scottish law; and (3) the policy's territorial scope wording. For employees permanently seconded overseas, local admitted cover is required. The "temporary overseas" extension in many Lloyd's EL wordings typically covers up to 90–180 days abroad per employee per year — underwriters must check the specific policy territorial clause. VER
