Lloyd's Risk Code W3 — Coverage Scope

W3 — Employers Liability (UK)

Lloyd's risk code W3 covers employers' liability insurance for UK-based risks. The policy responds when an employee suffers bodily injury, disease or death arising out of and in the course of their employment, and the employer is held legally liable. EL is compulsory for virtually all UK employers under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — minimum indemnity limit £5 million per occurrence. [L1]

W3 vs W5/W6 — Key Distinction

W3 covers UK domestic employers' liability under English/Scottish/Welsh/Northern Irish law. W5 and W6 cover US Workers' Compensation — a no-fault statutory benefit scheme with fundamentally different structure: US WC pays medical, wage replacement and disability benefits regardless of fault, with strict state-by-state regulatory requirements. UK EL is fault-based; claimant must establish employer negligence or breach of statutory duty. [I2]

Compulsory Minimum — £5m per Occurrence

The EL (Compulsory Insurance) Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/2573) set the minimum limit at £5 million per occurrence. [L2] In practice, Lloyd's syndicates and commercial insurers typically write limits of £10m, £25m or unlimited indemnity, with higher limits arranged via layers. The certificate of insurance must be displayed and kept available for HSE inspection — failure to hold EL cover carries a fine of up to £2,500 per day. [L3]

Coverage Taxonomy

Bodily Injury & Accident

Physical injury to an employee arising out of and in the course of employment — the core peril. Covers slips, trips, falls, manual handling injuries, machinery accidents and workplace fatalities. HSE reports approximately 135 fatal injuries and 400,000 non-fatal injuries to workers annually. [I1] Accounts for ~55–60% of EL claim frequency.

Occupational Disease — Asbestos & Mesothelioma

Long-latency occupational diseases — mesothelioma, asbestosis, pleural plaques — arising from historic asbestos exposure. Mesothelioma accounts for approximately 20% of EL claims value despite representing a small fraction of claim frequency. [I7] Causation governed by the "Fairchild exception" allowing material contribution to risk where multiple employers exposed the claimant. [L4]

Mental Health & Stress Claims

Work-related stress, anxiety, depression and PTSD claims have grown significantly. Employers owe a duty of care for foreseeable psychiatric harm. Key trigger: the employer was aware (or should have been aware) of the risk to a specific employee. COVID-19 drove a surge in key worker PTSD and stress claims 2020–21. [I5] Requires occupational health management evidence.

Employers' Liability (Statutory Duty)

Civil liability for breach of statutory duty under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, [L3] Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992, PPE at Work Regulations 1992, COSHH 1988 and other workplace safety regulations. Breach of statutory duty allows employees to sue in tort, often providing a stronger basis for claim than common law negligence alone.

Rehabilitation & Return to Work

Modern EL policies support rehabilitation management and early return to work (RTW) programmes. Insurers fund medical treatment, physiotherapy and occupational health assessments to reduce claim duration and long-term disability costs. Effective RTW management can reduce average claim costs by 20–40%. [I4] Some Lloyd's wordings include rehabilitation clauses as standard.

Defence Costs & Legal Proceedings

EL policies cover defence costs — solicitors, counsel, expert witnesses, court fees — in addition to damages. Costs can equal 30–50% of damages in contested cases. The MOJ portal (launched 2010) handles lower-value EL claims up to £25,000; claims above this value go through fast-track or multi-track litigation. LASPO 2012 reformed conditional fee arrangements significantly impacting claimant costs. [L6]

Regulatory Requirement — EL (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969: Every UK employer who employs one or more employees must hold EL insurance with an authorised insurer (FCA-authorised or Lloyd's). [L1] The minimum limit is £5 million per occurrence. [L2] The certificate of insurance must be displayed at each place of business (or accessible electronically since 2008). HSE enforces the requirement — inspectors can demand sight of the certificate. Failure to have valid EL cover: fine up to £2,500 per day. Exceptions are narrow: most family businesses with only family member employees are exempt. Self-employed sole traders with no employees are exempt. Crown bodies are exempt.
HSE Enforcement: The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) is both the regulator for workplace safety (under the HSWA 1974 [L3]) and the enforcer for EL compulsory insurance compliance. HSE improvement notices, prohibition notices and criminal prosecution carry unlimited fines and imprisonment for directors. HSE reports approximately 400,000 non-fatal workplace injuries annually (RIDDOR). [I8] Lloyd's syndicates writing W3 must ensure RIDDOR obligations are factored into claims notification procedures.
