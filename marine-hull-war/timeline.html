2001AV9/11 — Aviation War Market Collapses; Marine War Tightens
Four aircraft destroyed by terrorist hijacking; total aviation hull war losses ~$2bn. EST Lloyd's and company market aviation war capacity evaporated within 24 hours. Governments established temporary aviation war guarantee schemes. Marine war tightened simultaneously as terrorism premium was loaded across all W-series lines. The post-9/11 period defined modern terrorism exclusion language across war risk policies.
2002WARMV Limburg — VLCC Struck by Suicide Boat (Yemen)
The French VLCC Limburg was struck by an explosive-laden dhow in the Gulf of Aden on 6 October 2002, killing one crew member and spilling ~90,000 barrels of oil. VER The attack by al-Qaeda was the first major post-9/11 maritime terrorist attack on a commercial vessel. Lloyd's war risk rates for Gulf of Aden transits rose immediately; the incident demonstrated that VLCC tankers were viable terrorist targets.
2004REGISPS Code — International Ship & Port Security Code Enters Force
The International Ship and Port Facility Security (ISPS) Code entered into force on 1 July 2004, establishing mandatory security protocols for vessels over 500 GT operating internationally and their port facilities. VER War underwriters began requiring ISPS compliance as a condition of war risk cover; non-compliant vessels faced exclusions or premium loadings. ISPS certification became a standard pre-binding check for marine war (W) policies.
2005–08PIRSomali Piracy Begins Escalating — Gulf of Aden
Somali piracy emerged as a major threat from 2005, exploiting Somalia's failed-state governance vacuum. Pirates operated dhow motherships to extend range into the Indian Ocean, boarding slow-moving vessels and demanding ransom. By 2008, the Gulf of Aden — the key Europe–Asia shipping lane — was declared a JWC Listed Area. War risk (W) underwriters faced rapidly increasing piracy claims under CL.280 Clause 1.5. VER
2008PIRMV Sirius Star — Supertanker Seized by Somali Pirates
The Sirius Star, a 330,000 DWT VLCC owned by Saudi Aramco, was seized by Somali pirates on 15 November 2008 — the largest vessel ever taken by pirates. VER Laden with $100m of crude oil, the vessel was held for two months before a $3m ransom secured its release. The hijacking demonstrated pirates' ability to seize supertankers and triggered immediate JWC zone expansion and significant AWRP increases for Indian Ocean transits. War risk (W) hull losses — primarily capture, seizure and ransom costs — were absorbed across Lloyd's.
2009REGBMP1 Published — Best Management Practice for Piracy
The first edition of Best Management Practice for Protection against Somalia Based Piracy (BMP) was published in 2009 by BIMCO, ICS, INTERTANKO, INTERCARGO and other industry bodies. VER BMP provided practical guidance: vessel hardening, razor wire, citadels (crew refuge spaces), UKMTO reporting, speed and manoeuvring protocols. Lloyd's war risk underwriters began treating BMP compliance as a warranty condition; non-BMP vessels faced exclusions or higher AWRP. BMP went through multiple editions (BMP4 2011, BMP5 2018) as the threat evolved.
2010PIRSomali Piracy at Peak — 49 Vessels Hijacked in 2010
2010 was the peak year of Somali piracy: 49 vessels successfully hijacked, 1,181 seafarers taken hostage. VER AWRP for Indian Ocean transits reached maximum levels. Total insured losses (hull, cargo and ransom) across the Lloyd's market during the peak piracy period (2008–2012) are estimated at several billion dollars across the war and cargo markets combined. The introduction of privately contracted armed security guards (PCASP) from 2011 was the single most effective countermeasure.
2011MKTArmed Guards Endorsed — War Risk Market Adapts to PCASP
After considerable debate, the shipping industry (IMO, flag states and P&I clubs) endorsed the use of Privately Contracted Armed Security Personnel (PCASP) on vessels transiting the High Risk Area (HRA). VER Lloyd's war risk underwriters followed with guidance frameworks; armed guards dramatically reduced the rate of successful piracy — no BMP-compliant vessel with PCASP aboard was successfully hijacked after their deployment. AWRP began declining as piracy effectiveness collapsed from 2012 onwards.
2012–15MKTSomali Piracy Collapses — Marine War Market Softens
By 2012, Somali piracy had collapsed almost entirely due to armed guards, BMP hardening, naval interdiction and deteriorating pirate finances. AWRP for Indian Ocean transits fell from peak levels to near-zero by 2015. The marine war market entered a prolonged soft cycle — standard war risk rates declined significantly. War underwriters maintained capacity but profits compressed. The soft cycle lasted until the Ukraine/Red Sea crisis of 2022–23. EST
2014AVMH17 — Malaysia Airlines B777 Shot Down Over Ukraine (AW)
Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 was shot down by a Russian BUK missile over eastern Ukraine on 17 July 2014, killing all 298 aboard. VER The aviation war (AW) hull loss was approximately $97m. The incident foreshadowed the broader Ukraine conflict and led to immediate rerouting of commercial aircraft away from eastern Ukraine. Aviation war (AW) underwriters increased premiums for Ukrainian airspace. The JRC investigation confirmed Russian state involvement, raising issues of government-linked loss attribution in AW policies.
2015–18WARYemen War — Houthi Attacks on Vessels Begin; Gulf Risk Elevated
The Yemen civil war (from 2015) brought Houthi rebels, Iranian-backed, into the conflict. From 2016, Houthi forces conducted drone and missile attacks on vessels in the Red Sea, Bab-el-Mandeb Strait and Gulf of Aden. Initial attacks targeted Saudi-coalition linked vessels, but commercial shipping was increasingly affected. Lloyd's war risk underwriters began loading AWRP for Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb transits from 2017–18. VER
2019WARStrait of Hormuz — Iran Seizes MT Stena Impero (UK-Flagged)
Iran seized the British-flagged oil tanker MT Stena Impero in the Strait of Hormuz on 19 July 2019, in retaliation for the UK's detention of the Iranian tanker Grace 1 in Gibraltar. VER The seizure — held for 10 weeks — was a textbook capture and detention claim under CL.280 Clause 1.2. Lloyd's war risk underwriters paid hull war claims; the vessel was released unharmed. AWRP for Strait of Hormuz transits increased significantly in 2019. The episode demonstrated state-sponsored vessel seizure as a live war risk tool.
2020MINEGulf of Oman — Mystery Tanker Attacks (Limpet Mines)
A series of attacks on tankers in the Gulf of Oman and Gulf of Aden from May 2019 to mid-2020 — involving suspected limpet mine attachments attributed to Iran — damaged six vessels including the MT Front Altair and MT Kokuka Courageous. EST The incidents were covered under marine hull war (W); mine detonation is explicitly covered under CL.280 Clause 1.3. The attacks demonstrated new asymmetric maritime tactics and led to increased AWRP for Gulf of Oman transits.
2020AVPS752 — Ukraine International Airlines B737 Shot Down (Iran)
Ukraine International Airlines Flight PS752 was shot down by Iranian Revolutionary Guard missiles on 8 January 2020, killing all 176 aboard. VER Iran initially denied responsibility before admitting the shoot-down was accidental. The aviation war (AW) hull loss was approximately $43m. The incident raised again the question of civilian airspace vulnerability in conflict-adjacent regions, and reinforced the NOTAM-based airspace closure protocols that became standard practice during the Ukraine war from 2022.
2022 (Feb)WARRussia Invades Ukraine — Black Sea War Risk Crisis
Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine on 24 February 2022 trapped approximately 64 commercial vessels in Ukrainian Black Sea ports (Odessa, Mykolaiv, Mariupol). VER The JWC immediately designated the Black Sea as a High Risk Area. 48-hour war cancellation notices were issued market-wide. Naval mines drifted into international shipping lanes; several vessels were struck and damaged. Total marine hull war losses from Ukraine are estimated at over $1 billion. EST The parallel aviation war/confiscation event (Russia retaining ~400–500 leased Western aircraft) was the largest single AW event in history.
2022–23MINEBlack Sea Grain Corridor — War-Risk-Insured Humanitarian Shipping
The UN-brokered Black Sea Grain Initiative (July 2022) allowed vessels to transit the Black Sea to export Ukrainian grain despite the war. Specialist war risk cover was arranged for corridor vessels, with the Lloyd's war market providing bespoke hull war and cargo war policies. VER The arrangement demonstrated the war market's willingness to provide coverage even in active conflict zones when adequately compensated and with appropriate risk management frameworks. Russia's withdrawal from the corridor in July 2023 ended the arrangement.
2023 (Nov–Dec)WARHouthi Red Sea Campaign Begins — JWC Listed Area Designated
From November 2023, Houthi forces dramatically escalated attacks on commercial vessels in the Red Sea, ostensibly in support of Hamas following the 7 October 2023 Gaza conflict. Vessels of multiple nationalities and ownership were targeted indiscriminately with drones, anti-ship ballistic missiles and helicopter-borne boarding parties. VER The JWC designated the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and Bab-el-Mandeb Strait as a Listed Area in December 2023. War risk rates for Red Sea transits surged approximately 10-fold within weeks.
2024 (Jan)WARMV Marlin Luanda — Tanker Set Ablaze (Houthi Missile Strike)
The Marshall Islands-flagged MV Marlin Luanda was struck by a Houthi anti-ship ballistic missile on 26 January 2024, causing a major fire. VER The vessel — a chemical/product tanker — suffered significant damage. The incident was one of the clearest examples of direct missile damage triggering a marine hull war (W) claim under CL.280 Clause 1.1 (hostile act by a belligerent-aligned non-state armed group). The attack reinforced the market's move to per-transit rather than annual AWRP for Red Sea transits.
2024MKTRed Sea Rerouting & War Market Hard Cycle
By mid-2024, the majority of major container lines (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Evergreen) had suspended Red Sea transits and rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days per voyage and dramatically increasing freight rates. VER For the war risk market, Red Sea AWRP stabilised at 0.3–1.0%+ per transit (vs ~0.07% pre-crisis). War risk capacity remained available but at higher prices; the market entered the most profitable war underwriting cycle since the peak Somali piracy era. EST
2024REGBMP6 Published — Updated Best Management Practice
The sixth edition of Best Management Practice (BMP6) was published in 2023/24 to address the changed threat environment — specifically drone and missile attacks in the Red Sea, which BMP5 (2018, focused on Somali piracy) did not address. BMP6 provided updated guidance on drone detection, electronic warfare, speed and positioning protocols, convoy options and UKMTO reporting for the Red Sea HRA. Lloyd's war risk underwriters began incorporating BMP6 compliance expectations into AWRP assessments for Red Sea transits. VER
