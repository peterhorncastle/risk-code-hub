Market Cycle — Key Eras

2008–12 — Somali Piracy Peak

War market hardened significantly. AWRP for Indian Ocean/Gulf of Aden reached multi-year highs. War syndicates profitable despite large individual piracy claims. BMP/PCASP introduction drove sharp reversal from 2012.

2012–21 — Prolonged Soft Market

Somali piracy collapsed; rates fell year-on-year. Gulf of Guinea maintained modest AWRP; Iran/Hormuz elevated in 2019. Overall war market loss ratios benign; base rates at historic lows by 2021. Many syndicates reducing war capacity or combining with other marine classes.

2022 — Ukraine Hard Reset

Russia invades Ukraine — Black Sea JWC designation, vessel detentions, mine damage. $1bn+ estimated war losses. 48-hour cancellations issued; Black Sea AWRP surged from near-zero to 0.5–3.0%+ per voyage. AW (aviation confiscation) event simultaneously: ~$10–15bn.

2023–24 — Red Sea Crisis

Houthi campaign from November 2023; JWC Listed Area December 2023; Red Sea AWRP 10-fold surge. First vessel total losses (MV Rubymar, MV True Confidence). Cape rerouting by container lines. War market most profitable since Somali piracy peak. EST

ROE Scenarios — Lloyd's Marine War Portfolio ILLUS

Bull Scenario
+18–28%
Houthi campaign ends; Red Sea AWRP normalises at higher-than-pre-crisis level (0.10–0.15%); no new major conflict theatre; Black Sea/Ukraine resolution; aviation confiscation claims settled below full exposure; elevated base rates retained
Base Scenario
+8–15%
Red Sea campaign continues at current intensity; 2–4 vessel total losses per annum; AWRP maintained 0.3–0.5% per transit; Black Sea risk persists; aviation confiscation claims partially settled; base rates elevated but sustainable combined ratio
Bear Scenario
−20–40%
Major fleet sinking event (VLCC or ULCV in Red Sea); Hormuz closure; VLCC used as weapon (tanker war scenario); Iran–Israel direct conflict destroying multiple vessels; AW confiscation settled at full exposure; war between major powers
