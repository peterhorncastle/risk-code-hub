Two-Tier War Risk Pricing Structure

Marine hull war is priced on a two-tier basis: a base world-wide war rate (annual, covering standard world-wide trading including most benign areas) plus Additional War Risk Premium (AWRP) charged per-transit or per-call when a vessel enters a JWC Listed Area. These two components are priced and often placed separately.

Total War Premium = Annual World-Wide War Rate (% of Insured Value)
                  + Additional War Risk Premium (AWRP per transit/call)
                    × Vessel Type Loading
                    × Flag State Factor
                    × Owner Experience Factor
                    × Security/BMP Compliance Discount

All rates illustrative and market-sensitive. War risk rates change rapidly with geopolitical conditions. ILLUS

Additional War Risk Premium (AWRP) — JWC Listed Areas ILLUS

AWRP is charged per-transit or per-call for vessels entering JWC Listed Areas. Rates are agreed between broker and leading Lloyd's war syndicate prior to transit; all followers then bind at those terms. AWRP changes rapidly — the rates below reflect approximate market levels at time of writing and should not be relied upon for actual placement.

Key Rating Factors

Vessel Type & Trade

  • Tankers (VLCC/Suezmax): higher profile target
  • Container vessels: high-value cargo; liner routes
  • Bulk carriers: lower-value; slower speed (higher piracy risk)
  • Offshore/OSV: static position near assets; higher risk
  • Passenger/cruise: zero tolerance for crew/passenger risk

Trade Route & JWC Areas

  • Declared itinerary vs. open trading warranty
  • Number of JWC Listed Area transits p.a.
  • Port of call risk within Listed Areas
  • Routing flexibility / Cape option available
  • Seasonal routing patterns (Red Sea summer vs. winter)

Flag State & Registration

  • Flag state diplomatic relationships with conflict parties
  • Russian/Iranian flags: near-unplaceable in Lloyd's market
  • Open registry flags (Marshall Islands, Panama, Liberia): standard
  • State-owned vessels (state company affiliation) attract scrutiny
  • Classification society and ISM compliance

Vessel Age & Condition

  • Older vessels: higher survey requirements
  • Vessel condition affects speed and evasion capability
  • Technical management quality
  • AIS transponder operation (war underwriters require AIS on)
  • Vessel speed (piracy protection: >18 knots in HRA)

Security & Risk Management

  • BMP6 compliance status
  • PCASP (armed guards) employed in HRA
  • UKMTO registration and reporting
  • Citadel / refuge space fitted
  • Anti-drone measures (electronic countermeasures for Red Sea)
  • Convoy participation (where available)

Owner's Experience & Claims History

  • Prior war risk claims (especially prior piracy seizures)
  • Owner's operational practices in HRAs
  • Quality of shipmanagement company
  • Sanctions screening status (OFAC, UK, EU)
  • Beneficial ownership transparency

Constructive Total Loss (CTL) & Detention — Key Claims Concepts

War risk claims differ fundamentally from H&M claims in their detention/CTL provisions. A vessel captured or detained but not physically destroyed may still be a constructive total loss if the recovery cost or delay renders recovery economically unviable.

Worked Example A — VLCC Red Sea Transit (2024 Conditions) ILLUS

Risk: 300,000 DWT VLCC — Persian Gulf to Rotterdam transit via Red Sea / Suez
VesselVLCC, Marshall Islands flag, 8 years old, ISM certified
Insured hull value$120m (agreed value)
Annual world-wide war rate~0.07% p.a. = ~$84,000 p.a. ILLUS
Red Sea transit AWRP~0.5% per transit × $120m = ~$600,000 per transit ILLUS
Security measuresBMP6 compliant; PCASP 4 guards; UKMTO registered; AIS on; anti-drone watch
Security discount~10% AWRP reduction for full BMP6 + PCASP ILLUS
Net AWRP per transit~$540,000 per Red Sea transit ILLUS
Owner decisionReroute Cape of Good Hope — additional voyage cost ~$1.5m (fuel + time); avoids $540,000 AWRP. Owner selects Cape route. ILLUS

Illustrative. In 2024, most container operators rerouted Cape; tanker operators (motivated by higher freight rates for Middle East crude) made individual transit/Cape assessments. Many bulk carriers accepted the AWRP and transited, particularly vessels carrying lower-value bulk cargo where the voyage economics justified the risk.
