The F.C.&S. Clause — Foundation of the War Market The Free of Capture and Seizure (F.C.&S.) clause in H&M (hull & machinery) policies excludes all war perils from the standard hull policy, creating the separate war market. Without F.C.&S., H&M underwriters would bear war risk that is unpriceable on a standard actuarial basis. The war market specialist — writing code W — takes the risks excluded by F.C.&S. This bifurcation has been the structural foundation of the Lloyd's marine market since the nineteenth century. VER

Coverage Architecture — Institute War & Strikes Clauses Hulls (CL.280)

The Institute War and Strikes Clauses Hulls—Time (1983, CL.280) is the standard Lloyd's marine war wording. Six covered perils under Clause 1:

Clause 1.1 — War & Hostilities

  • War, civil war, revolution, rebellion
  • Insurrection or civil strife
  • Hostile act by/against a belligerent power
  • Includes declared and undeclared wars

Clause 1.2 & 1.3 — Capture, Seizure & Mines

  • Capture, seizure, arrest, restraint, detainment
  • Consequences of detention (CTL)
  • Derelict mines, torpedoes, bombs
  • Derelict weapons of war

Clause 1.4 & 1.5 — Strikes & Terrorism / Piracy

  • Strikers and locked-out workmen
  • Labour disturbances, riots
  • Terrorists or persons acting from political motive
  • Piracy (Schedule risk under war, not H&M)

Clause 1.6 — Confiscation & Expropriation

  • Confiscation by a government
  • Expropriation by a government
  • Note: own government often sub-limited or excluded
  • Distinguishes confiscation from nationalisation

Extended Perils (WX)

  • Breach voyage cover (trading in excluded areas)
  • Chemical / biological attack (sub-limited)
  • Cyber war extensions (contested post-NotPetya)
  • War delay / loss of hire (separate)

Key Exclusions (CL.280)

  • Nuclear / radiological contamination (NBCR)
  • Wilful misconduct of owners
  • Ordinary judicial process (arrest by own country)
  • Trading warranty breach (unless WX breach voyage)
  • War between P5 powers (some wordings)
48-Hour War Cancellation Clause Standard in all hull war policies: underwriters may cancel war cover at 48 hours' notice (7 days for War Protection & Indemnity under some P&I club rules). This gives the market the ability to re-price or withdraw capacity within 48 hours of a major escalation — as occurred when Russia invaded Ukraine (February 2022) and during the Houthi Red Sea campaign (late 2023). Cancellation triggers automatic re-rating negotiations. VER

Joint War Committee (JWC) — High Risk Listed Areas

The Joint War Committee (JWC) — a Lloyd's / IUA joint body — publishes and updates the Hull War, Strikes, Terrorism and Related Perils Listed Areas. Vessels transiting Listed Areas require specific underwriter approval and attract Additional War Risk Premium (AWRP). VER

Aviation War (AW) — Hull War for Aircraft

The AVN52 exclusion clause in standard aviation H&M (hull & machinery) policies excludes war, hijacking and related perils — the aviation equivalent of the marine F.C.&S. clause. Code AW covers these excluded aviation war perils, including:

AW PerilExamplesNotes
War and hostilities (aviation)Aircraft destroyed by missile/military action; MH17 (Malaysia Airlines, 2014, shot down over Ukraine by BUK missile)State v. state or non-state armed conflict
HijackingDiversion by armed persons; politically motivated aircraft seizureAW covers hijacking where AVN52 applies; H&M does not
SabotageExplosive devices placed on aircraft by political actorsDistinguished from criminal acts not politically motivated
Governmental confiscationAircraft seized by foreign government; sanction-related detention (Russia 2022 — ~$10bn+ leased aircraft stranded)Own-country confiscation exclusion common
Terrorism (aviation)9/11 attacks (four hijacked aircraft); USS Cole (maritime parallel)Post-9/11: market re-structured extensively; aviation war pools formed temporarily
Russia 2022 — Aviation War's Largest Modern Loss Event Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine (February 2022), Western lessors were unable to recover approximately 400–500 leased aircraft stranded in Russia as Moscow refused repatriation. Total hull value involved was estimated at $10–15 billion. EST Aviation war/confiscation policies were triggered; claims disputes between lessors, airlines and underwriters are ongoing. The event dwarfs any previous aviation war loss and has fundamentally reshaped aviation war capacity and pricing.

Market Participants

Lloyd's Specialist War Syndicates

The marine war market is concentrated among a small number of Lloyd's specialist syndicates with long-standing expertise. War risks are a leading Lloyd's specialism globally — the market's reputation for writing difficult political risks dates to the Lloyd's Coffee House era.

P&I Clubs (War P&I)

P&I clubs (Gard, Skuld, Standard, Britannia etc.) provide war P&I (third-party liability for war events) separately from hull war. Coordination between hull war underwriters and war P&I is critical in total loss scenarios. War P&I is pooled through the International Group of P&I Clubs (IG).

Specialist War Brokers

Marine war is placed by specialist brokers with expertise in JWC areas, AWRP negotiations and political risk assessment. Major shipbroking firms maintain dedicated war risk desks (Marsh, WTW, Gallagher, Tysers/BMS).

JWC (Joint War Committee)

Joint body of Lloyd's and IUA underwriters that monitors geopolitical risk globally and designates/updates Listed Areas. JWC market bulletins are the primary reference for AWRP triggering and area-specific rate adjustments.

UKMTO (UK Maritime Trade Operations)

Royal Navy-operated maritime security coordination centre based in Dubai. UKMTO provides real-time intelligence to commercial vessels transiting high-risk areas (HRA) and is the primary reporting point for incidents. Essential reference for war underwriters monitoring evolving threats.

Reinsurance Market

Marine war is predominantly retained within the Lloyd's market with limited traditional reinsurance. Catastrophe scenarios (world war, major state conflict destroying multiple vessels simultaneously) are a key tail risk. Some syndicates purchase industry loss warranty (ILW) protection.
