War Risk Programme Architecture

Marine hull war risk is placed as a separate policy from the Hull & Machinery (H&M) policy, reflecting the F.C.&S. (Free of Capture & Seizure) exclusion carved out of all standard H&M wordings. A typical fleet programme combines a world-wide base war policy (annual) with ad hoc AWRP endorsements for JWC Listed Area transits, supplemented by P&I war cover, crew K&R/PA arrangements, and facultative reinsurance placements. VER

Why War Risk is Separate Institute Time Clauses Hulls (ITCH) and equivalent H&M wordings exclude "war, strikes, terrorism and related perils" via the F.C.&S. clause. This creates a coverage gap that shipowners must fill through a dedicated war risk policy. The Lloyd's War & Strikes market and certain mutuals (Hellenic, Swedish Club) are the principal providers of this cover.

Policy Mechanics — Annual Policy + AWRP Endorsements

Annual World-Wide Base Policy

  • 12-month period; covers world-wide transits including international waters
  • Applies CL.280 perils to all fleet vessels as scheduled
  • Base rate on insured value (typically 0.035%–0.12% p.a. post-2022)
  • 48-hour cancellation clause: underwriter may cancel or re-price at 48 hours' notice; owner must then find alternative cover
  • New vessels added by endorsement; sold vessels deleted (return of pro-rata premium)

AWRP Endorsement Process

  • Owner/operator identifies transit through JWC Listed Area
  • Broker contacts lead underwriter for AWRP rate (per voyage or per day)
  • Endorsement issued specifying vessel name, voyage, duration, AWRP amount
  • Payment: premium typically billed on closing of voyage or monthly if multiple transits
  • Sub-rates for anchorage vs. transit; higher for overnight anchoring in HRA

War & P&I Coordination

  • Hull war policy covers physical loss/damage to vessel and CTL claims
  • P&I Club war rules cover third-party liabilities (injury, pollution, cargo) arising from war perils
  • Coordination clause essential: avoid overlap on salvage/collision war scenarios
  • P&I war cancellation typically mirrors hull war 48-hour notice
  • IG Group P&I war pooling: major losses shared above $10m retention

Crew K&R and PA Integration

  • MLC 2006 Art. 4.2: shipowner must carry insurance for crew death, disability, and repatriation
  • KM-coded maritime PA covers AD&D for seafarers transiting war zones
  • K&R policy (separate) covers ransom, negotiation costs, and post-incident care
  • Piracy K&R typically requires UKMTO registration and BMP compliance as a condition precedent
  • K&R and hull war policies must not contain conflicting ransom coverage provisions

48-Hour Cancellation Clause — Programme Implications

Critical Programme Risk — Cancellation at Escalation The 48-hour war cancellation clause is standard in CL.280-based policies. When geopolitical risk escalates rapidly (as with Ukraine Feb 2022 or Red Sea Dec 2023), underwriters issued mass cancellation notices within hours of JWC listing decisions. Owners who could not place replacement cover faced "war risk holes" in their programme. Effective fleet programme management requires contingency placement arrangements and pre-agreed reinstatement terms. EST

War Syndicate Reinsurance Structure EST

War Risk RI — Unique Characteristics Unlike H&M reinsurance which uses quota share and excess of loss on standard attritional/catastrophe splits, marine war risk reinsurance must address correlated catastrophe scenarios (e.g., port closure trapping multiple vessels, simultaneous strikes on a fleet). Underwriters manage accumulation through vessel scheduling and per-location PML controls.

Proportional (Quota Share) RI

Smaller war risk syndicates cede 20–40% of premium to RI panel. RI panel receives same % of losses. Maintains underwriting capacity for large scheduled fleets while limiting net PML exposure per syndicate.

Excess of Loss RI — Per Occurrence

Covers single major loss events: CTL of high-value vessel (>$100m), seizure of multiple vessels simultaneously. Layer structure typically $50m xs $50m to $200m xs $100m. Scarce and expensive post-2022.

Catastrophe Aggregate RI

Covers accumulation of war losses across a fleet in a defined period (e.g., Red Sea campaign). Triggers on aggregate loss exceeding retention. Rarely purchased; most syndicates manage via PML scheduling.

Facultative RI — Single Vessel

For vessels above syndicate's automatic binding capacity (typically >$100m insured value). Facultative RI placed alongside primary placement. Broker markets slip to reinsurers simultaneously with primary slip.

Multinational Fleet Programmes — Admitted Cover Requirements EST

Marine War — Largely Non-Admitted Unlike P&I (via Club network) or H&M (some admitted requirements), marine hull war risk is largely placed on a non-admitted basis through the London market. Most flag states and port states accept Lloyd's or IUA market policies. However, a small number of jurisdictions (e.g., Brazil, China, India) may require locally admitted war cover for vessels flagged or trading domestically. Shipowners should verify flag state requirements for each vessel in the fleet. EST

Aviation War (AW Code) — Programme Structure Differences

AVN52 — Aviation War Exclusion

Standard aviation H&M policies exclude war perils via AVN52, equivalent to the marine F.C.&S. clause. The AW code (Aviation War) covers these excluded perils: war, hi-jack, confiscation, government seizure, nuclear/biological/chemical terrorism.

Russia 2022 — Confiscation Crisis

~520 western-leased aircraft (value ~$10bn) stranded in Russia after sanctions. Coverage dispute: was confiscation a "war" or "government seizure"? Multiple insurers contested. Lessors/underwriters faced long-running litigation. Major market loss event for AW underwriters.

Aviation War Pool (AWAS/Airline War Pools)

Airlines and lessors access aviation war cover through airline war pools and specialist Lloyd's syndicates. Coverage mirrors marine war structure: annual base policy + specific geographical endorsements for high-risk routes. AVN52C exclusion carve-backs used.

Cyber/War Attribution — Aviation

GPS spoofing, runway lighting interference, and cyber attacks on avionics create attribution ambiguity (war vs cyber vs mechanical). Aviation war underwriters are developing specific cyber-war exclusion/inclusion language similar to Lloyds CY/CZ marine cyber developments.
