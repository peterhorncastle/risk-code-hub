Best Management Practice (BMP6) — The Primary Risk Control Framework

BMP6 (published by BIMCO, ICS, INTERTANKO, IMO and other bodies) is the industry standard for self-protection against piracy and armed robbery in designated High Risk Areas. For the Red Sea/Gulf of Aden HRA (the primary active area as of 2024), BMP6 incorporates specific guidance on drone and missile threats that BMP5 (2018, piracy-focused) did not address. VER

Pre-Transit Planning

  • Route and timing assessment vs. current JWC Listed Area
  • UKMTO registration (Gulf of Aden Voluntary Reporting Scheme)
  • Company security plan review and crew briefing
  • Confirm AWRP endorsement from war risk underwriters
  • Convoy / escort options assessed (EU NavFor Aspides, US-led coalition)
  • Speed assessment: maximum safe speed in HRA

Vessel Hardening

  • Razor wire / anti-boarding measures at waterline
  • CCTV monitoring all external access points
  • Citadel / safe muster point with independent comms
  • Fire hoses rigged for use as water barrier
  • External lighting system (night threat detection)
  • AIS maintained on (AIS-off is a warranty breach)

Drone / Missile Threat (Red Sea Specific)

  • Radar watch: continuous lookout for fast-approaching contacts
  • Manoeuvring drills to reduce attack effectiveness
  • NBAI (Naval/Ballistic Anti-Impact) radar alerts
  • Electronic countermeasures (ECM) — specialist equipment
  • Avoid known attack corridors (within 50nm of Yemen coast)
  • Helicopter-boarding countermeasures (speed, manoeuvre)

PCASP — Armed Guards

  • Minimum 4 personnel for HRA transits (BIMCO guidance)
  • Arms licensed by flag state authority
  • Firearms held in secure onboard armoury
  • Rules of engagement (RoE) pre-agreed with master
  • PSC documentation prepared for port inspections
  • Weapons declaration at port entry (flag state coordination)
UKMTO — UK Maritime Trade Operations (Dubai) UKMTO is the Royal Navy's maritime security coordination centre, based in Dubai, providing 24/7 intelligence to commercial vessels transiting the Gulf of Aden / Indian Ocean HRA. Vessels should register with UKMTO on entry to the Voluntary Reporting Area and report position/status regularly. UKMTO coordinates with naval task forces (EU NavFor Atalanta/Aspides, Combined Task Force 151, CMF) and provides immediate incident response. Lloyd's war risk underwriters treat UKMTO registration as a risk management best practice and, in some wordings, a warranty. VER

Transit / Rerouting Decision Framework — Red Sea (2024)

The decision whether to transit the Red Sea or reroute Cape of Good Hope involves balancing war risk AWRP cost, voyage time/fuel cost, and freight rate uplift for Red Sea routes. Underwriters should understand operators' decision logic.

Vessels Most Likely to Still Transit Red Sea (2024) Bulk carriers carry lower-value cargo (coal, grain, fertiliser) with thin freight margins. For these operators, the Cape detour's additional fuel cost and time often exceeds the AWRP. Many bulk carriers and some product tankers (Middle East crude oil premium freight rates) continue to transit, paying AWRP. War risk underwriters writing bulk carrier fleets maintain elevated HRA exposure relative to container operators who have Cape-rerouted. EST

Sanctions Compliance — A Non-Negotiable Pre-Condition

Lloyd's war risk underwriters operate under strict sanctions frameworks. Providing coverage to a sanctioned vessel, owner or cargo is a criminal offence in multiple jurisdictions. War underwriters must conduct sanctions screening before binding and before paying any claim. VER

  • OFAC (US Office of Foreign Assets Control): US-link nexus (USD transactions, US persons, US-owned vessels) triggers OFAC compliance. OFAC SDN list must be checked: vessel, owner, beneficial owner, charterer, cargo counterparty and flag state
  • UK OFSI (Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation): HM Treasury sanctions list. Post-Ukraine 2022: comprehensive Russia sanctions including named vessels, Russian entities and oligarch-owned tonnage
  • EU Sanctions: EU Regulation 833/2014 and amendments: Russian-flag vessels and specified Russian commodities excluded from EU port access and EU-insurer coverage
  • UN Sanctions: UNSC resolutions on North Korea, Iran, Libya — vessel calls at sanctioned ports can void insurance coverage
  • Dark fleet / shadow fleet: Vessels transporting Russian, Iranian or Venezuelan oil outside sanctions using AIS manipulation, ship-to-ship transfers and opaque ownership are a growing market concern. War underwriters must screen for these vessels in their fleet programmes
AIS Manipulation / Dark Vessel Behaviour Vessels involved in sanctions evasion frequently disable or spoof their AIS transponder. Most Lloyd's war risk wordings now contain explicit provisions that losses are excluded or void where AIS was disabled/manipulated at or around the time of loss. From an underwriting perspective, any evidence of prior AIS manipulation is grounds for non-renewal or declination. UKMTO and maritime intelligence services (Windward, Lloyd's List Intelligence, BIMCO) provide AIS monitoring data. VER

Risk Management Guidance for Shipowners

Before Transiting Any JWC Listed Area

  • Notify your war risk underwriter: Obtain AWRP endorsement before entering any JWC Listed Area — entering without prior endorsement may void coverage
  • Brief the master and crew: Ensure the crew understand the BMP6 procedures, citadel location and UKMTO reporting obligations before entering the HRA
  • Confirm PCASP arrangements: If armed guards are required by your war risk policy or BMP guidance, ensure a licensed provider is arranged and weapons are legally cleared for the flag state and port calls
  • Sanctions check the entire voyage: Review charterer, cargo, ports of call, and cargo counterparties against all applicable sanctions lists before departure

During an Incident

  • Activate citadel if boarded: Send Mayday on VHF 16; report to UKMTO; crew to citadel; do not resist armed boarding parties
  • Preserve evidence: Maintain CCTV recordings, log all communications, photograph any damage — critical for subsequent war risk claims
  • Notify war risk underwriters immediately: Sue & Labour obligations require prompt notification; failure to notify can prejudice the claim
  • Do not pay ransom without insurer involvement: K&R policies require underwriter involvement in ransom negotiation; independent ransom payments may void K&R recovery
