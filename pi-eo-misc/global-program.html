Programme Structure Options

Single Global Master Policy

A single Lloyd's or London market PI policy with worldwide (or near-worldwide) territorial scope. Suitable for professional firms providing services from a single domicile (typically UK) to clients globally but without overseas offices. Standard approach for most E8/ED/EE risks below £50m revenue. Claims covered wherever the services are delivered. EST

Master + Local Admitted Policies

A master Lloyd's policy sits above a grid of locally admitted policies in jurisdictions that require admitted coverage (USA, Germany, Brazil, India, China). The master provides "difference in conditions" (DIC) and "difference in limits" (DIL) coverage where local policies are narrower or lower-limit. Suitable for multi-office global professional services firms. EST

USA/Canada Specialist Policy + Global Master

US and Canadian PI markets are priced and underwritten separately from the global E8/ED/EE book. US-admitted PI for US-domiciled professional services operations; separate Lloyd's policy for non-US global coverage. Dual policy structure avoids US jury awards contaminating global programme pricing and avoids US-driven claims aggregating against global limit. EST

E9 Binder / MGA Facility (SME Portfolios)

Volume SME miscellaneous PI is distributed via Lloyd's E9 binder/MGA arrangements. Coverholders operate delegated authority to bind risks typically up to £5m limit / £5m revenue. Online platforms increasingly common for sub-£1m limit risks. Lloyd's oversight via Lloyd's Coverholder Conduct Standards; capacity performance management via Lloyd's Franchise Performance Directorate. VER

8-Step Global PI Programme Placement Workflow

1
Global Scope & Structure Assessment
Map all countries where professional services are delivered (not just where offices are located). Identify regulatory requirements for admitted cover in each jurisdiction. Confirm split of revenues by jurisdiction. Agree on single global vs master+local programme structure.
2
US/Canada Separation Decision
If revenues from USA/Canada >15%, assess whether US-admitted policy is required. US-admitted policy provides better claims defence in US courts; non-admitted (E&S) is an alternative but less preferred by US courts. Confirm whether US professional work is delivered from a US entity or from UK.
3
Limit Analysis & SIR Optimisation
Determine appropriate limit: regulatory minimums by jurisdiction; client contractual requirements (government contracts typically require £5m+); frequency/severity analysis based on claims history. SIR level calibrated against cash flow capacity for self-funded small claims.
4
Prepare Market Submission
Compile: firm profile and services description; revenue by jurisdiction and by sub-service type; 5-year claims history; current policy wording; regulatory PI requirements; engagement letter template; description of quality/review processes; any known circumstances or pending matters.
5
Market Approach & Lead Selection
Approach specialist PI lead syndicates (Beazley, Hiscox, Brit, Markel, TMHCC). Obtain competitive lead terms. For E9 binder risks: approach appropriate MGA coverholder. Select lead based on: wording quality, limit capacity, claims service reputation, and price.
6
Co-Insurance & Excess Layer Placement
Fill co-insurance on primary layer; place excess layers with appropriate market participants (Lloyd's, specialist PI carriers, US-admitted insurer for US layers). Confirm DIC/DIL mechanism for local policies integrates correctly with master programme.
7
Local Admitted Policy Coordination
Arrange locally admitted policies in required jurisdictions (Germany, France, USA, India, Brazil, Australia). Confirm wording back-to-back with master where possible; document all DIC/DIL conditions. Provide master insurer with local policy schedules for programme file.
8
Ongoing Programme Management
Annual programme review: revenue updates, new jurisdiction activities, staff growth, claims reporting. Mid-term notification of material changes (new US client engagements, M&A activity, change in sub-class of work). Claims co-ordination across the tower if multi-layer event occurs.

Illustrative Case Study: Meridian Strategy Group — Global PI Programme Rebuild

Profile: £35m fee income management consultancy; 180 consultants; offices in London, New York, Frankfurt, Singapore; work delivered in 22 countries; sub-specialisms: operational transformation (60%), M&A advisory (25%), technology strategy (15%).

Loss history: Three claims in prior five years: £480k settlement (ERP advisory failure, UK, 2020); £1.2m settlement (M&A strategy advice, German client, 2022); ongoing notification re: public sector procurement advisory (potential quantum £800k, 2023).

Renewal challenge: Incumbent insurer declined renewal after two significant claims; three other markets declined on M&A advisory concentration; company needed to rebuild programme from scratch.

Solution: Specialist PI broker structured: (1) restricted M&A advisory sublimit (£2m xs £500k SIR specifically for M&A engagements); (2) separate US-admitted policy for New York office ($5m, US-admitted carrier); (3) Lloyd's master policy £10m each and every (three syndicates, lead Beazley); (4) German admitted policy €5m (fronted local insurer); (5) Singapore admitted policy SGD 5m (Lloyd's Singapore facility). Total programme premium: £820k p.a. vs £290k expiring — driven primarily by claims loading.

Run-Off Coverage Note: Professional firms that cease trading, merge, or are acquired must arrange run-off PI cover — typically a 6-year extended reporting period (ERP) tail. Without run-off, claims arising from work done before cessation but notified after the last in-force policy expires are uninsured. ERPs are available from incumbent insurers at inception of cessation; cost typically 100–200% of the final annual premium for a 6-year run-off. Directors should ensure run-off is arranged before winding up a professional services company. VER
