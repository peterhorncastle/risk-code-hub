Rating Framework

Miscellaneous PI premium is calculated on a revenue-based rate on line (RoL) basis — the primary exposure driver is the professional service revenue that gives rise to liability, not the asset values at risk. Unlike property lines, the limit of indemnity does not directly correlate to a "sum insured"; it represents the maximum claim the market will fund per claim and in aggregate for the policy period.

Standard Miscellaneous PI Premium Formula
Annual Premium = Professional Revenue × Base Rate × Profession Risk Factor × Claims Loading × Limit Factor × Geographic Factor × Deductible Credit

Where:

  • Professional Revenue = gross fees/revenue from professional services (£, 12-month)
  • Base Rate = profession-specific base rate (% of revenue) — see benchmark table
  • Profession Risk Factor = multiplier for specific risk characteristics within the profession (0.7–2.5×)
  • Claims Loading = experience rating based on 3–5 year claims history (0.8–3.0×); clean = 1.0×
  • Limit Factor = upward adjustment for limits above reference (typically £1m each & every claim); reference limit = 1.0×
  • Geographic Factor = additional loading for USA/Canada exposure (1.0× UK only; 1.3–2.0× with US services)
  • Deductible Credit = reduction for excess of loss (self-insured retention) (0.7–0.95×)

Key Rating Factors — E8/E9 Miscellaneous PI

Profession Type & Risk Category

0.7× – 2.5× base rate

The primary rating driver. Insurance brokers and management consultants carry different intrinsic risk profiles to event managers or training providers. Profession-specific rate tables are essential. High-risk: M&A advisers, restructuring consultants, insurance brokers (retail). Lower-risk: training providers, event managers, basic HR consultants. EST

Fee/Revenue Level & Complexity

Proportional to revenue with cap

Revenue is the rating base. For large professional firms (£50m+ fees), portfolio diversification generally reduces risk relative to a single large-engagement boutique at the same revenue level. Single-client concentrations above 20% of revenue warrant investigation. EST

3–5 Year Claims History (APH)

0.8× – 3.0× loading

Actual premium-to-loss history over the prior 3–5 years. Claims-free: 0.8–0.9× credit on renewal. Single major claim: 1.5–2.0× loading. Two or more claims: 2.0–3.0× loading or declination. Claims frequency (high-count, low-severity) often more adverse than single large claim in isolation. EST

Limit of Indemnity

Reference: £1m each & every

The reference limit varies by profession and regulatory requirement. FCA-authorised brokers have minimum regulatory requirements. Higher limits (£5m–£25m) used by large consulting firms attract a limit factor loading reflecting increased severity potential. "Any one claim" basis preferred by underwriters over "aggregate" for standard professional services. EST

Retroactive Date

Critical term — not a premium factor per se

Full prior acts (no retroactive date) is standard for clean renewal risks with unbroken coverage history. A restricted retroactive date (e.g., inception date) significantly reduces the policy's commercial value but may be used where historical activities are not known or documented. Any gap in cover creates an uninsured window that can only be closed by extended reporting period (ERP) endorsement on the expiring policy. VER

Geographic Scope of Services

1.0× UK only → 2.0× with USA/Canada

USA/Canada exposure carries a significant premium loading (30–100%) due to: higher litigation frequency, higher jury awards, class action exposure, and US-specific regulatory PI triggers. Underwriters must establish: are services provided in or merely delivered to US clients from a UK base? The distinction affects coverage scope and pricing materially. EST

Regulatory Status

FCA/RICS/RIBA: positive factor

FCA-regulated firms (insurance brokers, financial advisers) are subject to ongoing supervision — a positive underwriting factor for risk quality. RICS/RIBA/CIPD membership signals professional standards compliance. Unregulated professions (management consultants, PR agencies) require underwriters to assess internal risk governance as a proxy for professional standards. EST

Client Type: Retail vs Commercial

+15–30% loading for retail consumer clients

Retail/consumer-facing professions (estate agents, travel agents, some HR) carry Consumer Duty/FSMA obligations and higher consumer redress expectations. Commercial-to-commercial advisory businesses typically have lower PI frequency but potentially higher severity per claim. Assess: what proportion of revenue is from individual consumers vs corporate clients? EST

Additional Rating Factors — ED Design Professionals

Design Complexity & Specification Scope

Key severity driver

Interior fit-outs to residential standard: lower risk. Commercial fit-outs with structural or M&E interface: higher risk. Industrial product design (physical performance specifications): higher risk. Urban planning/masterplan advice: higher risk due to long-tail and public interest disputes. Architects post-Grenfell face heightened fire safety specification scrutiny. EST

IP Clearance Process

Critical due diligence factor

Does the designer have a documented IP clearance process (trade mark searches, design right checks) for brand/identity work? Absence of a clearance process is a significant PI risk indicator for graphic and brand designers. Underwriters should confirm clearance procedure as part of presentation. EST

Post-Grenfell Fire Safety (Architects)

+20–50% loading for non-residential

Following the Building Safety Act 2022 and Grenfell Inquiry, architects providing specification advice on higher-risk buildings (HRBs: 7+ storeys or 18m+) face enhanced scrutiny. Fire safety specification errors are a significant claims driver. Underwriters should check whether architects work on HRBs and whether they have current fire safety CPD. VER

Contract Scope: Design Only vs Design & Build

Design & Build → higher risk

An architect providing design-only services (ED) has cleaner liability boundaries than one also acting as contractor or project manager. Design & Build roles blur the PI / construction liability boundary; underwriters should confirm whether the scope is design advice only, or whether construction supervision and project management are included (which may require specialist E6/E7 treatment). Pre-Bind Checklist

REQUIRED Full description of professional services rendered (not just profession name)
REQUIRED Gross professional fees/revenue (last completed year + projected current year)
REQUIRED 5-year claims history (all claims, irrespective of whether notified or paid under prior policies)
REQUIRED Retroactive date continuity — confirm unbroken PI cover history or explain any gaps
REQUIRED Geographic scope of services — specifically: any USA/Canada engagements? Any overseas offices?
REQUIRED Regulatory status — FCA/RICS/RIBA/CIPD/other professional body authorisation or membership
PREFERRED Client type split (% retail consumers vs % corporate/commercial clients)
PREFERRED Single largest client by revenue (assess concentration risk)
PREFERRED Sub-classes of work (e.g. M&A advisory %, restructuring %, operational consulting % — for management consultants)
PREFERRED IP clearance procedure (for design professionals — ED)
PREFERRED Any work on higher-risk buildings (HRBs) in last 5 years (for architects — ED)
INFO Professional indemnity policy wording preference (MTC, COP, bespoke)
INFO Any known circumstances or pending disputes that may give rise to a claim
INFO Limit requirement (FCA regulatory minimum; contractual client requirement; own risk assessment)

Automatic Referral Triggers

Referral Required: The following risk characteristics require referral to a senior underwriter before binding or quoting. Standard delegated authority does not apply.

USA/Canada Revenue >30%

US/Canada geographic concentration triggers specialist PI pricing, potential punitive damages exclusion review, and US defence cost analysis.

3-Year Loss Ratio >80%

Poor historical performance requires technical pricing review, potential sublimit requirements, or specific exclusions for recurring claim types.

Single Client >30% Revenue

Concentration risk — one client loss could be catastrophic. Underwriter must understand the relationship and whether the concentration is reducing over time.

Any Undisclosed Known Circumstances

Any indication of a potential claim not yet notified — Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation obligation; must be disclosed and evaluated before binding.

M&A / Restructuring Advisory >20% Revenue

Higher severity exposure. Requires review of engagement letters, disclaimer language, and whether advice given is "strategic opinion" or implementable recommendation.

Revenue >£20m (E8/ED Risks)

Large firm PI carries aggregation risk (many simultaneous claims) and potentially systemic exposure. Requires specialist pricing and possibly proportional reinsurance analysis.

Limit >£10m Each and Every

High-limit risks require senior approval; co-insurance or XOL tower analysis may be required. Check whether claim notification history supports limit adequacy.

ESG / Sustainability Advisory as Primary Business

Emerging risk area. Standard wording coverage scope for ESG advisory errors unclear; requires wording review and potential coverage confirmation endorsement.
