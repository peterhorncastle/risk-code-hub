Prevention Controls

PREVENT

Robust Engagement Letters & Scope Definition

The single most effective PI loss prevention measure. Engagement letters must: define the exact scope of professional services; state what is not included; identify assumptions on which advice is based; include liability limitation clauses (where enforceable); and obtain signed acceptance. Scope creep without documented variation orders is a primary PI trigger. VER

PREVENT

Client Due Diligence & Conflict Checks

AML/KYC obligations for regulated firms (FCA-authorised brokers, financial advisers). For all miscellaneous professions: conflict of interest checks before accepting engagements; creditworthiness assessment for large retainer arrangements; verification that client instructions are authorised. Insurance brokers: dual-agency conflicts (Lloyd's ICA compliance). VER

PREVENT

Advice Quality Review Process

Peer review of significant advice deliverables before issue to clients. Management consultants: partner/director review of all reports and recommendations. Insurance brokers: second-sign-off on significant coverage recommendations. Design professionals (ED): drawing/specification sign-off by qualified principal before issue. Documented evidence of review process is key to PI defence. EST

PREVENT

IP Clearance (Design Professionals — ED)

Prior to delivering any brand identity, graphic design, or product design work to clients: conduct formal trade mark/registered design searches via IPO and EUIPO; document search date and scope; flag any potential conflict to client in writing; recommend independent IP solicitor opinion for significant brand identities. Failure to clear is the standard ED IP infringement PI trigger. VER

PREVENT

Contractual Liability Limitation

The Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 and Consumer Rights Act 2015 restrict liability limitation for consumer-facing professionals. For business-to-business professionals: contractual limitation of liability to a multiple of fees (e.g. 1–5×) is standard practice; "reasonable" under UCTA. The limitation must be proportionate and clearly drawn to the client's attention. VER

PREVENT

CPD and Competence Maintenance

Mandatory CPD requirements: FCA-authorised brokers (35hrs p.a.); RIBA architects (35hrs p.a.); RICS members (20hrs p.a.); CIPD HR professionals (continuing professional development requirements). For unregulated professions (management consultants, PR agencies): voluntary CPD as evidence of current competence. Post-Grenfell: fire safety CPD mandatory for building-work architects. VER

Detection Controls

DETECT

Client Satisfaction Monitoring

Systematic client satisfaction reviews at project milestones and at completion. Dissatisfied clients rarely notify PI directly without first raising concerns with the firm. Early identification of dissatisfied clients allows proactive resolution before PI escalation. Management consultant firms: NPS tracking at project completion. Insurance brokers: post-claim satisfaction surveys. EST

DETECT

Contract Milestone & Deadline Tracking

Critical deadline management is essential for property managers (rent reviews, lease renewals, service charge reconciliation), estate agents (offer acceptance periods, search validity), and event managers (booking deposits, cancellation notice periods). Diary management system failures are a direct PI trigger. Central management of all critical dates with alert systems is the control. EST

DETECT

Regulatory Change Monitoring

Insurance brokers: FCA regulatory update tracking (Policy Statements, Consultation Papers, Supervisory Statements). HR consultants: employment law update monitoring (HMRC, ACAS, employment tribunal developments, IR35 guidance). Architects: Building Regulations, Building Safety Act, fire safety standard updates. Failure to track regulatory changes and update advice accordingly is a latent PI trigger. VER

DETECT

Data Protection Compliance Monitoring

ICO self-assessment and annual GDPR/UK GDPR compliance review for all firms handling personal data in the course of professional services (HR consultants, marketing agencies, travel agents, estate agents). Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) maintained and reviewed. Breach notification monitoring (72-hour ICO notification window). Non-compliance is a direct PI trigger for data advisory professionals. VER

Response Controls

RESPOND

Prompt Claims Notification (Critical)

All PI policies operate on a claims-made basis. The notification obligation is one of the most litigated coverage issues. Key principle: when a professional becomes aware of circumstances that may give rise to a claim, they must notify insurers — even if no formal claim has been made. Failure to notify circumstances on time can result in coverage being denied under a subsequent policy. Document all client complaints immediately. VER

RESPOND

Preserve Engagement Documentation

Upon receipt of a complaint or potential claim notification, immediately preserve: all engagement letters and variation orders; all advice memoranda, reports, and recommendations; all client correspondence (email, letters, meeting notes); all working papers and research. Document retention policies should require 6–10 years for significant professional services engagements. Physical and electronic document hold procedures must be activated. VER

RESPOND

Avoid Admissions and Unilateral Remediation

Insurance Act 2015 and standard PI policy conditions require insurer consent before making admissions of liability, settling claims, or incurring remediation costs that the insured expects to recover. Unilateral remediation without notifying insurers first is a common coverage avoidance issue. Firms must notify insurers before incurring any cost associated with resolving a complaint. VER

RESPOND

Instruct Panel Solicitors Early

Most PI policies provide access to insurers' panel solicitors for legal advice at the notification stage, before a claim has formally crystallised. Early legal advice on coverage, liability, and response strategy is significantly cheaper than late-stage intervention. Insurers' panel firms have expertise in specific PI sub-classes (broker PI, design professional PI, management consultant PI). Engage early. EST

Recovery Controls

RECOVER

Subrogation Co-operation

Upon settlement of a PI claim, the insurer steps into the shoes of the insured and may pursue recovery against third parties responsible for the loss (sub-consultants, suppliers, introducers). The insured has a contractual obligation to co-operate with the insurer's subrogation efforts — provide documents, attend meetings, and give evidence if required. Failure to co-operate can breach policy conditions. VER

RECOVER

Post-Claim Process Review

Following resolution of a PI claim, the professional firm should conduct a root cause analysis: what specific act, error, or omission gave rise to the claim? What process control failed? What change to engagement procedures, documentation, or review processes would prevent recurrence? Document the review and retain — this is evidence of improved risk management relevant to future PI renewals and underwriter assessment. EST

RECOVER

Reputation and Client Relationship Management

PI claims, even if successfully defended or settled, can damage professional relationships and firm reputation. A proactive client communication strategy during claims management — appropriate within legal constraints — can preserve relationships. Law firm/professional firm crisis communications specialists can assist with managing reputation impact where the claim is publicly known. EST

Before a Claim — Proactive Steps

1Maintain unbroken PI cover — do not allow policy to lapse; use ERP if ceasing practice
2Keep retroactive date as far back as possible; any restriction limits protection for prior work
3File all engagement letters, advice documents, and correspondence systematically — minimum 6 years retention
4Include notification provisions in engagement letters — inform clients where to direct complaints
5Attend PI-specific CPD — understand what triggers notification duty, what constitutes a "circumstance"
6Review engagement letters annually — update scope, limitation of liability, and GDPR data processing provisions
7Ensure continuity of cover on acquisition of another firm — retroactive date merger and known circumstances disclosure

During & After a Claim

1Notify insurers immediately upon receipt of a claim or awareness of circumstances — do not delay
2Do not make any admission of liability, offer of settlement, or remediation without prior insurer consent
3Preserve all relevant documentation under legal hold — do not delete emails, overwrite files, or amend records
4Co-operate fully with appointed lawyers and loss adjusters — provide all requested documents promptly
5Keep confidential — do not discuss the claim publicly or with other clients; limit discussion to those with need to know
6Maintain ordinary business operations — the claim does not suspend ongoing PI coverage for new work
7After resolution: conduct root cause analysis, update procedures, document improvements for next renewal
8At renewal: disclose the claim fully and accurately — material information non-disclosure can affect future coverage
Insurance Act 2015 — Fair Presentation at Renewal: The Insurance Act 2015 imposes a "fair presentation of risk" obligation at every renewal. All claims and known circumstances (including those that did not result in a formal claim) must be disclosed. The insurer is entitled to all information a "prudent underwriter" would consider material. Proportionate remedy for non-disclosure may include: additional premium, policy amendment, or in extreme cases voidance. Professionals must disclose claims-in-progress, recent settlements, and known potential disputes — even if they believe the claim to be without merit.
