Market Cycle 2010–2024

2010–2015 — Post-GFC Softening

GFC-era hardening (2009–2010) gives way to aggressive competition as new capacity enters. MGA/binder volume expands. Rates decline steadily 5–10% per year. Combined ratios benign as GFC claims settle. Profitability good; premium adequacy erodes quietly. EST

2016–2019 — Latent Loss Emergence

GFC-era claims continue running off; IR35 advisory claims emerge; management consultant public sector failures crystallise. Rates broadly flat to modest increases (+5% for claims-affected segments). Some MGA capacity withdrawn after poor binder performance. Insurance Act 2015 changes notification dynamics. EST

2020–2022 — COVID and Hardening

COVID-19 triggers EE travel/events mass notifications; BI broker PI claims emerge post-test case. Lloyd's performance management restricts E9 binder capacity. Rates increase 15–30% across E8/EE; design professional (ED) rates up 10–20%. Some insurers exit leisure travel E&O entirely. EST

2023–2024 — Selective Stabilisation

Rate increases slow but remain positive (+5–10% for claims-affected risks). Consumer Duty impact on broker PI being priced in. ESG advisory recognised as new risk segment — limited capacity, higher rates. Clean risks with strong governance: flat to modest reductions available. Market highly selective on loss-active accounts. Recession-driven claims surge; major management consultant claim £3m+; insurance broker Consumer Duty mass-notification event; 115% combined ratio. Capital erosion. ILLUS

Pro-Cyclicality Warning: Miscellaneous PI is demonstrably pro-cyclical. Rates fall during economic expansions (when advisory businesses grow revenues and claim frequency appears low), then rise too slowly as recessions reveal accumulated advice failures. The 2010–2015 soft market, combined with expanding management consulting revenues and new advisory categories (ESG, gig-economy HR, digital marketing), created a structural under-pricing condition that only began correcting after COVID-19 and Consumer Duty exposures materialised. Underwriters should maintain pricing discipline independent of recent loss experience.
