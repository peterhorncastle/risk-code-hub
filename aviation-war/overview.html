Core Clause — AVN 48B

The War, Hi-jacking and Other Perils Exclusion Clause (AVN 48B)

Every standard aviation all-risks policy (Hull & Liability — Lloyd's codes H2/H3/L2/L3) contains the AVN 48B exclusion clause. This clause removes from the all-risks policy all losses arising from war, invasion, acts of foreign enemies, civil war, revolution, insurrection, military or usurped power, martial law, seizure, confiscation, nationalisation, expropriation, hi-jacking, terrorism, sabotage, malicious damage, and electromagnetic interference. The aviation war policy (RX) exists specifically to reinstate these excluded perils — it is a separate contract that sits alongside the all-risks policy and covers the layer that AVN 48B removes. Without an RX policy, airlines and lessors have a significant uninsured gap. [L8]

Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

RX — Aviation War & Confiscation

Direct and facultative aviation war insurance for airlines, aircraft lessors and operators. Covers the war, terrorism, hi-jacking, confiscation, expropriation, nationalisation, sabotage, malicious damage and allied perils that are excluded from standard aviation all-risks policies by the AVN 48B clause. Written on a fleet or individual aircraft basis. Lloyd's is the dominant global market. [I4]

RX vs H2/H3 — Critical Distinction

The aviation hull and liability hub (H2/H3/L2/L3/AO/AP) covers mechanical failure, weather, pilot error, bird strike and all standard aviation perils. It does not cover war perils — AVN 48B excludes them entirely. RX is the separate policy that reinstates the war layer. Together, H2/H3 + RX provide complete aviation coverage. Each is a distinct contract with separate limits, premiums and underwriters. [L8]

7-Day Cancellability — Unique Market Feature

Aviation war policies are typically written on a 7-day cancellable basis: underwriters may cancel coverage for specific aircraft or entire fleets operating in or over designated conflict zones on 7 days' notice (48 hours for individual aircraft added to a war zone list). This is a fundamental structural feature — it allows the market to exit rapidly escalating war risk exposures before losses materialise. Standard cancellation for the whole policy is typically 30 days. [I4]

Coverage Taxonomy

Hull War

Physical damage to or total loss of the aircraft hull arising from war perils. Paid on an agreed value basis (the hull value agreed at inception). Triggered by shoot-downs, missile strikes, MANPADS attacks, bomb damage, and confiscation/seizure that amounts to permanent loss of the aircraft. The dominant coverage component by premium for most fleets. [I4]

Liability War

Third-party liability of the airline or operator for death, bodily injury or property damage to passengers or third parties arising from war perils. The MH17 2014 shoot-down is the defining liability war loss — 298 passengers killed, with liability claims against Malaysia Airlines under the Montreal Convention. Typically written with a separate limit from hull war. [I5]

Confiscation / Expropriation / Nationalisation

Government or state actor seizure of aircraft without adequate compensation. The defining modern loss is Russia 2022 — approximately 900 leased aircraft (~$10–13bn value) confiscated when lessors attempted to repossess following Western sanctions. Russian airlines, at the direction of the Russian government, refused to return the aircraft. Claims filed across the Lloyd's market and international reinsurers. Litigation ongoing in multiple jurisdictions. [I7]

Hi-jacking

Unlawful seizure of an aircraft by non-state actors — passengers, crew or ground operatives using force or threat. Classic terrorism-era peril (PFLP, Black September — 1970s); now less frequent but high severity. The Ryanair FR4978 Belarus 2021 forced landing represents a state-directed variant — a MiG-29 intercepted and forced down a commercial aircraft to arrest a political dissident, testing the hi-jacking/confiscation boundary. [L3]

Malicious Damage / Sabotage

Deliberate damage to an aircraft by malicious act — bomb placement (Lockerbie 1988), incendiary devices, MANPADS shoulder-launched surface-to-air missile attacks. Covers ground sabotage by non-crew as well as in-flight attacks. Includes deliberate acts by persons acting on behalf of or in connection with any political organisation, government or faction. [I1]

Electromagnetic Pulse / GNSS Spoofing

Emerging peril under aviation war policies — electronic warfare including GPS/GNSS spoofing and jamming that disrupts aircraft navigation systems. Active in the Baltic, Eastern Mediterranean, and Middle East (particularly near Russia, Iran and conflict zones). Coverage status debated: some market participants argue GNSS spoofing is a war peril if state-directed; others characterise it as a navigation risk covered by all-risks. Currently an unresolved coverage boundary. [I2]

Market Structure: Aviation war is a specialist Lloyd's class written by a small number of dedicated war-risk syndicates and supported by international reinsurers. The market is traditionally lead/follow: one lead underwriter (typically a major Lloyd's syndicate) sets terms, conditions and rate, with following underwriters signing participations. Premium volumes are modest relative to the all-risks hull market but exposures can be catastrophic — the Russia 2022 confiscation event represented a loss exceeding the accumulated GWP of the entire aviation war market over several years. Lloyd's is the acknowledged global leader for aviation war capacity. [I4]
Russia 2022 — Litigation Note: The approximately 900 aircraft confiscated in Russia following the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine represent the largest single loss event in aviation war insurance history — estimated at $10–13bn insured value. The majority of these claims remain subject to active litigation in multiple jurisdictions (Ireland, US, UK, Bermuda). Russian courts have refused to recognise foreign judgments. The full insured loss has not yet been definitively settled as of May 2026. Underwriters carrying significant RX exposures should review their reserving positions carefully in light of ongoing legal developments. [I7]
