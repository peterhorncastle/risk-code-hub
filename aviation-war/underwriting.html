Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Aircraft Agreed Value (Hull War)

Hull war premium is computed as a percentage of the agreed value of the aircraft hull — the value agreed between insured and underwriter at policy inception and payable in full on total loss. Modern narrowbody aircraft (Boeing 737 MAX, Airbus A320neo) agreed values: $35–55m. Widebody (Boeing 777, A350): $120–250m. Business jets: $5–80m. Post-Russia 2022, agreed value verification has become a key underwriting focus. EST

Rate as % of agreed value: see conflict zone table below
High Impact

Route Network & Conflict Zone Overfly

The single most critical pricing factor: does the operator overfly or land in designated conflict zones? Aircraft operating worldwide routes with no conflict zone exposure (e.g., intra-European, domestic Australia) attract minimum war rates. Airlines overflying Yemen, Ukraine, Sahel, South China Sea, or landing in conflict-adjacent states attract significant loadings. Post-MH17, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository (Doc 10108) is the reference standard for zone designation. EST

Loading: 0.5× (no overfly) to 5.0× (active conflict zone operations)
High Impact

Operator's Airspace Management & NOTAM Compliance

Does the operator have a structured NOTAM (Notice to Airmen) review process? Does operations management review ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository updates before route planning? Operators with documented airspace management policies, NOTAM sign-off procedures, and conflict zone risk committees attract lower rates. Operators without structured processes attract loading. EST

Controls credit: −10 to −25% for best-practice airspace management systems
High Impact

Hull War vs Liability War Split

Hull war (physical damage to aircraft) and liability war (third-party claims arising from war perils — e.g., MH17 passenger liability) are priced separately. Liability war rates depend on the passenger-miles flown over conflict zones, the aviation liability limits required (often $1.5bn+ per occurrence for major airlines under ICAO Annex 17 / Montreal Convention), and whether the airline carries high-value passengers on war-exposed routes. VER

Liability war component: 20–40% of total aviation war premium for major carriers
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

7-Day Cancellability Usage

Underwriters assess whether the insured has aircraft exposed in areas where the 7-day (or 48-hour) cancellability might be triggered. Airlines operating in politically volatile regions must understand that underwriters can withdraw war cover — leaving a coverage gap — if conditions deteriorate rapidly. Proactive operators demonstrate procedures for alternative routing within the cancellation notice period. EST

Positive factor: documented 48-hour rerouting plan for each conflict zone; reduces rate
Medium Impact

All-Risks Hull Rate Attachment

Aviation war is traditionally priced as a percentage uplift on the all-risks hull rate, not as a standalone rate. The relationship between all-risks rate and war rate reflects the view that a higher-value, better-maintained fleet (lower all-risks rate) also attracts more disciplined war risk management. The war rate is commonly expressed as a percentage of the all-risks hull premium, or independently as a percentage of aircraft agreed value (see zone table). EST

War rate: commonly 5–25% of all-risks hull premium or 0.02–2.00% of agreed value directly
Medium Impact

MANPADS Threat Assessment

Portable surface-to-air missiles (MANPADS — Man-Portable Air Defence Systems) are effective against aircraft below approximately 15,000ft and are the primary shoot-down threat to commercial aircraft on approach and departure. Operators with routes requiring approach/departure through MANPADS-threatened zones (Somalia, parts of Libya, Yemen, parts of Africa) attract additional loading. Most MANPADS systems have maximum effective ceiling of 12,000–15,000ft. EST

MANPADS loading: +20 to +100% for routes through designated MANPADS threat zones
Medium Impact

Confiscation / Expropriation Risk

Post-Russia 2022, confiscation exposure is a critical underwriting focus for aircraft lessors. Key factors: lessee nationality and ownership (state-controlled carriers present higher confiscation risk), jurisdiction of aircraft registration (aircraft registered in Russia face effective confiscation if sanctions imposed), lease term (longer leases in high-risk jurisdictions increase exposure), and return-of-aircraft contractual protections. EST

Confiscation loading: significant uplift for aircraft on lease to state carriers in sanctioned/at-risk states
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Aircraft Age and Agreed Value

Older aircraft have lower agreed values; a total loss represents a smaller absolute claim. Some underwriters also view older aircraft as less likely to be deployed in conflict zone operations (economic case for operating new aircraft on premium international routes is stronger). Age alone is not a strong rating driver but agreed value accuracy is critical.

Lower Impact

Reinsurance Structure Behind War Underwriters

The Lloyd's aviation war market extensively reinsures its largest risks. Russia 2022 demonstrated that the reinsurance chain matters: where claims exceed individual syndicate capacity, reinsurance treaty structures and retrocession arrangements determine net loss. Underwriters with strong reinsurance support can offer higher limits at competitive rates.

Lower Impact

Crew Nationality & Hostage Risk

Some crew nationalities attract higher kidnap-for-ransom risk in specific regions (particularly Sub-Saharan Africa and parts of the Middle East). Hostage situations — while covered under liability war — create extended claims development. Operators with diverse crew nationalities on high-risk routes attract modest loading. Kidnap & ransom (K&R) policies are a complementary purchase for airlines with high-risk operational profiles.

Lower Impact

Passenger Route Demographics

High-value routes (business class heavy, high-net-worth passengers) on war-exposed itineraries create larger per-head liability war exposures under the Montreal Convention. A widebody aircraft with premium cabin passengers from high-income countries on a Ukraine/Russia-adjacent route theoretically has higher per-passenger liability war exposure than a budget carrier on the same route.

Premium Rating Framework
// Aviation War RX — indicative rating framework

Hull War Premium = Agreed Value × Zone Rate %
  × Route Exposure Factor // proportion of operations in each zone
  × Operator Controls Credit // −25% (best-practice) → +50% (no airspace mgmt)
  × Fleet Size Factor // volume discount for large fleets
  × Confiscation Add-on // additional % where lessee in sanctioned state

Liability War Premium = Passenger-Miles in War Zones
  × Per-Seat Liability Rate // varies by zone and passenger profile
  × Limit Factor // scaled from base liability war limit

Total RX GWP = Hull War Premium + Liability War Premium

Russia 2022 — Confiscation Coverage Complexity: The Russia 2022 confiscation event has raised multiple complex coverage questions: (1) Does the RX policy cover confiscation by a state that has not formally declared war? (2) Where aircraft are Russian-registered but leased from Western lessors, is the lessor the named insured or the airline? (3) Do sanctions themselves constitute a 'government action' that could trigger a separate policy exclusion? (4) Where lead underwriters have paid, are following market reinsurers bound to follow? These questions are actively litigated and underwriters should seek specific legal advice for any Russia 2022 claim exposure. EST
