Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Aviation (Offences) Act 1984 (UK) — Hi-jacking Offences

    Aviation (Offences) Act 1984, Chapter 24. Amends and consolidates UK law relating to offences against and on board aircraft. Implements the Hague Convention (1970) and Tokyo Convention (1963) into UK domestic law. Creates specific criminal offences for unlawful seizure of aircraft, endangering the safety of aircraft, and related acts. Relevant to the legal framework within which aviation war hi-jacking perils are defined and insured.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL Tokyo Convention 1963 — Offences on Board Aircraft

    Convention on Offences and Certain Other Acts Committed on Board Aircraft, signed Tokyo 14 September 1963. Establishes jurisdiction of the state of aircraft registration over offences committed in flight; grants the aircraft commander powers to restrain persons endangering safety. Ratified by 186 states. The first international legal framework addressing hi-jacking and in-flight offences — underpins the definition of the aviation war hi-jacking peril in RX policies. Available at ICAO treaty database.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL Hague Convention 1970 — Unlawful Seizure of Aircraft

    Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Seizure of Aircraft, signed The Hague 16 December 1970, entered into force 14 October 1971. Obligates signatory states to make hi-jacking a serious offence in domestic law and to prosecute or extradite offenders. Ratified by 185 states. The Hague Convention framework directly informs the legal definition of hi-jacking as an aviation war peril and is the international framework within which RX hi-jacking claims are assessed. See also: Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Civil Aviation (Montreal 1971).

  • [L4]
    LEGAL Chicago Convention 1944 (ICAO) — Annex 17 (Security) & Annex 2 (Rules of the Air)

    Convention on International Civil Aviation, signed Chicago 7 December 1944. Establishes ICAO and the international framework for civil aviation. Annex 17 (Security): Standards and Recommended Practices for aviation security — anti-hijacking, airport security, crew screening. Annex 2 (Rules of the Air): governs aircraft operation in flight, including rules for flight in conflict zones and response to interception. Amended periodically; current edition available at ICAO.int. Central to the aviation war risk management framework.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL EU Regulation 2022/334 — Russia Sanctions; Aircraft Asset Freeze

    Council Regulation (EU) 2022/334 of 28 February 2022 amending Regulation (EU) No 833/2014, implementing restrictive measures in view of Russia's actions destabilising the situation in Ukraine. Includes prohibition on providing aircraft and aircraft parts to Russian persons; requirement to terminate leases; asset freeze provisions applicable to sanctioned Russian entities. EU implementing regulation that triggered the mass obligation on aircraft lessors to repossess aircraft from Russian airlines. Available at EUR-Lex.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Aviation Security Act 1982 (UK) — Hi-jacking and Sabotage Offences

    Aviation Security Act 1982, Chapter 36. Consolidates and extends UK law on aviation security. Creates specific offences: hi-jacking (s.1), destroying or damaging aircraft (s.2), other acts endangering safety (s.3), offences in relation to airports (s.4). Implements the Montreal Convention 1971 into UK domestic law. Key legislation for the UK criminal law framework applicable to aviation war perils covered by RX policies.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL UK OFSI — Russia Sanctions Guidance for Aircraft Lessors 2022

    Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), HM Treasury. General licences and guidance issued 2022 relating to implementation of Russia financial sanctions, including guidance specific to aircraft lessors and insurers. Covers: obligations on insurers paying claims relating to Russian-sanctioned entities; requirements for licence applications to make payments; interaction with aviation war claims and confiscation insurance. Available at gov.uk/ofsi. Updated periodically throughout 2022–2024.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL AVN 48B — War, Hi-jacking and Other Perils Exclusion Clause

    AVN 48B — the aviation market standard War, Hi-jacking and Other Perils Exclusion Clause. Published by the Lloyd's Aviation Underwriters Association (LAUA) and recognised internationally. Excludes from aviation all-risks policies: war (declared and undeclared), civil war, revolution, insurrection, seizure, confiscation, nationalisation, expropriation, hi-jacking, terrorist acts, malicious damage, sabotage, and electromagnetic interference. The RX aviation war policy exists to reinstate these excluded perils. Available from Lloyd's Market Association (LMA).

  • [L9]
    LEGAL TRIA 2002 / 2019 — Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (USA)

    Terrorism Risk Insurance Act 2002, Pub. L. 107-297, signed 26 November 2002. Establishes a federal programme for sharing insured commercial property and casualty losses resulting from acts of terrorism. Extended by Terrorism Risk Insurance Extension Act 2005, Terrorism Risk Insurance Program Reauthorization Act 2007, 2015, and 2019 (TRIPRA 2019, extended to 31 December 2027). Provides federal reinsurance backstop for certified terrorism losses above a deductible. Applicable to US aviation war terrorism component; does not apply to state-actor war or confiscation. Available at treasury.gov/TRIP.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY IATA — Safety Report 2023; Conflict Zone Overfly Incidents

    International Air Transport Association, IATA Safety Report 2023. IATA Geneva (2023). Documents fatal accident rates, safety performance by region, and specific analysis of conflict zone overfly incidents and NOTAM compliance. Key source for aviation war loss frequency analysis; provides airline safety performance data used in underwriting. Available at iata.org. Also: IATA Conflict Zone Risk Assessment Framework 2023 — guidance for airlines on assessing and managing conflict zone overfly risk.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY ICAO — Conflict Zone Information Repository (Doc 10108) 2023

    International Civil Aviation Organization, Doc 10108 — Conflict Zone Information Repository Annual Summary 2023. ICAO Montreal (2023). The authoritative international database of known and potential risks to civil aviation from armed conflict. Maintained by ICAO based on submissions from member states and international organisations. Lists current conflict zones with associated risk levels, NOTAM references, and recommended minimum safe altitudes. Available at icao.int/CZIR. The primary underwriting reference for conflict zone rate loading.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY Cirium — Global Commercial Aircraft Fleet & Lessor Statistics 2023

    Cirium (formerly Ascend by Flightglobal), Fleet Forecast and Lessor Report 2023. Cirium London (2023). The definitive industry database for commercial aircraft fleet data: aircraft type, age, operator, lessor, registration, agreed value, and delivery schedule. As of 2023: approximately 26,000 commercial aircraft in service globally; approximately 50%+ owned by aircraft lessors. Key data source for aviation war agreed value verification and portfolio analytics. Available at cirium.com.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's — Aviation War Market Bulletin (RX) and Russia 2022 Update

    Lloyd's of London aviation market bulletins and performance data for the RX aviation war class. Lloyd's Corporation, London. Includes: Lloyd's aviation war market bulletin providing guidance on conflict zone list maintenance, 7-day cancellability procedures, and war policy wording standards; specific Russia 2022 confiscation market guidance issued March–December 2022; Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) aviation war working group papers. Available via Lloyd's market participants through Lloyd's market systems. Some materials available at lma.com.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY MH17 — Dutch Safety Board (DSB) Crash Investigation Report 2015

    Dutch Safety Board (Onderzoeksraad voor Veiligheid), Crash of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 — Final Report. The Hague (October 2015). Official investigation confirming that Boeing 777-200 (registration 9M-MRD) was destroyed by a Buk surface-to-air missile. Report includes: missile type (9N314M); detonation point (above and left of cockpit); structural failure analysis; flight path reconstruction. The authoritative source for MH17 loss facts used by aviation war insurers in claim assessment. Available at onderzoeksraad.nl.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY JLT Specialty / Marsh — Aviation War Insurance Market Review 2024

    Marsh McLennan (incorporating JLT Specialty), Aviation War & Terrorism Insurance Market Review 2024. Marsh, London (2024). Annual review of the aviation war and terrorism insurance market covering: market capacity and key participants, rate movement by segment (hull war, liability war, confiscation), post-Russia 2022 structural changes, new wording developments (sanctions clauses, GNSS spoofing coverage position), and emerging risks. Key broker intelligence source for aviation war underwriters. Available through Marsh client portal or aviation team.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY AerCap — Annual Report 2022; Russia Aircraft Confiscation Disclosure

    AerCap Holdings N.V., Annual Report 2022. AerCap, Dublin (March 2023). Contains detailed disclosure of Russia aircraft confiscation impact: approximately 113 aircraft and 9 engines stranded in Russia (at time of initial disclosure); agreed values totalling approximately $3.5bn; insurance claims filed; litigation strategy. AerCap subsequently revised exposure figures as partial settlements and aircraft accounting developed. Available at aercap.com. The most detailed public-domain disclosure of a major Russia confiscation claim.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY Air Lease Corporation — Russia Sanctions Aircraft Impact 2022

    Air Lease Corporation, Annual Report 2022 and Q1 2022 Investor Disclosure. ALC, Los Angeles (2023). Discloses ALC's Russia aircraft exposure at time of sanctions: approximately 27 aircraft with net book value approximately $800m on lease to Russian airlines. Describes lessor response: lease terminations, insurance claim filings, litigation strategy. ALC provides quarterly updates on Russia claim developments. Available at airleasecorp.com. Key secondary disclosure source for lessor-side confiscation claim mechanics.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY Allianz — Aviation Insurance Market Review 2023 (War Segment)

    Allianz Global Corporate & Specialty (AGCS), Aviation Insurance Market Review 2023. Allianz, Munich (2023). Includes specific section on aviation war and terrorism insurance market following Russia 2022: capacity changes, rate hardening by segment, confiscation coverage developments, GNSS spoofing emerging risk assessment, and market structure post-loss. Allianz is a major participant in aviation war reinsurance; the review provides a reinsurer's perspective on market developments. Available at agcs.allianz.com.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY IATA — Conflict Zone Risk Assessment Framework 2023

    International Air Transport Association, Conflict Zone Risk Assessment Framework — Guidance for Airlines. IATA Geneva (2023). Provides airlines with a structured methodology for assessing overfly risk: stage 1 (conflict zone identification using CZIR and NOTAMs), stage 2 (threat assessment — MANPADS, SAMs, artillery, air-to-air), stage 3 (minimum safe altitude determination), stage 4 (route decision and management approval). Developed post-MH17 in collaboration with ICAO. The industry-standard framework for airline conflict zone risk management; increasingly referenced by underwriters as a control requirement. Available at iata.org.
