Aviation War Programme — 5-Layer Structure
Aviation war is placed as a lead/follow programme at Lloyd's. One lead underwriter (typically a dedicated Lloyd's aviation war syndicate) sets terms, rate, and conditions. Following underwriters sign participations up to 100%. For very large fleet programmes ($5bn+ agreed value), international reinsurers in London, Bermuda, Paris and Zurich participate alongside Lloyd's. The programme structure below reflects a typical major airline or large lessor placement.
5

XL Aggregate Reinsurance — Top Layer

Stop-loss and aggregate XL reinsurance purchased by Lloyd's syndicates to protect against frequency of attritional losses and single-event catastrophe (Russia-scale confiscation). Retrocession market (Bermuda, London, continental Europe) absorbs the peak layer. Post-Russia 2022, retrocession market capacity for aviation war catastrophe has reduced significantly and repriced substantially. EST

4

Lead Lloyd's Aviation War Syndicate

The lead underwriter at Lloyd's sets the terms, rate, policy wording and conditions for the entire programme. The lead participates for a meaningful share of the programme (typically 10–25%). Lead-follow discipline means following market underwriters are contractually bound by the lead's claims settlement decisions for binding clauses — though disputes arise on large losses. Lloyd's is the acknowledged global leader for aviation war capacity and expertise. VER

3

Following Market — London and Bermuda

Additional Lloyd's syndicates, London company market insurers, and Bermuda specialist aviation war insurers participate to fill the programme. The following market signs at terms set by the lead. For very large programmes ($5bn+ agreed value), global reinsurers (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re) may participate directly as co-insurers or as reinsurers of the Lloyd's layer. VER

2

7-Day Cancellable Individual Aircraft Endorsements

Aircraft operating in or over designated conflict zones are endorsed individually onto the policy with specific conflict zone conditions. The 48-hour (individual aircraft war zone) and 7-day (fleet) cancellability provisions attach at this level. Conflict zone lists are reviewed and updated by underwriters, typically in response to NOTAM changes, CZIR updates, or deteriorating geopolitical intelligence. Airlines and lessors receive the updated conflict zone list and must manage their operations accordingly. EST

1

TRIA — USA Terrorism Component (US Carriers Only)

For US-based airlines and operators, the Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) 2002 / 2019 provides a federal backstop for certified acts of terrorism. TRIA applies to the terrorism component of aviation war cover only — not to state-actor war perils, confiscation, or hi-jacking by non-terrorist actors. Separate from the commercial aviation war programme; coordinated by US domestic insurer with TRIA certification and federal co-insurance. VER

Jurisdiction Profiles
United Kingdom

UK — Lloyd's Dominant Market

Lloyd's of London is the world's leading aviation war market by capacity and expertise. UK regulatory framework: PRA/FCA; Lloyd's Corporation oversight. Aviation Act 1982 and Aviation Security Act 1982 provide the UK statutory framework for hi-jacking and sabotage offences. OFSI (Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation) administers UK sanctions — aircraft lessors must comply with OFSI guidance on Russia-related sanctions. VER VER

United States

USA — TRIA Backstop; Surplus Lines

US aviation war cover is placed predominantly through Lloyd's and London surplus lines for large risks. TRIA (2002/2019) provides the federal terrorism backstop for US-domiciled carriers. FAA oversees airworthiness; TSA oversees aviation security. OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) administers US sanctions — OFAC Russia sanctions (2022) triggered the lessor obligation to repossess aircraft. US courts have jurisdiction over several Russia confiscation claim proceedings. VER

Ireland

Ireland — Lessor Capital of the World

Ireland is the global headquarters of the world's largest aircraft lessors — AerCap (Dublin), SMBC Aviation Capital (Dublin), Air Lease Corporation (formerly with Dublin operations). As of 2022, Irish-registered lessors owned approximately 50%+ of the global commercial fleet by value. Post-Russia 2022, Irish courts have become the primary forum for lessor confiscation claims against Russian airlines. Irish aviation financing law (particularly Cape Town Convention) and IATA registration frameworks are central to lessor legal rights. VER

Russia

Russia — Aircraft Confiscated; No Enforcement

Following the invasion of Ukraine (February 2022), Russia passed legislation permitting Russian-registered aircraft (including those owned by Western lessors) to remain in Russian jurisdiction. Russian aviation authority Rosaviatsia issued re-registration certificates for Western aircraft, creating parallel domestic registration for aircraft still subject to Western type certificates. Russian courts have declined to recognise Western judgments requiring aircraft return. Enforcement against Russian airlines through Russian courts is currently not viable. VER VER

Ukraine

Ukraine — Airspace Closed Feb 2022

Ukraine closed its airspace to all civil aviation on 24 February 2022. Ukrainian commercial carriers (UIA, SkyUp, Windrose) have had their aircraft relocated to safe-haven airports outside Ukraine. Some Ukrainian-registered aircraft have sustained damage from Russian missile and drone attacks on Ukrainian airports. Claims under hull war and business interruption policies are ongoing. The conflict is an active aviation war loss zone; the ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository lists Ukraine airspace as restricted. EST

Middle East

Middle East — Gulf Carriers and Overfly Risk

Gulf carriers (Emirates, Etihad, Qatar Airways) operate networks that route through or adjacent to Yemen, Iran, Iraq, and other conflict-adjacent zones. Overfly risk assessment and NOTAM monitoring is a core operational requirement for Middle East-based carriers. The shoot-down of PS752 (January 2020) near Tehran demonstrated that commercial aircraft near active military operations face real shoot-down risk. GPS spoofing is now regularly recorded over parts of Iraq, Iran, and the eastern Mediterranean. VER

Russia 2022 Litigation — Multi-Jurisdiction Status (May 2026): As of the date of this hub (May 2026), the aviation war confiscation claims arising from Russia 2022 remain the most complex, largest, and most legally uncertain claims in aviation insurance history. Multiple proceedings are active simultaneously across Ireland, Bermuda, the UK (London Commercial Court), the United States, and other jurisdictions. No single judicial determination has resolved the fundamental coverage questions. Underwriters and reinsurers carrying Russia 2022 exposure should: obtain specialist aviation insurance legal advice in each relevant jurisdiction; maintain full IBNR reserves pending resolution; monitor Irish courts for precedent-setting decisions; and consider whether partial settlement offers are economically preferable to prolonged multi-jurisdiction litigation. VER
