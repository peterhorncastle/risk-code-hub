PREVENT Controls
PREVENT

NOTAM Monitoring — Daily Review

Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs) are the primary mechanism by which airspace authorities communicate restrictions, closures, and hazards — including conflict zone risks. A structured daily NOTAM review process by the operations team, focusing on routes with any conflict zone proximity, is the most fundamental aviation war risk mitigation control. NOTAMs are issued in near-real time as conflicts evolve. [I2]

PREVENT

ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository

ICAO Doc 10108 — the Conflict Zone Information Repository — is the authoritative international database of known risks to civil aviation from armed conflict. Airlines must consult the CZIR before route planning through any area with potential conflict exposure. CZIR is updated by member states; underwriters increasingly require evidence of systematic CZIR consultation as a condition of war cover. [I2]

PREVENT

7-Day Cancellability — Proactive Use

Airlines and lessors must understand and actively manage the 7-day (fleet) and 48-hour (individual aircraft in conflict zone) cancellability provisions in their RX policies. Waiting for underwriters to cancel is a reactive posture; proactive operators brief their operations teams on the cancellability mechanism and maintain contingency routing plans that can be activated within the cancellation notice period. [I4]

PREVENT

Aircraft Positioning — Avoid Overnight in Risk States

A key confiscation mitigation is avoiding leaving aircraft on the ground overnight in jurisdictions with elevated confiscation risk. The Russia 2022 confiscation was made possible partly because aircraft were physically in Russia at the moment of sanctions. Lessors now require lessee notification and approval before any aircraft is based, registered, or left overnight in jurisdictions classified as elevated confiscation risk. [I7]

PREVENT

Crew Briefings — Hijack Response Protocols

ICAO Annex 17 (Aviation Security) requires airlines to train crew on hijack response protocols. Standardised procedures — non-confrontation, compliance with hijackers' demands, communication with ground, use of cockpit duress codes — reduce the risk of casualties during a hijacking event. Crew awareness of state-directed forced landing procedures (as in Belarus FR4978) is an emerging training requirement. [L4]

PREVENT

Lessor Monitoring — Know Where Your Assets Are

Aircraft lessors must maintain real-time visibility of the location and operational status of their entire fleet. Post-Russia 2022, leading lessors have implemented enhanced fleet tracking systems that provide daily confirmation of aircraft location, operational status, and jurisdiction. Any aircraft entering a high-risk jurisdiction triggers automatic review; potential early redelivery negotiations or lease suspension procedures can then be initiated. [I7]

DETECT Controls
DETECT

Real-Time Airspace Monitoring

Commercial tools including FlightRadar24, OpsControl, and aviation intelligence platforms provide real-time tracking of all commercial aircraft globally. Airlines and underwriters can monitor whether aircraft are inadvertently operating in restricted airspace, detect deteriorating conditions in conflict zones before NOTAMs are issued, and verify that aircraft have departed war-risk areas as planned. [I1]

DETECT

GNSS/GPS Integrity Monitoring

GPS spoofing detection requires aircraft navigation systems to cross-reference GPS position data with inertial reference systems (IRS/INS) and other navigation aids. Airlines operating in known spoofing environments (Eastern Mediterranean, Baltic, Middle East) should implement enhanced navigation integrity monitoring procedures and crew training on recognising and responding to GPS anomalies. [I2]

DETECT

Geopolitical Intelligence Subscriptions

Airlines and lessors with significant war-zone exposure should subscribe to dedicated geopolitical intelligence services — Verisk Maplecroft, Control Risks, Healix, or similar providers. These services provide early warning of deteriorating political situations, sanctions risk assessments, and conflict zone trajectory analysis that supplements NOTAM and CZIR data with forward-looking intelligence. [I10]

DETECT

NOTAM Management System — Auto-Alert

Manual NOTAM review is error-prone and resource-intensive. Dedicated NOTAM management systems (e.g., Navtech, Jeppesen, Lido/Route) automatically parse and categorise NOTAMs by route relevance and severity, alerting operations teams to new conflict zone restrictions. Auto-alert configuration for designated country codes or geographic bounding boxes ensures no relevant NOTAM is missed. [I1]

Conflict Zone Overfly Pre-Planning Checklist (8 Items)

1
ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository (Doc 10108) consulted for all segments overflying or landing within 300nm of active or recent armed conflict — confirmed in flight planning record [I2]
2
State safety programme — ICAO Universal Safety Oversight Audit Programme (USOAP) compliance of destination and overflown states reviewed; non-compliant states flagged for enhanced review [L4]
3
Bilateral overflight agreement and permissions confirmed with host state; current overflight permit in force; payment of overflight fees current (lapsed fees = permit revocation risk) [L4]
4
Insurance notification: broker and lead underwriter advised of any new or changed routing over designated conflict zones; confirm 7-day/48-hour cancellability exposure understood by operations team [I4]
5
Pilot-in-command (PIC) authority confirmed: pilot has explicit authority to deviate from filed flight plan and avoid conflict zone airspace without prior management approval if safety judgement requires it [L4]
6
MANPADS threat altitude review: minimum safe altitude through conflict zone segments confirmed to be above the effective ceiling of known MANPADS systems in the relevant region (typically 15,000ft minimum) [I1]
7
IATA Conflict Zone Risk Assessment consulted: IATA Safety information is reviewed; airlines check their state's civil aviation authority conflict zone guidance for currency [I10]
8
7-day cancellability mechanism understood: operations management, flight operations, and the CEO are briefed that underwriters may cancel war cover for conflict zone operations on 7 days notice (fleet) or 48 hours (specific aircraft/zones); contingency routing exists [I4]
Lessors — Russia 2022 Confiscation Lessons

Post-Russia 2022: Lessor Risk Management Requirements

The Russia 2022 confiscation event has permanently changed best-practice risk management for aircraft lessors. Key lessons and required actions:

  • Know your lessee: verify nationality, ownership structure, and state control of every lessee before lease execution — state-controlled carriers in politically unstable jurisdictions require enhanced due diligence
  • Monitor sanctions: subscribe to real-time OFAC (US), OFSI (UK), EU sanctions monitoring services; trigger protocol for immediate action if any lessee or jurisdiction is added to sanctions lists
  • Include return-of-aircraft clauses: lease agreements must include robust redelivery provisions, including mandatory deregistration cooperation from the operator's aviation authority and specific jurisdiction of dispute resolution
  • Avoid long leases in high-risk jurisdictions: minimise lease terms to state-controlled carriers in jurisdictions with elevated confiscation risk; consider shorter terms with break provisions
  • Registration jurisdiction: avoid registering aircraft in the lessee's jurisdiction where that jurisdiction has elevated confiscation risk — third-country registration (e.g., Bermuda, Cayman, Isle of Man) provides some protection
  • Insurance notification: immediately notify brokers and war underwriters if any lessee is subject to sanctions action; do not wait for the situation to deteriorate further [I7]
Emerging Risk — GNSS/GPS Spoofing: Systematic GPS jamming and spoofing is now regularly recorded over the Eastern Mediterranean (near Cyprus, Greece), the Baltic Sea (near Russia/Finland/Estonia border), and parts of the Middle East (Iraq, Iran, Israeli border). Spoofing provides false GPS signals that cause aircraft navigation systems to report incorrect positions — potentially inducing autopilot deviations into restricted airspace or causing controlled flight toward terrain. The insurance coverage position is currently debated: if spoofing is state-directed electronic warfare, it may be an aviation war peril under RX; if it causes a navigation accident, it may be an all-risks (H2/H3) claim. Underwriters and brokers should urgently clarify coverage position for clients with regular operations in affected regions. [I2]
Top 7 Actions for Insureds
  • 1
    Implement formal NOTAM review policy: Documented daily NOTAM review process for all routes; sign-off by flight operations or safety officer before any new conflict zone routing is added. Keep records available for underwriter review at renewal.
  • 2
    Know your 7-day cancellability exposure: Brief all relevant operations, legal and finance staff on the 7-day (fleet) and 48-hour (individual aircraft) cancellability provision. Develop contingency routing for each conflict zone overflown; test annually. Do not allow the cancellation period to pass without an operational response plan.
  • 3
    Notify underwriters proactively: Do not wait for underwriters to identify new conflict zone exposures; proactively advise your broker whenever a new conflict zone route is added, a lessee operates in an elevated risk jurisdiction, or a geopolitical event creates new exposure. Early notification preserves coverage; late notification creates disputes.
  • 4
    Subscribe to geopolitical intelligence: Static NOTAM review is not sufficient for sophisticated war risk management. Real-time geopolitical intelligence subscriptions (Verisk Maplecroft, Control Risks) provide early warning of deteriorating situations before they reach NOTAM or CZIR status.
  • 5
    Review agreed values annually: Ensure aircraft agreed values in the RX policy reflect current aircraft market values, particularly post-Russia 2022 where used aircraft values have shifted significantly. Under-agreed value = under-insurance at total loss.
  • 6
    Audit lessor portfolio for confiscation exposure: Aircraft lessors should conduct an annual review of all leases for confiscation exposure: lessee jurisdiction, state control, sanctions screening, aircraft registration, and lease term. Flag any lessee in an at-risk jurisdiction for enhanced monitoring.
  • 7
    Review GNSS spoofing response procedure: Update aircraft operations manual and crew training to include procedure for GPS anomaly detection and response. Ensure crews know how to cross-check GPS data with IRS/INS and other navigation aids, and know to report suspected spoofing to ATC and the operator immediately.
