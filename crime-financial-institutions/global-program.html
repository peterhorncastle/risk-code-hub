Module 08 — Global Programme Structure

Multinational FI crime programme design, US regulatory bonds, coverage towers, admitted requirements and cross-border coordination

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater Programme Builder

A multinational financial institution requires a layered BB crime programme that navigates admitted requirements in regulated jurisdictions, coordinates with D&O and cyber towers, and meets statutory bond obligations across multiple regulatory regimes. Lloyd's leads substantial shares of London-market global programmes, typically on the master policy layer with local admitted policies fronted by network carriers in jurisdictions requiring local licencing. Coordination with Other Insurance Lines

FI crime programmes must be structured to avoid gaps and overlaps with related lines. The boundaries between BB, D&O, Cyber, E&O/PI, and Commercial Crime are complex and negotiated at programme design stage. VER

BB ↔ D&O Boundary

  • D&O covers directors/officers acting in their capacity as such — management decisions
  • BB covers employees (and sometimes directors) for dishonest, fraudulent or criminal acts
  • Key boundary: rogue executive who is also a director — which policy responds first?
  • Practitioners: place both; insist on coordination clause; confirm no Other Insurance provision creates gap
  • Priority of payment clause in D&O should not prejudice BB recovery VER

BB ↔ Cyber Boundary

  • Computer crime cover (BB): first-party loss from unauthorised system access; traditionally covers direct financial loss
  • Cyber policy: broader — includes third-party liability, notification costs, business interruption, ransomware, crisis management
  • Key overlap: BEC loss may trigger both; avoid double-recovery via coordination clause
  • Medidata ruling (2nd Cir. 2018): BB computer crime clause interpreted broadly to include BEC; expanded coverage expectation
  • SWIFT heist scenario: typically BB computer crime primary; cyber excess for BI and third-party claims VER

BB ↔ E&O / PI Boundary

  • E&O covers negligent errors in professional services; no dishonesty element required
  • BB covers intentional dishonest/fraudulent acts with manifest intent
  • Boundary: employee makes negligent trade error (E&O) vs. employee deliberately mis-marks positions (BB)
  • Combined FI program bundles BB + E&O + D&O with single aggregate deductible; simpler claims handling
  • Avoid gap: employee negligence that enabled fraud — may fall between both policies without careful drafting EST

BB ↔ Commercial Crime

  • Commercial Crime policy (non-FI): standard ISO form; typically narrower than BB; suited to corporates
  • FI programme (BB / Bankers Blanket Bond): broader; includes securities, counterfeiting, premises coverage
  • Holding companies: subsidiary FI entities may need BB; parent corporate entity may use Commercial Crime
  • Ensure parent-subsidiary aggregation clause avoids double-counting between policies
  • Some programme architects place single composite policy across corporate and FI with sublimits per entity type EST

Global Programme Placement Workflow

1

Programme Audit & Exposure Mapping

Broker conducts enterprise-wide loss exposure map: headcount by entity, payment volumes, treasury positions, SWIFT transaction volumes, IT estate, regulatory bond obligations. ACFE Fraud Risk Assessment questionnaire completed by CFO/CAO. VER

2

Regulatory Bond Inventory

Legal confirm all mandatory bond requirements by jurisdiction (FINRA, FDIC, IRDAI, SUSEP, MAS, APRA, etc.). Establish minimum limits matrix; identify admitted requirement jurisdictions requiring local fronts. VER

3

Programme Architecture Design

Broker drafts tower structure: SIR/captive layer; primary limits; sublimits for SE/computer crime; excess layers. Agrees on discovery period, retroactive date, aggregate limits and reinstatements. Coordinates with D&O, Cyber and E&O programme design. EST

4

Lloyd's Slip Preparation & Submission

Market Reform Contract (MRC) slip prepared; schedules of insured entities; declarations on controls (SWIFT CSP, dual auth, mandatory holiday, DMARC); loss history 5 years; financial statements. Submitted to Lloyd's lead syndicate. VER

5

Lead Underwriter Negotiation

Lead underwriter (often Beazley, Hiscox, Zurich) reviews, may require additional information (control attestation, CISO interview, penetration test summary). Rating finalised; coverage terms negotiated including social engineering sublimit, computer crime definition, subsidiary schedule. VER

6

Following Market Placement

Lead sets the market; broker syndicates risk to following markets at lead terms or negotiated discount. Large programmes (>$50m TIV crime exposure) may require International Insurance Market participation for capacity. EST

7

Local Policy Issuance & Fronting Arrangement

Network carrier (e.g. AIG, Chubb, Zurich, Allianz, or Lloyd's local entity) issues admitted local policies in each required jurisdiction. Fronting fee typically 2–5% of local premium. 100% ceded to London programme reinsurers via pre-agreed treaty. VER

8

Annual Review & Renewal

Claims experience review; loss development reserving; control attestations renewed (SWIFT KYC-SA); new subsidiary schedule; updated regulatory bond limits. Market briefing at renewal; major losses trigger mid-term review. VER

Aggregate Stop-Loss & Reinstatements

Large FI crime programmes frequently negotiate aggregate protections to cap the insured's total annual loss recovery — important where frequency of smaller losses (BEC, card fraud) could erode limits faster than individual per-occurrence sublimits suggest. EST

⚠️ Annual Aggregate vs Per-Occurrence — Key Distinction Discovery-basis BB policies typically have a single per-occurrence limit and an annual aggregate limit. Where social engineering or computer crime sublimits apply, each BEC incident may erode the sublimit rapidly. Negotiate: (1) separate sublimit aggregate for BEC distinct from main programme aggregate; (2) reinstatement provisions — typically 1 free reinstatement at 100% additional premium for occurrence layer; (3) aggregate stop-loss on excess layers triggered once primary aggregate is exhausted. Case Study — Global Investment Bank Programme Restructure 2023

🏦 Pan-European Investment Bank — £18bn Assets Under Management

Illustrative composite based on typical mid-large IB programme characteristics — ILLUS ILLUS

Background: London-headquartered investment bank with operations in UK, Germany, France, Switzerland, US (SEC-registered broker-dealer), Singapore and Hong Kong. Following a near-miss BEC incident (attempted £3.2m transfer foiled by callback procedure), the CFO initiated a comprehensive crime programme review. Previous programme was legacy structure: single London primary policy, no social engineering cover, no excess layer, inadequate for FINRA minimum bond given expansion of US broker-dealer net capital.

Previous Programme (2022)

Single Lloyd's BB policy, £10m limit, £250k deductible. No SE endorsement. No computer crime sublimit beyond policy limit. No US admitted layer (FINRA non-compliance risk identified by external counsel). Total premium: £185,000 p.a.

New Programme (2023 Renewal)

Restructured 3-layer programme: (1) £2m SIR + captive participation; (2) Primary £25m composite BB (Lloyd's lead: Beazley 30%); (3) £25m xs £25m excess (Hiscox / Zurich / AIG). US admitted layer ($15m, AIG fronting, ceded to London). Total premium: £610,000 p.a. (+230%)

Coverage Enhancements

Social engineering endorsement: £7.5m sublimit (separate annual aggregate). Computer crime: £20m sublimit, SWIFT CSP compliance warranty. Discovery period extended to 36 months. FINRA-compliant US admitted policy replacing non-compliant gap. Lloyd's Brussels fronts EU entities post-Brexit.

Controls Required by Underwriters

Dual authorisation on payments >£50k. Mandatory holiday policy (min. 10 consecutive days). DMARC / DKIM / SPF fully deployed. SWIFT KYC-SA attestation provided. MFA on all finance systems. Quarterly internal audit of payment processes. Annual penetration test (summary shared with lead underwriter).

✅ Programme Outcome The restructured programme delivered: regulatory compliance in all 7 jurisdictions; £50m total crime limit vs. £10m previously; specific BEC and computer crime protection where none existed; captive participation reducing gross premium by ~£80,000. The 230% premium increase reflected both limit increase and the near-miss incident, not market hardening alone. Post-restructure, the bank passed a FINRA examination without deficiency on bond requirements. ILLUS

Network Carrier Fronting — Key Considerations

📋 Fronting Arrangement Mechanics A fronting carrier issues an admitted local policy in a jurisdiction requiring local licencing. The fronting carrier bears no net risk — 100% of the premium and risk is ceded back to the reinsurers (usually the London programme) via a reinsurance agreement. The fronting fee (typically 2–5% of local premium) compensates the fronting carrier for balance sheet usage, regulatory filings, and claims handling. The insured retains all rights under the local policy; in the event of fronting carrier insolvency, London reinsurers are obligated to perform directly (cut-through clause). VER
