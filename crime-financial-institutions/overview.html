Risk Code BB — Scope & Definition

Lloyd's risk code BB covers fidelity and financial institution crime insurance for entities whose primary business is financial services. It is distinct from BC (commercial crime/fidelity for non-financial corporates) and from BD (miscellaneous crime). The BB market originated in the American Bankers Blanket Bond (BBB) — a composite policy covering the principal crime and fidelity exposures of banks — which was adapted into the Lloyd's market from the 1960s onwards.

Bankers Blanket Bond — Historical Foundation

The Bankers Blanket Bond (BBB) was first standardised in the United States in the 1920s and remains the conceptual foundation of all modern FI crime policies. The term "blanket" means all employees are covered without individual scheduling — an important distinction from named-employee fidelity bonds. Key features that have persisted into modern Lloyd's BB wordings: employee dishonesty (fidelity), on-premises loss, in-transit loss, forgery and alteration, securities loss, and computer crime (added progressively from the 1980s). VER

Coverage Taxonomy — BB Insuring Agreements

Modern Lloyd's BB policies are structured around discrete insuring agreements, each covering a defined category of crime or dishonesty. Underwriters may write all agreements together or offer modular coverage.

🏦 Employee Dishonesty / Fidelity

  • Loss caused directly by dishonest acts of employees
  • Requires manifest intent to cause loss and personal financial benefit
  • Covers theft, embezzlement, fraudulent trading, collusion
  • Discovery basis: loss discovered during policy period regardless of when committed
  • Key trigger: Barings/Leeson, SocGen/Kerviel, Adoboli/UBS VER

📋 Forgery & Alteration

  • Fraudulent signing of financial instruments in the insured's name
  • Covers cheques, drafts, promissory notes, bonds
  • Alteration of genuine instruments to increase face value
  • Third-party forgery (external cheque fraud, identity theft)
  • Excludes counterfeiting (currency notes are separate cover)

🏛️ On-Premises / In-Transit

  • Physical loss of money, securities, property on premises
  • Robbery, burglary, safe-cracking, mysterious disappearance
  • In-transit: loss while money conveyed by armoured car or employee
  • Covers vault damage from break-in (not fire unless added)
  • Less significant post-ATM era but still relevant for cash-intensive FIs

💻 Computer Crime / Electronic Funds Transfer

  • Unauthorised entry into computer systems to transfer funds
  • Fraudulent electronic instructions to debit accounts
  • Covers SWIFT messaging fraud, ACH fraud, wire fraud
  • Distinct from cyber liability (cyber BI, privacy, data costs not included)
  • Bangladesh Bank heist ($81m via SWIFT, 2016) VER

📧 Social Engineering / BEC

  • Business Email Compromise — fraudulent payment instructions
  • Impersonation of CEO/CFO/vendor directing wire transfers
  • Deepfake audio/video voice fraud (emerging 2023–24)
  • Requires voluntary transfer by employee (vs. unauthorised computer crime)
  • Sub-limited in most policies: typically $250k–$5m sublimit EST

📜 Securities & Counterfeiting

  • Loss through receipt of counterfeit securities or currency
  • Bearer bond fraud, fraudulent certificates of deposit
  • Loss arising from trading forged or stolen securities
  • Diminished with dematerialisation of securities
  • Residual exposure: physical settlement markets, private placements

Key Policy Definitions

Employee Dishonesty — Trigger Elements

Three elements required: (1) the act must be by an employee (as defined); (2) there must be manifest intent to cause loss; (3) the employee must intend to obtain financial benefit for themselves or a third party. Negligence alone does not trigger fidelity cover.

Discovery Basis

BB policies are written on a discovery basis — the loss is covered if it is discovered during the policy period, regardless of when the dishonest act occurred. Extended discovery periods (typically 12–24 months post-cancellation) are standard. VER

Loss Sustained vs. Discovery

Discovery basis (standard for BB) covers all losses discovered during the policy period. Loss sustained basis (used in some US forms) only covers losses both occurring and discovered within the period. Discovery basis offers broader protection for long-running embezzlement schemes.

Manifest Intent

The employee must manifestly intend to cause loss — reckless or negligent acts that cause financial loss to the employer do not trigger the fidelity insuring agreement. Proof standard varies: some wordings require criminal conviction; others require the insurer to establish dishonest intent on a balance of probabilities.

Computer Crime vs. Social Engineering

A key coverage boundary. Computer crime requires an unauthorised entry into the computer system. Social engineering/BEC involves an authorised employee being deceived into making a payment — a voluntary act that falls outside computer crime and requires a specific social engineering insuring agreement.

Financial Institution — Scope

BB cover is restricted to entities whose primary business is financial services: banks, savings institutions, credit unions, investment managers, hedge funds, broker-dealers, central counterparties, exchanges, insurance companies. Non-financial corporates with treasury functions require BC (commercial crime) cover instead.

Insured Entity Types — Risk Profile by Segment

Retail & Commercial Banks

  • Primary exposure: employee embezzlement, branch cashier theft, cheque fraud
  • Electronic exposure: ACH/wire fraud, SWIFT messaging attacks
  • FDIC-insured US banks: mandatory fidelity bond requirement
  • Large banks: programme limits $100m–$500m+ EST

Investment Managers & Hedge Funds

  • Primary exposure: rogue trading, fraudulent valuation, Ponzi schemes
  • SEC: Investment Company Act s.17(g) — registered funds must maintain fidelity bond equal to 10% of funds' value (min $2,500) VER
  • Hedge funds: unregulated but institutional investors require proof of cover
  • Key risk: redemption fraud, NAV manipulation

Broker-Dealers & Exchanges

  • FINRA Rule 4360: broker-dealers must maintain fidelity bond covering at least the minimum net capital requirement VER
  • Key risks: securities theft, rogue trading, clearing house fraud
  • Central counterparties (CCPs): systemic risk; specialist wordings required
  • Crypto exchanges: emerging BB sub-class; custody fraud, rug pulls

Payment Processors & Fintechs

  • Fastest-growing BB segment 2020–2024 EST
  • Primary exposure: BEC/social engineering, API fraud, synthetic identity fraud
  • Regulatory: PSD2/PSR 2023 (UK) authorised push payment (APP) fraud reimbursement obligations create fidelity-adjacent exposure
  • Crypto custodians: hot wallet theft, employee private key fraud

Insurance Companies

  • FI crime exposure: premium embezzlement, agent fraud, claims fraud collusion
  • Lloyd's syndicates themselves are insured FI entities under BB
  • Solvency II / PRA: operational risk capital charge includes crime loss scenarios
  • Reinsurance fraud: cedant manipulation of loss data

Central Banks & Supranationals

  • Sovereign FI crime cover: limited market; specialist Lloyd's placement
  • Bangladesh Bank ($81m SWIFT heist 2016) — archetypal central bank FI crime event VER
  • Development banks: IFC, EBRD require FI crime cover via Lloyd's or multilateral captives
  • Typically low attritional frequency but very high severity per event
