Why Event Cancellation Has Extreme Tail Risk: The bear scenario (−25% ROE) illustrates what the EC market learned from COVID: this class is exposed to correlated catastrophe events that can simultaneously affect the entire portfolio. Even with pandemic exclusions, a major terrorism wave, extreme weather season, or geopolitical crisis could produce correlated losses across many policy years simultaneously. The EC book must be sized accordingly — it is not a high-volume, low-severity class. One catastrophe year can eliminate multiple years of profitable underwriting. EST

Claims Development and IBNR Considerations
FactorImpact on Reserves
Short-tail nature (mostly)Most EC claims are notified quickly — a cancelled event produces an immediate claim notification. However, quantum dispute and extra expenses assessment can extend claims to 12–18 months.
COVID tail claims (2020–21 books)Coverage disputes from 2020–21 policies without pandemic exclusions continue to develop. Some disputes reach litigation — IBNR for contested COVID EC claims must be maintained in older books.
Large PN claims — long tailNon-appearance claims involving contested medical causation (was illness pre-existing? Was the individual truly incapacitated?) can extend to 2–4 years litigation.
Terrorism claims — criminal proceedingsWhere terrorism is the insured peril, criminal investigations may delay final claims resolution pending attribution of the event.
Prize indemnity — no IBNR neededPrize indemnity claims are instantaneous — prize won = trigger met. No development period. Clean, short-tail claims profile.
Climate attribution — pluvius reservesAs extreme weather becomes more frequent, pluvius frequency may increase beyond actuarial expectations. Prudent reserve strengthening for outdoor festival books in an era of increasing weather extremes.
