Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

PC — Event Cancellation

Covers financial loss suffered by the event organiser or promoter if a scheduled event is cancelled, postponed, abandoned, interrupted or curtailed due to an insured peril. Covers net revenue loss and unrecoverable costs. Perils typically include: adverse weather, terrorism/security threat, government order, death or injury of key performer, venue failure, and industrial action. [1]

PA — Contingency (General)

Bespoke contingency risks that do not fit neatly into the PC framework. Covers a wide range of one-off or recurring financial contingency exposures — film production abandonment, tournament cancellation, product launch events, award ceremonies. Written on an individual risk basis with tailored insuring agreements. [2]

PN — Non-Appearance

Covers the financial loss suffered by a promoter or employer if a named individual fails to appear at or complete a scheduled engagement due to death, bodily injury, sickness or other insured peril. Common for concert tours, sporting events, and corporate speaking engagements. Key extension: visa denial for international events. [3]

PU — Prize Indemnity

Covers the promoter of a competition or promotional event if a specified prize is won. The promoter pays a premium to transfer the risk of the prize being claimed: hole-in-one golf competitions, half-court basketball shots, lottery-style promotions, "guess the weight" competitions. Premium is a function of the probability of the prize being won. [4]

PZ — Contingency (Other)

Catch-all code for niche contingency risks. Includes pluvius / weather insurance (cover triggers if rainfall exceeds a measured threshold at a specified weather station during the event period), Coverage A/B weather schemes for outdoor venues, and other bespoke contingency structures not captured under PC/PA/PN/PU. [5]

Coverage Taxonomy

Cancellation / Postponement

The core insuring agreement: covers net revenue lost and unrecoverable advance costs (venue, artist fees, production, marketing) if the event is cancelled or postponed. Usually requires the cause to be a named insured peril — most policies are NOT all-risks. [1]

Non-Appearance of Named Individual

Covers the financial consequences if a named headliner, athlete, speaker or key individual fails to appear. Can cover the full event or only the portion of revenue attributable to that individual. Health clauses, age limits and pre-existing condition exclusions are standard. [3]

Extra Expenses (Rescheduling)

Covers the additional costs incurred to postpone and reschedule an event rather than cancel it outright — re-booking the venue, reprinting materials, artist re-engagement fees, additional marketing. Often subject to a sublimit. Incentivises organisers to mitigate by rescheduling. [1]

Prize Indemnity / Hole-in-One

Covers a promoter against the prize fund being claimed. Prize indemnity rates are actuarially calculated from the probability of the event occurring (golf handicap, distance from hole, competition format). Claims are simple: prize won = policy triggers. [4]

Weather / Pluvius Insurance

Automatic trigger if rainfall (or other weather metric) exceeds a defined threshold at a specified official weather station during the event hours. No proof of financial loss required once the weather trigger is met — parametric structure. Widely used for outdoor events, concerts and festivals. [5]

Broadcasting & Media Rights

Covers the forfeiture of TV and streaming rights income if the event does not take place or is significantly curtailed. Often a separate layer or endorsement given the very high values at stake — major sporting events can have broadcasting rights packages worth multiples of gate revenue. [2]

COVID-19 — The Defining Event for this Class: In March 2020, the global events industry shut down simultaneously and completely for the first time in history. Governments around the world imposed legal prohibitions on public gatherings. Thousands of events — concerts, festivals, sporting events, conferences, weddings — were cancelled with no possibility of rescheduling. Many event cancellation policies written in 2019 contained no pandemic or communicable disease exclusion — the market had not considered this scenario. The result: an estimated $4–6bn of insured event cancellation losses — the largest single event affecting this class in history. By 2021, every EC policy in the market included a pandemic/communicable disease exclusion as standard. The market restructuring was complete and fundamental. [I2]
Market Context (UK/Lloyd's): Event cancellation is written in Lloyd's predominantly by specialist contingency syndicates. The Lloyd's EC market is estimated at £400–600m GWP, making it a meaningful but specialised class within the broader Lloyd's portfolio. Post-COVID, the market has hardened significantly with pandemic exclusions now universal. The Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation obligation applies in full — underwriters must be provided with all material facts including venue contracts, artist health disclosures, and prior event claim history.
