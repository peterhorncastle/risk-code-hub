Global Programme Structure

Touring artists, mega-events, IOC/FIFA insurance model, jurisdiction profiles & case study
Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater
Why Global EC Programmes Are Different: A touring artist, global sporting body or international event organiser faces EC exposure in multiple jurisdictions simultaneously. The insurance challenge is (a) ensuring coverage applies across all territories where events take place; (b) complying with local admitted insurance requirements where they exist; (c) coordinating non-appearance, terrorism and EC covers written across different markets; and (d) managing the follow-the-leader/follow-the-settlements dynamics across a syndicated programme. Lloyd's is uniquely positioned for this market due to its global licences and acceptance of complex, bespoke risks. VER
Typical Global EC Programme — 4-Layer Structure
Layer 4 — Top

Excess of Loss Reinsurance

Catastrophe XL reinsurance purchased by the EC insurer(s) to protect against large single-event or aggregated losses. For a £100m+ global touring programme, XL RI may be placed on a per-risk or per-occurrence basis. Reinsurance terms significantly influence the primary insurer's pricing and capacity availability.

Layer 3

Lloyd's Global EC Master Policy

The Lloyd's-led EC master policy provides the primary global event cancellation, non-appearance (PN), terrorism (where purchased), and weather coverage across all event territories. Written on a "worldwide including USA/Canada" basis. The lead Lloyd's syndicate acts as claims leader. VER

Layer 2

Local Admitted Policies (Where Required)

Certain jurisdictions require locally admitted insurance for events taking place there — notably some US states, Brazil, and certain Asian markets. A locally admitted policy (often a fronting arrangement where the Lloyd's market is the reinsurer) satisfies local regulatory requirements. VER

Layer 1 — Base

Broadcaster / Media Rights Separate Layer

For events with significant broadcast rights values, a separate broadcast rights cancellation policy is typically placed as a standalone cover — often with specialist media insurers alongside Lloyd's. The broadcast rights layer has different coverage triggers (full event non-broadcast vs partial broadcast) and requires separate claims adjustment expertise. VER

Key Jurisdiction Profiles
United Kingdom

UK — Lloyd's Home Market; Pool Re for Terrorism

The UK is the deepest and most sophisticated EC market globally. Lloyd's provides EC, PN, pluvius and all contingency classes. Pool Re covers UK property terrorism losses — but event cancellation terrorism is NOT covered by Pool Re; it requires a Lloyd's terrorism extension. The Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation obligation applies to all commercial EC placements. VER

United States

USA — TRIA Does Not Apply to EC; Lloyd's Surplus Lines

The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) applies to commercial property and liability lines in the US — it does not apply to event cancellation insurance. US EC terrorism coverage must be arranged through Lloyd's surplus lines market. Admitted US EC capacity is limited; Lloyd's surplus lines placement is the dominant channel for complex US events. VER

France / EU

France — GAREAT for Adjacent Property; EC Separate

GAREAT (French terrorism reinsurance pool) covers property losses adjacent to terror events but does NOT cover event cancellation losses directly. French EC terrorism must be placed commercially. EU Solvency II rules require locally admitted insurance for some large events. The Paris Olympics 2024 involved a complex multi-layer public/private insurance programme. VER

Australia

Australia — Summer Outdoor Season; Weather-Heavy

Australia's summer outdoor event season (November–March) coincides with extreme weather risk — tropical cyclones in the north, severe thunderstorms and heat waves in the south. Australian outdoor events attract significant pluvius and weather EC premium loadings. The Cricket Australia insurance structure is one of the most studied EC programmes in the Asia-Pacific market. EST

Middle East

Middle East — Indoor Events Predominate; Political Stability Risk

The Middle East has developed a significant event market (Formula 1, world boxing, international concerts) predominantly in climate-controlled indoor venues — reducing weather EC risk substantially. Political stability risk (government order cancellation arising from regional political events) is the primary additional peril. Qatar World Cup 2022 and Saudi Arabia GP are notable examples. EST

Global Touring

Multi-Territory Touring — Follow-the-Leader Structure

Multi-territory touring programmes (artists, F1, tennis Grand Slams) typically use a follow-the-leader structure with one lead insurer (usually Lloyd's) and co-insurers following. Claims leadership by the primary Lloyd's syndicate; co-insurers follow settlements. The global tour PN cover for the artist is the most critical and complex element — visa denial perils require careful wording for multi-territory tours. VER

⚠ IOC and FIFA Host-City Indemnity Warning: The government indemnity provided by Olympic and World Cup host cities is a sovereign guarantee, not commercial insurance. The Tokyo 2020 postponement demonstrated that these indemnities were not designed to cover pandemic scenarios — the Japanese government bore approximately ¥200bn+ ($1.5bn+) in additional costs beyond any insurance recovery. For future mega-events, host-city governments and event bodies should review whether government indemnity language adequately covers force majeure events that fall outside commercial insurance (pandemic, extended political crisis) and ensure appropriate national reserve provisions or contingency budgets are in place.
