Underwriting Methodology

Rating factors, rate-on-revenue tables, worked examples & key exclusions

CRITICAL — Pandemic / Communicable Disease Exclusion: Since 2021, ALL Lloyd's event cancellation policies include a pandemic and communicable disease exclusion as standard non-negotiable wording. This MUST be disclosed clearly to every insured at placement and renewal. The COVID-19 loss experience proved that pandemic risk is a systemic, non-diversifiable catastrophe peril that the EC market cannot carry. There is no available market for pandemic EC cover as of 2026. Brokers must ensure insureds understand that a recurrence of COVID-style restrictions would produce a completely uninsured loss. EST
Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Revenue at Risk

The primary rating base for PC event cancellation is gross revenue at risk — total projected event income including ticket sales, sponsorship, concessions, merchandise and broadcast rights. The sum insured is typically the net revenue loss exposure (gross revenue less savings if event does not proceed). Insureds must disclose all revenue streams accurately. VER

Base rate applies to: Total Revenue Insured (TRI)
High Impact

Event Type — Outdoor vs Indoor

The single most impactful structural factor. Outdoor events carry weather risk that can increase base rates by 50–200% over comparable indoor events. Multi-day outdoor festivals face cumulative weather exposure — the probability that at least one day exceeds weather thresholds increases significantly with event duration. VER

Loading: Outdoor festival vs indoor conference: 2.0–3.0× base rate
High Impact

Event Date and Location

Weather seasonality and geography are critically important: UK summer festival season carries different weather risk to Middle Eastern indoor winter events. Hurricane zones (Florida, Gulf Coast) attract surcharges for Q3 events. Political stability of the event location affects terrorism and political cancellation pricing. VER

Hurricane zone loading: +25–50% for Q3 Florida outdoor events
High Impact

Cancellation Perils Selected

All-risks EC policies (covering any unforeseeable circumstance causing cancellation) are priced materially higher than named-perils policies (weather, terrorism, government order only). All-risks wordings carry adverse selection and moral hazard risk. Most Lloyd's EC policies post-2020 are named-perils with a defined list of covered causes of loss. VER

All-risks vs named perils: +30–60% premium loading for all-risks
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Terrorism Extension

Separately rated since 9/11 as a named-peril extension rather than a base inclusion. Premium loading reflects event profile (high-profile events in major cities carry higher terrorism risk than regional events), jurisdiction (Pool Re covers UK property terrorism — EC terrorism is separate), and capacity availability. VER

Terrorism extension: +0.05–0.15% of TRI depending on event profile
Medium Impact

Non-Appearance of Named Individual

PN cover is priced on the health, age and medical history of the named individual. Underwriters require a medical questionnaire (and sometimes a medical examination) for high-value covers. Pre-existing conditions, age over 50, extreme sports participation, and known health issues all attract significant loadings or sub-limits. VER

Age 60+ loading: +50–150%; pre-existing condition: potential exclusion
Medium Impact

Broadcast / Media Rights Exposure

Where broadcasting rights are included in the sum insured, the multiplier effect can be very significant — major sporting events often have broadcast rights packages worth 3–10× gate revenue. Broadcast rights carry their own claim adjustment complexity (partial rescheduling, edited highlight packages) and attract careful underwriting. VER

Broadcast rights endorsement: additional 0.1–0.3% of broadcast rights value
Medium Impact

Waiting Period / Franchise

Many EC policies include a waiting period (excess time before cover attaches) — e.g. rain must exceed threshold for 2+ hours before pluvius triggers, or event must be cancelled rather than merely delayed. The length of the waiting period/franchise significantly affects loss frequency and premium. VER

24-hour delay excess: −10 to −20% vs immediate trigger
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Rescheduling Ability

Events that can feasibly be rescheduled (corporate conferences, recurring club events) carry lower net loss exposure than truly unique, once-only events (World Cup final, Wimbledon fortnight) where postponement generates significant additional rather than recovered revenue.

Lower Impact

Contractual Sponsor Obligations

Where cancellation triggers sponsor refund or remediation obligations under sponsorship contracts, this increases the total financial exposure beyond gate revenue. Sponsor remediation costs are material for marquee events and must be declared at placement.

Lower Impact

Advance Payment Recovery

The proportion of artist/performer fees paid in advance that would be irrecoverable on cancellation affects net loss exposure. Where contracts allow for fee recovery on cancellation, this reduces the insurance exposure. Underwriters review artist and venue contracts at placement.

Lower Impact

Prior Claims Experience

Promoters and organisers with a history of EC claims — whether due to weather, artist cancellations or other perils — carry higher expected loss frequency. Adverse claims experience at the same venue or event series is a significant negative underwriting indicator.

Premium Rating Formula
// Event Cancellation PC — indicative rating formula
Base Premium = Total Revenue Insured (£) × Base Rate (% of TRI)

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Outdoor/Indoor Factor // 1.0× (indoor) → 3.0× (multi-day outdoor festival)
  × Season/Location Factor // 1.0× (low-risk) → 1.5× (hurricane zone summer)
  × Named vs All-Risks // Named = 1.0×; All-Risks = 1.30–1.60×
  + Terrorism Extension // +0.05–0.15% of TRI if selected
  + Non-Appearance (PN) // +0.10–0.50% of PN sum insured depending on health/age
  + Broadcast Rights Ext. // +0.10–0.30% of broadcast rights value
  × Waiting Period Credit // −10–20% for 24hr weather delay franchise
  × Claims Loading // −10% (5yr clean) → +100% (frequent adverse)

⚠ Pandemic Exclusion — Broker Disclosure Obligation: The pandemic / communicable disease exclusion is now a material term of every EC policy. Brokers have an obligation under the Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation rules to draw this exclusion to the client's attention clearly and explicitly. A client who suffers a COVID-style cancellation loss and was not made aware of the exclusion may have grounds for a complaint. The exclusion should be highlighted in the placement information, the policy schedule, and the renewal renewal advice letter. VER
