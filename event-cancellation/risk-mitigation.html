Prevention Controls (PREVENT)
Prevent

Indoor / Outdoor Contingency Planning

Every outdoor event should have a credible, documented bad-weather contingency plan — an indoor backup venue, a postponement window, or a defined curtailment protocol. The existence of a viable contingency plan may reduce EC premium and demonstrates professional event management to underwriters. [I9]

Underwriting credit: −5 to −10% on weather peril premium for documented indoor backup
Prevent

Terrorism Threat Monitoring — Police Liaison

Large events should establish a pre-event security liaison with local police and, for major events, with counter-terrorism units. Police-assessed security plans, search protocols and crowd management procedures are underwriting pre-conditions for terrorism extension cover above certain attendance thresholds. [L3]

Required for: terrorism extension, events 5,000+ attendees
Prevent

Key Person Health Monitoring

For non-appearance (PN) covers, underwriters require pre-tour medical examinations for named individuals. Organisers should arrange medical clearance before finalising tour schedules and ensure artist contracts include health disclosure obligations. Any health change during the lead-up period must be notified to the insurer immediately. [3]

Required: medical questionnaire for PN sums insured above £500k; exam above £5m
Prevent

Broadcast Contingency Planning

Events with broadcast rights exposure should maintain documented backup production plans — redundant camera systems, remote transmission capability, backup commentary positions. Demonstrating broadcast continuity planning may reduce the loading on broadcast rights extensions. [2]

Reduces broadcast rights extension loading for well-managed productions
Prevent

Supplier and Vendor Financial Health Checks

Venue insolvency and supplier failure are insurable perils in EC (under venue failure or force majeure clauses in some policies). Pre-event due diligence on venue financial stability, caterers, production companies and infrastructure suppliers reduces the risk of mid-event failure. [I9]

Reduces venue-failure claim risk; may be required for high-value venue deposits
Prevent

Force Majeure Clause Review

All artist contracts, venue agreements and sponsor contracts should be reviewed by legal counsel for force majeure and cancellation provisions. Robust force majeure clauses that align with the insured perils in the EC policy reduce disputes about whether cancelled obligations generate recoverable costs. [L2]

Reduces quantum dispute risk; improves recovery of advance costs
Detection & Early Warning Controls (DETECT)
Detect

Weather Monitoring Service — On-Site Meteorologist

For major outdoor events, on-site meteorologists provide real-time weather monitoring and 72-hour forecasting. Early identification of approaching weather events enables orderly contingency activation — postponement decisions made with adequate notice are less costly than last-minute cancellations. Pluvius triggers (official weather station rainfall measurement) should be confirmed pre-event. [5]

Detect

Real-Time Crowd Management

Automated crowd density monitoring, entry flow management and real-time attendance data reduce the risk of crowd-related curtailment events. Monitoring systems can trigger early intervention before dangerous situations develop — relevant to EC terrorism and venue safety clauses. [L9]

Detect

Communicable Disease Surveillance (Post-Pandemic)

While pandemic EC cover is no longer available, event organisers should maintain awareness of public health developments. A new communicable disease outbreak — though not insured — requires advance contingency planning. Early detection allows maximum lead time for postponement decisions that minimise unrecoverable costs. [I2]

Detect

Early Notification to Insurer

Most EC policies contain a 72-hour pre-event notification obligation: if circumstances exist that may lead to cancellation, the insurer must be notified immediately. Late notification can prejudice coverage. Event organisers should brief their risk manager and broker on the notification procedure and ensure it is triggered promptly whenever a potential cancellation circumstance arises. [L1]

Event Organiser Pre-Event Checklist

8-Point EC Policy Activation and Loss Mitigation Checklist

  • All event contracts (venue, artists, production, catering) have been reviewed by legal counsel for cancellation, force majeure and refund provisions — and the policy perils align with contract terms.
  • Audience ticket refund obligations have been quantified: how much would a full cancellation cost in ticket refunds? This is a key component of the sum insured. [L2]
  • Sponsorship activation triggers have been reviewed: does cancellation trigger sponsor refund obligations or loss of future sponsorship? These costs should be declared to underwriters.
  • The insurance certificate has been provided to the venue and reviewed against venue access requirements — many venues now require evidence of event cancellation cover before granting access.
  • The notification procedure is documented and distributed to the key event team: who to call (broker, insurer), within what timeframe (typically 72 hours of any incident), and what information to gather immediately.
  • The key contact tree is confirmed: broker emergency contact, insurer claims handler, loss adjuster pre-appointment for major events, legal counsel. Do not wait for an incident to identify these contacts.
  • A media and PR crisis plan is in place for managed communication if the event is cancelled — minimising audience confusion, refund delay disputes and social media escalation.
  • Post-cancellation revenue recovery plan has been considered: rescheduled date options, partial refund vs credit mechanisms, sponsor communication protocol. EC cover provides financial indemnity but prompt recovery planning also reduces the ultimate loss. [I9]
Post-COVID Pandemic Exclusion — What Is and Is NOT Covered: Since 2021, the following is the universal market position:

EXCLUDED: Any loss arising from (i) any communicable disease, pandemic, epidemic or public health emergency of international concern; (ii) any government or public authority prohibition, restriction or advice arising from a communicable disease; (iii) fear of attendance at an event due to communicable disease.

STILL COVERED (subject to other policy terms): Weather / pluvius triggers; Terrorism (where terrorism extension is purchased); Government order for non-communicable disease reasons (e.g., security threat, natural disaster); Non-appearance of named individual (illness not related to communicable disease); Venue failure / insolvency; Political cancellation (where included).

What should event organisers do? (1) Accept the pandemic exclusion as a fact of the current market — no alternative is available. (2) Build pandemic contingency reserves from own resources. (3) Review whether government schemes exist (the UK DCMS Culture Recovery Fund operated 2020–21 but has ceased). (4) Explore contractual force majeure protections in artist and venue agreements. [I10]
Weather / Pluvius Insurance Explained:

Pluvius (PZ code) is a parametric weather insurance that triggers automatically when a defined weather metric exceeds a threshold. The key components are:

1. Trigger metric: Usually millimetres of rainfall in a defined period (e.g., more than 5mm in any 2-hour period during event hours). Can also be wind speed, temperature, lightning probability, or composite weather indices.
2. Measurement station: An official meteorological station agreed at placement (usually within 5–15km of the event venue). The measurement from that station is the objective, indisputable trigger — removing subjectivity from claims.
3. Indemnity: On trigger, the policy pays the agreed sum insured or a proportion thereof — no requirement to prove actual financial loss. This parametric structure makes pluvius claims simple, fast and certain.
4. Basis of cover: Pluvius can be structured as (a) per-day coverage — pays if trigger exceeded on any individual day; (b) aggregate — pays if trigger exceeded over the total event period; or (c) tiered — multiple payouts at different rainfall thresholds.

Key underwriting consideration: Historical rainfall data for the specific location and event dates is the primary rating input. The closer the measurement station to the venue, the more accurate the trigger. Organisers should agree the measurement station at placement. [5]

Top 7 Actions for Event Organisers:
1. Understand the pandemic exclusion — your EC policy does NOT cover COVID-type cancellations. Confirm this with your broker in writing.
2. Agree the pluvius measurement weather station at placement — do not leave this to claims time.
3. Obtain pre-tour medical clearance for all named individuals covered under PN — disclose all known health conditions.
4. Establish police liaison for all events over 5,000 attendees where terrorism extension is purchased.
5. Document your weather contingency plan — an indoor backup venue is the most effective premium reduction available for outdoor events.
6. Review all artist and venue contracts for force majeure and cancellation provisions before signing — misalignment with EC policy perils creates gaps.
7. Know your 72-hour notification procedure — brief your event team before the event, not after a cancellation incident.
