Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Insurance Act 2015 (UK) — Fair Presentation of Risk

    Insurance Act 2015 (c. 4). Replaces the Marine Insurance Act 1906 duty of disclosure for non-consumer commercial insurance. Requires fair presentation: every material circumstance the insured knows or ought to know, in a manner reasonably clear and accessible. Applies in full to all event cancellation and contingency placements in the UK market. Particularly relevant to: (i) non-appearance covers — full health disclosure of named individuals; (ii) prior loss history; (iii) known pre-existing circumstances that may lead to cancellation. Proportionate remedies for breach including avoidance, reduction of claim, or no remedy depending on the nature of the breach.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL Consumer Rights Act 2015 — Ticket Refund Obligations

    Consumer Rights Act 2015 (c. 15). Event organisers have statutory obligations to refund ticket holders in the event of a cancelled event where the consumer did not receive what they contracted for. Refund obligations are a material component of the event organiser's financial loss on cancellation and must be included in the sum insured for EC policies. The CRA 2015 framework applies to consumer ticket contracts; commercial ticket arrangements are governed by the underlying contract terms. Organisers should quantify and disclose total refund exposure at EC placement.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL Terrorism Act 2000 — Statutory Definition of Terrorism

    Terrorism Act 2000 (c. 11). Section 1 defines terrorism as the use or threat of action designed to influence a government or to intimidate the public, made for the purpose of advancing a political, religious, racial or ideological cause, where the action involves serious violence, serious damage to property, endangerment of life, or serious risk to public health or safety. EC terrorism extensions typically adopt the TA 2000 definition of terrorism as the trigger for the terrorism peril. Post-9/11, terrorism is a separately rated, optional extension in EC policies — it is not included in base wordings as a default peril. Pool Re covers UK property terrorism losses but does NOT cover event cancellation.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL Health Protection (Coronavirus, Restrictions) Regulations 2020

    The Health Protection (Coronavirus, Restrictions) (England) Regulations 2020 (SI 2020/350) and equivalent regulations in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. These regulations prohibited public gatherings and events from March 2020. They constituted "government orders" within the meaning of many EC policy government-order perils — and were a key trigger for COVID-19 EC claims under policies that included government order as an insured peril without a communicable disease exclusion. The regulations and subsequent iterations demonstrate how government orders arising from public health emergencies can have systemic, portfolio-wide EC insurance consequences.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL Paris Agreement on Climate Change 2015

    Paris Agreement, adopted 12 December 2015, entry into force 4 November 2016. UNFCCC treaty committing signatory states to limiting global average temperature increase to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels. Relevant to EC pluvius and weather insurance as the scientific attribution of extreme weather events to climate change strengthens. Increasingly, flood events, heat waves and extreme rainfall that affect outdoor events are attributable to anthropogenic climate change — raising questions about long-term weather peril frequency trends and the adequacy of historical rainfall data as a pluvius pricing basis.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018

    Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 (SI 2018/634), implementing EU Directive 2015/2302. Applies where event tickets are sold as part of a package with travel and accommodation. Organisers of combined event-and-travel packages have statutory refund obligations on cancellation that extend beyond standalone ticket refunds. These obligations increase the quantum of EC insurance exposure for event packages and must be disclosed and included in the sum insured.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 — Ticketing Data

    UK General Data Protection Regulation (retained EU law) and Data Protection Act 2018 (c. 12). Event organisers collect and process significant volumes of personal data through ticketing platforms. On event cancellation, personal data must be handled in accordance with GDPR principles — data retention, refund processing and communication with ticket holders must comply. While not directly an EC insurance trigger, GDPR compliance obligations are an operational cost of cancellation management relevant to event operators' risk management framework.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL Equality Act 2010 — Accessibility Requirements for Event Venues

    Equality Act 2010 (c. 15). Event organisers have a duty to make reasonable adjustments to enable disabled persons to attend events. Failure to make reasonable adjustments can result in discrimination claims. Where venue failure or modification is required under EA 2010 obligations, additional costs arise that may interface with EC extra expenses provisions. Venue accessibility is also a consideration in venue selection and venue failure EC cover.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL Event Industry Forum — The Purple Guide (HSE Working at Events)

    Event Industry Forum and Health and Safety Executive, The Purple Guide to Health, Safety and Welfare at Music and Other Events (current edition 2024). The definitive health and safety guidance for UK event organisers. Sets out requirements for crowd management, emergency planning, security, fire safety, structures and welfare at events. EC underwriters increasingly require evidence of Purple Guide compliance for large outdoor events. Failure to comply with HSE guidance may prejudice EC claims where curtailment arises from health and safety breaches. Available at thepurpleguide.co.uk.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY Allianz Global — Event Cancellation Market Report Post-COVID 2022

    Allianz Global Corporate & Specialty (AGCS), Event Cancellation: Lessons from COVID-19 (2022). Analysis of the COVID-19 EC loss experience including claims frequency by event type, policy wording adequacy, the role of government-order perils, and market structural changes post-2020. Includes data on EC claims development for 2020–21 policy years. Available at agcs.allianz.com. Key reference for the $4–6bn COVID EC loss estimate.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY Swiss Re — COVID-19 Event Cancellation Claims Analysis 2021

    Swiss Re Institute, COVID-19 and the Insurance Industry: Event Cancellation Claims (2021). Quantitative analysis of the COVID-19 EC loss event — the largest in the class's history. Estimated insured EC losses of $4–6bn across 2020–21. Discusses the systemic nature of the loss, the policy wording gaps that allowed pandemic claims to succeed (particularly government-order perils without communicable disease exclusions), and the structural market response. Available at swissre.com.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's — Event Cancellation Market Bulletin 2021 (Post-COVID Rewrite)

    Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) and Lloyd's of London, Event Cancellation Class: Post-COVID Market Guidance (2021). Bulletin confirming the universal adoption of pandemic and communicable disease exclusions in all Lloyd's EC policies. Guidance on the structure of post-COVID EC policy wordings, communicable disease exclusion drafting, and terrorism extension terms. LMA Bulletin Y5267. Available through Lloyd's market participants. The definitive market reference for the 2021 structural rewrite.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY Wimbledon (AELTC) — COVID Insurance Claim £141m (2020)

    All England Lawn Tennis Club (AELTC) confirmed receipt of a £141m pandemic event cancellation insurance claim following the cancellation of Wimbledon 2020 due to COVID-19. Reported by The Guardian, BBC and Financial Times (June 2020). The claim was made on pandemic-specific EC cover purchased annually since 2003 — unique in the events industry — at an estimated cumulative premium of approximately £27m over 17 years. One of the largest single EC insurance claims ever paid. Represents the definitive case study in the value of proactive contingency risk management.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY Event Industry Forum — UK Events Industry Economic Impact Report 2023

    Event Industry Forum (EIF) and UK Events — Britain for Events: Economic Impact Report 2023. Estimates the UK events sector contributes approximately £85bn to the UK economy annually (combined direct and indirect impact). Covers music events, sporting events, conferences, exhibitions, corporate events, and public events. Provides context for the scale of EC insurance exposure in the UK domestic market. Available at eventscouncil.org.uk.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY PwC / Visit Britain — UK Events Sector Value £85bn 2023

    PricewaterhouseCoopers / VisitBritain, UK Events Sector: Economic and Tourism Value 2023. Corroborating analysis of the UK events sector economic contribution. Documents the recovery of the UK event industry post-COVID, ticket revenue trends, international visitor spending on events, and the sector's contribution to UK GDP and employment. Source for £85bn UK events sector value estimate used in this hub.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY AON — Prize Indemnity and Contingency Market Overview 2023

    AON plc, Prize Indemnity and Contingency Insurance: Market Overview and Pricing Trends (2023). Covers the prize indemnity (PU) market — hole-in-one insurance, promotional prize indemnity, lottery-style competitions, and sports promotion covers. Provides market size estimates ($50–80m GWP globally) and pricing methodology. Also covers pluvius / weather insurance for outdoor events and the growing use of parametric weather triggers. Available through AON broker network.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY IBIA — Sports Event Cancellation Impacts on Wagering 2023

    International Betting Integrity Association (IBIA), Sports Event Cancellation and the Wagering Market: Impact Assessment 2023. Analyses the impact of event cancellations — both COVID-era and subsequent — on global sports wagering markets. Relevant context for the EC market: the broader sports economy (including wagering) provides a proxy for the scale of sports event cancellation economic exposure. Documents how major event cancellations create downstream economic losses well beyond the promoter's direct revenue. Available at ibia.bet.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY Marsh — Entertainment and Events Risk Management Guide 2024

    Marsh McLennan, Entertainment and Events: Risk Management and Insurance Guide 2024. Comprehensive broker guidance covering the full range of entertainment and events insurance — EC, PN, terrorism, pluvius, venue liability, production insurance, and cyber. Includes case studies from live events, film production, and sporting events. Provides practical underwriting and claims guidance. Available through Marsh entertainment division. The definitive broker reference for entertainment and event risk management.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY DCMS — Culture Recovery Fund: Lessons for Event Cancellation Risk Post-COVID

    UK Department for Culture, Media & Sport (DCMS), Culture Recovery Fund: Final Evaluation Report (2023). The CRF provided £1.57bn to UK cultural organisations and events businesses affected by COVID-19 closures (2020–21). The Fund operated as a government substitute for the pandemic EC insurance that the market could not provide. Its cessation in 2022 leaves a funding gap for future pandemic-type events. DCMS evaluation documents lessons for sector resilience and the limitations of government emergency support as an insurance substitute. Available at gov.uk.
