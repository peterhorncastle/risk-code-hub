2000–08CEND

Zimbabwe Land Seizures — Mugabe Fast-Track Land Reform

President Mugabe's government seized ~4,000 white-owned commercial farms without compensation under the Fast-Track Land Reform Programme. VER Foreign agricultural investors and their lenders (principally UK and South African) suffered CEND losses. Agricultural output collapsed 60%. Marked the beginning of Zimbabwe's economic hyperinflation crisis. PRI claims among the largest agricultural CEND losses in Lloyd's history.

2002–13CEND

Venezuela — Chavez Nationalisation Programme

President Chavez nationalised PDVSA (oil), gold mines, steel mills, agricultural estates, telecoms (CANTV) and banks. VER Estimated $30bn+ investor losses. ConocoPhillips and ExxonMobil filed ICSID claims. ConocoPhillips awarded $8.7bn in 2019. ExxonMobil awarded ~$1.4bn. Lloyd's and AIG among insurers with significant exposure; one of the largest sets of PRI claim payments in history.

2006CEND

Bolivia — Gas Industry Nationalisation (1 May 2006)

President Morales deployed military to 56 gas fields on May Day 2006, nationalising the gas industry. VER Repsol, Total, Petrobras, British Gas and Pan American Energy all affected. ICSID proceedings initiated; bilateral settlements reached. Preceded by resource nationalism wave across Andean countries. Key event for Latin American PRI pricing.

2008–09CI/TR

Global Financial Crisis — Capital Controls & CI/TR Claims

Iceland capital controls (2008–2017) prevented foreign creditors from repatriating krona assets. VER Ukraine and several Eastern European states imposed informal currency restrictions. Argentina's parallel exchange rate divergence created de facto transfer restrictions. CI/TR covers widely triggered; tested policy waiting period and active government step provisions.

2009–12CEND

Ecuador — Occidental (OXY) ICSID Award $2.3bn

OXY's Ecuadorian oil licence was terminated by the government in 2006; ICSID Tribunal ruled in 2012 and awarded $2.3bn — at that time the largest ICSID award ever made. VER Ecuador challenged enforcement; eventually paid. Demonstrated enforceability of ICSID awards as a key element of PRI design: arbitral award default (AAD) covers gained traction following this case.

2011PV

Arab Spring — Libya, Egypt, Syria, Yemen

Political violence across MENA triggered widespread PRI claims. Libya: NATO intervention and Gaddafi's fall caused complete asset destruction and looting of foreign investments. VER Egypt: Mubarak's fall; tourism and retail assets damaged in Tahrir Square protests. Syria: civil war beginning 2011 caused total loss of foreign direct investment. Yemen: Houthi conflict from 2014 rendered most commercial operations impossible.

2012CEND

Argentina — YPF Nationalisation (Repsol, $5bn Settlement)

President Fernandez announced the nationalisation of YPF (majority stake held by Spain's Repsol) in April 2012. VER Spain filed formal diplomatic protest; EU imposed trade sanctions. Argentina agreed a $5bn compensation settlement in 2014. The event strained Argentina-EU investment relations and renewed European concern about Latin American resource nationalism. PRI claims paid on Repsol JV partner losses.

2014REG

Crimea Annexation — Russia's First Territorial Seizure in Europe

Russia annexed Crimea in March 2014; overnight, Ukrainian territory became Russian-administered. VER Foreign businesses with Crimean operations — particularly Ukrainian and Western European banks, utilities and telecoms — lost assets in legal limbo (no state recognised Russian jurisdiction). ~$3bn investor losses; PRI claims raised novel coverage questions about territorial seizure vs. traditional expropriation. The event triggered immediate market repricing of Russia/Ukraine/FSU risk.

2015MKT

Lloyd's Political Risk Market — Post-Venezuela Recapitalisation

Following Venezuela ICSID awards and Latin American losses, Lloyd's political risk syndicates materially repriced exposure across emerging markets. EST New entrants (specialist underwriting agencies: BPL Global, CHUBB PRI, Markel International) expanded capacity. The political risk market grew to ~$3bn GWP globally, with Lloyd's writing ~25–30% of total. Longer policy tenors (5–10 years) standardised for project finance. Shorter-term (1–3 year) investment risk capacity expanded.

2016PV

Turkey Coup Attempt — Political Violence Losses in NATO State

The attempted coup against President Erdoğan (July 2016) caused physical damage to government buildings, airports and infrastructure. VER Tourism collapsed; foreign investment paused. Demonstrated that political violence risk is not limited to developing markets — a NATO member state experienced a coup attempt causing substantial insured losses. Subsequently, Erdoğan's post-coup purges and government asset seizures created CEND exposure for foreign investors.

2019CEND

ConocoPhillips — $8.7bn ICSID Award Against Venezuela

ICSID Tribunal issued its final award of $8.7bn against Venezuela for the expropriation of ConocoPhillips' oil assets in 2007. VER Largest ICSID award at that time. Venezuela disputed and initially refused to pay; enforcement proceedings commenced across multiple jurisdictions. Demonstrated the long litigation tail of PRI (12 years from expropriation to award); long-tail reserving implications for insurers who paid claims at time of loss.

2020PV

Mali Coup (August 2020) — First Sahel Coup Wave

Military coup overthrew President Keïta in Mali; beginning of a wave of six coups across the Sahel over the following three years (Mali again 2021, Guinea 2021, Burkina Faso twice 2022, Niger 2023, Gabon 2023). VER French military expelled; Russian Wagner Group (later Africa Corps) moved in; mining licences revoked or renegotiated. West African gold and base metal miners — including Barrick, AngloGold, Endeavour Mining — faced severe PRI exposure. Sahel elevated to highest-risk region in Lloyd's PRI portfolio.

2021PV

Myanmar Coup — Tatmadaw Seizes Power (1 February 2021)

Myanmar military (Tatmadaw) overthrew Aung San Suu Kyi's government; martial law; Western sanctions imposed; civil war followed. VER TotalEnergies announced exit from the Yadana gas pipeline after coup; exited 2022 without compensation. Japan's JXTG, Petronas and other JV partners in Myanmar energy sector faced forced abandonment or CEND exposure. Combined investor losses estimated at several billion dollars.

2022CEND

Russia / Ukraine War — Largest PRI Event in History

Russia's invasion of Ukraine (24 February 2022) triggered the largest political risk insurance event in history. VER In Russia: Western multinationals faced forced exit (BP — $24bn write-down; Shell — $3.9bn; Renault — sold for 1 rouble; McDonald's — sold Russian estate). In Ukraine: war damage to physical assets. Western sanctions on Russia meant insurers could not deal with Russian entities to settle claims. Total estimated PRI exposure: $40bn+. Market outcome still unresolved as of 2024.

2022REG

Energy Charter Treaty Crisis — EU Member Withdrawals

Netherlands, France, Germany, Spain, Poland and other EU members announced withdrawal from the Energy Charter Treaty (ECT) following adverse arbitration rulings requiring compensation for fossil fuel phase-out policies. VER The ECT had been a key PRI framework for energy investors; its hollowing-out creates a significant treaty protection gap for European energy transition investments and investment into EU from non-EU states.

2022–23CI/TR

Argentina Parallel Rate / Sri Lanka Capital Controls

Argentina's "cepo" capital controls and parallel exchange rate (official rate vs. blue chip rate diverged by 100%+) triggered CI/TR claims for multinationals unable to repatriate peso profits. VER Sri Lanka's 2022 economic collapse imposed strict capital controls; foreign businesses unable to remit dividends or repay loans. Nigeria's CBN FX restrictions similarly frustrated profit remittances for UK and EU companies. Three simultaneous CI/TR stress events globally.

2023PV

Sudan Civil War — RSF vs. SAF, Complete Market Withdrawal

War between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) began April 2023; Khartoum became a war zone. VER All remaining foreign businesses evacuated; physical assets destroyed or looted. PRI political violence claims filed by telecoms, banking and energy sector investors. Sudan became completely uninsurable; market withdrew all capacity. One of the fastest-moving political violence loss events in recent history.

2023CEND

Niger Coup — French Uranium Mining Threatened

Military coup deposed President Bazoum (July 2023); junta demanded French military withdrawal; ECOWAS threatened military intervention. VER Niger supplies ~15% of France's uranium (via Orano/AREVA). Junta suspended uranium and gold exports; threatened nationalisation of Orano's Somaïr mine. PRI underwriters placed Niger on immediate referral list; all new PRI capacity for Niger suspended. Uranium price spiked. Orano eventually agreed to exit the country in 2024.

2023–24MKT

Market Hardening — Post-Ukraine Rate Reset

Following Russia/Ukraine, the global PRI market hardened sharply. EST Rates for sub-Saharan Africa and MENA increased 30–80%. Russia and Belarus excluded from all new placements. Policy tenors shortened from 5–10 years to 3–5 years maximum in high-risk markets. War exclusion wording tightened; many syndicates suspended new Ukraine risk. Total market GWP grew to ~$3.5bn by 2024 as demand surged from energy transition investors and de-risking supply chains.

2024REG

Gaza / Red Sea Crisis — PRI & Trade Credit Interface

Hamas attack (October 2023) and subsequent Israeli military operations in Gaza triggered broader Middle East instability. Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping (from November 2023) — nominally political violence — created a significant interface between marine war risk, trade credit and PRI covers. VER Western businesses with Israeli or Palestinian operations faced PV coverage questions; supply chain disruption through the Red Sea created indirect PRI losses for businesses dependent on Suez Canal routing.
