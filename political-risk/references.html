All content in the Political Risk hub has been compiled from publicly available primary legal instruments, international body publications, ICSID/UNCTAD databases, industry body reports, and academic literature. Where data is derived from proprietary market sources, estimates, or illustrative modelling, the relevant confidence badge (VER / EST / ILLUS / GAP) is applied throughout the modules.

Confidence Badge Framework

  • VER Verified — Sourced from a named primary or authoritative secondary source; fact-checked against original material
  • EST Estimated — Reasonable market estimate based on comparable data or disclosed ranges; specific to this hub's context
  • ILLUS Illustrative — Hypothetical example constructed to demonstrate a concept or calculation; not a real transaction
  • GAP Data Gap — Material data exists but is not publicly available; disclosure limitation noted

International Conventions & Treaties

#SourceRelevance to HubURL / Location
T1 ICSID Convention (Convention on the Settlement of Investment Disputes between States and Nationals of Other States), 1965 Foundation of investment arbitration; Article 54 enforcement mechanism; 158 contracting states as of 2024. Referenced throughout underwriting, risk-mitigation, global-program modules. icsid.worldbank.org
T2 MIGA Convention (Convention Establishing the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency), 1985; entered force 1988 Establishes MIGA's mandate to provide PRI; Article 18 subrogation rights; preferred creditor status. Referenced in global-program, risk-mitigation modules. miga.org/convention
T3 Energy Charter Treaty (ECT), 1994 (entered force 1998) Multilateral investment protection for energy sector; Part III investor protections; Article 26 ISDS; withdrawal crisis 2022–24. Referenced in history, timeline, risk-mitigation modules. energycharter.org
T4 UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules (2013 revision) Primary alternative to ICSID for investor-state arbitration; New York Convention enforcement. Referenced in underwriting, risk-mitigation, global-program modules. uncitral.un.org
T5 New York Convention on Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards, 1958 Enforcement of UNCITRAL awards in 170+ contracting states; distinction from ICSID Convention Article 54. Referenced in risk-mitigation module. uncitral.un.org
T6 OECD Convention on Combating Bribery of Foreign Public Officials in International Business Transactions, 1997 Anti-corruption obligation; FCPA/UK Bribery Act parallel; corruption exclusion in PRI policies. Referenced in risk-mitigation module. oecd.org
T7 Voluntary Principles on Security and Human Rights (VPSHR), 2000 Security risk management framework for extractive industries; breach can void PV coverage; referenced in risk-mitigation module. voluntaryprinciples.org

Key Case Law & Arbitral Awards

#Case / AwardSignificanceOutcome
C1 ConocoPhillips v Venezuela — ICSID Case No. ARB/07/30 Venezuelan oil industry nationalisation (2007); largest ICSID case by quantum at time of award. Landmark on valuation methodology (market value vs. book value) for expropriated oil assets. $8.7bn awarded to ConocoPhillips (2019); enforcement proceedings ongoing. VER
C2 Occidental Petroleum v Ecuador — ICSID Case No. ARB/06/11 Ecuador terminated OXY's oil licence (2006); ICSID awarded $1.77bn — one of the largest awards against Ecuador. Disputed and reduced on appeal to $1.06bn (2015). Final: ~$1.06bn paid by Ecuador. Landmark on proportionality in expropriation. VER
C3 Repsol v Argentina — ICSID Case No. ARB/12/38 (YPF nationalisation) Argentina's 2012 expropriation of Repsol's YPF stake; settled outside ICSID award for $5bn (2014). $5bn settlement (2014); established negotiated settlement as alternative to full arbitration. VER
C4 Yukos v Russia — PCA Cases AA226, AA227, AA228 Largest international arbitral award in history ($50bn, 2014) against Russia for expropriation of Yukos oil company. Overturned by Dutch courts (2021); restored by appeals (2021); enforcement complex post-2022. $50bn award (2014); enforcement status contested post-2022 sanctions environment. VER
C5 Vattenfall v Germany I & II — ICSID (ECT cases) Two ECT cases by Vattenfall against Germany: I (Hamburg environmental restrictions, settled 2011); II (nuclear phase-out compensation, €4.7bn, withdrawn 2021 after settlement). Both settled; precedent for regulatory expropriation via energy transition policy — driving ECT reform crisis. VER

Known Data Gaps

⚠ Material Data Gaps — Flagged for Future Enhancement

  • GAP 1 Lloyd's PR Class Loss Statistics: Lloyd's does not publish aggregate loss ratios, combined ratios, or gross written premium for the PR class separately. All market-level financial data in this hub is estimated from Berne Union aggregate (public + private market combined) and broker survey sources. A dedicated Lloyd's PR class performance disclosure would significantly improve the rates-analysis module.
  • GAP 2 Individual Insurer Exposure to Russia/Ukraine: While industry estimates for total insured exposure to Russia/Ukraine put the figure at $40bn+, individual insurer gross and net exposures are not publicly disclosed. The quantum of claims paid, reserved, or disputed by Lloyd's syndicates versus AIG, Zurich, AXA XL is commercially confidential. This hub uses Berne Union and broker estimates as proxies.
  • GAP 3 ECA Claims Data by Territory: National ECAs (UKEF, US Ex-Im, Euler Hermes) publish aggregate annual claims data but not detailed country-by-country or event-level claim records. This limits the granularity achievable in the claims database module for ECA-covered losses.
  • GAP 4 Sub-Sovereign CEND Pricing: Rating approaches for CEND by sub-sovereign entities (state-owned enterprises, municipal governments) versus central government counterparties are not publicly documented. The underwriting module treats these together with a government counterparty loading; individual syndicate approaches vary.
  • GAP 5 PRI Recovery / Subrogation Data: Aggregate recovery rates from subrogation proceedings (ICSID awards, BIT recoveries, settlement) are not publicly reported by commercial insurers. The hub notes the structural mechanisms but cannot quantify actual recovery rates for the Lloyd's PR book.
