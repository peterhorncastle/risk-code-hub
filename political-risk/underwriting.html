Rating Formula — Political Risk Insurance

Unlike property insurance, there is no standardised actuarial rating model for political risk. The market operates on a qualitative-plus-quantitative basis — country risk intelligence, proprietary scoring models and underwriter judgement all feed into a rate expressed as a percentage of the insured exposure (investment value, loan amount or revenue stream). The formula below represents the key variable structure used across most Lloyd's PRI syndicates.

Annual Premium = Insured Exposure × Base Country Rate
× Cover Type Factor (CEND / PV / CI / CF / FA)
× Tenor Factor (policy duration loading)
× Sector / Asset Class Factor
× Treaty Protection Credit (BIT / ICSID)
× Mitigation Credit (local partner / government relationship)
÷ Waiting Period Adjustment (longer waiting period = lower premium)

Country Risk Assessment — The Foundation

Country risk is the starting point for every PRI underwriting decision. Underwriters use a combination of external intelligence providers and proprietary scoring to assign each country a base rate bracket. The key analytical dimensions are political stability, rule of law, government effectiveness, regulatory quality, control of corruption, and historical expropriation track record.

🌍 Country Risk Scoring Sources

Oxford Analytica; Control Risks; Verisk Maplecroft; Eurasia Group; MSCI (formerly PRS Group); World Bank WBGI (Worldwide Governance Indicators); OECD Country Risk Classification; Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index; Freedom House Political Rights & Civil Liberties indices. Most underwriters subscribe to 2–3 services and blend with proprietary judgement.

🗺️ Base Rate Brackets by Risk Tier (illustrative)

Tier 1 — Low risk (OECD stable democracies): 0.10–0.30% p.a. of exposure ILLUS
Tier 2 — Moderate risk (Chile, Malaysia, Morocco, Jordan): 0.30–0.80%
Tier 3 — Elevated risk (Ghana, Kenya, Peru, Philippines): 0.80–1.80%
Tier 4 — High risk (Nigeria, Pakistan, Bangladesh): 1.80–3.50%
Tier 5 — Very high risk (DRC, Sudan, Venezuela, Afghanistan): 3.50–6.00%+ or declination

⚙️ Cover Type Multipliers (illustrative)

CEND only: 1.0× base (benchmark cover)
Political Violence only: 0.8–1.5× base (depends on conflict level)
CI/TR only: 0.6–1.2× base (FX regime and capital controls history)
Contract Frustration: 1.0–1.5× base (state entity counterparty risk)
Combined CEND + PV: 1.2–1.6× base
Full political risk (all covers): 1.5–2.2× base ILLUS

📅 Policy Tenor Loading

1 year: 1.0× (annual base)
2 years: 1.8× cumulative (not simply doubled — risk concentrates)
3 years: 2.5× cumulative
5 years: 3.8× cumulative
7 years: 5.0× cumulative
10 years: 6.5–8.0× cumulative ILLUS
Post-Ukraine 2022: maximum tenor shortened to 3–5 years in Tier 4/5 markets.

⚖️ Treaty & Mitigation Credits

BIT in force (host-investor country pair): −10 to −20% — treaty provides legal enforcement backstop
ICSID arbitration clause in investment agreement: −5 to −15%
Strong local partner (government-connected): −5 to −15%
Strategic asset (government needs it): −10% — harder to expropriate
ECA co-insurer (MIGA / UKEF): −10 to −20% — sovereign-to-sovereign deterrence ILLUS

⏳ Waiting Period & Retention

Standard waiting period (CEND creeping): 12–24 months — insured must demonstrate ongoing loss for this period before claim is recognised
Longer waiting period credit: 24m vs 12m waiting period = −5 to −15% premium credit
Deductible / self-insured retention: Typically 10–20% of loss — insured retains first slice
Outright CEND (decree nationalisation): No waiting period — immediate trigger on government act

The "Government as Counterparty" Risk — Contract Frustration Underwriting

Contract Frustration (CF) covers losses where a government or government-owned entity repudiates, cancels or frustrates a contract. The key underwriting factors are:

  • Counterparty type: Sovereign (ministry) = higher risk than state-owned enterprise = higher risk than private entity with government guarantee
  • Contract type: Concession agreements (30+ year terms) carry more frustration risk than short-term supply contracts
  • Dispute resolution: ICSID arbitration clause in contract = significant credit; local court only = high risk; ICC arbitration with neutral seat = moderate credit
  • Payment history: Has the government counterparty ever defaulted on a similar obligation? Default track record is the single most predictive CF underwriting factor
  • Economic stress: IMF programme status; foreign exchange reserves; debt sustainability — a government in financial crisis is far more likely to frustrate commercial contracts

Worked Example A — Mining CEND Risk (West Africa)

Risk Profile: Gold mine JV in Ghana — Australian miner 70%, Ghanaian state 30%

Insured Exposure (investment value)$120,000,000
CoverCEND (including creeping expropriation); Political Violence
Policy tenor3 years (renewable)
Waiting period (creeping CEND)12 months
Self-insured retention15% of any claim
Country (Ghana) — Risk tierTier 2–3; WGI Rule of Law 45th percentile VER
Base annual rate (Tier 2–3)0.70% ILLUS
Cover type factor (CEND + PV combined)1.35×
Sector factor (gold mining)1.40×
Tenor factor (3 years)2.50× cumulative
BIT credit (Australia–Ghana BIT in force)−15%
State JV partner credit (30% state ownership)−10%
Net rate calculation0.70% × 1.35 × 1.40 × 2.50 × 0.85 × 0.90 = 2.54% (3-yr cumulative) ILLUS
Annualised rate~0.85% p.a.
3-year Premium (indicative)$120m × 2.54% = ~$3,048,000 total / ~$1,016,000 p.a. ILLUS
