Lloyd's Risk Code — PR

Political Risk (PR) is a single Lloyd's risk code covering a broad class of insurance protecting businesses, investors and lenders against losses caused by political or governmental action in foreign countries. Unlike commercial property, the peril is not physical (fire, flood, wind) but sovereign — arising from the deliberate or coercive acts of governments, political movements, or civil instability. The class sits at the intersection of financial lines and property insurance: some covers indemnify physical damage to assets; others compensate for financial losses (blocked funds, frustrated contracts, investment expropriation).

Lloyd's has written political risk since the 1970s and remains the world's leading specialist market for this class, alongside AIG, Zurich and multilateral institutions such as MIGA (World Bank). The market hardened sharply following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 — the largest political risk event in modern insurance history.

CEND — The Core Political Risk Cover

CEND is the foundational political risk cover. It protects against the full spectrum of state interference with private property, from outright nationalisation (as seen in Venezuela's oil sector in 2007) to creeping expropriation — a series of regulatory or administrative measures that, individually, might not constitute taking but cumulatively destroy the value of an investment. Modern CEND policies also cover deprivation of use: situations where the government does not formally take the asset but prevents the insured from using it.

Creeping Expropriation — The Underwriter's Challenge

Classic expropriation is easy to identify: the government issues a nationalisation decree. Creeping expropriation is far harder to underwrite and even harder to claim on. It typically involves a sequence of: (1) excessive taxation or royalty increases; (2) forced local content requirements; (3) regulatory harassment or licence delays; (4) forced renegotiation of contracts; (5) refusal to allow repatriation of profits. Each step may be defensible in isolation, but together they constitute effective expropriation. Policies typically require the insured to wait 12–24 months and attempt to remedy the situation before a CEND creeping expropriation claim is recognised. The waiting period is a key underwriting variable.

Key Market Participants

Lloyd's Market

World's leading specialist PRI market. Syndicates: Beazley, Markel, Brit, Atrium, Chaucer, Canopius, RaetsMarine. Specialist political risk underwriters with deep country expertise. The Lloyd's PR market writes both investment risk and trade finance risk.

Commercial Insurers

AIG, Zurich, Chubb, Allianz, AXA XL. AIG Political Risk is the largest single writer globally. These insurers write larger lines and can support global programme structures with admitted paper across key risk territories.

MIGA (World Bank Group)

Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency — World Bank's political risk arm. Provides CEND, CI/TR, war & civil disturbance and breach of contract cover for investments in developing countries. AAA-rated; preferred by ECAs and development finance.

Export Credit Agencies (ECAs)

Governmental/quasi-governmental bodies supporting national exporters: UKEF (UK), EXIM Bank (US), Euler Hermes (Germany), Coface (France), Atradius DSB (Netherlands). Write political risk as part of export credit; essential for large infrastructure projects.

DFC (US Development Finance Corporation)

Successor to OPIC (Overseas Private Investment Corporation). US government body providing PRI and loans to US investors in developing countries. Can write $1bn+ per project; key for US-nexus investments in Africa, Asia and LatAm.

Brokers

Marsh Political Risk, Aon Political Risk & Credit, Willis (WTW) Political Risk are the three dominant brokers. Country risk intelligence from Oxford Analytica, Control Risks, Verisk Maplecroft. Deep specialist expertise required — not a commodity placement.
