Rate Index — Political Risk Market 2005–2024

The PRI market is structurally different from property or casualty lines: premiums are multi-year, claims are infrequent but very large, and the market is heavily influenced by geopolitical events rather than actuarial cycles. Loss Ratio Trend — PRI Market

The PRI loss ratio is inherently lumpy: long periods of low attritional losses punctuated by large events (Venezuela, Zimbabwe, Crimea, Ukraine). The market's long-tail nature means claims declared in year 3 of a 5-year policy may not be finally settled for 10–15 years. IBNR (incurred but not reported) and IBNER (incurred but not enough reported) reserves are substantial in this class. EST

Market Cycle Narrative

2005–2008 · Growth Phase — Commodities Boom

Commodity supercycle drove foreign direct investment into Africa, Latin America, Central Asia. PRI demand grew strongly; rates relatively soft as new capacity entered. Venezuela losses beginning to emerge but not yet at peak.

2009–2013 · Venezuela/LatAm Claims Wave

Chavez nationalisation programme producing claims; ICSID proceedings multiplied. Bolivia, Ecuador losses accumulating. CI/TR claims from GFC-related capital controls. Loss ratios elevated; capacity cautious but not retreating.

2013–2016 · Market Expansion

Arab Spring losses partly absorbed. ILS/alternative capital never entered PRI (too opaque). New Lloyd's syndicates and MGAs (BPL Global, Chubb PRI) expanded capacity. Sub-Saharan Africa rates competitive. Average global rate stable to slightly softer.

2017–2020 · Mixed — Crimea Legacy & New Events

Crimea claims still in dispute/negotiation. Sub-Saharan Africa (Congo, South Sudan, Nigeria, Mozambique) producing PV claims. CI/TR in Argentina, Turkey elevated. Market selective: tightened in Sub-Saharan Africa; competitive in Asia-Pacific and stable economies.

2021–2024 · Ukraine — Full Market Hardening

Russia/Ukraine war triggered the largest PRI event in history. Russia/Belarus entirely excluded. Ukraine capacity severely limited. MENA and Africa hardened 30–80%. Sahel coups made West Africa highest-risk region globally. Tenor shortening. Market GWP grew to ~$3.5bn as demand surged despite harder rates.
