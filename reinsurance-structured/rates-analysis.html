Module 05 — Economics & ROE

Structured / Finite — Economics & ROE

Finite economics turn on two things: the interest rate that discounts the reserves, and the reserve run-off itself. Higher yields make LPTs work; adverse development is the risk that undoes them.

The economic engine of a loss portfolio transfer is discounting: the higher the yield and the longer the tail, the more the present value falls below the nominal reserves — and the more room there is for a premium that benefits both sides. The Finite Economics in Words

High yields

LPTs work

When interest rates are high, discounting a long-tail book creates a large investment margin — the reinsurer can charge below nominal reserves and still profit, and the cedant gets P&L and capital relief.

Risk transfer

The real cover

The risk margin and aggregate limit are where genuine insurance risk sits — the reserve running off worse than expected. This is what makes it reinsurance, not a loan.

Adverse development

The downside

If reserves deteriorate badly, the LPT acquirer or the ADC writer pays — the whole point of the risk transfer, and the reason pricing the reserve distribution matters.

Reinsurer Return — What Drives It

Legacy return is investment income on the transferred reserves plus the risk and profit margins, less any adverse development. ILLUS

Expected

Adverse
