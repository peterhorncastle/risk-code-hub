Module 02 — History

Structured / Finite — History

Finite reinsurance rose, was abused, was reformed, and re-emerged as the disciplined legacy market of today.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

The story of finite reinsurance is a cycle of innovation, abuse and reform. What began as a legitimate way to finance and smooth liabilities was misused to manipulate earnings, drew a regulatory crackdown, and was rebuilt on a strict risk-transfer standard as the modern run-off market. VER

Era 1 — Financial reinsurance emerges (1970s–1980s)

Reinsurers began offering covers that used the time value of money and multi-year experience accounts to finance and smooth cedants' results — "financial" or finite reinsurance. Legitimate uses (funding long-tail liabilities, spreading volatility) grew alongside more aggressive ones. EST

Era 2 — Abuse & the earnings-management problem (1990s–2004)

Some finite deals transferred little real risk and were used to flatter reserves, capital or earnings — accounting for a loan as reinsurance. Regulators and auditors grew increasingly uneasy about where financing ended and reinsurance began. EST

Era 3 — The AIG / General Re scandal & crackdown (2005–2008)

A 2000–01 finite deal between AIG and General Re, used to bolster AIG's reserves without meaningful risk transfer, triggered SEC and DOJ action, executive prosecutions, and a market-wide tightening of the risk-transfer test. Finite reinsurance's reputation was badly damaged. VER

Era 4 — The modern legacy / run-off market (2010s–present)

Rebuilt on a strict risk-transfer standard, structured reinsurance re-emerged as the legacy market — specialist acquirers (Enstar, RiverStone, Compre, Premia, Marco and others) using LPTs and ADCs to take over discontinued books and old reserves. Solvency II Part VII transfers and capital pressure fuelled demand. VER

Key Ideas That Endure

Risk transfer is the line

The distinction between reinsurance and a loan — the lesson of the scandal — governs the whole class.

Time value of money

Discounting long-tail reserves is the economic engine of an LPT.

Finality

The modern market's real product is a clean, capital-releasing exit from legacy liabilities.
