Module 05 — Underwriting & Pricing

Structured / Finite — Underwriting & Pricing

Finite pricing is reserving plus the time value of money: the reinsurer values the liability, discounts it, adds margins for uncertainty and profit — and proves the deal transfers real risk.

Where cat reinsurance prices a future event, finite reinsurance prices an existing liability. The two building blocks are the loss portfolio transfer, valued off discounted reserves, and the adverse development cover, priced off the distribution of the ultimate reserve outcome. Both must pass the risk-transfer test. VER

The Two Building Blocks

LPT premium = PV(reserves) + risk margin + profit margin, capped by an aggregate limit.

where PV(reserves) = nominal reserves ÷ (1 + discount rate)mean term — the reinsurer keeps the investment income.

ADC expected loss = E[(U − a)⁺] − E[(U − b)⁺], where U is the ultimate reserve outcome, a the carried level and b the top of the cover.

ADC premium = expected loss × (1 + risk load) ÷ (1 − expenses − brokerage).

The Levers

Discount rate

The heart of LPT economics: a longer-tail book discounted at a higher yield has a lower present value, so the reinsurer can charge a premium well below nominal reserves and still profit from the investment income.

Risk margin

The load for the chance that reserves run off worse than expected — the genuine insurance risk the reinsurer takes on.

Aggregate limit

Finite covers are always capped; the limit (often ~110–150% of reserves) bounds the reinsurer's downside and is central to the risk-transfer test.

Experience account

On multi-year covers, a notional account tracks premiums, losses and interest; a favourable balance is shared back — the financing feature.

Worked Example 1 — Loss Portfolio Transfer

Setup: £100m of nominal reserves; mean payout term 6 years; 4% discount rate; 6% risk margin; 8% reinsurer profit margin. ILLUS

  • Discounted reserves = £100m ÷ 1.04⁶ ≈ £79.0m — the reinsurer keeps ~£21m of investment income over the run-off.
  • Risk margin = 6% × £100m = £6.0m; base = £85.0m.
  • Premium = £85.0m ÷ (1 − 8%) ≈ £92.4m.
  • Reserve relief to the cedant = £100m − £92.4m ≈ £7.6m of P&L benefit, plus the risk and admin off its books.

Worked Example 2 — Adverse Development Cover

Setup: £40m xs £100m carried reserves; expected ultimate £105m; reserve volatility £18m; risk load 30%; expenses 5%; brokerage 7%. ILLUS

  • Probability the ultimate exceeds the £100m carried level ≈ 61% (reserves are expected to develop a little adversely).
  • Expected loss to the £40m xs £100m layer ≈ £7–8m.
  • Premium ≈ expected loss × 1.30 ÷ 0.88 → roughly £11m, rate-on-line ~27%.
  • The cedant caps its reserve deterioration at £40m of cover for a fixed premium.

The Risk-Transfer Test

To be accounted for as reinsurance, a finite deal must transfer significant insurance risk — historically summarised by the "10-10" rule of thumb (a ~10% chance of a ~10% loss to the reinsurer). Deals that merely lend money dressed as reinsurance fail the test and must be booked as deposits. Getting this right is the single most important underwriting judgement in the class. VER

Pre-Bind Checklist

Independently reserve the book and set the payout pattern before pricing.
Choose a defensible discount rate and document it.
Prove the risk-transfer test — real chance of a real loss to the reinsurer.
Set the aggregate limit and any experience-account sharing.
Confirm the accounting and regulatory treatment with both sides' auditors.
