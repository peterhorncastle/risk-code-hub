Structured / Finite — Contract & Wordings

Every finite wording has to do one thing above all: evidence genuine risk transfer. The clauses around the experience account, the aggregate limit and finality are where that is proved.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Structured reinsurance lives or dies by whether it transfers real risk. Six features define the deal and demonstrate that transfer to auditors and regulators. VER

Risk-transfer evidence

The contract must show a real chance of a real loss to the reinsurer — the "10-10" style test — or it is a deposit, not reinsurance. This is the master clause.

Aggregate limit

Finite covers are capped; the aggregate limit bounds the reinsurer's payout and shapes how much risk is transferred.

Experience account

A notional account tracking premium, losses and interest; the balance-sharing terms decide how much upside returns to the cedant.

Commutation & finality

Terms for closing the deal and, for a transfer, achieving legal finality (e.g. a Part VII transfer) so the liability truly leaves the cedant.

Reserving & payout pattern

The agreed reserve estimate and payment pattern drive the discounting — and must be independently supportable.

Collateral / funds withheld

How the assets backing the reserves are held (transferred, in trust or withheld) protects both sides through the run-off.

Governance & Discipline

Before binding

  • Obtain an independent reserve view and payout pattern.
  • Document the risk-transfer test and clear it with both sides' auditors.
  • Agree the aggregate limit, experience-account and finality terms.

Through the run-off

  • Monitor reserve development against the pricing basis.
  • Manage collateral / funds-withheld and the experience-account balance.
  • Consider commutation to close a settled run-off.

⚠️ The deposit-accounting trap

A finite deal that transfers too little risk is not reinsurance — it is a loan, and booking it as reinsurance misstates the accounts. That is precisely what brought down the AIG / General Re participants. Prove the risk transfer, document it, and get the accounting confirmed before binding — or the "cover" becomes a liability of its own.
