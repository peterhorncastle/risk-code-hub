Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Industry Classification — NCCI Class Code

The fundamental WC rating unit. NCCI maintains over 700 class codes, each with a manual rate expressed as $ per $100 of payroll. Rates range from approximately $0.30 per $100 (clerical — code 8810) to $30+ per $100 for the most hazardous operations (logging code 2702, roofing code 5551). Class code assignment is auditable and requires review of actual duties performed. VER

Rate range: 0.3× (clerical) → 8.0× (logging/roofing vs clerical baseline)
High Impact

Payroll by Class Code

WC premium is calculated as (manual rate per $100) × (payroll ÷ 100) for each class code. Estimated payroll at inception is audited at policy expiry; premium adjusts upward or downward. Payroll audits are a critical revenue protection mechanism — misclassification and payroll understatement are the primary WC premium fraud vectors. Multi-state employers require payroll allocation by state of employment. VER

Audit adjustment: +20% to −15% typical variance on complex accounts
High Impact

Experience Modification Factor (EMR)

3-year weighted actual vs expected loss calculation, credibility-weighted by payroll size. EMR of 1.00 = average for class; EMR <1.00 = better than average (credit); EMR >1.00 = worse than average (debit). Applies as a direct multiplier to manual premium. For large accounts, EMR can swing ±40% of premium. Minimum payroll threshold for experience rating (approximately $10,000 standard premium) — smaller accounts are class-rated only. VER

EMR range in practice: 0.50 (excellent) → 2.00+ (poor); 1.15 = significant debit
High Impact

State of Domicile & Exposure States

WC rates and benefit structures vary dramatically by state. New York, California and New Jersey are consistently the highest-cost states; southern states (TX, FL, AL) have typically lower manual rates but complex regulatory environments. Multi-state employers must list all states of operation — the "all-states" endorsement provides residual coverage for states not individually listed. Monopolistic states (OH, WY, ND, WA) require state fund placement for Part One; stop-gap EL endorsement essential. VER

NY manual rate (clerical): ~$1.20/$100 vs national avg ~$0.60/$100
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Payroll Audit Provisions

Premium audit at policy expiry adjusts for actual payroll vs estimated. Audit disputes are common in rapidly growing businesses and seasonal operations. Retrospective rating plans (retro) offer large employers premium adjustment based on actual losses — aligning insured incentives with loss prevention. Min-max retro premiums (typically 50%–175% of standard premium) are used for large self-funded employers.

Retro premium range: 50%–175% of standard; SIR structures for EMR >1.00 risks
Medium Impact

Interstate Exposure — Multi-State Endorsement

Employers operating in multiple states need coverage in each jurisdiction. WC policies must specifically list the states where coverage applies; the all-states endorsement adds a residual catch-all for unlisted states but is not a substitute for proper state listing. State-specific loss costs and assessments complicate multi-state premium calculation. VER

Multi-state loading: +5–15% for administrative and state assessment complexity
Medium Impact

USL&H Exposure — Maritime Payroll Surcharge

Employers with payroll subject to the LHWCA (dock workers, ship repairers, offshore platform workers) require W6 USL&H coverage, rated separately from standard WC. USL&H rates are typically 2–4x the standard WC rate for equivalent job categories, reflecting higher benefit levels (no state caps), Jones Act tort exposure via Part Two, and higher claim severity. DBA (overseas contractors) also carries a surcharge for war-zone deployments. VER

USL&H surcharge: 2–4× standard WC rate; DBA war-zone: +50–100% on USL&H rate
Medium Impact

Stop-Gap / EL Limit Selected

The EL Part Two limit protects against common law suits that breach the exclusive remedy shield. Standard limits ($1m / $1m / $1m) are adequate for most employers; high-hazard industries with aggressive plaintiff bars (TX, NY, IL) may warrant $2m or $5m EL limits. Excess EL (umbrella / excess casualty) provides additional protection. Stop-gap endorsements in monopolistic states should mirror the EL limit on the WC/EL policy.

EL limit uplift ($1m → $2m): +8–12% additional premium
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Safety Programme Documentation

Written OSHA-compliant safety programme, OSHA 300 log review, DART rate vs BLS industry average. Best-in-class safety programmes (VPP Star status, industry safety awards) may support schedule credit. Absence of documented safety programme is a red flag for high-hazard accounts. VER

Lower Impact

Claims Management Quality

In-house risk management / claims coordination vs no formal claims contact; designated medical provider relationship; historical claims closure patterns. Rapid initial contact (3-day contact rule) and active return-to-work coordination correlate with significantly lower claim severity — typically 30–50% lower average claim cost. VER

Lower Impact

Return-to-Work Programme

Formal modified/transitional duty programme allows injured workers to return in a limited capacity before full recovery, dramatically reducing temporary disability duration and overall claim cost. Light-duty job bank and designated physician partnerships are key components. NCCI data shows employers with RTW programmes have 20–30% lower total WC claim costs. EST

Lower Impact

Drug Testing Policy

Post-accident drug testing (where state law permits) provides two benefits: it deters impaired working and may limit WC benefit eligibility where intoxication contributed to the accident (many states reduce or deny benefits for positive tests). DOT-regulated employers (transportation) have mandatory drug testing requirements that also function as WC risk controls. VER

Experience Modification Factor (EMR) — Formula ExplainedVER
// NCCI Experience Rating — simplified formula structure

EMR = (Actual Primary Losses + Ballast) + Z × (Actual Excess Losses)
      ÷ (Expected Primary Losses + Ballast) + Z × (Expected Excess Losses)

// Where:
Primary Loss   = Per-claim losses up to the primary threshold (typically $17,500)
Excess Loss    = Per-claim losses above the primary threshold
Ballast        = Credibility stabiliser based on expected losses (reduces volatility for small accounts)
Z              = Credibility factor (0–1.0; higher for larger payroll — full credibility at ~$2m+ std premium)

// Key implications:
High-frequency small claims  = full primary loss credit (maximum EMR impact)
Single catastrophic loss     = partially discounted via excess threshold (partial EMR protection)
Large employers             = near-full credibility (EMR closely tracks actual experience)
Small employers             = low credibility (EMR moves less from 1.00; heavy ballast weight)

Texas Non-Subscriber Alert: Texas is the only state where WC is elective for most private employers. Non-subscriber employers that opt out of WC lose their exclusive remedy protection and face uncapped common law tort suits — including pain and suffering, punitive damages and wrongful death — in which they cannot assert assumption of risk, contributory negligence or fellow-servant defences. A single serious injury against a Texas non-subscriber can generate verdicts of $5m–$25m or more. Lloyd's EL underwriters should specifically identify Texas payroll and non-subscriber status. Stop-gap EL does not replace WC for non-subscribers — a voluntary WC programme or Texas non-subscriber occupational accident policy is required. VER
NCCI Basic Manual — ADA & FMLA Coordination: The ADA (42 U.S.C. §12101) VER and FMLA (29 U.S.C. §2601) VER interact with WC in return-to-work programmes. Employers must coordinate WC restrictions with FMLA leave entitlement and ADA reasonable accommodation requirements — failure to accommodate a disabled worker returning from WC may generate a separate ADA claim. Modified duty offers that do not constitute reasonable accommodation under ADA may also be challenged. Underwriters should ask about RTW coordinator capability and ADA/FMLA interaction protocols.
