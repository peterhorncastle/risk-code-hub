Global Programme Structure

Multi-state WC architecture, monopolistic states, USL&H integration and multinational context
Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater
Scope: W5 and W6 cover US operations only — domestic WC under state statutes (W5) and federal maritime/overseas contractor coverage (W6/DBA). For US multinational employers deploying workers internationally (outside DBA scope), W4 (Overseas Workers Compensation) is required, providing voluntary compensation extending US-equivalent benefits overseas. This module covers the multi-state US programme architecture and the interaction of W5, W6, monopolistic states, and Texas's unique non-subscriber system.
4-Layer WC Programme Architecture

Illustrative Structure — Large Multi-State Employer

Layer 4 — Reinsurance / XL
Excess-of-loss reinsurance / catastrophe XL — per-occurrence limit above the primary WC policy; protects carrier from WTC-style aggregate events; also enables Lloyd's participation as reinsurer of large admitted WC programmes
Layer 3 — Excess WC / EL
Excess Workers Compensation (above statutory limit, if any) + Excess Employers Liability above primary $1m EL limit; typically $5m–$25m excess layer; Lloyd's market common for complex risks
Layer 2 — Primary WC (All States)
Primary WC policy — Part One (statutory benefits, no limit) + Part Two EL ($1m/$1m/$1m) — admitted carrier in each listed state; all-states endorsement as residual catch-all; stop-gap endorsements for monopolistic states; USL&H (W6) endorsement where maritime payroll present
Layer 1 — SIR / Retro
Self-Insured Retention (SIR) or Retro Premium — large employers may retain primary losses via SIR (fronted by admitted carrier) or retrospective rating plan; SIR typically $100k–$1m per occurrence; reduces premium but requires strong claims management and financial strength
All-States Endorsement vs Specific State Listing

All-States Endorsement — Residual Catch-All

  • Provides automatic coverage in any state not individually listed
  • Essential for employers with occasional or unplanned interstate operations
  • Does NOT replace specific state listings — listed states get the filed rates and specific coverages
  • Does NOT cover monopolistic states — these require separate state fund placement
  • Does NOT provide coverage in states the insurer is not licensed — must verify carrier admitted status
  • All-states endorsement coverage triggers 30 days after first employee begins work in unlisted state
  • Best practice: list all known states specifically; all-states as backup only

Specific State Listing — Primary Mechanism

  • Each state listed individually triggers the applicable statutory benefit structure and filed loss costs for that state
  • Ensures correct benefit levels, assessment surcharges, and state-specific exclusions/endorsements apply
  • Multi-state payroll allocation: payroll attributed to the state where work is performed (not employer domicile)
  • New York: unique "Other States" endorsement structure; must be listed separately
  • California: WCIRB rates (not NCCI); separate endorsement language required
  • Texas: non-subscriber election documented separately; stop-gap EL endorsement specific to TX
  • Annual audit verifies actual payroll per state — premium adjusts accordingly
6 Key Jurisdiction Profiles
NCCI States (~38 States)

Standard NCCI Framework

Approximately 38 states use NCCI loss costs and experience rating. NCCI files class loss costs; individual carriers apply loss cost multipliers (LCMs). Residual market: NCCI-administered assigned risk pool (NCCI Pool) is the market of last resort. EMR calculated by NCCI for all states where employer has qualifying payroll. Interstate experience rating applies.

Monopolistic States — OH, WY, ND, WA

State Fund Only — Private Excess EL Essential

Private insurers cannot write Part One WC in these four states — employers must purchase from the state fund. Private excess insurance can provide EL (Part Two equivalent) above the state fund's nominal EL coverage. Stop-gap EL endorsement is critical for employers in these states — without it, there is no protection against common law tort suits that breach the exclusive remedy shield. State funds vary in quality and service levels.[L1]

Texas — Unique Non-Subscriber System

Elective WC — Non-Subscriber Tort Exposure

Texas is the only state where private employers may elect not to purchase WC ("non-subscriber"). Non-subscriber employers lose all WC defences and face uncapped tort suits including pain and suffering and punitive damages. Major Texas employers (Amazon, Walmart, HEB) have historically been non-subscribers with self-insured occupational accident programmes. Non-subscriber occupational accident policies are NOT WC — they do not provide exclusive remedy protection. Lloyd's non-subscriber EL exposure can be significant for uninsured Texas employers.[L1]

Federal Maritime — LHWCA / DBA / FECA

W6 Territory — Federal Statutory Benefits

Maritime workers on navigable waters and adjoining lands (LHWCA), US contractors overseas (DBA), and federal employees (FECA) are outside state WC jurisdiction — their benefits are set by federal statute with no state caps. LHWCA benefits: 2/3 AWW, no medical dollar limit, death benefits 50% AWW to widow. DBA war-zone rates typically 2–4× standard USL&H. Lloyd's is a major market for excess maritime WC and DBA coverage for US government contractors.[L2][L3]

California — WCIRB Regulated

Independent Bureau; Highest Complexity State

California WC is regulated by the Workers' Compensation Insurance Rating Bureau (WCIRB) — not NCCI. California has its own experience rating plan, classification system (largely parallel to NCCI but with state-specific additions), and regulatory framework (WCAB, DIR, DWC). California historically the highest-cost and most litigation-intense WC state in the US. AB 1192 (safe patient handling), SB 1159 (COVID presumption), and SB 213 (agricultural workers) are recent California WC legislative developments. Lloyd's E&S market active in California high-hazard WC.[I6]

New York — Assessment-Heavy

High Benefits; Unique Surcharge Structure

New York WC is among the highest-cost states — high benefit levels, unlimited medical (like all states), unique assessment structures (SIF surcharge, reopened case fund assessment, WCB assessment) that add 30–40% to the base premium. New York requires separate endorsement language and has its own "other states" endorsement requirements. NYC concentration of workers dramatically amplifies the aggregate WC exposure for employers operating across boroughs.

Residual Market / NCCI Pool — Compliance Note: High-hazard employers refused by voluntary market carriers must be written through the state's assigned risk mechanism — typically the NCCI Pool (in NCCI states), California SCIF (state compensation insurance fund), or individual state assigned risk plans. Lloyd's syndicates do NOT participate directly in residual market pools but may write excess layers above pool primary coverage. Residual market rates are typically higher than voluntary market rates — employers assigned to the residual market have a strong financial incentive to improve EMR and safety performance to regain voluntary market eligibility. Residual market participation is a significant red flag indicator in WC underwriting; persistent residual market history suggests unresolvable hazard.[L9]
