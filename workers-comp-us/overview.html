Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

W5 — Workers Compensation (Standard)

Standard US Workers Compensation and Employers Liability insurance. Part One provides statutory WC benefits (medical, indemnity, rehabilitation) mandated by each state's WC statute with no policy limit — the insurer pays whatever the statute requires. Part Two provides Employers Liability (EL) protection against common law tort suits by employees, typically with a policy limit of $1m each accident / $1m each employee / $1m policy aggregate. Rated on payroll by NCCI class code.

W6 — USL&H / Maritime Workers Compensation

Workers Compensation for employees covered under the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (LHWCA, 33 U.S.C. §901) and related federal maritime statutes — dock workers, ship repairers, harbour workers, offshore platform workers. Also covers Defence Base Act (DBA) exposures for US contractors working overseas. W6 risks attract a maritime payroll surcharge and carry higher severity than standard WC due to Jones Act tort exposure and the absence of state benefit caps. Underwritten separately from W5 due to distinct statutory regime.

W5 vs W4 — Jurisdictional Boundary

W5 / W6 cover US workers under US statutes. For US employers deploying workers internationally, W4 (Overseas WC) provides coverage under a voluntary compensation endorsement extending US-equivalent benefits overseas. W6 DBA coverage is an exception — it covers US contractors overseas but under a US federal statute (42 U.S.C. §1651). Multi-state endorsements within W5 must list all states of operation; the all-states endorsement provides a residual catch-all.

Coverage Taxonomy

Part One — Workers Compensation

Statutory indemnification of injured workers: medical treatment (no dollar limit), temporary disability (60–80% of wage, state-specific), permanent disability (schedule or whole-person impairment), death benefits and rehabilitation. Insurer agrees to pay whatever the applicable statute requires. No sub-limits or deductibles on Part One. VER

Part Two — Employers Liability

Covers common law suits by employees or their dependants that are not barred by the WC exclusive remedy shield — e.g. third-party-over suits, dual-capacity doctrine, loss of consortium claims, employer negligence suits in states where the exclusive remedy is not absolute. Standard limits: $1m each accident / $1m each employee / $1m policy. Higher limits available. VER

USL&H Act Coverage (W6)

LHWCA (33 U.S.C. §901) provides WC-type benefits to longshore workers, harbour workers, ship repairers and those employed on the navigable waters of the US and adjoining land areas. Benefits: two-thirds of average weekly wage; no cap on medical; death benefits. Employers exposed to LHWCA must secure coverage — self-insurance or insurance. Surcharge applies due to higher severity. VER

Defense Base Act (DBA)

42 U.S.C. §1651 extends LHWCA benefits to US and non-US employees of US government contractors working overseas — on military bases, diplomatic facilities or under US government contracts. US military contractors in Iraq, Afghanistan, and other conflict zones are the major market segment. DBA is mandatory for covered contractors. W6 or standalone DBA policy required. VER

Federal Employees Compensation Act (FECA)

5 U.S.C. §8101 governs WC for US federal civilian government employees — administered by the US Department of Labor (OWCP). Lloyd's does not write FECA risks directly; however, the FECA framework is relevant context for understanding federal contractor obligations (DBA) and the structure of US federal programme. VER

Stop-Gap / EL Endorsement

In monopolistic WC states (OH, WY, ND, WA) where insurers cannot write Part One (state fund only), a stop-gap EL endorsement provides the Employers Liability (Part Two equivalent) protection against common law suits. Critical for multi-state employers with operations in monopolistic states — without it, there is a significant EL gap. Also used in Texas for non-subscriber employers purchasing voluntary WC.

Key Regulatory Framework: Workers Compensation in the USA is compulsory in 49 states (Texas operates an elective non-subscriber system). The National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) administers the experience rating plan and maintains the loss cost filing in approximately 38 states; independent rating bureaux operate in California (WCIRB), New York, and several other states. VER The residual market (NCCI-administered assigned risk pool or state assigned risk plan) provides coverage of last resort for high-hazard or non-standard employers. Four monopolistic states (Ohio, Wyoming, North Dakota, Washington) require WC placement with the state fund; private insurers may write excess EL but not Part One WC.
Opioid Crisis — Claim Cost Impact: The US opioid epidemic significantly inflated WC medical costs from approximately 2008–2018. RAND Institute research estimated opioid prescribing in WC added an estimated 15–20% to claim costs during peak years. EST Many states have now enacted prescription drug monitoring programmes (PDMPs) and adopted Official Disability Guidelines (ODG) to manage prescribing. Despite regulatory progress, opioid-related claim reopening, long-tail disability and mortality remain active reserving concerns for WC carriers writing pre-2018 accident years.
