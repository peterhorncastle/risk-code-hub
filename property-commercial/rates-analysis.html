Rate Index — Commercial Property 2005–2024

The commercial property market has been highly volatile — two major soft cycles (2005–2011, 2013–2016) punctuated by sharp hardening events (post-Katrina 2005, post-2017 HIM, post-COVID/Ian 2022). By 2024, non-cat property rates are broadly adequate while US cat-exposed (Florida, California) risks command significant risk-adjusted premium increases. ILLUS

Market Cycle Narrative

2001–2004 · Hard — Post-9/11

9/11 triggered immediate 30–50% rate increases on US commercial property and terrorism covers. New terrorism exclusions drove TRIA creation. Cat capacity reduced; rates adequate through 2004.

2005 · Katrina Shock — Brief Hard Turn

Katrina/Rita/Wilma 2005 season caused $60bn insured losses. US cat rates increased 40%+. Lloyd's Gulf Coast capacity withdrawn or repriced. Lasted 2 years before new capital eroded gains.

2006–2011 · Soft — Capital Influx

Post-Katrina capital influx (Bermuda Class of 2006) created overcapacity. Rates fell steadily 2007–2011. 2010/2011 nat cat year ($380bn total losses) absorbed capacity without market-turning rate response.

2012–2016 · Soft Continuation

Benign US hurricane seasons; ILS capital growth; alternative capital ~25% of cat reinsurance capacity. Rates fell 3–8% annually. Attritional losses (flooding, SCS) began dragging combined ratios above 100.

2017–2018 · HIM Season — Hard Turn

Harvey/Irma/Maria $43bn combined insured losses. First year in a decade that cat losses significantly exceeded the market's expected annual loss. Rates increased 15–25% on cat-exposed risks; reinsurance rates up 30–40%.

2019–2020 · Partial Softening

COVID disruption to renewals; some softening in non-cat lines. COVID BI losses (2020) caused significant BI combined ratio deterioration; non-damage BI exclusions adopted universally.

2021–2024 · Sustained Hardening

Ian 2022 ($60bn), Turkey 2023, 2023 SCS record year ($64bn US). Reinsurers repriced significantly at Jan 2023. US cat property rates increased 25–50% in 2022–2024. Florida market near-crisis; California E&S hardening. Market broadly adequate but SCS attritional losses persist.
