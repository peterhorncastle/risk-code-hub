Lloyd's Risk Codes — P2 to P7 and B2 to B5

Commercial property sits at the heart of the Lloyd's market. The P codes cover direct and facultative non-marine property, segmented by coverage basis and geography; the B codes cover binding authority (binder) business written through coverholders on the same risk base. Together these ten codes represent the largest single class group at Lloyd's by premium volume.

Coverage Architecture — What Commercial Property Insures

Commercial property policies operate on two key coverage bases: named perils (covering only losses caused by explicitly listed events — FLEXA: Fire, Lightning, Explosion, Aircraft impact — plus extensions) and all risks (covering all fortuitous physical damage unless expressly excluded). All-risks wordings dominate large commercial accounts; named perils remain common in developing markets and for commodity risks written on ISR (Industrial Special Risks) wordings.

🔥 Material Damage — Buildings & Contents

  • Buildings: fabric, foundations, fixtures, plant machinery
  • Contents: stock, raw materials, finished goods, equipment
  • Reinstatement or indemnity (actual cash value) basis
  • Average / co-insurance clause if underinsured
  • Demolition and debris removal costs
  • Architects' fees and local authority requirements

📊 Business Interruption (BI)

  • Loss of gross profit / gross revenue during indemnity period
  • Standard indemnity period: 12, 24 or 36 months
  • Maximum indemnity period (MIP) — critical choice
  • Waiting period / time excess (typically 72 hrs for storm/flood)
  • Increased cost of working (ICOW)
  • Contingent BI: supplier/customer interruption

🌊 Catastrophe Natural Perils

  • Windstorm / hurricane / typhoon
  • Flood (surface water, river, storm surge)
  • Earthquake and fire following earthquake
  • Hailstorm, tornado, ice storm
  • Subsidence and landslip
  • Volcanic eruption; tsunami

🔷 Specialty Covers

  • Terrorism (POOL RE / TRIA / standalone)
  • Malicious damage and riot
  • Computer breakdown / electronic equipment
  • Machinery breakdown (engineering interface)
  • Accidental damage (AD) extension
  • Goods in transit; money

🏗️ Additional Expense & Extensions

  • Professional fees (architects, surveyors)
  • Local authority / building regulations compliance
  • Expediting expenses for emergency repairs
  • Property in the open; external storage
  • Deterioration of stock (cold storage)
  • Glass breakage; signs; neon

🚫 Standard Exclusions

  • War, invasion, nuclear / radiological perils
  • Gradual deterioration, wear & tear, inherent defect
  • Cyber physical damage (post-2020 market exclusion)
  • Communicable disease / pandemic (post-COVID)
  • PFAS / forever chemical contamination (emerging)
  • Wilful acts of the insured

COPE Underwriting Framework

Commercial property underwriting revolves around the COPE framework — the four pillars that determine both the probability and severity of loss. Every risk submission should be assessed against all four dimensions before rating, and deficiencies in any pillar drive loading or declination.

COPE — The Four Pillars of Property Underwriting

  • Construction: Frame type (frame, joisted masonry, masonry non-combustible, modified fire resistive, fire resistive); roof type and age; cladding materials; glazing; basement vulnerability; Building Regs compliance and fire stops
  • Occupancy: SIC / occupancy class; fire load; hazardous processes or storage; cooking or hot work; 24-hour attendance vs. unoccupied periods; storage height and density; ignition sources
  • Protection: Automatic fire suppression (sprinklers — NFPA 13 or LPC standard); fire detection and alarm systems; fire doors and compartmentation; security; risk management programme; BAFE-registered contractors; fire authority response time
  • Exposure: Distance to flood zone (FEMA / Environment Agency maps); CRESTA zone for cat; proximity to industrial hazards; conflagration potential; wildland-urban interface (WUI) for wildfire; subsidence risk (clay soils)

Property Valuation Methods

Reinstatement Value (RV)

Cost of rebuilding to the same specification using current materials and labour rates. Excludes land value. Preferred basis for most commercial risks. Subject to underinsurance (average) if below actual rebuild cost.

Indemnity Value (ACV)

Market value or RV less depreciation. Common for plant/machinery and older stock. Can result in significant under-recovery at claim — insured may prefer RV basis even at higher premium.

Day-One Reinstatement

UK standard: sum insured = current RV, with automatic index-linked uplift of 15–50% to cover construction cost inflation during repair period. Avoids average clause gap on large losses.

Agreed Value / Blanket

Blanket limits across multiple locations without itemised values. Common for multi-site retail/industrial. Requires total declared value (TDV) schedule. Simplifies administration but demands accurate aggregate assessment.

Major Perils — Loss Drivers

🔥 Fire & Explosion

Historically dominant peril. Large single-risk fires (warehouses, heritage buildings, HazMat sites) still drive attritional and large-loss results. Arson is a significant contributor in commercial lines.

🌊 Flood

Fastest-growing nat cat peril globally. 2011 Thailand floods ($43bn) showed supply chain BI aggregation. UK Surface Water Flooding gap. NFIP limitations in US drive Lloyd's E&S demand.

🌀 Windstorm & Hurricane

Largest single-year loss driver. Atlantic hurricane season (Katrina 2005, Harvey/Irma/Maria 2017, Ian 2022). European windstorm (Lothar/Martin 1999, Kyrill 2007, Ciaran 2023). Cat model dependency.

🌏 Earthquake

San Francisco 1906 — birth of cat reinsurance. Northridge 1994 ($44bn total). Japan Tōhoku 2011 ($35bn insured). Turkey-Syria 2023 ($7bn insured; $90bn total). CRESTA zone rating critical.

🔥 Wildfire

Fastest-emerging peril in US/Australia/southern Europe. California wildfires 2017–2021 drove market withdrawal. Paradise CA (Camp Fire 2018, $12bn insured). Maui Lahaina 2023. WUI expansion a systemic risk.

⚙️ Business Interruption

Frequently exceeds material damage in large losses. COVID BI test case (FCA v Arch, UKSC 2021): ~£1.2bn industry payout UK. Supply chain BI (contingent) — post-COVID reunderwriting of triggers and extensions.
