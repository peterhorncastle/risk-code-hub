Programme Tower Structure — Large Corporate Property

A large multinational corporate will typically structure its property programme as a layered tower with a self-insured retention (SIR) at the base, primary layer lead by a Lloyd's or composite insurer, successive excess layers, and a catastrophe XL at the top. The SIR and primary layer handle attritional losses; the cat XL is sized to protect the balance sheet against single major nat cat events.

Multinational Programme Coordination

🌐 Master Policy (International Programme)

  • Master policy placed in London (Lloyd's) covering worldwide risks
  • Covers gaps not picked up by admitted local policies (DIC/DIL)
  • IMP (International Master Policy) — Lloyd's standard form
  • Governing law: UK or agreed jurisdiction; English language
  • Aggregate TIV declared on schedule; cat PML managed at master level

🏛️ Local Admitted Policies

  • Required in countries where non-admitted insurance is illegal or heavily regulated
  • Network insurer (AIG, Zurich, AXA XL, Chubb) fronts local admitted paper
  • 99%+ reinsured back to Lloyd's master; fronting fee typically 5–10% of premium
  • Key admitted markets: Brazil, China, India, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, South Korea
  • Admitted policy must comply with local tariffs and mandatory covers (e.g., Brazil SUSEP)

⚖️ DIC / DIL Structure

  • DIC (Difference in Conditions): Fills coverage gaps between local and master policy (local may only cover named perils; master fills to all-risks)
  • DIL (Difference in Limits): Tops up limit where local policy limit is below master programme limit
  • DIC/DIL endorsement on master policy — must be carefully drafted to avoid overlapping claims
  • Claims handling: local insurer handles below SIR; London market handles DIC/DIL excess

🏢 Captive Integration

  • Large multinationals may fund the SIR and some primary layer through a captive insurer (Bermuda, Guernsey, Cayman)
  • Captive issues local policies; reinsures to Lloyd's IMP above captive retention
  • Advantage: premium cash flow retained; loss experience visible; customised covers
  • Solvency II / IAIS equivalent compliance required for EU-based captives
  • Cell companies (PCCs): smaller multinationals can rent a captive cell vs forming standalone

Reinsurance Architecture — Lloyd's Property Syndicate

  • Cat XL reinsurance: Syndicates purchase their own cat XL from the reinsurance market (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, RenRe, Everest) to protect against aggregated cat losses within their portfolio. Cat budget (annual expected loss) × 3–5× = typical cat XL attachment point.
  • Risk XL: Protects against large single-risk losses (e.g., £50m warehouse fire); typically attaches at 2–5× average sum insured.
  • Aggregate stop-loss: Caps the aggregate of all losses in a year below a combined ratio threshold; more expensive; provides more predictable combined ratio outcome.
  • Facultative reinsurance: Single-risk placement for very large or unusual risks that exceed a syndicate's treaty capacity; placed risk-by-risk with specialist fac re underwriters.
  • Lloyd's Central Fund: Provides additional security above syndicate capital; Lloyd's "chain of security" rated A (Standard & Poor's) gives Lloyd's paper investment-grade status globally.
