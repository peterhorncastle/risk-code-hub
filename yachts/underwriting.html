Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Vessel Agreed Value

The agreed value (hull, machinery, and equipment) is the primary rating base. Established by survey at inception for vessels over a specified age or value threshold (typically 15+ years or £100k+ agreed value). Annual revaluation required for superyachts (24m+) where market values fluctuate significantly. Agreed value inflation post-COVID requires underwriter vigilance. EST

Primary rating base: rate × agreed value; higher values attract proportionally lower rates (size benefit)
High Impact

Navigation Limits

The single most important geographic rating variable. Inland/coastal limits attract the lowest rates; bluewater/worldwide navigation (including piracy-risk zones) the highest. Seasonal endorsements (Mediterranean summer, Caribbean winter) allow rate adjustments for lay-up periods. Navigation limit breach can void cover — must be clearly communicated to insured. EST

Loading: Coastal 1.0× → Offshore 1.3× → Bluewater/worldwide 1.6–2.0× (ex-piracy zones)
High Impact

Vessel Type & Size

Motor yachts have higher fire frequency than sailing yachts (engine room ignition sources). Superyachts (24m+) have higher absolute loss amounts but lower loss ratios (better maintained, survey-compliant). Racing yachts require separate endorsement and different base rate. Foiling yachts (AC75 class, IMOCA foiling) require bespoke assessment. EST

Sailing cruiser 1.0× base; motor yacht +15–25%; superyacht specialist rates; racing endorsement separately rated
High Impact

Owner / Skipper Experience

RYA Yachtmaster Offshore/Ocean qualifications are the standard Lloyd's benchmark for owner experience. Commercially crewed superyachts require OOW-level MCA certificates. Offshore miles logged, prior cruising history, and professional skipper use all affect the experience assessment. Inexperienced owners on offshore navigation limits attract significant loadings. EST

RYA Yachtmaster credit: −10 to −15%; inexperienced owner + offshore limit: +30 to +50%
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Racing Endorsement

Racing cover is excluded under standard cruising policies and separately rated as an endorsement. Rates depend on the race category (club racing, offshore passage racing, ocean racing), number of racing days, and offshore area. RORC OSR compliance is a standard condition. Aggregate limits apply for racing syndicate/fleet programmes. VER

Racing endorsement: +0.05–0.20% of agreed value per annum depending on race category
Medium Impact

Charter Use

Commercial charter (bareboat or crewed charter) significantly changes the risk profile — increased utilisation, inexperienced charterers, higher wear rates, and commercial crew employment liability. MCA commercial code (LY3/MCA Cat) compliance required for Lloyd's commercial charter endorsement. Rate loading reflects the step-change in exposure. VER

Commercial charter endorsement: +25 to +50% H&M loading; separate P&I/liability limit required
Medium Impact

Lay-Up Warranties

Vessels laid up ashore or in a secure marina for a defined period (typically winter, Oct–Apr in North Europe) attract a reduced in-commission rate for the lay-up period. Lay-up warranties require written notification to underwriters; breach by using the vessel during lay-up without prior endorsement can void the seasonal discount and attract claims dispute. EST

Lay-up credit: −30 to −50% of the in-commission rate for the lay-up period
Medium Impact

Crew Qualifications (Superyacht)

For commercially crewed superyachts, the Master's and Chief Officer's MCA STCW certification level is a key rating input. MCA OOW/Master certificate (Yacht) with appropriate GT limits confirms competence for vessel size. MLC 2006 crew welfare documentation compliance is reviewed. Under-qualified crew significantly increases exposure. VER

MCA Master (Yacht) 3000GT: standard rate; downgrade loading if crew qualifications are below vessel requirement
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Home Port & Mooring

Marina security, tidal range, exposure to prevailing weather, and hurricane zone status (Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico) all affect the mooring risk. Vessels permanently based in Category 5 hurricane zones require hurricane endorsements and/or haul-out warranties for the June–November season. Marina theft statistics and security cameras are supportive evidence.

Lower Impact

Safety Equipment

EPIRB (406MHz, registered to vessel), Category A or B liferaft, jacklines, PLBs for crew, AIS transponder (Class B), VHF DSC radio, and flares (current date) are standard Lloyd's safety equipment requirements for offshore navigation limits. Some syndicates offer equipment credit for above-standard fit-out (SART, satellite communicator). VER

Lower Impact

Survey Requirements

Vessels over 10 years old and/or with agreed value over £100k typically require a pre-inception Lloyd's survey (marine surveyor approved). Vessels 15+ years old and superyachts typically require 5-yearly out-of-water surveys. Survey currency is a standard underwriting condition — lapsed survey warranty can void cover for structural or osmosis-related claims.

Lower Impact

Claims History

Three-year claims history is standard Lloyd's yacht underwriting input. Frequency of small claims (grounding, collision, theft) is a stronger predictor of future losses than single large losses. Claims-free discounts of 5–15% are common on renewal. Claims-laden history (3+ claims in 5 years) may make the risk unacceptable to standard yacht syndicates. EST

Osmosis and Pre-Existing Conditions: GRP (fibreglass) hulls are susceptible to osmotic blistering when water permeates the gelcoat over time. Osmosis is a maintenance/latent defect issue and is universally excluded from yacht hull policies as a non-fortuitous peril. Survey reports at inception frequently note osmosis condition — severe osmosis is a material fact requiring disclosure. Post-survey epoxy barrier coatings and osmosis treatment are typically owner responsibilities; failed treatment costs are not covered. EST
Navigation Limit Warranty — Automatic Voidance Risk: Navigation limit clauses in yacht policies typically operate as warranties under the Marine Insurance Act 1906 (as modified by the Insurance Act 2015 for commercial policies). Breach of a navigation warranty voids cover from the date of breach — not just for the incident caused by the breach. Underwriters should ensure the policy clearly states the consequences of navigation limit breach and that insureds understand prior endorsement requirements. This is particularly relevant for superyachts making ocean passages outside their agreed navigation limits. VER
