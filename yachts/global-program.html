Multinational Yacht Fleet — 4-Layer Programme Structure
L4

Excess of Loss / Aggregate Reinsurance — Catastrophe Protection

Top layer: catastrophe XL reinsurance protecting the underwriting portfolio from accumulation losses (Caribbean hurricane season, Mediterranean nat cat). Per-occurrence and aggregate protections designed around realistic disaster scenarios (RDS) for the fleet. Lloyd's syndicates purchase outwards reinsurance independently; fleet programme administrators seek coordinated RI efficiency. VER

L3

Lloyd's Fleet Master Policy — Hull & Machinery (Agreed Value)

Master hull policy covering all vessels in the fleet on agreed value basis. Single policy with per-vessel schedules — agreed values, navigation limits, vessel class, survey currency, MCA compliance status. Blanketed perils (sinking, fire, grounding, storm, collision, theft) with specific exclusions (racing without endorsement, war, navigation limit breach). Annual review of agreed values required. EST

L2

Per-Vessel H&M — Individual Agreed Value Coverage

Each vessel within the fleet carries individual agreed value H&M coverage. Charter vessels rated with commercial charter endorsement; owner-operated vessels at standard rates. Vessel additions and deletions managed via fleet schedule endorsement. Mid-term additions (new vessel deliveries, acquisitions) reported and endorsed promptly. Survey requirements met per vessel class and age. EST

L1

P&I (Yacht P&I Clubs or Lloyd's) — Third Party Liability

Base layer: third-party liability (P&I-style cover) for each vessel and the fleet operator. Commercial charter fleets typically require combined P&I and passenger liability limits of £5–50m. Yacht P&I clubs (e.g., Pantaenius P&I, UK Club yacht division, Markel One) or Lloyd's liability syndicates provide capacity. Passenger liability under EU Regulation 392/2009 (Athens Convention) relevant for EU-flagged passenger-carrying yachts. VER

Seasonal Navigation Endorsement Structure: Multinational yacht fleet programmes typically involve vessels operating in multiple geographic regions across the year — Mediterranean season (April–October), Caribbean season (November–April), and Atlantic crossings between. The fleet master policy must incorporate seasonal navigation endorsements that: (1) confirm the applicable navigation limit for each vessel in each season; (2) grant prior approval for standard ocean passages (e.g., ARC Atlantic Rally November); (3) confirm hurricane zone endorsements and haul-out warranty requirements during June–November; and (4) activate war/piracy endorsements for vessels transiting the Red Sea or Gulf of Aden. Failure to manage seasonal endorsements is a common source of coverage disputes on fleet programmes. EST
Jurisdiction Profiles
🇬🇧

United Kingdom

Home of Lloyd's and the world's leading yacht insurance market. Key regulatory bodies: MCA (Maritime and Coastguard Agency), RYA, UK Ship Register.

  • Merchant Shipping Act 1995 governs UK-flagged vessels VER
  • MCA LY3 compliance for commercially operated yachts 24m+
  • VAT applies at 20% on UK charter services
  • Insurance Act 2015 governs fair presentation for all UK placements VER
  • RYA qualification framework: standard for owner experience rating
🇬🇷🇮🇹

Mediterranean (Greece / Italy)

The world's largest charter yacht market by vessel count. Strict national charter licensing regimes apply in addition to MCA/flag state requirements.

  • Greek charter licence (EOET) required for all commercial charter operations in Greek waters VER
  • Professional skipper requirement: HNOA-licenced under Greek PD 284/2000 VER
  • Italian charter registry (RINA) — separate licence for Italian waters
  • Port Authority (Limenarchio) authority over charter vessel inspections
  • VAT harmonisation under EU law — yacht charter VAT complex
🇻🇬🇦🇬

Caribbean (BVI / Antigua)

Premier charter cruising ground. Hurricane zone June–November requires specific underwriting provisions.

  • British Virgin Islands: major bareboat charter market; BVI Ship Registry
  • Antigua: Antiguan flag popular for superyachts; major refit base
  • Hurricane season (June–November): haul-out warranties or hurricane hole endorsements required
  • No Jones Act restriction (unlike USVI) — foreign-flagged yachts may charter freely EST
  • Caribbean Superyacht Association standards for fleet operators
🇺🇸

United States / Florida

World's largest recreational boating market by vessel count. Complex regulatory environment for foreign-flagged charter yachts.

  • USCG inspection requirements for passenger-carrying vessels in US waters
  • Jones Act (Merchant Marine Act 1920): foreign-flagged yachts generally cannot carry passengers between US ports for hire — major constraint on superyacht charters in US waters
  • OFAC sanctions compliance — all US placements subject to OFAC screening
  • Florida: major superyacht refit and homeport state; state sales tax on vessels
  • Homeowner's policy endorsement common for small craft; Lloyd's for high-value
🇫🇯🇻🇺

Pacific Islands (Fiji / Vanuatu)

Bluewater cruising destination; growing charter market. Remote location creates distinct underwriting challenges.

  • Worldwide navigation limit required — standard territorial limits insufficient
  • Cyclone season November–April: tropical storm exposure
  • Limited SAR infrastructure — self-rescue capability important
  • Vanuatu flag popular for Pacific-based charter vessels — light-touch regulation
  • Piracy risk: historically low compared to Indian Ocean / West Africa
🇦🇪

Middle East / Red Sea / Dubai

Growing superyacht homeport (Dubai Marina, Abu Dhabi). Red Sea transit presents significant security risk.

  • Dubai Maritime City Authority (DMCA) regulations for UAE-based vessels
  • Gulf of Aden / Red Sea: war/piracy exclusion zone — specific endorsement required
  • Houthi USV/drone threat 2024: surface drone attacks on commercial and private vessels
  • No Jones Act equivalent — charter market open to foreign-flagged vessels
  • OFAC / OFSI sanctions: Middle East connections require enhanced UBO due diligence

Sanctions — Russian Beneficial Owner Checks (Post-2022): Since February 2022, all Lloyd's yacht placements (particularly superyachts) require rigorous beneficial ownership due diligence. OFAC (US), OFSI (UK), and EU sanctions lists must be screened against all ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) with 25%+ interest in the vessel-owning entity. Non-disclosure of a sanctioned UBO constitutes a material non-disclosure under the Insurance Act 2015 and can void the policy from inception. Brokers have enhanced KYC obligations; Lloyd's syndicates should request beneficial ownership certification for any vessel with connections to Russian, Belarusian, or other sanctioned-jurisdiction persons. Complex ownership structures (BVI holding companies, Cayman trusts, Panama SPVs) require enhanced due diligence to identify the ultimate economic beneficiary. VER
