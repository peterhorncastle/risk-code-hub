Lloyd's Risk Code — O (Yachts)

O — Yachts (Pleasure Craft)

Lloyd's risk code O covers pleasure craft of all types: sailing yachts, motor yachts, motor cruisers, racing yachts, and superyachts/megayachts. The code encompasses vessels used for leisure and recreational purposes, and commercially crewed charter vessels. Standard H&M cover is written on an agreed value basis, meaning the vessel value is agreed at inception and paid in full on total loss without depreciation dispute. EST

Market Segmentation by Vessel Size

The Lloyd's O market is broadly segmented by vessel length: Small craft (under 8m) are commonly excluded from Lloyd's books or handled by mass-market insurers; Standard yachts (8–24m) form the core Lloyd's private client and broker market; Superyacht (24–60m+) is the specialist high-value segment where Lloyd's syndicates dominate. Megayachts (80m+) are the bespoke ultra-high-net-worth segment, often valued at £50–500m+. VER

Agreed Value vs Market Value

Yacht policies almost universally use agreed value rather than market/actual cash value (ACV). The agreed value is established at policy inception based on survey, comparable sales, and condition — it removes post-loss valuation disputes that plagued early yacht insurance. For superyachts, agreed value may be reviewed annually as values change significantly. Some superyachts have appreciated substantially, making undervaluation a risk for insurers as well as owners. EST

Coverage Taxonomy

Hull & Machinery (H&M)

Core physical damage cover for the vessel, machinery, and equipment on an agreed value basis. Covers sinking, fire, grounding, collision, storm damage, and accidental damage perils. Machinery breakdown (separate from collision damage) may be included or excluded. Total loss (constructive or actual) triggers payment of full agreed value. EST

Third Party Liability

P&I-style cover for pleasure craft — liability to third parties for bodily injury, property damage, and environmental pollution arising from yacht ownership/operation. Limits typically range from £1m (small craft) to £50m+ (superyacht). Legally required in some jurisdictions (e.g., France, Italy for vessels over certain length). VER

Personal Accident

Cover for accidental death, permanent disability, and medical expenses for crew and passengers. Commercially crewed superyachts have MLC 2006 obligations for crew welfare. Owner-operated yachts often include PA as an add-on to the yacht policy or rely on personal travel insurance — a coverage gap at commercial/private boundary. VER

Racing Cover

Additional perils endorsement covering racing incidents — collisions with competing vessels, jury rig damage, righting costs for knockdowns, salvage during racing. Typically excluded under standard cruising cover and separately rated. Racing cover is essential for ocean racers (Fastnet, Sydney-Hobart, Jules Verne Trophy contenders). EST

Salvage & Wreck Removal

Covers the costs of salving a distressed vessel and, critically, wreck removal obligations imposed by port or coastal authorities. Wreck removal liability can exceed the vessel's agreed value — particularly for superyachts grounding in environmentally sensitive or busy waterways. Some syndicates sublimit wreck removal; others offer full cover. VER

Charter Liability

Enhanced liability and H&M cover when the vessel is chartered to third parties (commercial bareboat or crewed charter). MCA commercial code compliance typically required; charter use changes the risk profile significantly — increased utilisation, less experienced charterers, higher wear exposure. A separate commercial charter endorsement or policy is usually required. VER

Navigation Limits — Key Underwriting Variable
Navigation limits define where a yacht may operate and are the single most important geographic rating variable in yacht underwriting. A breach of navigation limits — sailing outside the agreed area without prior endorsement — can void cover entirely. The standard navigation limit categories used by Lloyd's yacht syndicates are:

Coastal Waters: Within territorial limits (typically 12nm from shore) — lowest risk, suitable for inland and sheltered sailing.
Territorial Limits: Within a defined geographic area (e.g., UK coastal, Western Mediterranean) — standard for seasonal Mediterranean yachts.
Offshore: Extended to national EEZ limits or defined ocean areas — step up from coastal, covers offshore racing.
Bluewater / Worldwide: Unrestricted worldwide navigation, including ocean crossings — highest risk rating, piracy zone exclusions may apply (Gulf of Aden, Gulf of Guinea, Somali Basin).

Seasonal endorsements (e.g., Caribbean season Oct–Apr, Mediterranean season Apr–Oct) are common — vessels hauled out or in lay-up attract reduced rates. EST

Navigation Limit Breach — Coverage Voiding Risk: Sailing outside agreed navigation limits without a prior endorsement is a common cause of coverage disputes. Yacht owners venturing into the Mediterranean under a UK Coastal limit, or crossing the Atlantic under a North Sea limit, may find their hull cover voided on a claim. Underwriters should ensure insured understands navigation limits explicitly; brokers should obtain seasonal or extended navigation endorsements in advance of planned passages. Piracy exclusion zones (Gulf of Aden, Gulf of Guinea) require specific war and piracy endorsements even where worldwide navigation is granted. VER
