PREVENT Controls
Prevent

Pre-Passage Weather Routing Service

Professional weather routing services (PredictWind, MetBob, Commanders' Weather) provide passage-specific forecasts with go/no-go advice. For ocean crossings and offshore races, routing services are highly cost-effective compared to the risk of encountering severe weather. Some Lloyd's syndicates offer premium credits for vessels subscribing to professional routing services on bluewater passages. [I9]

Prevent

Hurricane Preparedness Plan

Vessels permanently based or regularly operating in Atlantic/Pacific hurricane zones (June–November) should have a written hurricane preparedness plan: storm track monitoring protocols, haul-out or designated hurricane hole procedures, decision timelines (72-hour window for haul-out), canvas removal, and shore contact designation. Written plans are increasingly an underwriting condition for Caribbean-based fleets. [I6]

Prevent

Engine Room Fire Suppression

Fixed engine room fire suppression systems — CO2, FM-200, or Novec 1230 — are the most effective countermeasure to the leading motor yacht total loss peril. Systems must be maintained annually, discharge nozzles checked for obstructions, and the suppression agent quantity verified against engine room volume. CO2 systems for superyachts should comply with IMO/SOLAS standards for the suppression agent quantity. [L5]

Prevent

Bilge Pump Monitoring

Automatic bilge pump systems with cycle counter monitoring can provide early warning of hull water ingress before it becomes a flooding emergency. High-capacity electric bilge pumps with automatic float switches and remote alarm (linked to a monitoring system or helm alarm) should be installed. Vessels left unattended at moorings — particularly motor yachts — are most vulnerable to undetected flooding. [I9]

Prevent

Crew Safety Briefing Protocol

A formal safety briefing for all crew and guests at the start of each passage — covering liferaft stowage, EPIRB activation, MOB procedure, flare locations, fire extinguishers, and emergency stops — is standard practice on commercial yachts and should be adopted by private owners. Written briefing records demonstrate due diligence and support liability defence if required. [L2]

Prevent

Maintenance Plan Adherence

A documented maintenance schedule — engine servicing intervals, standing rigging replacement intervals (10–15 years for stainless rod rigging), running rigging inspection, stem fitting inspection, and through-hull valve servicing — prevents the gradual deterioration that causes catastrophic failures. Underwriters increasingly require evidence of maintenance records for superyachts and high-value coverage renewals. [I9]

DETECT Controls
Detect

AIS / GPS Tracking (24/7 Position Monitoring)

Automatic Identification System (AIS Class B) transponders enable continuous vessel tracking. For superyachts and charter fleets, shore-based tracking systems (Maxsea, FleetBroadband) provide real-time position data, speed, and course — enabling rapid response if vessel deviates unexpectedly. AIS is also the primary means for traffic separation and collision avoidance, reducing collision frequency. [L5]

Detect

Engine Room Temperature / CO Alarms

Engine room heat sensors (thermal detection at defined temperature thresholds) and CO gas detectors provide early fire/incomplete combustion warning before a full fire develops. Infrared camera systems for continuous engine room monitoring are increasingly common on motor superyachts — they detect heat anomalies (exhaust leaks, bearing overheating) before they become fire initiators. [I9]

Detect

EPIRB / PLB Registration & Testing

406MHz EPIRBs registered to the vessel on the COSPAS-SARSAT database are the primary distress alerting tool for vessels in extremis. EPIRBs must be tested monthly (self-test function) and hydrostatic release serviced every 2 years. Crew Personal Locator Beacons (PLBs) — registered to individuals — provide man-overboard detection capability and should be carried by all crew offshore. [L5]

Detect

Satellite Communications — Bluewater Safety Net

For bluewater passages beyond VHF range, satellite communication systems (Iridium SBD, Garmin inReach, Fleet One) provide position reporting and emergency messaging capability. Regular scheduled position reports to a shore contact (every 24 hours minimum on ocean passages) ensure rapid SAR activation if contact is lost. Some syndicates require sat-comms for bluewater navigation limit endorsements. [I3]

Hurricane Preparedness Checklist (Atlantic / Pacific Zone Vessels)

8-Step Hurricane Action Protocol (72-Hour Window)

  1. Storm Track Monitoring (72-hour window): Subscribe to NHC/NWS tropical weather advisories. Begin monitoring when a tropical system is within 72 hours of potential impact on your operating area. Decision point for haul-out or departure must be made no later than 48 hours before projected arrival.
  2. Haul-Out or Hurricane Hole Decision: If a Category 2+ storm is forecast to pass within 100nm, strongly favour haul-out. Hurricane holes (well-sheltered anchorages such as Simpson Bay lagoon interior, Nanny Cay, English Harbour) provide storm protection for vessels that cannot be hauled. Notify marina/boatyard immediately to secure a haul-out slot — availability disappears rapidly as storm tracks firm up.
  3. Remove Canvas, Sails, and Windage: Strip all bimini covers, Sunbrella canopies, sail covers, and dodger panels before the storm. Sails should be removed and stowed below. Minimising windage dramatically reduces the load on mooring lines and cleats.
  4. Secure Lines and Add Extra Mooring: Use chafe protection on all dock lines. Double up lines at least 48 hours before storm arrival. For vessels remaining afloat, bow, stern, and spring lines should be led to shore anchors or heavy dock cleats with maximum catenary to absorb surge.
  5. Engine Flushed with Fresh Water / Fuel topped up: Top up the fuel tank (departure may be necessary at short notice). Flush raw-water cooling systems on outboards with fresh water. Battery banks fully charged to power bilge pumps throughout the event.
  6. Battery Isolation / Electrical Systems: Isolate non-essential electrical systems to preserve battery power for bilge pumps. Check shore power connections are waterproof. Disconnect antenna cables to avoid lightning strike propagation through electronics.
  7. EPIRB Activation Check & Safety Equipment: Verify EPIRB is set to "armed" (not manual only). Liferaft in accessible location if needed for emergency. VHF on Channel 16 monitoring. Flares in accessible waterproof bag. Personal PLBs with crew.
  8. Shore Contact & Notify Underwriters: Designate a shore contact who knows the vessel's position and will initiate SAR if contact is lost. Notify underwriters or broker if vessel is displaced from its agreed navigation limits or home port during the storm event — policy warranty conditions may require prior notification. [I6]
Chartering Checklist — When Renting the Vessel to Third Parties

Commercial Charter — Pre-Season Insurance & Compliance Review

  • MCA Commercial Code Compliance: Confirm the vessel holds a current MCA Certificate of Compliance (or equivalent flag state commercial code certificate) for the operating area and number of passengers. LY3 / MCA Category (0/1/2/3) must match the intended charter operating area. Annual renewal of compliance certificate required. [L2]
  • Charter Endorsement Active: Confirm the Lloyd's yacht policy has a commercial charter endorsement in force before commencement of any charter. Chartering without endorsement voids the H&M cover — the standard yacht policy excludes commercial charter use. Obtain endorsement confirmation in writing from the underwriter/broker.
  • Charter Agreement Insurance Clause Review: The charter agreement should include a clause requiring charterers to hold adequate personal liability insurance; specify the owner's insurer; and contain indemnity language protecting the owner from charterer-caused losses that fall below the policy deductible or are otherwise excluded. Legal review recommended.
  • Passenger Liability Confirmation: Verify the policy's third-party liability limit adequately covers the maximum number of passengers carried commercially. Most jurisdictions require minimum liability limits for passenger-carrying vessels (e.g., EU Reg 392/2009 for maritime passenger liability). [L4]
  • Crew Certification Check: All crew operating commercially must hold appropriate STCW certification for their role and vessel GT/length. Master's OC/Officer of the Watch certificate — relevant for vessel size. Commercially operating without certified crew voids MCA commercial certificate and may void the insurance. [L3]
  • Greek Charter Licensing (if applicable): Charter operations in Greek waters require a HNOA (Hellenic Naval Officers' Association) professional skipper if the owner is not licensed, and the vessel must have a Greek charter licence (EOET). Requirements under Greek Presidential Decree 284/2000 are strictly enforced. [L6]
7 Top Risk Mitigation Actions for Insureds
01

Keep Navigation Limits Current

Obtain prior endorsement for any passage outside agreed navigation limits. Never assume existing cover extends to unendorsed areas. [I3]

02

Annual Agreed Value Review

Review agreed value annually against current rebuild estimates — especially post-COVID, where costs have risen 30–40%. Underinsurance costs fall on the insured if agreed value is below rebuild cost. [I2]

03

Maintain Survey Currency

Ensure Lloyd's survey (condition) is current. Lapsed survey warranty can void hull cover for structural claims — particularly relevant for GRP vessels over 15 years. [I10]

04

Hurricane Plan & Haul-Out Decision

Have a written hurricane plan before the June season starts. Pre-book haul-out slots in hurricane-prone areas — availability disappears when a storm tracks toward the marina. [I6]

05

Test & Register EPIRB

406MHz EPIRB registered and tested. Hydrostatic release serviced per manufacturer schedule. Crew PLBs carried on offshore passages. [L5]

06

Disclose Charter Use in Advance

Obtain charter endorsement before any commercial charter commences. Chartering without endorsement is one of the most common causes of total coverage void on yacht claims. [L2]

07

Disclose Beneficial Ownership Accurately

Post-2022, Lloyd's beneficial ownership checks for superyachts are rigorous. Non-disclosure of sanctioned UBOs voids the policy and may expose owners and brokers to regulatory liability. [L7]

Engine Room Fire — Leading Superyacht Total Loss Cause: Engine room fires in motor yachts and superyachts are the single most common cause of total or constructive total loss in the superyacht segment. They typically originate from fuel system failures (perished fuel hose, fuel filter leaks onto hot exhaust manifolds), generator faults, or electrical short circuits. Once an engine room fire takes hold in a GRP/composite vessel, it is extremely difficult to extinguish and spreads rapidly to accommodation. Prevention — fixed suppression systems, regular fuel hose inspections, infrared engine room monitoring — is far more effective than detection. [I9]
