Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

LE — Legal Expenses Insurance

Lloyd's risk code LE covers legal expenses insurance (LEI) — both before-the-event (BTE) and after-the-event (ATE) — for individuals, SMEs and corporates. Policies pay solicitors' fees, barristers' fees, court fees and expert witness fees when the insured is involved in a legal dispute, whether pursuing a claim or defending one. Also covers costs and disbursements insurance for litigation funders. VER

BTE vs ATE — The Fundamental Divide

Before-the-event (BTE) LEI is purchased before a dispute arises, typically as a standard add-on to motor, home or commercial package policies. It is a mass-market product with low individual premiums. After-the-event (ATE) LEI is purchased after a specific dispute has arisen, to cover adverse costs risk (losing party paying the winner's legal costs). The ATE market was fundamentally reshaped by LASPO 2012. VER

Key Distinction from Liability Insurance

Legal expenses insurance is fundamentally different from liability insurance. Liability insurance pays the other party's claim against the insured. Legal expenses insurance pays the insured's own legal costs — either to pursue a claim or to defend against one. LEI enables access to justice without the insured bearing legal costs directly. Commercial LEI covers employment disputes, contract disputes, tax investigations, GDPR disputes and regulatory investigations. VER

Coverage Taxonomy

BTE — Pursuit (Claimant Costs)

Costs of the insured bringing a claim: solicitors' and barristers' fees, court fees, expert witness fees on the claimant side. Most common in employment disputes (unfair dismissal), consumer disputes and personal injury claims. BTE panels provide approved solicitors to manage costs. VER

BTE — Defence (Respondent Costs)

Costs of the insured defending a claim brought against them: solicitors' fees for defending employment tribunal, contract or regulatory proceedings. Important for SME employers defending unfair dismissal, discrimination or redundancy claims. Often bundled with commercial package policies as standard. VER

ATE — Adverse Costs (After-the-Event)

Purchased after a specific dispute arises. Covers the risk that the insured loses and must pay the winning party's legal costs (adverse costs order). Post-LASPO 2012, ATE premiums are no longer recoverable from the losing party — the insured or funder must bear this cost directly. Critical for commercial litigation and clinical negligence. VER

Employment Disputes

The highest-frequency commercial LEI trigger. Covers unfair dismissal, wrongful dismissal, discrimination, redundancy, TUPE, equal pay and whistleblower claims. ACAS early conciliation now mandatory pre-tribunal. VER Employment tribunal claims surged post-COVID — redundancy, furlough disputes and flexible working disputes all generated significant LEI activity. VER

Tax Investigation (HMRC Enquiry)

Covers solicitors' and accountants' fees for responding to HMRC investigations and enquiries: VAT investigations, corporation tax enquiries, PAYE compliance checks, IR35 status disputes. A significant commercial LEI sub-market — specialist tax investigation insurance is available as a standalone product or bundled with commercial LEI. EST

Contract Disputes & Property

Covers costs of commercial contract disputes (breach of contract, debt recovery above small claims threshold), property disputes (boundary disputes, lease terms, landlord/tenant), GDPR/data protection disputes and regulatory investigations. GDPR data breach disputes are the fastest-growing commercial LEI trigger since 2018. VER

Access to Justice & the Jackson Reforms: Legal expenses insurance is politically and socially significant because it enables individuals and SMEs to pursue legal claims they could not otherwise afford. Lord Justice Jackson's Review of Civil Litigation Costs (2010) VER transformed the UK market. The resulting LASPO 2012 reforms made success fees and ATE premiums non-recoverable from the losing party — damaging the claimant funding model. However, Qualified One-Way Cost Shifting (QOCS) for personal injury partially restores balance, protecting individual claimants from adverse costs orders. The tension between access to justice and controlling litigation costs remains a defining dynamic of the LE market. EST
LASPO 2012 — Fundamental Market Discontinuity: The Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 (in force April 2013 for costs provisions) made ATE insurance premiums and conditional fee agreement success fees non-recoverable from the losing party. VER This eliminated the pre-LASPO ATE market model where the losing defendant paid the claimant's ATE premium. Post-LASPO, ATE premiums must be funded by the claimant or litigation funder — fundamentally changing pricing, coverage structure and market participants. The personal injury ATE market collapsed in volume; the clinical negligence and commercial ATE markets adapted at higher premium rates.
