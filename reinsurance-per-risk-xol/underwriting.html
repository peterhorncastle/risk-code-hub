Per-Risk XoL — Underwriting & Pricing

Per-risk layers are priced two ways and reconciled: burning cost from the cedant's own large-loss history, and exposure rating from an exposure curve when the history is too thin to trust.

A per-risk layer has no proportional cession to fall back on — its price is the expected cost of losses that pierce it, loaded for risk and expenses. Two methods estimate that expected cost, and a good underwriter reconciles them. VER

The Burning-Cost Formula

Burning cost = Trended historical losses to the layer ÷ Subject premium

Technical rate = Burning cost × (1 + risk load) ÷ (1 − expenses − brokerage)

Layer premium = Technical rate × Subject premium

Rate-on-line = Layer premium ÷ Limit  ·  Payback = 1 ÷ Rate-on-line

The Two Pricing Methods

Burning cost (experience)

Take the cedant's actual losses to the layer over (say) 5–10 years, trend them to today for inflation and development, average them and divide by subject premium. Reliable for low working layers hit often; unreliable for high layers with few or no losses.

Exposure rating

Use an exposure curve — a function giving the share of each risk's value that falls in the layer — applied to the risk profile (the distribution of sums insured). Essential for high layers where the history is silent but the exposure is real.

Reconciliation

Blend the two: weight burning cost where data is credible, exposure rating where it is not. Divergence between them is a signal to investigate the risk profile or the loss history.

Exposure Curves — The Idea

An exposure curve maps a retention (as a fraction of the sum insured) to the fraction of the expected loss that sits below it. Subtracting gives the share in the layer. Curves such as the Swiss Re / Lloyd's property curves and the MBBEFD family encode how severe losses tend to be relative to the sum insured — steep curves mean losses are usually small, flat curves mean total losses are common. Riebesell's rule of thumb (doubling the limit adds a fixed percentage to the price) is the old shorthand for the same shape. EST

Worked Example — A Property Working Layer

Setup: £4m xs £1m per risk; subject premium £50m; average annual losses to the layer £1.0m; trend factor 1.15; risk load 30%; expenses 5%; brokerage 10%. ILLUS

  • Trended losses to layer = £1.0m × 1.15 = £1.15m
  • Burning cost = £1.15m ÷ £50m = 2.30% of subject premium
  • Loaded = 2.30% × 1.30 = 2.99%; ÷ (1 − 0.05 − 0.10 = 0.85) = 3.52% technical rate
  • Layer premium = 3.52% × £50m ≈ £1.76m
  • Rate-on-line = £1.76m ÷ £4m = 44%; payback ≈ 2.3 years
  • Margin over trended losses ≈ £0.61m — the layer's earnings for capital and volatility.

Pre-Bind Checklist

Confirm the layer — limit, attachment and whether it is each-and-every-loss or aggregate.
Trend and develop the historical losses properly before computing burning cost.
Build an exposure-rated view from the risk profile and reconcile it with burning cost.
Agree the number and cost of reinstatements.
For long-tail casualty, agree the indexation / stability clause.
Check for any single-risk accumulation the layer would double-count against the cat programme.
