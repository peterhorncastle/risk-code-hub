Per-Risk XoL — Contract & Wordings

A per-risk layer's terms decide exactly which losses reach it and how often: the each-and-every-loss basis, the reinstatements, the indexation clause and the definition of "one risk".

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Because a per-risk layer is non-proportional, its wording is its cover. Six clauses do most of the work of defining what pierces the layer and what the reinsurer pays. VER

Each-and-every-loss (EEL)

The layer responds to each individual loss separately against its own attachment — the defining basis of per-risk XoL, distinct from an aggregate.

Definition of "one risk"

What counts as a single risk (a location, a policy, an insured interest) determines whether losses combine or stay separate — the most negotiated wording in the treaty.

Reinstatements

Restore the limit after a loss for a reinstatement premium; the number and cost cap how many large losses a year the layer will answer.

Annual aggregate deductible (AAD)

An aggregate retention the cedant absorbs before the each-and-every layer engages — trims attritional frequency out of the layer.

Indexation / stability clause

On long-tail casualty, moves the attachment and limit with an index so the real retention holds as inflation develops the claim.

Hours / occurrence limits

Where a per-risk treaty can catch accumulation, an occurrence cap stops many risks from one event overwhelming the layer.

Counterparty & Programme Hygiene

Before binding

  • Check reinsurer security rating against your minimum.
  • Confirm the definition of "one risk" and how it interacts with the cat programme to avoid gaps or double-counting.
  • Agree reinstatements, any AAD and the indexation basis for long-tail layers.

During the period

  • Monitor large losses against attachments and remaining reinstatements.
  • Trend and develop losses for the next renewal's burning cost.
  • Watch for single-risk losses that also touch the cat tower.

⚠️ The "one risk" trap

The definition of a single risk decides whether two damaged buildings, or a chain of connected policies, combine into one loss or stay separate. Get it wrong and the cedant either fails to recover an intended loss or exposes the reinsurer to unpriced accumulation. Confirm the definition — and how it meshes with the catastrophe occurrence definition — before binding.
