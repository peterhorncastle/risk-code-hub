Module 02 — History

Per-Risk XoL — History

Non-proportional reinsurance grew up alongside proportional treaties and overtook them for large-loss protection as pricing science matured.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Excess of loss began as a way to protect a net line without ceding away premium, and became the dominant form of large-loss reinsurance as burning-cost analysis and exposure curves let underwriters price a layer they might never see a loss in. VER

Era 1 — Early non-proportional cover (1880s–1930s)

As sums insured grew, cedants wanted protection against a single catastrophic loss without the cost of a full proportional cession. Early excess-of-loss and "excess" treaties emerged at Lloyd's and among the professional reinsurers to cap the large-loss tail. EST

Era 2 — Burning cost formalised (1930s–1960s)

The burning-cost method — trended layer losses over subject premium — became the standard way to price working layers. It suited the frequently-hit low layers of property and marine books where enough losses existed to average. EST

Era 3 — Exposure curves & pricing science (1960s–1990s)

Exposure curves (Swiss Re, Lloyd's, later the MBBEFD family) let underwriters price high layers where the history was silent, using the distribution of sums insured. Riebesell's rule gave a practical shorthand. Per-risk XoL displaced surplus for much large-loss protection. VER

Era 4 — Casualty, indexation & the long tail (1980s–2000s)

Applied to long-tail casualty, per-risk XoL needed the indexation (stability) clause to keep the real retention constant as inflation eroded it over years of development. Asbestos and other latent claims tested the model and its wordings. VER

Era 5 — Modern working-layer discipline (2000s–present)

Per-risk XoL is now the standard working-layer protection beneath the catastrophe tower. The 2023 hard market pushed attachments up and rates-on-line higher across risk-XL as well as cat, before the 2025–26 softening began to ease terms. EST

Key Ideas That Endure

Burning cost

Trended layer losses over subject premium — still the first number every underwriter computes for a working layer.

Exposure curves

The tool that lets a high layer be priced with no losses in the history.

Indexation

The clause that keeps a long-tail retention real as inflation works through years of development.
