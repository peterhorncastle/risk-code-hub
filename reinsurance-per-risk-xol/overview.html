Per-Risk Excess of Loss — Overview

Non-proportional reinsurance that caps the cost of any single large risk. The cedant keeps a per-risk retention and the reinsurer pays the excess above it, up to a limit — protecting the net account from one big fire, energy or liability loss without ceding away premium on the whole book.

Per-risk excess of loss (also "risk XL" or working excess) attaches to a single risk, not a single event. Where proportional reinsurance shares every pound of premium and loss, per-risk XoL is non-proportional: the reinsurer pays nothing until a single risk's loss pierces the attachment, then pays the excess up to the limit. It is the classic protection for the large-loss tail of a property, energy, marine or casualty book. VER

How a Per-Risk Layer Works

Attachment (retention)

The per-risk amount the cedant keeps net on any one risk — e.g. £1m. Losses below it never reach the reinsurer.

Limit

The width of the layer above the attachment — £4m xs £1m pays up to £4m once a single loss exceeds £1m.

Each-and-every-loss

Per-risk XoL responds to each individual loss separately, unlike an aggregate that sums a year of losses.

Working vs higher layers

Low "working" layers are hit often and priced on experience; high layers are rare and priced on exposure curves.

Reinstatements

After a loss the limit is restored for a reinstatement premium, so the layer can respond to more than one large loss a year.

Indexation / stability clause

On long-tail casualty layers, indexation moves the attachment and limit with inflation so the real retention holds over time.

Market Participants

Cedants

Property, energy, marine and casualty insurers protecting the net line against single large losses.

Reinsurers

Treaty reinsurers writing working and higher risk-XL layers; the same panels that write cat XoL.

Brokers

Treaty brokers structuring the retention, limit and reinstatements and marketing the placement.

Actuaries / pricing

Burning-cost analysis and exposure-curve rating; the two methods that price the layer.
