Per-Risk XoL — Programme Structuring

The per-risk tower protects the net line against single large losses; the catastrophe tower sits alongside it for accumulation. Together they cap both severity per risk and severity per event.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

A property reinsurance programme usually runs two towers side by side: a per-risk tower that caps any single large loss, and a catastrophe tower that caps any single large event. Below both may sit proportional cover; above both, facultative. The per-risk tower is built in layers from a working layer up. VER

The Per-Risk Tower

LayerRole
Net retentionWhat the cedant keeps on any one risk below the working layer
Working layer (e.g. £4m xs £1m)Hit most years; priced on burning cost; earns a steady margin over frequent losses
Middle layers (e.g. £10m xs £10m)Hit occasionally; burning cost and exposure rating both matter
High layers (e.g. £50m xs £40m)Rarely hit; exposure-rated; genuine large-single-risk tail protection
FacultativeFills the top of any individual risk beyond the treaty's total capacity

How Per-Risk and Cat Fit Together

Two different triggers

Per-risk answers one big loss on one risk; cat answers one event across many risks. A large factory fire is a per-risk loss; a windstorm hitting a thousand homes is a cat loss.

Avoiding gaps and overlaps

The definitions of "one risk" and "one occurrence" must mesh so a loss is covered once, in the right tower, without double recovery or an unintended gap.

Net line to the cat tower

The per-risk tower first reduces single-risk severity; the cat tower then protects what remains of the net account against accumulation.

The Placement Workflow

1

Exposure & loss submission

The cedant and broker present the risk profile (distribution of sums insured) and the large-loss history.

2

Layer design

Retention, working-layer attachment, higher layers and total capacity are set against the risk profile.

3

Pricing & terms

Burning cost and exposure rating price each layer; reinstatements, AAD and indexation are agreed.

4

Marketing & subscription

The broker builds a reinsurer panel across the layers and signs down.

5

In-force & renewal

Monitor piercings and reinstatements; re-trend the loss history for the next renewal.

Case Study — A Regional Property Insurer

A property insurer with a £50m subject premium wants to cap its exposure to any single large fire while keeping premium on the bulk of its book.

The problem

A single large industrial fire could cost several million and swamp the net result; proportional cover would cede away premium on thousands of small, well-priced risks.

The structure

A per-risk tower — £4m xs £1m working layer, then higher layers to £90m — caps single-risk severity; a separate cat tower protects against windstorm and flood accumulation.

The terms

Two reinstatements on the working layer, an AAD to trim attritional frequency, and a tight "one risk" definition meshed with the cat occurrence wording.

The result

The insurer keeps its premium and its profitable small risks, and caps the cost of any single large loss to its £1m retention plus reinstatement premiums.
