Stop-Loss — Programme Structuring

The stop-loss sits at the very top of the programme — the backstop over everything else. It catches what the per-risk and catastrophe towers leave in the net result.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Where per-risk and catastrophe covers protect against defined kinds of loss, the stop-loss protects the net result itself. It sits above the whole programme and answers a bad year that survives everything below — frequency, attrition and any accumulation the specific covers did not catch. VER

Where Stop-Loss Sits

Order of operationsWhat it does
1 · Gross bookThe cedant's full written portfolio
2 · Proportional (QS / surplus)Shares premium and losses; relieves capital before the non-proportional covers
3 · Per-risk XoLCaps single large losses on the net line
4 · Catastrophe XoLCaps single large events on the net line
5 · Net resultWhat remains of the whole-year loss ratio after all the above
6 · Stop-loss (backstop)Caps that net whole-year loss ratio above the attachment

Why It Is Bought Last

Avoiding duplication

A stop-loss should sit on the net result after the specific covers, so the cedant does not pay twice for the same protection.

Catching the residual

Frequency of mid-size losses, attrition and any gap the towers leave all flow into the net loss ratio the stop-loss backstops.

Capital efficiency

Pricing the towers first, then a stop-loss on what remains, is usually cheaper than a low stop-loss doing all the work alone.

The Placement Workflow

1

Net-result submission

The cedant presents its whole-account loss-ratio history net of the covers below the stop-loss.

2

Aggregate modelling

The reinsurer builds the net loss-ratio distribution and sets a candidate attachment and exhaustion.

3

Alignment & terms

Co-participation, cap, loss-ratio definition and risk-transfer test are agreed.

4

Marketing & subscription

The broker places the layer, often with a small panel given its tail nature.

5

In-force & renewal

Track the developing net loss ratio; re-model the aggregate for renewal.

Case Study — A Mutual Entering a New Class

A mutual insurer with a modest balance sheet launches a new specialty class and cannot absorb a bad first year.

The problem

With no loss history for the new class, a single poor year could threaten the mutual's capital — but a full quota share would cede away most of the upside.

The structure

A stop-loss at 90%–130% loss ratio on the new class caps the worst-case first-year result while the mutual keeps the normal-year economics.

The terms

10% co-participation, a firm annual cap, a clear accident-year loss-ratio definition and a risk-transfer test to satisfy the auditors.

The result

The mutual can enter the class knowing its downside is capped, and buys down the stop-loss as the book builds its own credible history.
