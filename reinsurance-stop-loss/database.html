Stop-Loss — Loss Database

A stop-loss is pierced not by one loss but by a bad year. These are the kinds of year that push a whole account's loss ratio into a stop-loss layer.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Figures are indicative for training. What matters for a stop-loss is not a single headline loss but how a whole year's losses accumulate into the aggregate loss ratio — from any mix of frequency, attrition and catastrophe. EST

Bad-year typeWhat happensWhy it matters for stop-loss
Drought / crop failureA season's weather ruins a large share of an agricultural bookThe classic whole-account stop-loss trigger — one season, one loss ratio VER
Multiple mid-size cats in one yearSeveral events, none individually huge, sum to a heavy yearFrequency the per-event cat cover may not fully catch — the stop-loss does VER
Attritional deteriorationLoss creep across the book lifts the base loss ratioNo single loss, but the aggregate breaches the attachment EST
Systemic event (COVID-19)One systemic shock hits many policies across a whole accountAggregate loss ratios pushed to extremes on affected lines VER
New-line mispricingAn unproven class prices badly in its first yearExactly the worst-case a new-line stop-loss is bought to cap EST
Reserve strengtheningPrior-year deterioration recognised in one calendar yearCan push a calendar-year aggregate into the layer — define the trigger carefully EST
Reading the database A stop-loss is the only structure that answers all of these at once — that breadth is its value and its danger. The Underwriting module explains why it must be priced off the whole aggregate distribution, and the Contract & Wordings module explains how co-participation and a clear trigger stop it from simply paying for a cedant's underwriting mistakes.
