Stop-Loss — Contract & Wordings

Because a stop-loss covers the whole account, its wording is mostly about alignment — the co-participation, the trigger definition and the cap that stop it from simply paying for a cedant's bad underwriting.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

A stop-loss creates moral hazard by design — once a bad year is already breaching the layer, the cedant's marginal loss is the reinsurer's. Six features keep the cedant's incentives aligned and the trigger clean. VER

Co-participation

The cedant retains a share (e.g. 5–10%) of losses within the layer, so it always keeps skin in the game even after the attachment is breached.

Attachment above expected

Setting the attachment loss ratio clearly above the expected loss ratio means the cover answers genuinely bad years, not normal variation.

Annual aggregate limit (cap)

The exhaustion point caps the reinsurer's total payout — a stop-loss is always finite, never open-ended.

Loss-ratio definition & trigger

Exactly which premiums and losses count, and whether the trigger is accident-year, underwriting-year or calendar-year, decides what breaches the layer.

Reserve / IBNR treatment

How incurred-but-not-reported losses and reserve movements enter the loss ratio must be defined to prevent manipulation of the trigger.

Risk-transfer test

The deal must transfer real insurance risk — not merely smooth earnings — to satisfy accounting and regulatory standards.

Counterparty & Programme Hygiene

Before binding

  • Check reinsurer security rating against your minimum.
  • Confirm the loss-ratio definition, accounting year and co-participation precisely.
  • Check the stop-loss does not simply duplicate the per-risk and cat towers beneath it.

During the period

  • Track the developing loss ratio against the attachment through the year.
  • Monitor reserve movements that could move the trigger.
  • Keep underwriting discipline — the co-participation exists precisely for this.

⚠️ The moral-hazard trap

A stop-loss that attaches too low, with too little co-participation, can pay for ordinary underwriting variance and blunt the cedant's incentive to manage the book once the layer is in reach. Attach clearly above expected, keep a meaningful co-participation, and make sure the deal transfers real risk — or it becomes financing dressed as reinsurance.
