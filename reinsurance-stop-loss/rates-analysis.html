Stop-Loss — Rates & ROE

Stop-loss pricing turns on the aggregate loss-ratio distribution: where you attach on that distribution sets how often the layer pays and what it costs.

The Attachment Trade-Off in Words

Low attachment

Frequent, expensive

Attach near the expected loss ratio and the layer pays most years; high rate-on-line, short payback, and real moral-hazard risk. Rarely good value.

Mid attachment

Balanced backstop

Attach a comfortable margin above expected (e.g. 90% when expected is 80%); pays roughly a 1-in-5 to 1-in-10 year — genuine bad-year protection.

High attachment

Rare, cheap tail

Attach well into the tail; pays only in a disaster year; low rate-on-line and long payback for pure catastrophe-of-the-account cover.
