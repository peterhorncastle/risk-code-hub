Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

BC — Commercial Crime / Fidelity

Direct and facultative commercial crime insurance for corporate entities (non-financial institutions). Covers employee dishonesty, forgery, computer fraud, EFT/funds transfer fraud, social engineering/BEC, money & securities, and counterfeit currency. Typically written on a discovery basis with a 60–90 day discovery period post-expiry.

BC vs BB — Key Distinction

BC covers corporate/commercial entities: retailers, manufacturers, professional services firms, government bodies, non-profits. BB (bankers blanket bond) covers financial institutions — banks, investment managers, broker-dealers. BC does not cover investment trading losses, credit risk or market risk perils. ERISA fidelity bond requirements apply to employee benefit plan sponsors regardless of BC/BB categorisation.

Coverage Trigger — Discovery Basis

Commercial crime policies are typically written on a discovery basis: the policy in force at the time the loss is discovered responds, regardless of when the act occurred. Retroactive date provisions and extended discovery periods (60–180 days post-cancellation) are critical negotiating points. Occurrence basis policies exist but are rare and more expensive.

Coverage Taxonomy

🏭 Employee Dishonesty

Theft, embezzlement or fraudulent misappropriation of money, securities or property by an employee. The dominant commercial crime peril — accounts for ~55–65% of all BC claims by frequency. Per-employee or blanket limits available. EST

📄 Forgery & Alteration

Losses from forged or altered cheques, promissory notes, drafts, bills of exchange. Includes incoming cheque fraud, counterfeit instrument acceptance and altered payee/amount fraud. Increasingly relevant as cheque fraud resurges in digital-print era.

💻 Computer & Funds Transfer Fraud

Fraudulent electronic instruction causing a transfer of money or securities via a computer system. Covers both internal (employee-initiated) and external (hacker-directed) EFT fraud. Policy wording boundary with cyber crime cover is a key negotiation point. VER

📧 Social Engineering / BEC

Business Email Compromise (BEC): employee is deceived by a fraudulent email impersonating an executive or vendor, voluntarily transferring funds. Now the highest-frequency high-value commercial crime peril globally. Sublimit typically $100k–$500k in SME policies; up to full limit for large corporates. EST

💰 Money & Securities

Theft, disappearance or destruction of money and securities on or off premises, including robbery, burglary, and mysterious disappearance. Covers cash in transit, safe/vault losses and employee theft of cash. Declining relative importance as cashless transactions increase.

🏛️ ERISA Fidelity Bond

US statutory fidelity bond required under ERISA §412 for any person handling funds of an employee benefit plan. Minimum 10% of plan assets up to $500k ($1m for plans holding employer securities). Separate from the commercial crime policy; must be standalone or rider. EST

Regulatory Classification (UK): Commercial crime / fidelity insurance is classified as a non-life insurance contract under FSMA 2000. It is not a regulated financial product in the same way as investment products. Policies are sold on a B2B basis to commercial entities; FCA Consumer Duty does not apply to commercial lines but fair presentation obligations under the Insurance Act 2015 apply in full. In the US, commercial crime policies are regulated at state level; surplus lines placement through Lloyd's is common for complex/large risks. VER
⚠ BEC / Social Engineering Coverage Gap: Many BC policies include social engineering as a sub-limit (often $100k–$250k) rather than at full policy limit. The Medidata v Federal Insurance 2018 decision confirmed that computer fraud coverage can apply to BEC where a fraudulent email causes a fund transfer via computer systems. However, courts remain split. Underwriters must review policy wording carefully — ISO CR 00 21 "computer fraud" language may or may not respond to BEC without specific social engineering endorsement.
