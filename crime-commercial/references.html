Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Guarantee Society (1840) — First Corporate Fidelity Bond

    Historical reference: the Guarantee Society (est. London 1840) is widely cited as the first corporate fidelity guarantee company, replacing personal surety arrangements. Referenced in: Robert Holtom, Protective Covenants in Commercial Contracts (1920); Lloyd's market historical archives.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL ISO Commercial Crime Policy — CR 00 21

    Insurance Services Office (ISO) standardised Commercial Crime Policy form CR 00 21 and related endorsements. The authoritative standard wording for US commercial crime insurance, covering employee dishonesty, forgery/alteration, computer fraud, funds transfer fraud, money & securities, counterfeit currency, and extended period of indemnification. Discovery basis trigger standard.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL Sarbanes-Oxley Act 2002 (SOX) — §302 and §404

    Pub. L. 107-204, 116 Stat. 745 (2002). Section 302: CEO and CFO personal certification of financial statements and internal controls. Section 404: management assessment and auditor attestation of internal control over financial reporting (ICFR). Section 806: whistleblower protection for employees of publicly traded companies reporting securities fraud.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL ERISA §412 — Fidelity Bond Requirement (US)

    Employee Retirement Income Security Act 1974, 29 U.S.C. §1112. Requires every person who "handles" funds or other property of an employee benefit plan to be bonded. Bond amount: 10% of plan assets handled in preceding year; minimum $1,000; maximum $500,000 ($1,000,000 for plans that hold employer securities). Bond must be from approved surety (US Treasury Circular 570).

  • [L5]
    LEGAL Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (UK) — Failure to Prevent Fraud

    Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, Chapter 56. Part 4, §199: corporate criminal liability for failure to prevent fraud by associated persons. In force: September 2025 (per Home Office commencement order). Applies to "large organisations" (500+ employees, £36m+ turnover, £18m+ balance sheet). Guidance issued by Home Office December 2024. Unlimited fine on conviction.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Insurance Act 2015 (UK) — Fair Presentation of Risk

    Insurance Act 2015 (c. 4). Replaces the Marine Insurance Act 1906 duty of disclosure for non-consumer commercial insurance. Requires fair presentation: every material circumstance the insured knows or ought to know; in a manner reasonably clear and accessible. Proportionate remedies for breach. Applies to all commercial BC placements in the UK market.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL Medidata Solutions v Federal Insurance Co. — 2nd Circuit (2018)

    Medidata Solutions, Inc. v Federal Insurance Co., 729 F. App'x 117 (2d Cir. 2018). Second Circuit holds that computer fraud coverage in Federal Insurance's commercial crime policy responds to a BEC loss where fraudsters spoofed executive email to direct fund transfers via computer systems. Key circuit precedent in favour of BEC coverage.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL American Tooling Center v Travelers Casualty — 6th Circuit (2019)

    American Tooling Center, Inc. v Travelers Casualty and Surety Co., 895 F.3d 455 (6th Cir. 2018). Sixth Circuit holds that Travelers' computer fraud coverage does NOT respond to a BEC loss because the fund transfer was not "directly" caused by fraudulent computer use — human decision-making intervened. Creates circuit split with Medidata (2nd Cir.). Social engineering endorsements now essential to close coverage gap.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL Apache Corp v OneBeacon Insurance — 5th Circuit (2016)

    Apache Corp v OneBeacon Insurance, 662 F. App'x 252 (5th Cir. 2016). Fifth Circuit holds that computer fraud coverage does not respond to a BEC loss where Apache was deceived by email into changing supplier payment details, transferring $7m. Court holds loss was not "directly" caused by computer use — email was "merely incidental" to the fraud. Adverse precedent for BEC coverage under computer fraud agreements.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY Commercial Blanket Bond — Historical Development

    History of fidelity and surety bonding in the United States: Surety Association of America historical publications; Holtom, R., Fidelity and Surety Bonding (Rough Notes, 1975). The Commercial Blanket Bond (CBB) replaced individual schedule bonds in the early 20th century, enabling employers to cover all employees under a single limit.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY EDP / Computer Crime Emergence — 1960s–1970s

    Parker, D.B., Crime by Computer (Scribner, 1976) — seminal early work on computer-enabled fraud. US Department of Justice, Computer Crime: Criminal Justice Resource Manual (1979). Documents the emergence of salami-slicing, payroll manipulation and early EDP fraud exposures that insurance markets were slow to address.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY EFT Fraud Development — 1980s

    Baker, S. and Hurst, P., The Limits of Trust: Cryptography, Governments, and Electronic Commerce (Kluwer, 1998); Federal Reserve Bank of New York research on wire transfer fraud in the 1980s savings and loan crisis period. Documents the emergence of SWIFT and telex fraud and the insurance coverage debate around "telephone fraud" and EFT fraud.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY ACFE Report to the Nations — 2024 Edition

    Association of Certified Fraud Examiners, Report to the Nations: 2024 Global Study on Occupational Fraud and Abuse. ACFE, Austin TX (2024). Published biennially since 2002. Key 2024 findings: median fraud loss $145k (up from $117k in 2022); median duration 12 months; 43% detected by tip; organisations without whistleblower hotlines suffer twice the median loss. Available at acfe.com. The definitive underwriting reference for commercial crime frequency and severity.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) — BEC 2023 Annual Report

    Federal Bureau of Investigation, Internet Crime Complaint Center, 2023 Internet Crime Report. BEC remains the highest financial loss category: $2.9bn in 2023 reported losses across 21,489 complaints. Median BEC loss per incident: $117k. Available at ic3.gov. Note: IC3 data significantly underestimates total BEC losses — most incidents not reported to FBI.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY BEC Coverage Circuit Split — Law Firm Analysis

    Wiley Rein LLP, The BEC Insurance Coverage Circuit Split: Where Do We Stand? (2020); Covington & Burling, Social Engineering and Commercial Crime Insurance: Navigating the Coverage Maze (2021). Analyses of Medidata (2d Cir.), Apache (5th Cir.), American Tooling Center (6th Cir.) and their implications for commercial crime policy wording. Essential reading for BC underwriters placing US risks.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY Arup Deepfake Fraud — 2024

    Arup press statement confirming $25m loss from deepfake video call fraud, reported February 2024. Hong Kong authorities confirmed investigation. Covered by: BBC News, Financial Times, Reuters. Represents the first high-profile corporate loss confirmed to involve fully AI-generated video deepfake impersonation. Has triggered market-wide commercial crime policy wording review for AI-enabled fraud coverage adequacy.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's Commercial Crime Market — LMA Working Papers

    Lloyd's Market Association (LMA), Financial Lines Working Group publications on commercial crime and cyber/crime interface. LMA5567: Crime and Cyber interface guidance (2021). Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y5258 on social engineering and computer crime coverage. Syndicate-level data: estimated Lloyd's BC GWP £300–500m (est., not publicly disclosed at sub-class level).

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY CISA / NCSC BEC Guidance — Email Security

    UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), Business Email Compromise: How to Protect Your Organisation (2023). US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), Business Email Compromise (BEC): The $43 Billion Scam (2022). FBI/CISA joint advisory on DMARC, SPF, DKIM email authentication as primary BEC countermeasure. Available at ncsc.gov.uk and cisa.gov.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY US Treasury FinCEN — BEC Suspicious Activity Report Analysis

    Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), Financial Trend Analysis: Business Email Compromise in the United States (2022). Analyses SARs filed by US financial institutions relating to BEC. Documents money mule account networks, cryptocurrency conversion patterns and overseas wire routing. Subrogation intelligence for BC insurers pursuing BEC recovery.
