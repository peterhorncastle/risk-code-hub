Programme Tower — Multinational BC Structure
Reinsurance

Aggregate / XL Reinsurance

Stop-loss or aggregate XL purchased by Lloyd's syndicate. Protects against portfolio accumulation from large BEC events or correlated employee fraud losses. Cyber aggregate interaction managed at this layer.

Lloyd's / Wholesale Market

Global Master Policy — Excess Layer

Lloyd's syndicate provides the global master policy: high-limit excess cover and DIC/DIL wrap for subsidiaries where local admitted cover is inadequate. Typically £5m–£50m excess of local retained limits. Flexibility on social engineering sublimit and AI fraud wording.

Primary Layer

Master Commercial Crime Policy (Admitted)

Primary admitted policy in domicile jurisdiction (typically UK or USA). Covers all insuring agreements: employee dishonesty, forgery, computer fraud, funds transfer fraud, money & securities, ERISA bond (if US). Typically £1m–£10m primary limit per occurrence.

Local Admitted Policies

Local Admitted Cover — Key Subsidiaries

Where local regulations require admitted insurance (e.g. Brazil, China, India, Mexico), locally admitted commercial crime or fidelity policies are issued. Premium remittance and local claims payment compliance managed. DIC/DIL ensures no gap between local cover and master policy.

US ERISA Bond (if applicable)

ERISA Fidelity Bond — Statutory US Requirement

Where a US employee benefit plan exists, ERISA §412 requires a fidelity bond of at least 10% of plan assets (min $1,000, max $500k; $1m for plans holding employer securities). Can be a rider to the BC policy or standalone. Must name the plan as an insured. VER

Self-Retained

Deductible / SIR

Self-insured retention aligned to the insured's risk appetite and cash flow: typically £25k–£250k for mid-market; £500k–£2m for large multinational. Higher SIR improves premium economics and deters attritional claims frequency.

Jurisdiction Profiles

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Market leader — Lloyd's and London company market. Non-admitted Lloyd's placement available. Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation applies. ECCTA 2023 "failure to prevent fraud" offence (in force Sep 2025) increases demand. FCA does not regulate BC as a consumer product. Admitted policy recommended for FCA-regulated firms. VER

🇺🇸 United States

State-regulated; surplus lines through Lloyd's common for complex/large risks. ISO CR 00 21 standard form widely used. ERISA bond mandatory for plan sponsors — must be admitted. Social engineering coverage highly variable by state and form. FBI IC3 annual BEC data drives market awareness. Circuit split (Medidata v Apache) on BEC computer fraud coverage unresolved. VER

🇪🇺 European Union

Commercial crime is not harmonised at EU level — each member state has its own insurance regulatory framework. Passporting allows some cross-border placement. Key markets: Germany, France, Netherlands have active local commercial crime markets. Non-admitted Lloyd's placement requires care — some EU jurisdictions restrict it materially post-Brexit. Local admitted policy recommended for subsidiaries in Germany, France, Italy, Spain.

🇦🇺 Australia

APRA-regulated admitted market. Lloyd's admitted in Australia; can write BC directly. Commercial crime ("crime policy" or "business crime") is a standard product. ASIC oversight of financial services; commercial policyholders have less regulatory protection than consumers. Payroll fraud and AP fraud are the most common BC claims. Cybercrime Act 2001 relevant to computer fraud coverage definitions.

🇯🇵 Japan

FSA-regulated; admitted insurance required. Major Japanese insurers (Tokio Marine, Sompo, Mitsui Sumitomo) offer commercial crime products. Lloyd's operates as a foreign insurer with admitted capacity. Japan Inc.'s "inside fraud" culture (concealment over disclosure) means crime losses are systematically under-reported; long-running frauds (Olympus, Daiwa) are a known systemic risk. BEC fraud growing rapidly in corporate sector.

🌍 Emerging Markets

Brazil, India, China and Mexico require admitted local insurance for crime cover — non-admitted not permitted. Local insurer fronting with Lloyd's reinsurance is the standard structure. Claims recovery mechanisms are weaker; average fraud duration longer; local law enforcement cooperation variable. Premium loading of +25–50% vs developed markets typical. High prevalence of payroll fraud, vendor fraud and cash manipulation in manufacturing/industrial sectors.

Lloyd's Commercial Crime Market: Lloyd's syndicates are particularly active in: (1) high-limit excess layers above admitted primary cover; (2) full-limit social engineering and BEC cover where admitted markets cap sublimits; (3) DIC/DIL global master policies for multinationals; (4) non-standard risks (e.g. senior management fraud risk, known control weaknesses being remediated, post-loss placements). Lloyd's flexibility on coverage terms and its global network of approved coverholders make it the market of choice for complex BC programmes. VER
⚠ ERISA Compliance Alert: ERISA §412 fidelity bond requirements apply to any "plan official" — employee, officer or trustee who handles plan assets. The bond must be maintained in force at all times. The BC policy does not automatically satisfy ERISA requirements — the bond must specifically name the plan or all plans as an insured, and must be from an approved carrier on the US Treasury's approved surety list. Non-compliance exposes the plan sponsor to ERISA civil penalties. Confirm compliance at each renewal. VER
