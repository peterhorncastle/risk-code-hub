Prevention Controls (PREVENT)
Prevent

Segregation of Duties

No single individual should have end-to-end control over a financial process: initiation, approval, and reconciliation must be split across different personnel. The most fundamental internal control — its absence is the primary enabler of employee dishonesty fraud.

Loss ratio credit: −15 to −25%
Prevent

Dual Authorisation for Payments

Any electronic payment above a defined threshold (typically £5k–£25k depending on business size) requires approval from a second authorised signatory. Eliminates the single point of failure that enables most BEC and EFT fraud. MFA on payment platforms is the technical implementation.

Loss ratio credit: −10 to −20% for BEC sublimit; enables full-limit BEC
Prevent

Mandatory Annual Leave

Requiring employees in financial roles to take at least 2 consecutive weeks of annual leave per year is a proven fraud deterrent: long-running embezzlement schemes typically require the fraudster's continuous presence to conceal. ACFE data confirms mandatory leave is associated with 35% shorter fraud durations. [I4]

Loss ratio credit: −5 to −10%
Prevent

Supplier/Vendor Verification

Before paying any new supplier or changing bank details for an existing supplier, a callback verification to a known, pre-registered telephone number is mandatory. The callback must use a number independently sourced — not one provided in the email or letter requesting the change. The single most effective BEC prevention measure.

Loss ratio credit: −15 to −30% for BEC exposure specifically
Prevent

Email Security (DMARC/SPF/DKIM)

Implementing DMARC, SPF and DKIM email authentication controls prevents domain spoofing — attackers cannot send emails that appear to come from the organisation's own domain. DMARC enforcement at "reject" policy level closes the most common BEC delivery mechanism. [I9]

Loss ratio credit: −10 to −15% for BEC; supports full-limit SE cover
Prevent

Background Screening

Pre-employment criminal record checks, financial credit checks and thorough reference verification for all employees in roles with financial authority. Periodic re-screening for long-serving employees in sensitive roles. Reduces placement of dishonest individuals in positions of trust.

Loss ratio credit: −5 to −8% on employee dishonesty component
Detection Controls (DETECT)
Detect

Anonymous Whistleblower Hotline

ACFE reports 42% of all occupational frauds are detected by tip — the single most common detection method, outperforming internal audit, management review and external audit combined. An independent anonymous hotline with genuine non-retaliation policy is the highest-ROI fraud detection investment. [I4]

Detect

Payroll Audit — Ghost Employee Detection

Regular reconciliation of payroll data against HR headcount records, including comparison of bank sort codes/account numbers across employees to detect duplicate accounts. Automated payroll analytics flagging accounts receiving payments to the same bank details as other employees or known vendors.

Detect

AP Vendor Master Controls

Vendor master file changes (new suppliers, bank detail changes) are logged, independently approved and periodically audited. Regular review of suppliers with no matching purchase orders, low-volume high-value transactions, and round-sum payments — classic red flags for fictitious vendor fraud.

Detect

Transaction Monitoring / Data Analytics

Automated anomaly detection across financial transactions: Benford's law analysis, unusual payment patterns, after-hours authorisations, round-sum payment flags, and new payee controls. Tools like IDEA, ACL or embedded ERP analytics significantly reduce fraud duration and loss size.

BEC / Social Engineering Specific Fraud Checklist

Pre-Payment BEC Verification Checklist (Finance / AP Teams)

  • Any request to change supplier bank details is treated as high-risk by default — regardless of how official the email appears.
  • New supplier set-up or bank detail change requires callback to a pre-registered, independently verified telephone number (not one provided in the request email).
  • Any email claiming to be from the CEO, CFO or another senior executive requesting urgent, confidential or unusual wire transfer is escalated to a second authorised person before action is taken.
  • Requests that create time pressure ("must be done today before close of business", "strictly confidential") are treated as fraud red flags, not genuine urgency.
  • Email domain of the sender is verified — domain spoofing (e.g. c0mpany.com vs company.com) is checked character by character for new or unusual correspondents.
  • Payment above threshold requires dual authorisation from two separate individuals who have each independently verified the payee details.
  • Overseas wire transfers to new beneficiary accounts require additional senior approval and a 24-hour processing delay where possible to enable recall if fraud is identified.
  • Any voice call or video call requesting urgent payment authorisation — especially from an "executive" — triggers verification via a separate, pre-registered contact channel before any action is taken. AI deepfakes can convincingly simulate voice and video. [I7]

🤖 AI Deepfake Fraud Warning: The 2024 Arup incident ($25m lost via deepfake video call) represents a qualitative escalation in social engineering capability. Fraudsters can now fabricate convincing video calls using AI-generated avatars of company executives, making traditional visual/audio verification inadequate. Underwriters should note: (1) many SE endorsements were drafted before AI deepfakes were a practical threat — review wording for adequacy; (2) verify coverage explicitly includes AI-generated impersonation; (3) insist insureds implement out-of-band verification procedures for high-value authorisations (e.g. in-person, pre-agreed code word, or physical token). [I7]
⚠ UK Economic Crime & Corporate Transparency Act 2023 — Failure to Prevent Fraud: In force from September 2025. Large UK organisations (500+ employees, £36m+ turnover, £18m+ balance sheet) commit a criminal offence if an "associated person" (employee, agent, subsidiary) commits fraud for the organisation's benefit and the organisation does not have reasonable fraud prevention procedures. Penalties: unlimited fines. Commercial crime insurance does not cover criminal fines but does cover the underlying fraud losses. Risk management programmes become a compliance requirement, not just a best practice. [L5]
Top 7 Actions for Commercial Insureds:
1. Implement dual authorisation and MFA on all payment platforms — this alone prevents most BEC losses.
2. Deploy DMARC email authentication at "reject" policy — eliminates domain spoofing attacks.
3. Establish a formal callback procedure for all new supplier set-up and bank detail changes.
4. Review social engineering sublimit — if <£500k, consider endorsement or standalone FTF cover.
5. Implement mandatory 2-week annual leave for all employees with financial system access.
6. Launch an anonymous whistleblower hotline — the single highest-ROI fraud detection measure.
7. Train finance teams specifically on deepfake fraud — out-of-band verification is now essential for high-value transactions.
