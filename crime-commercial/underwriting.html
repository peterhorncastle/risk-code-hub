Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Industry / Business Type

The single most important rating factor. Retail, financial services and government entities face highest employee theft frequency. Manufacturing and professional services face high BEC severity. Construction faces high AP fraud and payroll ghost employee exposure.

Base rate loading: 0.6× (low-risk professional) to 3.0× (high-cash retail)
High Impact

Revenues / Employee Count

Premium scales primarily with revenue (as a proxy for funds at risk and AP transaction volumes) and employee count (as a proxy for employee dishonesty frequency exposure). ACFE data confirms small businesses (<100 employees) suffer proportionately higher losses. [I4]

Base rate: £0.10–£0.35 per £1,000 revenue depending on class
High Impact

Internal Controls Quality

Segregation of duties, dual authorisation for payments above threshold, independent bank reconciliations, mandatory annual leave, and IT access controls are the primary fraud deterrents. Absence of any of these materially increases expected loss frequency. SOX compliance (US public companies) is a strong positive indicator. [L3]

Controls credit: −20 to −35% for best-in-class SOX/ISO 27001 environment
High Impact

Claims History (3–5 Year)

Prior crime losses are the strongest predictor of future losses. Single large loss may reflect unusual circumstances; pattern of smaller losses indicates systemic control weakness. ACFE data shows organisations that have suffered fraud are more likely to suffer again within 2 years. [I4]

Loading: +50% to +200% for adverse prior loss; credit: −10% for 5-year clean
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Limit of Indemnity / Deductible

Commercial crime limits range from £100k (SME) to £100m+ (multinational). Increased limit factors (ILFs) reflect the severity distribution of crime losses — the largest frauds are often at the tail. Deductibles of £25k–£250k are common for mid-market; they deter nuisance claims and reduce moral hazard.

ILF (£1m vs £250k): approx 1.8–2.2×; deductible £50k credit: −8 to −12%
Medium Impact

Social Engineering / BEC Sublimit

Whether social engineering is covered within the policy limit or as a sublimit (typically £100k–£500k) significantly affects exposure. Full limit BEC cover is available for best-controlled risks; sublimits are standard for SME. AI-enabled deepfake coverage is an emerging negotiation point. [I7]

Full-limit BEC endorsement: +15 to +30% additional premium
Medium Impact

Geographic Scope

Worldwide cover increases exposure vs UK-only or US-only policies. High-risk jurisdictions (certain EM markets, countries with weak legal systems for recovery) attract surcharges. US exposure attracts ERISA bond compliance consideration. Multinational companies need master + local admitted structure consideration.

Loading: +15 to +40% for worldwide including high-risk jurisdictions
Medium Impact

Discovery Period / Extended Discovery

Standard discovery period post-cancellation: 60–90 days. Extended discovery periods (180 days to 12 months) available at additional premium. Critical for M&A situations (acquisition of entity with unreported ongoing fraud). Longer periods increase insurer's 'tail' exposure. [L2]

180-day extended discovery: +5 to +10% of annual premium
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

External Audit Quality

Big Four / top-10 external auditors with strong fraud-risk procedures provide moderate assurance. However, auditors are not specifically tasked with detecting fraud, and several large frauds have passed undetected by major firms. Audit quality is a supportive but not determinative factor.

Lower Impact

Whistleblower Programme

ACFE reports that 42% of frauds are detected by tip. Anonymous hotlines and non-retaliation policies are among the most cost-effective fraud prevention measures. Their presence supports rate credit; their absence (particularly in large organisations) is a moderate negative indicator.

Lower Impact

Background Screening

Pre-employment background checks (criminal record, reference, financial credit) reduce the incidence of dishonest employees. Particularly relevant for roles with financial authority or cash/securities access. Not a standard underwriting requirement but cited as a positive control.

Lower Impact

IT Security / Cyber Controls

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on payment systems, email security (DMARC/SPF/DKIM), privileged access management and payment confirmation controls directly reduce BEC exposure. Strong IT security enables higher BEC sublimits or full-limit cover at competitive rates.

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Industry Hazard Factor // 0.6× (low) → 3.0× (high-cash retail)
  × Employee Count Factor // scales with workforce size
  × Controls Loading // −35% (best-in-class) → +50% (weak)
  × Claims History Loading // −10% (5yr clean) → +200% (adverse)
  × Limit Factor (ILF) // scaled from base £250k limit
  × Deductible Credit // −8% to −20% depending on level
  × Geographic Scope Factor // 1.0× (UK only) → 1.4× (worldwide)
  + BEC/SE Endorsement // +15–30% if full-limit social engineering

⚠ BEC / Social Engineering Coverage Gap: The "voluntary parting" exclusion can bar BEC claims under standard commercial crime wordings — the employee's voluntary transfer of funds at the direction of a fraudster may be characterised as the insured's own authorised act. A dedicated social engineering endorsement (or standalone FTF cover) is essential to close this gap. Ensure policy wording uses "at the direction of a person impersonating" language to bridge the voluntary parting exclusion. Review after the Medidata/Apache circuit split. [I6]
