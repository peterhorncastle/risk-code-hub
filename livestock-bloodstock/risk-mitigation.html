Risk Control Framework — PREVENT
PREVENT — NB Livestock

Biosecurity Protocols (APHA/DEFRA Guidance)

Comprehensive biosecurity is the primary defence against disease ingress. APHA's Code of Practice for Biosecurity (cattle, sheep, pigs, poultry) provides the standard against which underwriters assess farms. Key measures: controlled entry points, visitor movement logs, dedicated farm footwear for visitors, vehicle disinfection at farm entrance, separate clothing for farm staff. Poor biosecurity is the single most common contributing factor in post-FMD and post-avian flu claims disputes. EST

PREVENT — NB Livestock

Vaccination Programme (Where Available)

While no commercial FMD or Avian Influenza vaccine is currently in routine use in the UK, vaccines exist for many significant livestock diseases: IBR (infectious bovine rhinotracheitis), BVD (bovine viral diarrhoea), Johne's disease, Clostridial diseases, Maedi Visna (sheep), PRRS (pigs). Vaccination reduces individual animal mortality claims frequency significantly. For NX bloodstock: equine influenza and tetanus vaccination are mandatory under BHA Rules of Racing for all racehorses. VER

PREVENT — NB Livestock

Disease Surveillance Participation (BCMS / APHA)

Participation in BCMS cattle movement traceability is legally required for all UK cattle keepers. Active engagement with APHA's surveillance schemes — including voluntary participation in the Bovine TB testing programme, Johne's disease screening and BVD PI calf testing — demonstrates proactive disease management and provides verifiable data for claims assessment. Movement records are the primary evidence base for livestock mortality claims verification. VER

PREVENT — NX Bloodstock

Bloodstock Veterinary Pre-Purchase Examination

A thorough pre-purchase veterinary examination (PPE or "vetting") is required by all Lloyd's underwriters for bloodstock agreed values above £50,000. The PPE includes clinical examination, radiography of key joints (hocks, knees, fetlocks, navicular), ophthalmological examination, and assessment of reproductive soundness for breeding animals. The PPE report is lodged with the insurer and defines pre-existing conditions that may be excluded or loaded on the initial policy. EST

PREVENT — NX Bloodstock

Transit Biosecurity (Air Transport)

Air transport of thoroughbreds requires careful biosecurity preparation to minimise "shipping fever" (pleuropneumonia following travel stress). Pre-flight health checks, veterinary clearance, appropriate feeders and haynet provision in air stalls, and limitation of transit duration (maximum 8 hours recommended for high-value horses) reduce post-transit respiratory disease risk. Transit insurance notification and route approval are standard policy conditions for international air transport. VER

PREVENT — NB Livestock

Imported Animal Quarantine Controls

All livestock imported into the UK must comply with APHA import health certification and quarantine requirements. The standard quarantine period is 21–30 days depending on species and country of origin. During quarantine, imported animals must be kept separate from existing stock. Underwriters may require disclosure of any importations within the previous 12 months and may apply geographic surcharges for animals from higher-risk regions. Import of pigs from ASF-affected EU states is currently suspended under UK biosecurity rules. VER

Risk Control Framework — DETECT
DETECT — NB Livestock

DEFRA/APHA Movement Tracing (Mandatory Cattle Passports — BCMS)

The BCMS (British Cattle Movement Service) requires a cattle passport to accompany every bovine animal throughout its life. All movements must be registered within 3 days. The system enables APHA to rapidly trace contact premises during a disease outbreak — a critical tool for limiting epidemic spread. For NB insurers, BCMS data provides objective head-count verification and movement history that underpins claims assessment for mortality losses during disease events. VER

DETECT — NB Livestock

Early Clinical Sign Reporting

Prompt recognition and reporting of clinical signs of notifiable diseases — blister lesions (FMD), sudden high mortality with neurological signs (ASF), respiratory distress with high mortality in poultry (Avian Influenza) — is a legal obligation under the Animal Health Act 1981 and a condition of livestock insurance policies. Delayed reporting may constitute a breach of policy conditions and prejudice claims. Farmers must report suspicion of any notifiable disease to APHA within 24 hours. VER

DETECT — NB & NX

AI / Technology-Based Monitoring (Smart Barn Systems)

Precision livestock farming technology is transforming early disease detection. Automated body weight monitoring, feeding pattern analysis, rumination sensors, activity trackers (RFID ear tags with movement analysis) and thermal imaging cameras can detect individual animal health deterioration 24–48 hours before visible clinical signs. Early detection enables prompt veterinary intervention — reducing both mortality rates and the severity of individual insurance claims. Underwriters increasingly recognise smart barn technology as a biosecurity credit. EST

DETECT — NX Bloodstock

Veterinary Call-Out Threshold Clarity

A clear policy on the threshold for calling the attending veterinarian is essential for minimising NX mortality claims. Delayed veterinary attendance is a common factor in preventable racehorse deaths — particularly for colic (which can progress to fatal intestinal rupture within hours without surgery), limb fractures (where prompt humane destruction prevents further injury), and post-foaling complications. Insurance policy conditions typically require prompt veterinary attendance and authorisation before humane destruction of an insured animal. VER

Biosecurity Checklist — Farm Assessment (8 Priority Items for NB Underwriters)

  • Vermin control programme: Documented rodent and wild bird exclusion programme for feed stores, poultry units and pig buildings — vermin are primary disease vectors for Salmonella, ASF (wild boar) and avian flu transmission.
  • Separate footwear for visitors: Disposable boot covers or dedicated farm footwear available at all entry points; boot-dip or spray disinfectant (APHA-approved disinfectant at correct dilution) maintained at farm entrances.
  • Vehicle disinfection point: Functional vehicle disinfection point at farm entry gate — wheel arch spray or drive-through disinfection bath for livestock vehicles. Essential for FMD prevention as virus survives in soil on vehicle tyres.
  • Quarantine area for new arrivals: Dedicated isolation facility (minimum 21 days) for all newly purchased livestock before introduction to main herd/flock — separates new risk from established animals.
  • Dead animal disposal protocol: Documented procedure for disposal of fallen stock using DEFRA-licensed knackerman or on-site burial (where permitted). No illegal on-site incineration or burial. Prompt disposal prevents disease spread and vermin attraction.
  • Water supply security: No open water sources shared with wild waterfowl (high avian flu risk); mains water or protected bore hole supply; regular water quality testing for poultry and pig units.
  • Feed storage security: All feed stored in vermin-proof, covered facilities; separate storage from areas accessible to wild birds; no outdoor feeding of livestock where wild bird access is possible.
  • Record keeping — movement register: Up-to-date livestock movement register (statutory requirement for cattle; best practice for sheep, pigs and poultry); veterinary visit records; medication records. Available for APHA inspection and insurance claims verification on demand.

Disease Outbreak Response Protocol — FMD / Avian Influenza

When notifiable disease is suspected or confirmed on an insured premises, the following steps must be followed immediately. Delay in any step may compromise both disease control and insurance claims validity.

  1. Notify APHA within 24 hours of suspecting any notifiable disease — FMD, Avian Influenza, ASF, Newcastle Disease. Use the APHA emergency disease hotline: 03000 200 301. This is a legal obligation under the Animal Health Act 1981. Failure to notify is a criminal offence. VER
  2. Impose premises movement standstill immediately. No livestock, people (other than essential care), vehicles, equipment, manure or slurry to leave the premises until APHA authorises movement. Do not move animals to market, neighbours, or other farms.
  3. Do NOT dispose of carcases. All dead animals must remain on the premises in situ pending APHA veterinary visit. Do not move, bury, burn or dispose of any dead or suspect animals. APHA requires post-mortem examination for disease confirmation.
  4. Inform your insurer immediately — call the 24-hour claims notification line on your livestock insurance policy. Prompt notification is a policy condition; delay may prejudice your right to claim. Obtain a claim reference number. Document all APHA communications.
  5. APHA Government Vet visit — APHA will send an Approved Veterinary Inspector (AVI) to confirm diagnosis. Co-operate fully. The AVI's assessment initiates the government compensation process and the restrictions on movement.
  6. Compensation valuation process — a government valuer (typically an RICS-qualified agricultural valuer) will attend to value culled animals. Retain your own independent valuer if you believe the government valuation is below market value — this is the basis of your commercial insurance top-up claim.
  7. Document all losses meticulously — maintain records of each animal culled (ear tag / passport number, date, weight/value); veterinary bills; welfare costs during movement restriction; any business interruption losses. All required for commercial insurance claim.
NX Bloodstock — Stallion Infertility Monitoring Protocol: For covering stallions insured for permanent infertility, an annual pre-season fertility assessment by an approved equine reproductive specialist is a mandatory policy condition. The assessment must include: semen collection and analysis (sperm motility, morphology and concentration), a clinical reproductive examination, and a declaration by the attending veterinarian confirming the stallion is fit to cover mares. Mid-season monitoring (after 30 and 60 coverings) is required for stallions over £5m agreed value or those with any prior fertility concern noted in the pre-cover report. Any reduction in fertility indicators must be reported to the underwriter immediately — failure to report declining fertility may be treated as non-disclosure. EST
Top 7 Risk Mitigation Actions for Livestock & Bloodstock Insureds
Action 01

Join APHA's Herd Health and Monitoring Scheme and maintain up-to-date biosecurity documentation — unlocks premium credits and strengthens claims position.

Action 02

Ensure all cattle passports are current in BCMS; conduct a livestock head count audit at least annually — discrepancies at claim time are the most common cause of NB claims disputes.

Action 03

Implement a documented vaccination programme for all preventable diseases relevant to your species — IBR/BVD for cattle, PRRS for pigs, equine influenza for racehorses.

Action 04

Install vehicle disinfection and visitor footwear protocols at farm entrance — the single highest-impact biosecurity measure for FMD prevention, at minimal cost.

Action 05

For commercial poultry: maintain mandatory housing capability year-round and eliminate wild bird water access points — the two most effective H5N1 avian flu transmission reduction measures.

Action 06

For NX bloodstock: schedule annual pre-season stallion fertility assessments and veterinary pre-purchase examinations — non-compliance voids permanent infertility cover.

Action 07

Keep insurer informed of all significant changes — new importations, changes in stocking density, veterinary concerns, new species added to farm — in-year changes that affect risk profile require mid-term endorsement.

Animal Welfare Act 2006 — Duty of Care: All livestock and bloodstock insureds have a statutory duty of care under the Animal Welfare Act 2006 to ensure the welfare of animals under their control. The Act requires provision of: a suitable environment, a suitable diet, ability to exhibit normal behaviour patterns, any need to be housed with, or apart from, other animals, and protection from pain, suffering, injury and disease. Wilful neglect or failure to provide veterinary care is both a criminal offence and a ground for policy avoidance or claims rejection. Underwriters conducting farm inspections or reviewing claims should assess welfare compliance as part of their standard process. VER
