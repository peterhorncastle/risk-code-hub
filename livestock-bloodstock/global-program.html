International Bloodstock Programme — 4-Layer Structure
Layer 4 — Top

XL Aggregate Reinsurance (Catastrophe Stop-Loss)

Catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance providing aggregate protection against correlated mortality events — a single disease event (equine herpes, equine influenza) affecting multiple insured horses simultaneously, or a single transit disaster. XL aggregate reinsurance absorbs losses above a specified aggregate retention. Essential for any syndicate carrying significant NX bloodstock accumulation from major stud operations or racing circuits. Priced based on expected correlation of individual equine mortality events. VER

Layer 3 — Master

Lloyd's International Master Bloodstock Policy

The core of the global bloodstock programme — a Lloyd's master policy covering all named horses across all territories where they operate. Agreed value per horse, with automatic reinstatement for new acquisitions within pre-agreed value limits. The master policy is the primary source of coverage for elite racing and breeding operations whose horses move across jurisdictions. Covers mortality, permanent infertility, prospective foal cover and transit on an all-risks basis. Administered by a London specialist bloodstock broker with global access to Lloyd's syndicates. VER

Layer 2 — Local

Local Admitted Livestock Insurance (Per Country)

Certain jurisdictions (particularly the USA, Australia and some EU states) require locally admitted insurance policies for commercial livestock operations — Lloyd's non-admitted placement may not be permissible for all risks or may require surplus lines compliance. Local admitted policies provide regulatory compliance and may interface with state-level disease compensation schemes (e.g. USDA/APHIS ELAP in the USA). The local policy must dovetail precisely with the master policy to avoid gaps or double insurance.

Layer 1 — Base

Government Disease Compensation Schemes (Gap-Fill Layer)

Government compensation is the base layer for NB livestock disease losses in most countries — not insurance but a statutory payment scheme triggered by compulsory slaughter under a disease control order. The commercial insurance programme provides a gap-fill above government compensation — bridging between government valuation methodology and actual market value. Understanding each jurisdiction's disease compensation scheme (payment rates, eligible species, valuation methodology, payment timeline) is essential for structuring the commercial insurance gap-fill layer. VER

Jurisdiction Profiles — Key Global Markets
UNITED KINGDOM

UK — Lloyd's Dominant; APHA Regulatory; BCMS Traceability

  • Lloyd's syndicates dominate NX bloodstock; NFU Mutual and other admitted carriers for NB livestock
  • APHA (Animal and Plant Health Agency) is the competent authority for disease control and compensation
  • BCMS provides lifetime cattle movement traceability — essential for NB claims verification
  • DEFRA disease compensation under Animal Health Act 1981 — commercial top-up policies fill the gap
  • BHA (British Horseracing Authority) — regulatory body for flat and NH racing; equine passport mandatory
  • Tattersalls Newmarket — primary UK NX new business pipeline VER
IRELAND

Ireland — Coolmore/Godolphin Hub; Shared FMD Risk with UK

  • Home to Coolmore Stud (Galileo, Frankel, Camelot at stud) — highest concentration of NX stallion value globally
  • Irish Horseracing Regulatory Board (IHRB) — regulates Irish racing and breeding; equine passport system
  • Agricultural diseases shared risk with UK — common border disease control zones (FMD, Avian Flu)
  • Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (DAFM) — animal disease compensation schemes
  • BVD eradication programme — mandatory testing and slaughter of PI cattle creates NB claims volume
  • Goffs (The Curragh) and Tattersalls Ireland — major yearling and breeding stock sale venues
UNITED STATES

USA — USDA/APHIS; State Agriculture Depts; Keeneland Hub

  • USDA/APHIS (Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service) — federal disease control authority; ELAP compensation scheme for qualifying livestock losses
  • State agriculture departments — primary livestock regulation; disease compensation varies significantly by state
  • Keeneland (Lexington, KY) and Fasig-Tipton — primary NX yearling insurance pipeline
  • Surplus lines (E&S) placement commonly required for NX risks exceeding admitted carrier capacity; Lloyd's dominant for elite bloodstock
  • Equine Infectious Anaemia (EIA) Coggins test — required for interstate horse movement; material to NX underwriting
  • PRRS (Porcine Reproductive and Respiratory Syndrome) endemic in US pig herds — primary NB pig peril EST
AUSTRALIA

Australia — AQIS Strict Biosecurity; Magic Millions; Drought Exposure

  • AQIS (Australian Quarantine and Inspection Service) — world's most stringent biosecurity controls; strict import requirements for all livestock
  • Australia is FMD-free — fiercely protected status; single FMD incursion would be catastrophic for export market worth A$30bn
  • Magic Millions (Gold Coast) — primary Australian NX yearling sale; growing international profile
  • Racing Australia / state racing authorities — regulatory framework for thoroughbred racing and breeding
  • Drought and bushfire exposure — multi-year droughts cause mass livestock destocking; a critical NB natural perils risk
  • Australia's livestock industry is export-dependent — UK and Asian disease events affect Australian livestock values via trade EST
FRANCE

France — Haras Nationaux Breeding; PMU Racing; FMD-Free Status

  • France-Haras (formerly Les Haras Nationaux) — national stud organisation; French breeding industry is major supplier to European racing
  • PMU (Pari Mutuel Urbain) — national off-course betting operator that funds significant prize money; underpins commercial bloodstock values
  • France is FMD-free and maintains strict border controls; shares EU Animal Health Regulation framework (2016/429)
  • Arqana (Deauville) — primary French NX yearling and breeding stock sale market
  • French agricultural insurance (Groupama, Pacifica) serves the NB domestic market; Lloyd's for high-value NX
  • France-based Coolmore France (Grosbois) and Darley (Banstead) stallion satellite operations create cross-border NX programme needs
UNITED ARAB EMIRATES

UAE — Meydan/IFAH; Dubai World Cup; Summer Rest; Heat Mortality

  • Meydan Racecourse (Dubai) — hosts the Dubai World Cup (world's richest race; $12m prize); attracts leading horses from global racing circuits
  • IFAH (International Federation for Animal Health) — international animal health regulatory framework relevant to UAE biosecurity
  • UAE operates two racing seasons — winter (Oct–Mar) and summer rest period. Horses stabled in UAE during summer face extreme heat (45°C+) and humidity mortality risk
  • Dubai Racing Club — regulatory authority; equine testing and passport requirements
  • Arabian horse racing — a separate major NX sub-class in the UAE; Arabian horse values exceed thoroughbred values in the Gulf states
  • Racing camel insurance — a specialist Lloyd's exotic livestock sub-class for the Gulf market; individual racing camel values reach AED 20m+

Case Study — Global Thoroughbred Operation (Godolphin / Coolmore Model): Lloyd's International Bloodstock Programme

Risk Profile: A major global thoroughbred operation maintains horses in training in the UK, France, USA and Australia simultaneously; covering stallions at stud in Ireland (5 stallions, combined agreed value £150m); broodmares in Kentucky (80 mares, average agreed value £200k each); yearlings in Newmarket training preparation (30 animals, average agreed value £500k); active racehorses at Dubai training base for winter season. Total NX portfolio: £220m+ agreed value across 4 continents. EST

Programme Structure: Lloyd's international master bloodstock policy — a single policy document issued by a Lloyd's specialist bloodstock syndicate (with co-subscribing syndicates for capacity). The master policy covers all horses worldwide on an agreed-value all-risks mortality basis, with permanent infertility extension for all 5 covering stallions. Automatic addition of new purchases within pre-agreed value limits (up to £5m per horse without specific notification); horses above £5m require individual agreement and veterinary examination confirmation.

Transit Extensions: Annual blanket transit extension covering all air, sea and road movements of covered horses between training bases. Specific notification required for international air transit of horses above £2m agreed value. Dubai winter season transit (UK/France to UAE) is a high-frequency risk event — multiple horses moved by specialist equine air cargo operators (Peden Bloodstock, Flying Stallions) each October and returning March/April.

Government Compensation Interface: Irish government disease compensation applies to the Irish stud premises under DAFM provisions; UK DEFRA applies to Newmarket training facilities. Programme is structured so that government compensation is the first layer — the Lloyd's master policy responds only above government compensation and for non-government-compensable losses.

Key Underwriting Considerations: Accumulation of stallion values at the Irish stud (£150m+ on a single premises) represents a catastrophic single-site loss potential. The main exposure: catastrophic disease event (equine herpes virus myeloencephalopathy — EHVM) affecting multiple stallions simultaneously. EHVM can cause neurological damage and death in adult horses — a potential total-loss event for a stud operation. Underwriters apply an accumulation loading to the Irish stud element and require segregation of stallion boxes at the stud. EST

Lloyd's Market Position — Global Bloodstock: The Lloyd's bloodstock market is the undisputed global centre for NX thoroughbred insurance. No other insurance market combines the capacity (individual agreed values up to £50m+), underwriting expertise (specialist bloodstock syndicates with deep veterinary and bloodstock industry knowledge), policy flexibility (permanent infertility, prospective foal, agreed value basis) and global programme coordination capability that Lloyd's provides. The market is served by a small number of specialist Lloyd's bloodstock brokers — including BFL, Falcon Insurance and others — who act as the intermediary between major bloodstock clients and Lloyd's syndicates. These brokers maintain the relationships with major studs and racing operations worldwide that channel the annual premium flow into the London market. VER
