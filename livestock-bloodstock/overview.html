Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

NB — Livestock (General)

Mortality insurance for farm animals — cattle, sheep, pigs, poultry, goats — against death from accident, illness or disease. Extends to aquaculture (fish farms, shellfish), zoo animals and exotic livestock. Cover is typically written on an agreed-value basis per head or as a blanket sum for the herd or flock. Disease epidemic cover for notifiable diseases such as Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) and Avian Influenza is either excluded (government compensation schemes apply) or available as a government-compensation top-up extension. EST

NX — Bloodstock (Thoroughbred & Competition)

Mortality and infertility insurance for thoroughbred racehorses, competition horses and breeding stock. Individual insured values range from £50,000 to £50m+. Cover includes mortality (death from accident, illness or humane destruction), permanent infertility (stallions and broodmares — loss of breeding function), prospective foal cover (foal in utero), transit mortality and theft. Lloyd's is the dominant global market for elite bloodstock — virtually all horses of significant value worldwide are insured through London market syndicates. Veterinary examination required at agreed values above £50,000. EST

NB vs NX — Key Distinctions

NB covers agricultural and commercial livestock in which value is typically assessed as a herd or flock (commodity value). NX covers individual named animals of exceptional value — assessed on agreed value basis with veterinary certification. The primary perils differ: NB is driven by disease epidemic accumulation (a single outbreak can kill thousands of animals); NX is characterised by individual high-severity claims and the specific infertility risk for breeding stallions and broodmares that is unique to this class. Government compensation for notifiable disease (FMD, Avian flu) applies to NB but rarely to NX. VER

Coverage Taxonomy

Mortality Cover

Death of the insured animal from any cause — accident, illness or disease — including humane destruction on veterinary advice to relieve incurable suffering. The core peril for both NB livestock and NX bloodstock. For livestock, excludes culling under government slaughter orders (government compensation applies). For bloodstock, typically includes humane destruction where racing or breeding career is unrecoverable. VER

Permanent Infertility

Loss of breeding function — specific to stallions and broodmares in the NX bloodstock class. A covering stallion permanently unable to cover mares represents a total economic loss independent of mortality. A stallion insured for £10m as a stud sire whose infertility is confirmed by veterinary examination triggers a full infertility claim. Annual fertility assessments are required for high-value breeding stallions. Permanent infertility of broodmares is also insurable. VER

Prospective Foal Cover

Insurance of a foal in utero — covering death of the unborn foal from any cause (including stillbirth and death of the dam). Attach-point: typically 45–90 days before expected foaling date; cover terminates when foal stands and sucks (or at 24–48 hours after birth at insurer's option). A specialist NX extension of significant commercial importance given the value of foals from elite sire/dam matings — a Galileo or Frankel foal may be worth £500k–£2m before birth. VER

Transit Cover

Mortality risk during transportation — road, rail, sea or air. Air transit of thoroughbred racehorses to international race meetings is a significant accumulation exposure: a single aircraft loss could involve multiple high-value insured horses. Transit cover is typically written as a short-period extension to the annual mortality policy and requires prior notification. Biosecurity protocols during transit are a key underwriting concern — disease transmission risk in transit. VER

Disease Epidemic Extension

Extension covering losses from notifiable disease outbreaks (FMD, Avian Influenza, African Swine Fever) above government compensation levels. Standard NB livestock policies exclude government-compensated disease slaughter. Top-up extensions are available to bridge the gap between government valuation and market value, and to cover ancillary losses (movement restrictions, welfare feed costs, business interruption). Accumulation risk is extreme — a single FMD outbreak in a restricted zone can affect thousands of insured animals. VER

Theft

Theft of livestock or bloodstock — relevant for high-value breeding stock, pedigree cattle and horses. Cattle rustling remains a real agricultural crime (NFU data reports 3,000+ cattle stolen per year in the UK). High-value bloodstock theft is rarer but has occurred — yearlings and mares are the most vulnerable. Cover typically requires evidence of violent or forcible entry and police report. Livestock identification systems (BCMS cattle passports, equine passports) facilitate recovery and claims verification. VER

Lloyd's Dominance in Bloodstock: Lloyd's syndicates are the global centre of gravity for thoroughbred bloodstock insurance. Virtually all horses of significant value worldwide — from Coolmore's covering stallions in Ireland to Godolphin's global racing string — are insured through the London market. This concentration reflects Lloyd's historical specialism, its capacity for high individual agreed values, its willingness to cover unusual perils (permanent infertility, prospective foal) and the international expertise of specialist bloodstock syndicates and Lloyd's bloodstock brokers. The Tattersalls and Keeneland sales circuits generate the annual pipeline of newly-insured young stock that underpins market growth. VER
Disease Epidemic Risk — NB Livestock: Disease outbreaks represent the catastrophic accumulation peril for livestock insurance. The UK FMD 2001 outbreak resulted in 6 million animals slaughtered and a total economic cost of £8bn (Anderson Inquiry 2002). Insured losses are estimated at £1.2bn. The 2021–2024 UK Avian Influenza season was the worst on record, with over 40 million birds culled. African Swine Fever (ASF) has swept through Asia since 2018, with China estimated to have lost 50%+ of its total pig population. Government compensation schemes (DEFRA/APHA in the UK) provide a first layer of payment but often at below-market rates and with lengthy assessment delays — creating a strong demand for commercial insurance top-up cover. EST
