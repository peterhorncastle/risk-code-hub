Rating Factors — High Impact (NX Bloodstock)
High Impact NX

Individual Horse Value (Agreed Value Basis)

Agreed value is the single most critical NX underwriting factor. The insured and insurer agree the value at inception — this becomes the sum payable on death or confirmed permanent infertility without depreciation. For horses above £50,000 agreed value, veterinary examination is required. Above £500,000, a specialist equine insurance veterinarian must provide a health certificate and confirmation of suitability for the proposed use (racing, breeding, competition). EST

Rate range: 1.5–4.0% of agreed value per annum (est.)
High Impact NX

Age and Use of Animal

A stallion actively at stud covering 100+ mares per season is rated very differently from a 2-year-old in flat race training or a 15-year-old broodmare. Racing horses face acute injury risk; older mares face foaling complications. Covering stallions face permanent infertility risk. The expected remaining economic career must be assessed — a stallion at peak breeding demand commands the highest insured values and the most complex rating. VER

Age loading: +25–50% for active racehorses aged 2–4 vs breeding stock
High Impact NX

Racing Schedule and Competition Level

International competition — particularly jump racing (National Hunt) and high-intensity flat racing — carries significantly higher mortality risk than light flat racing or breeding activity. Travel to international race meetings adds transit risk. Horses entered for the Grand National or Cheltenham Gold Cup face statistically elevated mortality risk and attract rate loadings. Some underwriters apply course-specific loadings for known high-risk venues. VER

Competition loading: +10–30% for NH racing; +5–15% for international travel
High Impact NX

Breeding Record and Stallion Book Size

For covering stallions, the size and quality of the book of mares (mares to be covered each season) directly determines economic value and infertility risk exposure. A stallion covering 200 mares at £50,000 per covering generates £10m annual revenue — infertility is a £100m+ loss of future earnings. Annual fertility assessment (sperm motility, morphology, count) is a required condition for high-value stallion infertility cover. VER

Infertility premium: additional 0.5–1.5% of agreed value for permanent infertility extension
Rating Factors — High Impact (NB Livestock)
High Impact NB

Species and Farm Type

Species drives fundamentally different disease risk profiles. Poultry (particularly intensive egg and broiler units) face catastrophic single-event avian flu loss at scale. Pigs face ASF/PRRS/PED accumulation risk. Dairy cattle face individual mortality from clinical diseases (mastitis, milk fever, displacement of abomasum). Beef cattle face respiratory diseases. Each species has a distinct loss frequency and severity distribution requiring separate rating tables. EST

Base rate by species: poultry 0.8–2.5%; pigs 0.5–1.5%; cattle 0.3–1.0%; sheep 0.4–1.2% (% of value)
High Impact NB

Geographic Location — Disease Risk Zone

Proximity to previous FMD, Avian Influenza or ASF outbreak zones is a critical rating factor. Farms in APHA-designated Temporary Control Zones or Surveillance Zones during active outbreaks may face movement restrictions that materially increase loss severity. Farms adjacent to wild bird habitats (estuaries, water bodies) carry elevated avian flu risk. Some underwriters apply postcode-level zoning surcharges based on historical outbreak mapping. EST

Zone loading: +15–40% for farms within previous Restricted Zone perimeter
High Impact NB

Herd / Flock Size and Accumulation

Large intensive units create catastrophic single-event accumulation exposure. A 500,000-bird laying hen unit insured as a single risk represents a potentially total-loss accumulation if avian flu enters the unit. Conversely, smaller mixed farms have greater natural diversification. Underwriters must assess the maximum probable loss (MPL) for a single disease event — which may be 100% of insured animals for highly contagious diseases in intensive housing. EST

Accumulation loading: +20–60% for units above 100,000 birds or 1,000 cattle
High Impact NB

Claims Experience (3–5 Year)

Prior livestock mortality claims are the strongest predictor of future losses. A farm with repeated individual animal mortality claims may indicate poor herd health, inadequate veterinary care, or biosecurity failures. Pattern losses (multiple animals from the same disease in a single year) attract significant rate loadings. A clean 5-year record qualifies for the maximum merit credit across all NB livestock species classes. EST

Loading: +30–100% for adverse prior loss pattern; credit: −10–15% for 5-year clean
Rating Factors — Medium Impact (NB & NX)
Medium Impact NB & NX

Veterinary Care Quality

Regular veterinary oversight — herd health schemes, annual health checks, prompt attendance for sick animals — materially reduces mortality frequency. For NX bloodstock, the quality of the attending veterinary surgeon (and the racing yard's vet programme) is assessed. Farm assurance schemes (Red Tractor, QMS for Scottish beef) provide proxy quality signals. Absence of regular vet involvement is a negative indicator. VER

Vet care credit: −5 to −15% for demonstrated best-practice health scheme participation
Medium Impact NB

Biosecurity Measures

APHA-compliant biosecurity — visitor controls, vehicle disinfection, footwear protocols, quarantine facilities for new arrivals — directly reduces disease ingress risk. Farms certified under APHA's Herd Health and Monitoring Scheme attract preferential terms. Poultry farms with indoor housing systems face lower avian flu transmission risk than free-range units during outbreak periods. Biosecurity attestation is increasingly a condition of cover. EST

Biosecurity credit: −10 to −20% for APHA-compliant documented biosecurity programme
Medium Impact NB

Disease Surveillance Participation

Participation in BCMS cattle movement traceability, mandatory BVD testing programmes, avian influenza surveillance schemes and PRRS monitoring for pigs all demonstrate a proactive disease management culture. Movement records enable accurate head counts for claims verification and rapid assessment of exposure during outbreak. BCMS data access is now a standard claims documentation requirement for all UK cattle mortality claims. VER

Surveillance credit: −5 to −10% for full BCMS participation and BVD-accredited status
Medium Impact NX

Transit Risk (Air Transport)

Air transport of thoroughbreds to international race meetings — Dubai, Hong Kong, the USA, Japan — carries specific mortality risk from hypoxia, shipping fever (travel stress-induced respiratory disease), transit injuries and in-flight veterinary emergencies. Air transit is one of the most hazardous phases of a racehorse's life — mortality rates are elevated in the 72 hours post-long-haul flight. Prior notification of transit and route approval are standard policy conditions. VER

Air transit premium: additional 0.25–0.75% for each international air transit notified
Medium Impact NB

Vaccination Records

Vaccination against preventable diseases (IBR — infectious bovine rhinotracheitis; BVD — bovine viral diarrhoea; Johne's disease; Maedi Visna in sheep) materially reduces disease mortality claims frequency. For NX bloodstock, equine influenza and tetanus vaccination records are required. Some underwriters mandate vaccination as a policy condition; others apply credits for voluntary vaccination programme participation. VER

Vaccination credit: −5 to −12% for documented up-to-date vaccination programme
Medium Impact NB

Imported Animal Quarantine Controls

Importation of livestock (particularly cattle, sheep and pigs from disease-endemic regions) represents one of the highest-risk disease ingress pathways. APHA-compliant quarantine periods (21–30 days for most species) and pre-import health certification are required by UK law. Farms with documented import quarantine protocols attract credits; farms with recent import history from higher-risk jurisdictions attract loadings. VER

Import loading: +10–25% for farms with recent importation history from higher-risk jurisdictions
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact NB

Alarm and Security — Theft Prevention

CCTV, perimeter fencing, lighting, and registered stock markers (ear tags, freeze brands) reduce livestock theft exposure. For high-value bloodstock, alarm systems for stabling and stud farms are a standard policy condition. Livestock identification technology (GPS tracking for high-value animals, RFID ear tags) assists post-theft recovery and claims verification.

Lower Impact NB

Farm Building Standards

Well-maintained buildings — adequate ventilation, appropriate stocking densities, clean water supply, secure feed storage — reduce housing-related respiratory disease and ammonia toxicity in intensive livestock units. Farm assurance scheme audits provide a standardised assessment of building standards that underwriters can reference without individual site inspections.

Lower Impact NB & NX

Insurance Basis — Named Individual vs Unnamed Flock

Named individual animals (always the case for NX, optional for high-value NB pedigree stock) require positive identification and agreed-value assessment but provide clarity on loss events. Unnamed flock or herd cover (standard for commercial livestock) requires accurate head counts at inception and periodic audit. Mixed approaches (named pedigree bulls, unnamed commercial cows) are common in dairy beef operations.

Lower Impact NX

Stallion Infertility Monitoring

For covering stallions, annual pre-season fertility assessment (sperm motility, morphology, concentration) is a standard condition of permanent infertility cover. Mid-season monitoring requirements vary by underwriter and agreed value. A stallion with declining fertility indicators over consecutive seasons may face infertility cover withdrawal or significant premium loading — the horse may be a 'going concern risk' even before confirmed infertility.

Warning — FMD / Avian Influenza Government Compensation Shortfall: The most common source of dispute in NB livestock claims is the gap between DEFRA/APHA government compensation and the insured's actual market value for culled animals. Government valuations are typically based on "market value at time of slaughter order" — which may reflect a depressed market caused by the very outbreak that triggered the slaughter. Commercial insurance top-up cover must be explicitly endorsed, with agreed basis of valuation clearly defined (independent market value vs government valuation). Underwriters should confirm the valuation methodology in the policy schedule. EST
NX Bloodstock — Agreed Value Basis: Unlike most property insurance (which operates on an indemnity / market value basis), NX bloodstock policies are written on an agreed value basis — the value stated in the schedule is the sum payable in full on death or confirmed permanent infertility. This reflects the difficulty of objectively valuing a thoroughbred racehorse after death — there is no established second-hand market for dead racehorses. The agreed value must be supported by veterinary examination, sale records, or professional valuation from a recognised bloodstock agent. For values above £1m, Lloyd's underwriters typically require a formal bloodstock agent's valuation letter at inception. VER
