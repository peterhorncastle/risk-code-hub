Marine Cargo Insurance

The oldest insured peril in commercial history — from Lloyd's Coffee House in 1688 to the Red Sea crisis of 2024. Marine cargo covers physical loss and damage to goods in transit by sea, air, road and rail, and remains one of Lloyd's largest and most internationally traded classes.

Institute Cargo Clauses — ICC A, B and C

The Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC), maintained by the Lloyd's Market Association and the International Underwriting Association, are the standard policy wordings for marine cargo worldwide. The 2009 edition (effective 1 January 2009) is the current version; the 1982 edition remains widely used in some markets. VER

ICC (A) — Broadest

All Risks

  • ✔ All risks of loss or damage (subject to exclusions)
  • ✔ Fire and explosion
  • ✔ Washing overboard / jettison

  • ✔ Vessel grounding / stranding / collision
  • ✔ General average / salvage charges
  • ✔ Both-to-blame collision

  • ✘ Inherent vice, ordinary leakage, wilful misconduct

General Average — The Unique Marine Concept

What is General Average?

General Average (GA) is an ancient principle of maritime law — when a voluntary sacrifice is made or extraordinary expenditure incurred to save a voyage (vessel + cargo + freight), the resulting loss is shared proportionately by all parties who benefit. A captain who orders cargo jettisoned to refloat a grounded vessel creates a GA event: the cargo owner whose goods were jettisoned can claim contribution from all other cargo owners (and the shipowner), in proportion to the value of property saved.

  • York-Antwerp Rules 2016 govern most modern GA adjustments — the rules are incorporated by reference into virtually all bills of lading VER
  • Average Adjusters (members of the Association of Average Adjusters) calculate each party's proportion — the process typically takes 18–36 months for a large event
  • GA Security: Cargo interests must provide GA security (cash deposit or bank/insurer guarantee) before their cargo is released from the vessel or terminal. Cargo insurers provide GA guarantees to policyholders — without insurance, cargo owners may need to lodge substantial cash deposits before recovering their goods
  • Ever Given (Suez Canal, 2021): GA declared; estimated GA contribution of 11.75% of cargo value; total GA fund ~$900m across thousands of cargo interests EST
  • Underwriting relevance: GA is an insured peril under ICC A, B and C (General Average and salvage charges clause). GA claims are complex, slow to resolve, and often result in cargo insurers paying GA contributions on behalf of insureds who may have had no physical loss themselves

Incoterms 2020 — Who Insures the Cargo?

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms), published by the International Chamber of Commerce, define the point at which risk in goods passes from seller to buyer — and therefore who has the insurable interest and should be buying insurance. VER

IncotermMeaningRisk Transfer PointWho InsuresMinimum Insurance Required
EXW Ex Works At seller's premises Buyer — from collection onwards None mandated; buyer's responsibility
FCA Free Carrier When handed to buyer's carrier Buyer — from named place None mandated
FOB Free on Board When goods on board vessel Buyer — from ship's rail None mandated — but buyer almost always insures
CFR Cost & Freight When goods on board vessel Buyer — risk travels with cargo; seller pays freight None mandated — buyer insures
CIF Cost, Insurance & Freight When goods on board vessel Seller must provide minimum insurance (ICC C) for buyer's benefit ICC (C) minimum — 110% of CIF value
CIP Carriage & Insurance Paid At named carrier Seller must provide insurance for buyer's benefit ICC (A) minimum — 110% of CIP value (upgraded from ICC C in Incoterms 2020)
DAP / DPU / DDP Delivered (various) At destination Seller bears risk to destination; seller typically insures None mandated; seller's commercial interest

Market Participants

Lloyd's Syndicates

Atrium (1221), Talbot (1183), MS Amlin (2001), Beazley (623), Chaucer (1084), Tokio Marine Kiln — Lloyd's marine cargo market remains the global benchmark for specialty and complex cargo risks. Syndicates typically lead on open cover wordings and commodity trade finance structures.

Company Market

Allianz (including AGCS), Zurich, AXA XL, Swiss Re (corporate), Munich Re, Generali — major company market followers on Lloyd's-led open covers and standalone commodity placements. Allianz and AXA XL maintain specialist marine cargo underwriting teams in London.

National Markets

European and Asian national markets (Germany — Versicherungsverein; Scandinavia — Gard, Skuld for some cargo elements; Korea — Samsung Fire, KB Insurance; Japan — Tokio Marine, Sompo) write domestic and regional cargo. Lloyd's often reinsures national market excess.

Commodity Trade Finance Banks

ING, BNP Paribas, Standard Chartered, Société Générale — banks financing commodity trades (oil, metals, soft commodities) routinely require cargo insurance as a condition of the financing facility. The bank is typically named as loss payee or additional insured. Trade finance cargo is a major London market segment.

Freight Forwarders & 3PLs

DHL, Kuehne+Nagel, DB Schenker, DSV — major freight forwarders arrange cargo insurance either on behalf of clients or under their own liability cover (VL code). BIFA (British International Freight Association) standard trading conditions cap freight forwarder liability at £2 per kilo — cargo insurance fills the gap.

War Risk Market

Institute War Clauses (Cargo) are written separately from ICC coverage — typically by a specialist subset of Lloyd's syndicates. War risk rates are set by the Joint War Committee (JWC) which publishes its High Risk Area (HRA) list. Red Sea / Gulf of Aden: elevated war risk rates from late 2023 following Houthi attacks. VER
