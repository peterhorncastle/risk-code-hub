Confidence Badge Framework

  • VER Verified — Named primary or authoritative secondary source; fact-checked

  • ILLUS Illustrative — Hypothetical example constructed to demonstrate a concept or calculation

Legislation & Statutes

#SourceRelevanceURL
L1 Marine Insurance Act 1906 (UK) Foundational statute for English marine insurance law. Codifies insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, subrogation, warranties (seaworthiness, legality), valued/unvalued policies, general average, measure of indemnity. Referenced throughout all modules. legislation.gov.uk
L2 Insurance Act 2015 (UK) Reforms to pre-contractual duty of disclosure (fair presentation) replacing utmost good faith avoidance with proportionate remedies. Applies to all commercial marine cargo placements in the UK market. Referenced in history and underwriting modules. legislation.gov.uk
L3 Enterprise Act 2016 (UK) Introduced implied term of payment within a reasonable time; damages remedy for late claims payment — addresses a gap in the MIA 1906 regime. Relevant to cargo claims handling obligations. legislation.gov.uk

International Conventions & Cargo Liability Regimes

#SourceRelevanceURL
C1 Hague-Visby Rules (Visby Protocol 1968, SDR Protocol 1979) International sea carriage liability regime — carrier liability cap of 2 SDR/kg or 666.67 SDR/package (whichever greater). These limits are why cargo insurance is essential; carrier liability rarely covers actual cargo value. Referenced in overview and VL underwriting sections. UK Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1971
C2 Hamburg Rules (1978, effective 1992) UN convention replacing Hague-Visby for signatory states; higher carrier liability limits; adopted by ~34 states (not UK, USA, major shipping nations). Relevant to VL code cargo liability underwriting for trades to/from Hamburg Rules states. uncitral.un.org
C3 CMR Convention (1956) — Road Carriage International road carriage liability — limits carrier liability to 8.33 SDR/kg. Applies to international road cargo across most European states. Directly relevant to VL code road freight forwarder liability underwriting. unece.org
C4 York-Antwerp Rules 2016 International rules for the apportionment of General Average — incorporated by reference into virtually all bills of lading. The 2016 edition (updated from YAR 1994) is the current version governing GA adjustments for most modern open covers. Referenced throughout history, overview, and global programme modules. comitemaritime.org
C5 Warsaw Convention (1929) / Montreal Convention (1999) — Air Carriage Air cargo carrier liability — Montreal Convention (in force 2003) limits carrier liability to 19 SDR/kg for cargo. Most major trading nations have adopted. Relevant to V code air freight transit underwriting and VL code air forwarder liability. icao.int

Market Clauses & Policy Wordings

#SourceRelevanceURL
W1 Institute Cargo Clauses (A), (B), (C) — 2009 Edition (LMA/IUA) Current market standard policy wordings for marine cargo. Defines covered perils, exclusions, transit clause (warehouse-to-warehouse), classification clause, and General Average provisions. The single most important reference document for cargo underwriting. lmalloyds.com (member access)
W2 Institute War Clauses (Cargo) 2009 & Institute Strikes Clauses (Cargo) 2009 Standard wordings for war and strikes extensions to ICC cover. War clauses include 48-hour cancellation provision; strikes clauses typically non-cancellable. JWC High Risk Area list determines where additional premium applies. Referenced throughout underwriting and rates modules. lmalloyds.com (member access)
W3 Institute Classification Clause (ICC) — Current Version Specifies vessel class and age requirements for cargo cover to attach automatically. Vessels not meeting requirements require specific agreement. Standard inclusion in open covers. Referenced in underwriting and risk mitigation modules. lmalloyds.com
W4 Incoterms 2020 (ICC International Chamber of Commerce) International commercial terms defining risk transfer point between buyer and seller. Determines who has insurable interest and therefore who should be purchasing cargo insurance. CIF/CIP terms require minimum insurance standards. Referenced in overview module. iccwbo.org
W5 UCP 600 — Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (ICC, 2007) Article 28 specifies insurance document requirements for letter-of-credit transactions — minimum coverage basis (ICC A or equivalent), value (110% CIF minimum), transferability. Essential reference for commodity trade finance cargo placements. iccwbo.org

IMO & Regulatory Standards

#SourceRelevanceURL
R1 IMDG Code — International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code (IMO) Mandatory classification, packing, marking, documentation, and stowage requirements for dangerous goods by sea. 9 classes. Key to fire risk underwriting (misdeclared DG), DG endorsements, and fire/explosion claims investigation. imo.org
R2 CTU Code (IMO/ILO/UNECE, 2014) Container/Cargo Transport Unit Code — guidance on packing containers, weight distribution, securing, ventilation. Industry best practice standard; CTU Code compliance referenced as evidence of good risk management in underwriting. imo.org/CTUcode
R3 ISPS Code — International Ship and Port Facility Security Code (IMO, 2004) Mandatory security framework for ships and ports on international voyages. ISPS-compliant ports have better cargo security controls. Non-ISPS intermediate port calls may affect warranty compliance. imo.org/ISPS
R4 ISM Code — International Safety Management Code (IMO SOLAS Chapter IX) Mandatory safety management system for vessels over 500 GT on international voyages. ISM compliance is a vessel quality indicator; non-ISM vessels are a cargo underwriting red flag. imo.org/ISM
R5 ISPM 15 — International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures (IPPC/FAO) Phytosanitary treatment requirement for wood packaging materials in international trade — heat treatment or fumigation. Non-compliance results in cargo rejection; relevant to claims for stranded/destroyed goods. ippc.int

Industry & Market Sources

#SourceRelevanceURL
M1 IUMI — International Union of Marine Insurers — Ocean Cargo Statistics (annual) Global marine cargo premium statistics by market segment; loss ratio data; market size benchmarks. Primary source for the ~$25bn global marine cargo GWP figure (2023) used in the overview module. iumi.com/statistics
M2 Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) — Marine & Cargo Technical Updates Market-wide guidance on ICC clause interpretation, war risk developments, JWC High Risk Area updates, cargo accumulation modelling. JWC HRA list (Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, Gulf of Guinea, Black Sea) sourced from LMA/JWC. lmalloyds.com (member)
M3 Allianz Global Corporate & Specialty (AGCS) — Safety and Shipping Review (annual) Annual review of marine casualty trends, total losses, cargo claims by peril and region. Publicly available; frequently cited for vessel fire frequency, container loss overboard statistics, and top loss events. agcs.allianz.com
M4 Association of Average Adjusters — Annual Report General Average adjustment statistics, trends in GA declarations, GA reform discussions (York-Antwerp Rules usage). Key source for understanding GA claims complexity and resolution timescales referenced in overview and global programme modules. average-adjusters.com
M5 Cargo Theft Report — BSI / TT Club (annual) Annual analysis of cargo theft incidents by country, commodity, modus operandi. Primary source for theft hotspot data used in risk mitigation module (West Africa, South America, UK road routes). ttclub.com
M6 Paris MOU — Port State Control Annual Report Port State Control inspection and detention statistics by flag state and vessel type. Used in vessel quality assessment — carrier vetting, substandard vessel identification. Available free of charge. parismou.org

Known Data Gaps

⚠ Material Data Gaps — Flagged for Future Enhancement

  • GAP 1 Lloyd's Marine Cargo GWP: Lloyd's publishes aggregate marine market statistics but does not separate cargo from hull in publicly available data. The ~$5bn Lloyd's marine cargo GWP estimate is derived from IUMI global data and Lloyd's market share estimates — not from a disclosed Lloyd's source.
  • GAP 2 Lloyd's Marine Cargo Loss Ratio by Segment: Aggregate Lloyd's marine cargo loss ratio data is not publicly disclosed by peril, commodity, or trade lane. The loss ratio chart in the rates module uses IUMI aggregate data and market commentary, not disclosed Lloyd's class statistics.
  • GAP 3 Open Cover Rate Data: Marine cargo open cover rates are commercially sensitive and not published. All rate benchmarks in the underwriting module are market estimates based on publicly available broker surveys (AGCS, Marsh/Aon annual reviews) and industry commentary — not from a disclosed pricing database.
  • GAP 4 ULCV Accumulation PML Data: Lloyd's syndicates' actual per-vessel cargo accumulation PML figures are not publicly disclosed. The ULCV accumulation risk discussion is based on vessel capacity data (VER) and industry commentary about cargo value per vessel (EST).
  • GAP 5 Baltimore Bridge Collapse Insured Loss: At time of writing (April 2026), the final insured loss figure for the Dali/Baltimore bridge collapse is not settled. The $1.5bn+ figure is an early industry estimate; final insured loss will depend on the outcome of ongoing salvage, litigation, and claims settlement.
