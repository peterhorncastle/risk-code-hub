Underwriting Methodology

Rating factors, commodity risk benchmarks, open cover structure, accumulation management, and worked pricing examples for a containerised electronics trade and a bulk commodity voyage.

Overview History Timeline Claims Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

Marine cargo underwriting is primarily a frequency class — most cargo claims are relatively modest (transit damage, pilferage, short-landing). The severity tail comes from fire-at-sea, vessel sinking (especially on ultra-large container vessels), General Average events, and natural catastrophe at port. The underwriter's core job is to price the expected frequency of small claims against the capital cost of the low-probability, high-severity events that define the class's risk capital requirement.

1. Rating Formula — Open Cover Structure

Marine Cargo Premium Calculation (Open Cover Basis)

Annual Premium = (Estimated Annual Cargo Value) × Rate on Value (ROV) ROV = Base Commodity Rate × ICC Clause Multiplier × Trade Lane / War Risk Loading × Packing / Container Quality Factor × Insured Experience Factor (Loss Ratio Adjustment) × Accumulation / Limit Loading

All rates below are market estimates (ILLUS) — actual rates are commercially sensitive and vary by syndicate, account quality, and market cycle.

3. Key Rating Factors

Coverage

ICC Clause Selection

  • ICC (A): Base rate — benchmark for all risks. Theft, pilferage, non-delivery included. Most common for containerised, electronics, food, pharma
  • ICC (B): Rate reduction c.15–25% vs ICC A; no theft cover — used where theft risk is low or cargo is inherently theft-resistant (machinery, steel)
  • ICC (C): Rate reduction c.30–45% vs ICC A; basic perils only; used for bulk commodities (coal, steel, grain, timber) where all-risks premium would be uneconomic
Trade Route

Trade Lane & War Risk Loading

  • Standard transoceanic routes: No loading (Europe–Asia via Cape; Trans-Pacific; Trans-Atlantic)
  • Red Sea / Gulf of Aden (2024): +0.3–0.7% war risk surcharge for transit; most major carriers rerouting via Cape — no loading but +10–14 day delay
  • Gulf of Guinea / West Africa: +0.10–0.25% piracy/theft loading
  • Ukraine / Black Sea: JWC High Risk Area — additional war risk premium required; most cargo insurers withdrawn from new business
  • Air freight: Typically rated lower than sea on per-voyage basis (shorter transit) but per-unit values higher
Cargo Quality

Packing & Container Condition

  • Professional packing: Rate credit vs. self-packed; factory-packed preferred
  • Container condition: Survey required for one-trip or aged containers; rain or water damage risk from compromised door seals
  • CTU Code compliance: IMO/ILO/UNECE Container/Cargo Transport Unit Code — proper weight distribution, securing, ventilation
  • ISPM 15: Timber packing materials — phytosanitary treatment required for international trade; non-compliance creates cargo rejection risk
  • Dangerous goods declaration: IMDG compliance; misdeclaration is a leading cause of container fires — underwriter checks for DG screening procedures
Accumulation

Per-Vessel / Per-Location PML

  • Automatic limit (open cover): Maximum insured value per vessel or per sending — typically $5m–$50m depending on account size. Accumulation above automatic limit requires specific declaration
  • Vessel class check: ULCV (18,000+ TEU) commands higher per-vessel accumulation scrutiny; maximum value per vessel may reach $500m–$1bn+
  • Port accumulation: Tianjin 2015 exposed port accumulation risk. Cargo insurers now model maximum value in key ports (Rotterdam, Singapore, LA/LB, Tianjin, Dubai) and apply port PML stress tests
  • Warehouse accumulation: Stock throughput policies — warehouse section rated separately; limit per location required
Experience

Loss Record & Account Quality

  • 5-year loss history required for open cover renewals; loss ratio benchmarked against class average (typically 50–65% target)
  • Frequency claims (pilferage, short-landing, minor damage) indicate risk quality problems — poor handling, inadequate packing, high-theft trade lanes
  • Claims cooperation: prompt reporting, survey appointment, mitigation action all affect claims settlement quality and influence renewal pricing
  • Insured's risk management: cargo tracking systems, GPS monitoring, carrier vetting procedures — evidence of good management earns rate credit
Carrier

Vessel & Carrier Quality

  • Classification society: Lloyd's Register, DNV, Bureau Veritas — class certificate current; ISM Code compliance
  • Flag state: Open registries (Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands) — check flag state port state control (PSC) detention record; substandard vessels
  • Age warranty: Many Lloyd's cargo policies include an Institute Classification Clause — vessels over 25 years old or below class may require specific agreement or attract premium loading
  • Freight forwarder quality: FIATA/BIFA member forwarders operating to recognised standard trading conditions; NVOCC credentials

5. Worked Examples

Worked Example A · ILLUS

Electronics Open Cover — Singapore to Rotterdam (Sea/Air)

Risk Profile

  • Insured: UK electronics distributor
  • Commodity: Consumer electronics, laptops, mobile devices (c.15% contain li-ion batteries)
  • Trade: Singapore/Hong Kong → Rotterdam (sea) + occasional air freight (high-value items)
  • Annual cargo value: $45m
  • Per-vessel automatic limit: $3m
  • Policy: Open cover, ICC (A), warehouse-to-warehouse
  • Loss record (5yr): Loss ratio 48%; three frequency claims (pilferage/short); no large losses

Rating Calculation

  • Base commodity rate (electronics, ICC A): 0.30%
  • Li-ion battery loading (15% of shipments, +30%): +0.04%
  • Trade lane: Standard Asia–Europe; no JWC HRA loading (Cape reroute active 2024)
  • Packing: Factory-packed, professional FF used: −5%
  • Carrier quality: Named top-tier carriers only: −3%
  • Loss experience: 48% LR (below 60% target): −5% credit
  • Adjusted ROV: 0.34% × $45m = $153,000 p.a.
  • War risk (Institute War Clauses — Cargo): additional 0.025% = $11,250 p.a.

Indicative Annual Premium: ~$164,250 (basis $45m turnover; ICC A + Institute War Clauses) ILLUS

Worked Example B · ILLUS

Steel Coil Shipment — China to UK (Specific Voyage Policy)

Risk Profile

  • Insured: UK steel stockholder
  • Commodity: Cold-rolled steel coils (1,500 tonnes)
  • Trade: Shanghai → Teesport, UK (sea, 25 days)
  • Shipment value: $1.2m CIF (10% uplift = $1.32m insured value)
  • Policy: Specific voyage, ICC (B), vessel to be declared
  • Vessel: MV Pacific Star, 12yr old, Liberian flag, BV class, ISM compliant
  • Stowed below deck; properly secured per CTU Code

Rating Calculation

  • Base commodity rate (steel coil, ICC B): 0.12%
  • Vessel age (12yr — within Class Clause limits): no loading
  • Flag state (Liberia — clean PSC record): no loading
  • Stowage: below deck, well secured: −5%
  • Trade lane: East Asia to UK; no HRA loading
  • Voyage basis (specific, not annual): apply single-voyage factor ×1.0
  • Rate: 0.114% × $1.32m = $1,505 voyage premium
  • War risk (voyage endorsement): +$66

Indicative Voyage Premium: ~$1,571 (ICC B + War Clauses Cargo, basis $1.32m) ILLUS

6. Cargo Liability Underwriting (VL Code)

VL covers the legal liability of freight forwarders, logistics operators, and carriers to cargo owners — distinct from cargo insurers covering the cargo owner's own interest. Key considerations differ significantly from V code underwriting.

Coverage Basis

Liability Limit vs. Standard Terms

  • BIFA Standard Trading Conditions: Freight forwarder liability limited to £2 per kilo (UK) or SDR equivalent — vastly below actual cargo value for most goods
  • CMR Convention: Road carrier liability limited to 8.33 SDR per kilo
  • Hague-Visby Rules: Carrier liability: 2 SDR per kilo or 666.67 SDR per package (whichever greater)
  • VL covers the difference between the carrier's legal liability exposure and the cargo owner's actual loss — plus defence costs
  • Key question: Does the insured issue its own bills of lading (NVOCC)? If so, it may have assumed full carrier liability — significantly increases exposure
Key Rating Factors

VL Rating Considerations

  • Annual throughput value of goods handled
  • Type of operations: freight forwarder only (lower) vs. NVOCC (higher) vs. warehouse operator (higher)
  • Commodity types handled: high-value electronics, pharmaceuticals — higher liability; steel, bulk — lower
  • Own vehicles operated? Road haulier CMR exposure in addition to FF liability
  • Jurisdictions of operation: USA/Canada exposure substantially increases liability limits required (US courts award much higher damages)
  • Sub-contractors: Does the insured control sub-contractor quality? Sub-contractor insolvency (Hanjin 2016) creates stranded goods liability
