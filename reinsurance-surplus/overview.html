Surplus Treaty — Overview

The classic property treaty: proportional reinsurance where the cession varies risk-by-risk. The cedant keeps a fixed retention — a "line" — on every risk and cedes only the surplus above it, up to a set number of lines. It lets an insurer keep 100% of small risks and share only the large ones.

A surplus treaty is proportional — premium and losses are shared in the same ratio as the sum insured is ceded — but unlike a quota share that ratio is not fixed. The cedant sets a retention (the "line"), keeps that amount on every risk, and cedes the surplus above it up to a maximum number of lines. Small risks stay fully retained; large risks are shared. The effect is to homogenise the net account. VER

How the Cession Works

The line (retention)

The cedant fixes the amount it keeps net on each risk — e.g. £1m. This is one "line". Risks at or below the line are retained 100%.

The surplus

Anything above the retention is the surplus, ceded to the treaty. A £5m risk on a £1m line cedes £4m — an 80% cession on that risk.

Number of lines

The treaty caps capacity at N lines of the retention. A 9-line treaty on a £1m line provides £9m of surplus capacity, so £10m of total capacity.

The table of lines

A schedule setting retention by risk class/quality, so the cedant retains more of the risks it knows best and cedes more of the volatile ones.

Variable cession

Because the ceded share depends on sum insured, every risk cedes a different percentage — the defining feature versus quota share.

Homogenised net book

By capping every net retention at the line, the treaty flattens the spread of retained sums insured and stabilises the cedant's result.

Market Participants

Cedants

Property insurers with heterogeneous portfolios — commercial property, engineering, marine hull — where sums insured vary widely.

Reinsurers

Treaty reinsurers taking the surplus cession; the same panels that write quota share and property XoL.

Brokers

Treaty brokers structuring the retention, number of lines and commission, and marketing the placement.

Facultative market

Absorbs the top slice of risks that exceed the treaty's total capacity (retention + N lines).
