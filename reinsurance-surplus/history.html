Surplus Treaty — History

The surplus treaty is one of the oldest forms of reinsurance — the property market's answer to heterogeneous sums insured long before excess-of-loss existed.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Surplus reinsurance grew up with the professional reinsurers of the nineteenth century as the natural way to reinsure fire and property books where sums insured varied enormously. Its story is one of gradual displacement — first supplemented, then partly replaced, by excess-of-loss as modelling and capital markets matured. VER

Era 1 — Birth of the treaty (1840s–1900)

Cologne Re (1846), Swiss Re (1863) and Munich Re (1880) built their books on proportional treaties. For heterogeneous fire portfolios the surplus form was ideal: keep a retention on every risk, cede the surplus above it, and homogenise the net account without over-reinsuring small risks. VER

Era 2 — The property workhorse (1900–1970)

The table of lines, ceding commission and profit commission became standard. Surplus treaties were the backbone of property reinsurance worldwide; a cedant might run several surplus treaties stacked above its retention (first surplus, second surplus) before reaching facultative. EST

Era 3 — Excess-of-loss competes (1970–2000)

As catastrophe modelling and non-proportional pricing matured, per-risk and catastrophe excess-of-loss offered cheaper protection against the same large losses without ceding away premium. Many cedants moved from surplus to a higher net retention protected by XoL — surplus began a long, slow relative decline. VER

Era 4 — Capital & specialisation (2000–2020)

Solvency II made the capital value of proportional cover explicit, and quota share reclaimed ground for capital relief and fronting. Surplus persisted where it does best: heterogeneous commercial-property, engineering and marine-hull books with a wide spread of sums insured. EST

Era 5 — The specialist niche (2020–present)

Surplus is now a specialist rather than default tool — chosen when a book's spread of sums insured genuinely rewards a variable cession, and often combined with a quota share below and an excess-of-loss tower above. The 2023 hard market cut ceding commissions on proportional business before the 2025–26 softening began to restore them. EST

Key Ideas That Endure

The retention line

The single most important lever — it sets how much of every risk the cedant keeps and how much volatility it retains.

The table of lines

Scaling retention by risk class remains the elegant heart of surplus underwriting.

Homogenising the book

The founding purpose — flatten the net account so the retained portfolio behaves predictably.
