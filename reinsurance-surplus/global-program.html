Surplus Treaty — Programme Structuring

Where a surplus treaty sits in a property programme — often between a quota share below and an excess-of-loss tower above, with facultative filling the top of the largest risks.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Surplus is a middle layer of the property tower. A quota share may sit below it for capital relief on the whole account; the surplus treaty homogenises the retained sums insured; and an excess-of-loss tower then protects the net line against large single and catastrophe losses. Facultative fills the top of any risk that exceeds total treaty capacity. VER

How Surplus Sits In The Property Tower

Order of operationsWhat it does
1 · Gross bookThe cedant's full written property portfolio
2 · Quota share (optional)Cedes a flat % of the whole book for capital relief before the surplus works
3 · Surplus treatyCedes the surplus above the retention risk-by-risk — homogenising the net sums insured
4 · Net retained lineThe cedant's retention on each risk after the cessions
5 · Excess of lossProtects the net line against large single risks and catastrophe accumulation
6 · FacultativeFills the top of any risk that exceeds total treaty capacity

When Surplus Is The Right Tool

Wide spread of sums insured

Where a book runs from small shops to large factories, a flat quota share over-cedes the small risks; surplus keeps them net and shares only the large ones.

Retaining premium on good small risks

The cedant keeps 100% of the risks it prices best and knows most about, ceding away only the volatile large tail.

Non-cat, heterogeneous property

Its natural home is commercial property, engineering and marine hull — heterogeneous books where the variable cession genuinely earns its administrative cost.

The Placement Workflow

1

Portfolio & profile submission

The cedant and broker present the book, its spread of sums insured and loss history.

2

Retention & lines design

The table of lines, retention and number of lines are set to homogenise the net account and cap facultative need.

3

Commission & terms negotiation

Ceding commission (flat or sliding scale), profit commission, loss corridor and event limit are agreed.

4

Marketing & subscription

The broker builds a reinsurer panel to the required capacity and signs down.

5

Bordereaux & renewal management

Risk-by-risk cessions are reported and the loss ratio tracked against the sliding scale; re-underwrite at renewal.

Case Study — A Heterogeneous Commercial-Property Book

A regional insurer writes commercial property with sums insured from £100k to £20m and wants stable net results without over-reinsuring its small risks.

The problem

A flat quota share would cede away premium on thousands of small, well-priced risks; excess-of-loss alone would leave the large-risk sums insured lumpy on the net account.

The structure

A £1m retention with a 9-line surplus treaty (£10m total capacity) homogenises the net line; risks above £10m go facultative for the excess.

The terms

Sliding-scale ceding commission, a profit commission with carry-forward, and an event limit to cap windstorm/flood accumulation across the ceded book.

The result

The insurer keeps its profitable small risks whole, shares only the volatile large tail, and runs a smooth, predictable net account beneath its XoL tower.
