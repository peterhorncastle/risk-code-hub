Surplus Treaty — Underwriting & Pricing

Surplus pricing is proportional cession economics with one twist: the ceded share is computed risk-by-risk from the sum insured, the retention and the number of lines — not a single fixed percentage.

A surplus treaty shares premium and losses in proportion to the ceded sum insured. The one calculation that drives everything is the cession ratio for each risk. Once that is set, premium and losses flow pro-rata and the commission structure works exactly as in a quota share. VER

The Surplus Cession Formula

Ceded sum insured = min( Sum insured − Retention , Lines × Retention ), floored at 0

Cession ratio = Ceded sum insured ÷ Sum insured

Ceded premium = Cession ratio × Risk premium

Ceded losses = Cession ratio × Losses on the risk

Reinsurer result = Ceded premium − Ceded losses − Ceding commission − Brokerage − Profit commission

The Three Levers

Retention (the line)

Higher retention → the cedant keeps more of each risk → lower cessions and lower ceded premium, but more net volatility. The core capital-vs-stability trade-off.

Number of lines

More lines → more capacity above the retention → larger risks can be fully placed in the treaty rather than sent to facultative.

Ceding & profit commission

The reinsurer pays the cedant a ceding commission (its acquisition + expenses) and often a profit commission on the treaty's favourable result.

Worked Example 1 — A Single Large Risk

Setup: Retention (1 line) = £1m; treaty = 9 lines (£9m surplus capacity); a factory with SI £6m and risk premium £30,000. ILLUS

  • Ceded SI = min(£6m − £1m, 9 × £1m) = min(£5m, £9m) = £5m
  • Cession ratio = £5m ÷ £6m = 83.3%
  • Ceded premium = 83.3% × £30,000 = £25,000
  • The cedant retains £1m of SI (16.7%) and £5,000 of premium.
  • If the factory suffers a £3m loss, the reinsurer pays 83.3% = £2.5m; the cedant retains £0.5m.

Worked Example 2 — A Risk Beyond Treaty Capacity

Setup: Same £1m line, 9-line treaty (total capacity £10m); a risk with SI £14m. ILLUS

  • Surplus needed = £14m − £1m = £13m, but the treaty caps at 9 lines = £9m.
  • Ceded SI to treaty = £9m (cession 64.3%); the cedant retains its £1m line.
  • The remaining £4m sits outside the treaty — placed facultatively or retained as an unprotected exposure.
  • Lesson: the treaty homogenises up to its total capacity; above that, facultative fills the gap.

The Break-Even Loss Ratio

As with any proportional treaty, the reinsurer's break-even is set by the commission and brokerage it pays away. On a 30% ceding commission and 2.5% brokerage, the reinsurer breaks even at a 67.5% ceded loss ratio (100% − 30% − 2.5%). Profit commission then shares favourable experience back to the cedant. VER

Pre-Bind Checklist

Confirm the retention (line) and number of lines, and the total capacity they imply.
Review the table of lines — is retention appropriately scaled by risk class and quality?
Test the cession profile across the real portfolio, not just a headline risk.
Agree ceding commission (flat or sliding scale), profit commission and any loss carry-forward.
Check event limits / cat clauses — a surplus treaty can accumulate the same event across many risks.
Confirm what happens above total capacity — facultative support or a retained gap.
