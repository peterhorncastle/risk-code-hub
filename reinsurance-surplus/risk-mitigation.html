Surplus Treaty — Contract & Wordings

A surplus treaty's controls are the retention schedule that shapes what is ceded, and the clauses — event limit, sliding scale, profit commission — that shape what the reinsurer keeps of it.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Because a surplus treaty cedes a variable share of each risk, its risk controls start with the table of lines — how retention is set by class — and then use the same proportional clauses as quota share to shape the reinsurer's result. Six clauses do most of the work. VER

Table of lines

The retention schedule by risk class and quality — the surplus treaty's defining control. Set it well and the ceded book is balanced; set it poorly and the treaty inherits anti-selection.

Event / occurrence limit

Caps the reinsurer's share of any single event across all ceded risks — essential where the book accumulates (flood, quake, windstorm).

Sliding-scale commission

Ceding commission moves with the actual loss ratio between a floor and a cap — the treaty's automatic stabiliser.

Profit commission & carry-forward

Returns a share of profit to the cedant; a loss carry-forward means a bad year must be recouped before profit commission resumes.

Follow-the-fortunes / settlements

Binds the reinsurer to the cedant's good-faith claims decisions within treaty terms — the glue of the proportional relationship.

Cash-loss & funds withheld

Governs when large losses are paid in cash rather than via account, and whether premium is withheld by the cedant as security.

Counterparty & Collateral

Before binding

  • Check reinsurer security rating against your minimum.
  • For fronted / delegated surplus treaties, verify the back-end reinsurance panel and collateral — trace letters of credit to the issuing bank (the Vesttoo lesson).
  • Stress the table of lines against the real portfolio and confirm the event limit.

During the period

  • Track the ceded loss ratio against the sliding scale.
  • Monitor catastrophe accumulation across ceded risks against the event limit.
  • Watch counterparty credit and any concentration on the capacity panel.

⚠️ The accumulation trap in surplus cover

A surplus treaty spreads one event across every ceded risk it touches. Without an event limit, a single catastrophe flows through the treaty at each risk's full cession — turning a proportional property treaty into unpriced catastrophe cover. Always confirm the per-event cap and the table of lines before binding an accumulation-exposed book.
