Source categories, landmark loss events, confidence audit, and data gaps for the HA/AG hub.

Agricultural and forestry insurance data quality varies considerably by source. US MPCI data — published by USDA RMA — is the best-documented, publicly available crop insurance dataset in the world, providing county-level premium, indemnity, and participation data going back to 1975. European crop insurance data is fragmented across national programme administrators. Forestry loss data is the least transparent of any class — most large standing timber losses are settled under private facultative arrangements with no public reporting requirement. VER

Six Landmark Events in HA/AG Insurance History

December 1999

Windstorm Lothar — European Forestry Catastrophe

Windstorm Lothar struck France, Switzerland, and Germany on 26–27 December 1999, with wind speeds up to 200 km/h across northern France. Approximately 200 million cubic metres of timber were blown down across France, Switzerland, and Germany — the largest single windstorm forestry loss event in history. Total forestry losses were estimated at €3 billion; insured losses significantly less, as many forest estates were either uninsured or sub-limits had not anticipated event of this scale. The event fundamentally reshaped European forestry underwriting: windstorm became the primary modelled peril, timber cruise requirements were tightened, and sublimits specifically for windstorm blowdown were introduced. Lothar also demonstrated the crucial importance of immediate salvage — blown timber rapidly loses commercial value as bark beetle secondary infestation takes hold within 6–12 months in the affected temperate climate. VER

2012

US Drought 2012 — Largest MPCI Loss Event in History

The 2011–2012 drought was the most widespread drought in the continental United States since the 1930s Dust Bowl, affecting over 80% of US agricultural land. Corn and soybean yields collapsed: US corn yield fell to 123.4 bu/acre vs a trend yield of ~150 bu/acre. Total insured crop losses through the MPCI system reached $17.3 billion — nearly three times the previous record. The USDA RMA paid out approximately $17 billion in indemnities, with the programme recording a combined ratio approaching 300% for the drought year. The event demonstrated the systemic correlated nature of drought risk — when drought strikes, it strikes broadly and simultaneously across the whole portfolio. The 2012 loss was the primary driver of subsequent hardening in US MPCI reinsurance rates (2013–2015 vintage). VER

2003–2012

British Columbia Bark Beetle Epidemic — The Uninsured Catastrophe

The Mountain Pine Beetle (Dendroctonus ponderosae) epidemic in British Columbia was the largest recorded forest insect infestation in North American history, ultimately killing over 700 million cubic metres of standing lodgepole pine across 18 million hectares of BC forest. The epidemic, accelerated by milder winters that reduced natural beetle mortality, destroyed commercial timber volume equivalent to multiple decades of harvest. Despite the catastrophic scale — total economic loss exceeding C$100 billion in timber value — direct insured losses were minimal, because pest and disease is almost universally excluded from standing timber insurance policies. The epidemic remains the canonical example of why biological risk in forestry is uninsurable through conventional property mechanisms, and why forest owners cannot rely on insurance as a financial management tool for biological risk. VER

2018–2020

African Swine Fever (ASF) China — Livestock Catastrophe

African Swine Fever entered China's pig population in August 2018 and spread with devastating speed. By 2020, an estimated 40–50% of China's pig population — approximately 100 million animals — had been killed by ASF or culled as part of containment measures. China's pig herd at the time represented approximately half the world's total pig population. The scale of livestock insurance losses was unprecedented in the sector; Chinese agricultural insurance indemnities for livestock in 2018–2019 exceeded any prior year by a multiple. The episode demonstrated that livestock mortality events can have systemic, catastrophe-scale characteristics and fundamentally reshaped how reinsurers modelled aggregate livestock exposure in Asia. VER

2019–2020

Australia Black Summer — Wildfire Catastrophe & Climate Signal

Australia's 2019–2020 Black Summer bushfire season burned approximately 18.6 million hectares across south-eastern Australia from September 2019 to March 2020 — an area larger than England, Scotland, and Wales combined. Total insured losses across all classes reached approximately A$2.3 billion (including homeowners, farm property, commercial forestry, and vehicles). For the standing timber market, Black Summer represented confirmation that Australian plantation and native forest fire risk had been materially under-rated for years. The unprecedented scale — attributable to record heat, drought, and wind — triggered immediate re-rating of Australian AG standing timber risks (15–35% rate increases) and contributed to capacity withdrawal from the most exposed WUI-adjacent plantation zones. VER

2023

Canada Wildfire Season 2023 — New Record Scale

Canada's 2023 wildfire season burned approximately 18.4 million hectares — surpassing the previous national record by a factor of nearly eight. Over 230 individual wildfires burned simultaneously at the season peak. The city of Yellowknife (population 20,000) was evacuated. Fires burned in provinces that had not experienced large-scale wildfire in living memory, including Quebec and Nova Scotia. For the forestry insurance market, 2023 was the most significant single season in Canadian history — directly testing the adequacy of declared values, per-occurrence deductibles, and aggregate stop-loss protections across Canada-concentrated standing timber portfolios. The season reinforced the imperative of cat model review for Canadian forestry exposures. VER

Known Data Gaps

  • Lloyd's HA/AG split by sub-class: Lloyd's Aggregate Accounts report class-level performance but do not publicly distinguish between crop (HA) and forestry (AG) sub-classes, or between direct and reinsurance within each. No public source for Lloyd's-specific HA/AG combined ratio history by sub-class.
  • Forestry insurance market size: There is no authoritative global estimate for standing timber / forestry insurance premium volume. The vast majority of forestry insurance is written under private facultative arrangements with no public disclosure. Estimates range from $0.8bn to $2.5bn globally — a wide and poorly constrained range.
  • PMFBY reinsurance treaty terms: International reinsurers' actual economic terms for PMFBY participation are not publicly disclosed. Tender results indicate winning premium bids but not reinsurance structures. Published rate-on-line data for India PMFBY RI is unavailable.
  • Bark beetle insured loss data: Because pest and disease is excluded from virtually all AG standing timber policies, there is no meaningful insured loss figure for the BC bark beetle epidemic. The total economic loss (>C$100bn) is estimated but the insurance industry absorbed almost none of this loss directly.
  • Chinese agricultural insurance detailed loss data: China's agricultural insurance programme is the world's second-largest by premium volume but detailed programme-level loss data, reinsurance structures, and loss ratios are not publicly disclosed outside of Ministry of Agriculture annual reports (Chinese language only; limited international distribution).
