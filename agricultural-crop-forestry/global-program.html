Agricultural and forestry insurance is uniquely complex from a global programme perspective because of the deep involvement of government programmes — particularly in crop insurance. In the US, EU, Canada, India, and China, significant portions of primary crop insurance premium flow through government-backed structures with mandatory reinsurance cedants, tender processes, and admitted-only distribution. Lloyd's capacity enters primarily as reinsurance (excess-of-loss and quota share) of these national programmes, or as direct/facultative capacity for commercial risks not covered by government schemes. HA/AG Global Programme Structures

US MPCI Reinsurance Programme

The US FCIC/RMA operates a Standard Reinsurance Agreement (SRA) under which Approved Insurance Providers (AIPs) cede a quota share of crop premium and loss back to the federal government. Private reinsurers — including Lloyd's syndicates — buy reinsurance from the AIPs (a layers structure excess of AIP retained risk), providing capacity above government backstop thresholds. Lloyd's market capacity in US crop typically sits in per-occurrence excess-of-loss and stop-loss structures. VER

India PMFBY Tender Structure

India's Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) is tendered annually by state governments; winning insurers (typically 4–6 per season) are selected on premium bid. International reinsurers — including Lloyd's — submit capacity bids as part of the winning insurer's reinsurance structure. Lloyd's participates via quota share and per-risk / per-occurrence excess-of-loss layers. The PMFBY represents the world's largest crop insurance programme by policy count (~30 million farmers). VER

European Named-Peril & MPCI RI

European crop programmes (Spain's Agroseguro pool, France's CCR-backed MPCI, Italy's ISMEA programme) cede reinsurance to the private market through quota share or XL treaties. Lloyd's syndicates with specialist agricultural books write both proportional and non-proportional reinsurance layers on European crop programmes. Standalone direct hail cover for high-value specialty crops (wine regions, orchards) is written directly at Lloyd's under binding authorities with European MGAs. EST

Commercial Forestry — Direct & Fac RI

Large commercial forest estates (>10,000 ha) are typically placed via direct facultative insurance at Lloyd's — often led by a specialist forestry syndicate with following market. Mid-size estates (1,000–10,000 ha) may be placed via binding authority with a forestry specialist MGA. Cat reinsurance for domestic insurance companies with forestry portfolios is placed as per-occurrence XL with catastrophe model output (RMS/AIR European windstorm; North American wildfire) forming the basis for technical pricing. 8-Step International Forestry Placement Workflow

  1. Estate Survey & Valuation: Commission independent timber cruise / professional valuation for each estate in scope. For estates >500 ha, obtain formal valuation with species breakdown, age-class profile, and market value per m³. Declared values must be adequate — average clause applies. Update valuations every 3–5 years or following significant changes in timber prices.
  2. Risk Information Gathering: Compile full risk submission: estate maps with GPS coordinates, species composition (% by area), age class distribution, proximity to WUI / urban edge, access road map, fire break locations, irrigation sources if applicable, FSC/PEFC certification status, historical loss data for each estate (minimum 5 years).
  3. Admitted Requirements Analysis: For each country where estates are located, confirm admitted vs non-admitted requirements. Engage Lloyd's country office (if applicable) or local admitted fronting insurer where required by law. Germany, France, Spain typically require local policy for premium tax deductibility.
  4. Cat Model Run: For portfolios >£10m aggregate exposure, request cat model output from RMS European Windstorm Model and AIR North American Wildfire Model. Model output informs technical rate calculation and per-occurrence XL attachment point determination. Provide estate coordinates and declared values as model inputs.
  5. Submission to Specialist Market: Submit full risk package to specialist forestry underwriter — typically Lloyd's specialist forestry syndicate or dedicated forestry facility. Include timber cruise, valuation, estate maps, historical losses, cat model output, and confirmed FSC/PEFC status. Request indicative lead terms.
  6. Lead Syndicate Negotiation: Negotiate primary layer terms: sum insured, coverage basis (all-risk or named peril), sub-limits (reforestation, salvage, debris removal), deductible (per-occurrence or annual aggregate), and any warranty conditions (duty-to-salvage, notification period, biosecurity). Agree lead line size.
  7. Following Market Placement: Approach following market on agreed lead terms. For large commercial estates, may require multiple Lloyd's syndicates and company market participants to complete the placement. Confirm co-insurance slip endorsement and claims co-operation agreement for multi-syndicate placements.
  8. Local Policy Issuance & DIC/DIL Confirmation: Where local admitted policies are issued (Germany, France, etc.) for compliance, confirm that master Lloyd's policy provides difference-in-conditions (DIC) and difference-in-limits (DIL) coverage above local policy terms. Ensure no unintended gap or overlap between local and master coverage. Annual review trigger for regulatory changes.
Illustrative Case Study

Scandinavian Timber Group — Pan-European Forestry Programme Rebuild

A Swedish commercial forestry company with 220,000 ha of managed softwood plantations across Sweden, Finland, and Scotland approached Lloyd's following non-renewal of its expiring programme after two years of poor windstorm loss experience. The expiring programme had been placed on a single carrier basis with a domestic Swedish insurer; following two significant losses (a windstorm event in 2021: £4.2m and a fire event in Scotland in 2022: £3.1m), the incumbent declined renewal. EST

Programme Challenge 2 attritional losses in 24 months had produced a combined loss ratio of 184% for the incumbent insurer. No domestic Scandinavian market willing to lead without a material rate increase and cover restriction. FSC certification was current across all estates, but timber cruise had not been updated since 2019 — declared values potentially understated by 20–30% given timber price inflation.
Solution Implemented Updated timber cruise commissioned covering all three country estates — declared value revised from £95m to £118m. Submission made to Lloyd's specialist forestry underwriter with full loss narrative and loss prevention measures installed since 2022 (improved firebreaks in Scotland, windstorm monitoring sensors). Cat model run confirmed aggregate exposure within acceptable limits for Lloyd's capacity.
Structure Agreed Primary layer £25m: Lloyd's specialist forestry syndicate (40% line) + 3 following market syndicates. Buffer layer £20m xs £25m: Lloyd's capacity. Upper excess £15m xs £45m: London company market. Local admitted policies: Swedish and Finnish domestic insurers (SEK 20m each) with DIC/DIL wrap from Lloyd's master policy. Reforestation extension: £11.8m sublimit (10% of DV).
Outcome & Lessons Placement completed with 35% premium increase on a properly valued sum insured — total premium £387,000 vs £196,000 expiring (on old, understated declared value). Insured accepted the increase recognising the combination of loss experience, timber price inflation adjusting values, and improved cat model methodology. Key lesson: regular timber cruise updates protect against both under-insurance penalty and programme unplaceability following losses.
