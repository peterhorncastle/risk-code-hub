Module 02 — Timeline

Agricultural Crop & Forestry — Timeline

Twenty defining events from 1980 to 2024 spanning drought, flood, wildfire, windstorm, biological disasters, regulatory milestones, and market developments that shaped the HA/AG class.

Agricultural and forestry losses are driven by weather and biological events that interact with policy frameworks, subsidy structures, and commodity markets. This timeline traces the loss events and regulatory changes that define modern HA/AG underwriting. VER

1980
Regulatory

US Federal Crop Insurance Act — Private Market Created

The Federal Crop Insurance Act of 1980 allows Approved Insurance Providers (AIPs) to sell and service FCIC-reinsured crop insurance policies. Government reinsures and subsidises premiums — the fundamental structure of the US MPCI market. Participation remains low initially as farmers prefer to rely on disaster payments. VER

1988
Drought

US Great Plains Drought — $8bn Agricultural Loss

The most severe US drought since the 1930s affects the Great Plains and Corn Belt. Corn and soybean yields fall by 30–40% in affected areas. Total agricultural losses estimated at $8 billion. Government disaster relief payments far exceed crop insurance indemnities — triggering Congressional review of crop insurance programme structure. VER

1994
Regulatory

Federal Crop Insurance Reform Act — Participation Mandated

Congress mandates crop insurance purchase as a condition of receiving USDA farm programme benefits. Premium subsidies increased significantly. Participation in the US MPCI programme surges from below 30% of eligible acres to over 70% by 2000 — transforming crop insurance from a fringe product to a mainstream agricultural safety net. VER

1993
Flood

Great Midwest Flood — $5bn Crop Loss

The Great Flood of 1993 inundates 20 million acres of agricultural land across the Missouri and Mississippi River basins. Agricultural losses reach $5 billion. The flood demonstrates that US MPCI must cover both drought and excess moisture — and establishes flooding as a material US crop peril alongside drought. VER

December 1999
Windstorm

Windstorm Lothar — 200 Million m³ European Timber Destroyed

Windstorm Lothar sweeps across France, Switzerland, and Germany, destroying approximately 200 million cubic metres of standing timber — the largest single European forestry loss event on record. Total forestry losses estimated at €3 billion. The event triggers a fundamental repricing of European standing timber insurance and forces underwriters to model windstorm accumulation across forest estates. VER

2003
Drought / Heat

European Heat Wave — €13bn Agricultural Loss

The hottest European summer in 500 years causes catastrophic heat stress across crops in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the UK. French wheat yields fall 20%; wine harvest severely affected. Total European agricultural losses estimated at €13 billion. The event demonstrates that European temperate agriculture is vulnerable to heat extremes and accelerates MPCI development in Continental Europe. VER

2003
Wildfire

British Columbia Bark Beetle Epidemic Peaks

The mountain pine beetle (Dendroctonus ponderosae) epidemic in British Columbia — triggered by successive mild winters — devastates over 700 million cubic metres of pine forest between 2003 and 2012. The single largest forest insect infestation in North American history. Most standing timber policies either exclude or severely sub-limit pest/disease coverage, leaving forest owners largely uninsured. VER

January 2007
Windstorm

Windstorm Kyrill — Major European Forestry Loss

Windstorm Kyrill crosses northern Europe in January 2007, causing significant standing timber losses across Germany, Poland, Czech Republic, and the UK. German forestry losses from Kyrill estimated at €500 million. Combined with residual Lothar recovery costs, European standing timber underwriters face sustained poor loss experience through 2003–2008. VER

2008
Market

Global Food Price Crisis — Agricultural Risk Profile Elevated

Global food commodity prices spike to record levels in 2007–2008 — wheat, rice, corn, and soybean prices double or triple. The food crisis is driven by drought, ethanol demand, speculation, and export restrictions. It elevates food security and agricultural risk to a geopolitical priority, driving government investment in crop insurance infrastructure across emerging markets. VER

2012
Drought

US Corn Belt Drought — $17.3bn Record Insured Loss

The worst US drought since the 1950s devastates 80% of US agricultural land. Corn and soybean yields collapse — corn production falls 13% year-on-year. USDA RMA pays out $17.3 billion in crop insurance indemnities — the largest single-year US crop insurance payout on record. The event validates the MPCI programme's design but raises concerns about the programme's aggregate fiscal exposure. VER

2014
Regulatory

US Agricultural Act (Farm Bill) 2014 — Revenue Protection Dominant

The 2014 Farm Bill restructures federal farm support, phasing out direct payments and expanding crop insurance premium subsidies. Revenue Protection (RP) — which covers combined yield and price risk — becomes the dominant MPCI product type, overtaking yield-only protection. The integration of futures price into MPCI creates a structural link between crop insurance payouts and commodity markets. VER

2016
Regulatory

India PMFBY — World's Largest Crop Insurance Expansion

India's Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) replaces NAIS, offering simplified MPCI with the government paying 85–90% of premium. Over 56 million farmers enrol in the first year. By 2020, India's crop insurance programme is the world's largest by number of policies. International reinsurers — including Lloyd's syndicates — provide significant capacity through annual tender processes. VER

2017–2018
Wildfire

California Wildfire Season — Camp Fire & Wine Country Fires

California's 2017 and 2018 wildfire seasons produce record losses. The 2018 Camp Fire destroys the town of Paradise and burns 153,000 acres — the deadliest California wildfire in history. Agricultural losses in Napa and Sonoma wine regions (2017) and orchard/vineyard losses in subsequent years generate substantial HA crop insurance claims alongside property losses. Standing timber losses in forested areas create AG claims pressure. VER

2018–2020
Biological

African Swine Fever China — 100 Million Pigs Lost

African Swine Fever (ASF) spreads through China's pig population with devastating speed, killing an estimated 40–50% of China's pigs — approximately 100 million animals. Livestock insurance losses are on an unprecedented scale. The episode reshapes understanding of zoonotic disease as a potential agricultural insurance catastrophe and triggers review of livestock mortality aggregate controls by reinsurers. VER

2019–2020
Wildfire

Australian Black Summer — 18 Million Hectares Burned

Australia's 2019–2020 wildfire season burns 18.6 million hectares — the most destructive season on record. Agricultural losses include livestock mortality (approximately 1 billion animals), orchard and pasture destruction, and standing timber losses in commercial plantation forests of Victoria and New South Wales. Total agricultural insured losses estimated at A$2 billion. VER

2020–2022
Drought

La Niña Multi-Season Drought — South America & Australia

A sustained La Niña pattern drives multi-season drought across Argentina, Brazil (Paraná basin), and parts of Australia. South American soybean and corn yields fall significantly in 2021–2022; Argentina's agricultural GDP contracts. Total South American crop insurance exposure — much reinsured through Lloyd's and Swiss Re — approaches $5 billion across the drought period. EST

2023
Wildfire

Canada Wildfire Season — Record 18 Million Hectares Burned

Canada's 2023 wildfire season burns a record 18.4 million hectares — more than double the previous record set in 1995. Provinces including British Columbia, Alberta, Nova Scotia, and Quebec are severely affected. Commercial plantation losses and natural forest destruction generate significant AG standing timber claims. Smoke from Canadian fires affects air quality across North America. VER

2022–2023
Drought

European & Horn of Africa Drought — Climate Stress Intensifying

Extreme drought hits Western Europe in 2022 (Rhine, Loire, Po at historic lows); the Horn of Africa experiences its worst drought in 40 years, causing agricultural collapse in Ethiopia, Somalia, and Kenya. European crop losses generate MPCI claims across Spain, Italy, France, and Portugal. African index insurance programmes trigger payments at parametric thresholds, demonstrating their value in humanitarian contexts. VER

2023–2024
Market

Precision Agriculture Transforms Underwriting

Satellite monitoring (Planet Labs, Sentinel-2), AI-based yield modelling (Descartes Labs, Climate Corporation), and drone crop assessment reach commercial maturity. Lloyd's syndicates and large MGAs integrate satellite-based near-real-time crop monitoring into underwriting and claims workflows. The technology reduces both adverse selection (insurer can assess crop condition before binding) and moral hazard (remote monitoring detects unreported damage events). EST

2024
Biological

Avian Influenza — Sustained Global Outbreak & Poultry Insurance Losses

H5N1 avian influenza maintains its record global outbreak throughout 2022–2024, with over 100 million birds culled in the US alone since late 2021. Commercial poultry insurance losses are substantial — combination of direct mortality, mandatory culling costs, and business interruption. Outbreak also detected in dairy cattle (US 2024), expanding livestock insurance exposure. VER
