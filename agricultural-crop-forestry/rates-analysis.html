Agricultural and forestry insurance operates across two distinct sub-classes — crop (HA) and standing timber/forestry (AG) — with very different rate structures. Crop rates are heavily influenced by government subsidy mechanisms and USDA RMA county rate schedules in the US, while commercial forestry rates are driven by cat model output, WUI proximity, and species fire susceptibility. This analysis focuses on the commercial/reinsurance market view, excluding federally subsidised premium from the US retail MPCI market. EST

Four-Era Market Cycle

Era 1 · Pre-2012

Soft Market & Adverse Selection

The pre-2012 Lloyd's HA/AG market was characterised by competitive reinsurance pricing, as the 2000s had been relatively benign for major crop cat events in Europe. US MPCI reinsurance was attractive, with government subsidy effectively reducing private sector loss exposure. European hail rates were broadly flat or declining 2005–2011 as loss experience was manageable. Standing timber rates were low following a post-Lothar recovery. RoL index settled in the 90–100 range.

Era 2 · 2012–2016

2012 US Drought Shock & Hard Correction

The 2012 US drought — $17.3bn insured crop loss — was the defining market event. Lloyd's syndicates writing US MPCI reinsurance reported combined ratios exceeding 200% in 2012. Rates hardened 30–60% across US crop reinsurance lines immediately post-event. European hail markets also firmed 15–25% following concurrent European heat stress losses. RoL index peaked at approximately 145–160 by 2014–2015 before beginning a gradual softening cycle.

Era 3 · 2016–2020

Softening Cycle & Wildfire Emergence

Post-2012 hardening eroded steadily from 2016–2019 as several relatively benign crop loss years attracted capacity. The California wildfire seasons of 2017–2018 were primarily a homeowners/commercial property loss rather than a direct Lloyd's standing timber loss, but signalled the emerging scale of wildfire risk. Australian and Canadian wildfire losses began to register in forestry accounts. AG timber rates firmed 25–40% from 2018 as underwriters revised fire assumptions. Crop lines remained competitive.

Era 4 · 2020–2024

Climate Repricing & Selective Capacity

The 2019–2020 Australian Black Summer and 2023 Canadian wildfire season (18.4m ha) drove sustained hardening in AG standing timber lines — RoL index rising to 160–185 by 2024. Crop lines experienced moderate hardening driven by European drought losses (2018, 2022) and La Niña pattern losses in Australia and Latin America. Underwriters began applying explicit climate loading factors. Selective capacity withdrawal from highest-risk WUI-adjacent forestry further tightened supply.

Catastrophe Accumulation Warning: The stress scenario illustrates the key risk of agricultural and forestry insurance — drought and wildfire events can simultaneously affect millions of acres/hectares in the same geographic region, creating highly correlated losses across a portfolio. A £50m HA/AG portfolio with heavy US MPCI concentration has a realistic 1-in-10 year loss potential of 80–120% combined ratio. Cat modelling (RMS North America Crop, AIR European Windstorm) is essential for portfolios exceeding £10m aggregate exposure. Syndicates should monitor ENSO phase forecasts from NOAA/IRI quarterly as a forward-looking rate adequacy indicator.
