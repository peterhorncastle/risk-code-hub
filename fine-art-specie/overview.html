Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

FA — Fine Art

All-risks agreed-value insurance for artworks, antiques and collectibles. Covers paintings, sculptures, prints, drawings, ceramics, tapestries and decorative arts. Clients include private collectors, museums, galleries, art dealers and auction houses. Lloyd's is the dominant global market; many of the world's most significant private and institutional collections are insured here. VER

GS — Gold & Silver / Specie

Insurance of precious metals (gold, silver, platinum), loose gemstones (unset diamonds, rubies, emeralds), bullion in transit and specie held at refineries, mints or central bank vaults. Covers single shipments worth $50–200m+. Transit cover is a dominant exposure; storage cover at approved vaults available. High concentration of global gold bullion transit risk placed at Lloyd's. VER

JB — Jewellers Block

All-risks cover for jewellery businesses covering stock on premises, in transit, at exhibitions, in showcase displays and in customer custody for repairs/cleaning. The standard product for jewellery manufacturers, wholesale diamantaires, retailers and repair shops. Peak exposure events (Christmas, Valentine's Day, Baselworld/GIA gem shows) are a critical underwriting consideration. EST

CT — Cash in Transit / Specie

Insurance of cash, negotiable instruments, banknotes and other valuables during transportation by armoured vehicle or secure courier. Covers bank-to-bank transfers, ATM replenishment runs, casino cash movements and high-value retail cash collection. Typically written on a per-shipment or blanket annual basis. Territory is a key rating variable — South Africa and Brazil attract material surcharges. EST

Coverage Taxonomy

All-Risks Physical Loss / Damage

The core insuring agreement. Covers accidental loss or damage from any cause not excluded — including theft, fire, flood, transit damage, accidental breakage and malicious damage. Written on agreed value basis: the sum insured is the agreed settlement; no depreciation or underinsurance argument applies. Expert appraisal is critical. VER

Transit Cover (Nail-to-Nail)

Full cover from the moment an artwork or specie item leaves its home location to its return — encompassing packing, road/air transit, temporary exhibition, and unpacking. Also called "wall-to-wall." Loan exhibitions, art fairs and international transport are major transit exposures. Armoured vehicle specification and qualified art handling conditions apply. VER

Mysterious Disappearance

Unexplained disappearance — where no evidence of theft exists but an item cannot be accounted for. A standard insuring agreement in fine art policies, addressing the reality that inventory checks reveal missing items without apparent forced entry. Jewellers block policies also cover this for stock items. Subject to proof of ownership and valuation. VER

Restoration & Conservation Costs

Where a work of art is damaged (rather than destroyed), the policy pays the reasonable cost of professional conservation and restoration, up to the agreed value. Conservation must be carried out by approved specialist conservators. This distinguishes fine art cover from standard property insurance which would pay market value less salvage. VER

Title / Authentication Risk

A limited-specialist coverage addressing financial loss from forgery discovery or provenance dispute — e.g., where a purchased work is found to be a fake (Knoedler gallery fraud) or subject to Nazi-era looting restitution claim. Very few insurers offer this; typically a standalone product with rigorous due diligence requirements and low limits. VER

Third-Party Liability (Bailee)

Where a gallery, museum or jeweller holds artworks or jewellery belonging to a third party — as bailee — liability cover for loss of or damage to those items in custody. Museums holding loan exhibitions from other institutions, jewellers holding items for repair, auction houses holding consignments — all face bailee liability exposure. VER

Lloyd's Market Dominance: The global fine art and specie insurance market is centred at Lloyd's of London. The specialist syndicates operating in this class have developed unique wordings — including the "Velasquez" fine art wording and bespoke jewellers block clauses — that have become market standards. Lloyd's syndicates write a significant proportion of the world's major museum collections, private billionaire collections, central bank gold transit and the Antwerp diamond district's daily specie exposure. The Art Loss Register (headquartered in London) is the world's largest database of stolen art, and Lloyd's market participants are its principal supporters. VER
Emerging Risk — NFT / Digital Art: The 2021 boom in non-fungible tokens (NFTs), culminating in Beeple's "Everydays" selling for $69m at Christie's, raised novel coverage questions. A physical artwork has physical loss exposure; an NFT exists purely on a blockchain. Standard fine art policies require "physical loss or damage" — an NFT wallet hack or platform failure does not trigger physical loss. New digital art coverage products are in development, but as of 2026 no standard market wording exists. Underwriters must establish whether a client's "digital art" collection has physical manifestation (prints, displays) or is purely digital title. VER
