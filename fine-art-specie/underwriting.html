Rating Factors — High Impact (Fine Art / FA)
High Impact — Fine Art

Agreed Value of Collection & Appraisal Quality

The primary rating input for fine art is the agreed sum insured, derived from professional appraisal. Appraisal quality is critical: stale appraisals (over 3 years old for fast-appreciating markets), unqualified appraisers, or speculative market values create both over- and under-insurance risk. Lloyd's underwriters typically require RICS, ASA or accredited appraiser credentials. VER

Rate-on-value basis: 0.05% to 0.40%+ depending on security, transit & location factors
High Impact — Fine Art

Storage & Display Location

Location quality drives fine art rating materially. A Grade I listed building with museum-standard environmental controls, NSI Gold-certified intruder alarm, CCTV and 24-hour security guard is the best risk. A domestic residence with standard home alarm and no guard attracts a significant loading. Geographic location (flood zone, wildfire risk, earthquake exposure) also matters. VER

Museum-standard: 0.05–0.10% ROV; private unguarded: 0.15–0.35% ROV
High Impact — Fine Art

Transit Frequency & Destination

Loan exhibitions internationally are the highest-risk transit events. Frequency of loans, destinations (stable Western institutions vs. higher-risk jurisdictions), quality of transit contractors (qualified art handlers, climate-controlled vehicles, GPS tracking), and adequacy of borrowing institution security all affect transit premium. The "nail-to-nail" extension must be explicitly priced. VER

Annual loan to top-tier museum: minimal loading; multiple international loans: +20–50%
High Impact — Fine Art

Provenance & Title Clarity

Works with clear provenance documentation are preferred risks. Works with gaps in provenance covering 1933–1945 (the Nazi looting period) or from jurisdictions with weak export controls attract loading due to title risk and potential restitution claims under the Washington Principles 1998 and the Holocaust (Return of Cultural Objects) Act 2009. VER

Title dispute risk: typically excluded or sublimited; provenance gap: + underwriting scrutiny
Rating Factors — High Impact (Specie / JB / CT)
High Impact — JB / Specie

Total Stock Value & Peak Exposure

Jewellers block is rated primarily on stock value. Peak exposure events — Christmas season (December), Valentine's Day and Baselworld / GIA gem show attendance — can represent 2–4× average stock values. Declaration of maximum SI at peak and maintenance of adequate sums insured throughout the year are critical. Underinsurance at loss date can be argued by insurers even on agreed-value policies if declarations are materially inaccurate. VER

JB rate: 0.15–1.2% of maximum stock value depending on territory and security
High Impact — JB / Specie

Vault / Safe Certification

Vault and safe specification is the single most important physical security rating factor for specie and jewellers block. EN 1143-1 (European) and UL (US) certified safes/vaults are required for meaningful premium credit. Safe grade (I through VII under EN 1143-1) determines the cash resistance and attack resistance rating. Underwriters will not offer competitive terms without certified safe/vault specification. VER

EN Grade III+ safe: credit vs. domestic safe; EN Grade VI vault: maximum credit
High Impact — JB / CT

Transit Method (Armoured vs. Unarmoured)

Cash in transit and high-value specie transit risk is fundamentally determined by whether armoured vehicles with trained armed guards are used. Armoured CIT carriers (Brinks, G4S, Loomis) provide a materially different risk profile to courier or personal carriage. For JB, personal transit (jeweller carrying stock by hand or unarmoured vehicle) attracts the highest transit rates — up to 3–4× armoured equivalents. GPS tracking and route variation requirements are standard conditions. EST

Armoured CIT: 0.05–0.12% per transit; personal/unarmoured: 0.15–0.40%+
High Impact — JB / CT

Territory (High-Crime Jurisdiction Surcharge)

Geographic territory is a major JB and CT rating variable. South Africa, Brazil, Venezuela and certain other Latin American and African territories attract material surcharges due to documented high rates of armed robbery and CIT vehicle attacks. Antwerp (diamond district) is a high-concentration risk. Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia) attracts modest surcharges. UK, Switzerland and Singapore are preferred territories. VER

Brazil / South Africa CIT surcharge: 2–4× UK equivalent rate; Antwerp diamond: specific terms
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Alarm & CCTV Specification

NSI Gold-certified or NACOSS-approved intruder alarm with confirmed police response (URN) is the minimum standard for preferred FA/JB terms. CCTV with minimum 30-day retention, covering all entry points and display areas. Remote monitoring by a BSI-approved Alarm Receiving Centre (ARC). Absence of compliant systems results in either coverage exclusion or significant premium loading. VER

Medium Impact

Security Guard Specification

24-hour manned guarding by SIA-licensed officers is the gold standard for museum and gallery risks and large private collections. For jewellery retailers, a panic button (silent alarm to police) and dual-person close-of-business procedure are standard requirements. Remote CCTV monitoring without guards is acceptable for lower-value risks but not for agreed values above £2m per item. VER

Medium Impact

Exhibition / Art Fair Attendance

Exhibiting at Baselworld, GIA gem shows, Frieze Art Fair, TEFAF Maastricht, Art Basel Miami — concentrations of high-value artworks or jewellery in temporary venues with multiple access points and mixed visitor populations. Transit to and from fairs, in-fair display security (armoured showcases, armed guards) and overnight vault specification are all rating factors. Specific exhibition endorsements required; blanket policy cover may not respond. VER

Medium Impact

Prior Claims History (3–5 Year)

Art theft is sticky: properties previously targeted are at elevated risk of re-targeting. Prior transit damage claims can indicate inadequate handling procedures. A theft claim without adequate management response (security upgrade) is a strong loading factor. Mysterious disappearance claims require close scrutiny — insurers may impose inventory control conditions post-claim. VER

Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Conservation & Environmental Standards

Temperature (18–21°C) and relative humidity (45–55% RH) control reduces gradual deterioration risk and demonstrates the insured's commitment to proper stewardship. Relevant mainly for works on paper, photographs and textiles — which are particularly vulnerable to environmental damage. Museum-standard storage conditions attract a modest credit vs. non-climate-controlled domestic storage.

Lower Impact

Appraisal Recency

Current professional appraisal (within 3 years for actively appreciating sectors) ensures agreed values reflect market reality and reduces over-insurance / under-insurance claims disputes. Works in rapidly appreciating sectors (contemporary art, rare watches) may require annual appraisal updates. Stale appraisals trigger a requirement for re-submission before renewal.

Lower Impact

Collection Cataloguing & Documentation

A fully documented, digitally catalogued collection with photographs of each item, provenance records, exhibition history and condition reports is the insurer's best claims evidence — and the insured's best proof of ownership and value. Absence of documentation dramatically complicates both theft claims (Art Loss Register search) and total loss settlements. Cataloguing standards are increasingly an underwriting requirement for large collections.

Lower Impact

Art Loss Register Registration (Pre-Purchase)

Purchasing art after conducting an Art Loss Register search demonstrates due diligence in title and provenance. ALR registration of the collection supports rapid stolen art recovery if a theft occurs. Some fine art policies now include a pre-purchase ALR search as a condition of cover for new acquisitions above a threshold value (typically £50,000+).

Premium Rating Formula (Fine Art / JB)
// Fine Art & Jewellers Block — indicative rate-on-value formula
Base Premium = Agreed Value (£) × Base Rate-on-Value

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Security Factor // 0.7× (museum-grade) → 2.0× (minimal security)
  × Location Factor // 0.8× (UK/Swiss preferred) → 2.5× (high-crime territory)
  × Transit Loading // +0.02–0.10% per loan/transit event
  × Claims History Factor // −10% (5yr clean) → +100% (recent theft)
  × Concentration Factor // +loading for art fair / gem show exhibition
  + Art Fair Endorsement // per event: 0.05–0.15% of exhibit value
  − Deductible Credit // self-insured excess: −5% to −20%

Agreed Value Basis — Critical Principle: All fine art and high-value specie policies in the Lloyd's market are written on an agreed value basis. This means the sum insured stated in the policy schedule is the amount that will be paid in the event of total loss — there is no depreciation, no market value argument and no average (co-insurance) provision. This makes professional, current appraisal essential: if the agreed value is set too low (underinsurance), the insured bears the shortfall. Lloyd's underwriters have the right to refuse renewal at agreed values they consider speculative or unsupported by credible appraisal evidence. VER
