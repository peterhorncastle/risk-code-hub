Quota Share — Programme Structuring

Where a quota share sits in a reinsurance programme — as the capital-relief base beneath an excess-of-loss tower, and as the engine of fronting and MGA capacity.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Quota share is the base of a proportional-plus-excess programme. It cedes a share of the whole book to release capital and share results; excess-of-loss then sits on top of the cedant's net retention to cap single-risk and catastrophe losses. The two are complementary, not alternatives. VER

How QS Sits With XoL

Order of operationsWhat it does
1 · Gross bookThe cedant's full written portfolio
2 · Quota shareCedes a fixed % of the whole book — releasing capital and sharing every risk and loss
3 · Net retained bookWhat the cedant keeps after the QS cession
4 · Excess of lossProtects the net retained line against large single risks and catastrophe accumulation

Three Ways Quota Share Is Used

Capital relief

An established insurer cedes 20–40% of a growing book to hold its Solvency II SCR within appetite while it expands — the fastest capital lever available.

Fronting / MGA capacity

A fronting carrier issues the paper and cedes 80–100% to reinsurers via quota share, earning a fronting fee; the MGA underwrites under delegated authority.

New-line support

A cedant entering a new class or territory cedes a large share while the book is unproven, retaining more as experience builds and confidence grows.

The Placement Workflow

1

Portfolio & loss-ratio submission

The cedant and broker present the book, its history and a projected loss ratio.

2

Commission & structure negotiation

Cession %, ceding commission (flat or sliding scale), profit commission, loss corridor and event limit are agreed.

3

Marketing & subscription

The broker builds a panel of reinsurers to the required capacity and signs down.

4

Wording & collateral

Treaty wording and, for fronted deals, collateral / funds-withheld terms finalised.

5

In-force management & renewal

Track the loss ratio against the sliding scale and corridor; re-underwrite at renewal.

Case Study — A Fast-Growing MGA

An MGA writing £100m of specialty GWP needs capacity and capital it does not have, and a fronting carrier to issue the paper.

The problem

The MGA has distribution and underwriting talent but no balance sheet; the fronting carrier will only lend its paper if the risk is ceded on.

The structure

A ~90% quota share cedes most of the book to a panel of reinsurers; the fronting carrier retains a small share and earns a fronting fee; the MGA earns commission.

The terms

Sliding-scale ceding commission aligns the MGA's incentive with loss-ratio performance; a loss corridor and event limit protect the reinsurers; collateral secures the front.

The risk

Concentration on the capacity panel and the fronting carrier's own reinsurance quality — the Vesttoo lesson — is the dominant exposure to manage.
