Reinsurance — Proportional Treaty

Quota Share — Overview

The simplest and most capital-efficient reinsurance structure. Under a quota share (QS) treaty the reinsurer takes a fixed percentage of every risk in a defined book — sharing the same proportion of premium and losses — and pays the cedant a ceding commission for the business. It is the workhorse of proportional reinsurance: capital relief, capacity and partnership in one contract.

A "30% quota share" means the reinsurer takes 30% of every policy's premium and pays 30% of every loss — proportionally, from the first pound. Because the reinsurer inherits the cedant's acquisition and expense costs, it pays back a ceding commission, and often a profit commission if the treaty performs. Unlike excess-of-loss, QS is not about capping a peak loss — it is about sharing the whole book to release capital, add capacity and align the two parties' interests. VER

Why Cedants Buy Quota Share

🏦

Capital relief

Ceding a share of the book reduces required capital (Solvency II SCR, Lloyd's capital) proportionally — the fastest lever a growing insurer has.

🚀

Capacity & growth

Lets a cedant write more premium than its own balance sheet supports — essential for a new line, territory or a fast-growing MGA.

📉

Result smoothing

Shares every loss proportionally, dampening the net result — though it cedes profit as well as loss.

🤝

Expertise & alignment

The reinsurer shares the fortunes of the whole book, so it brings pricing, wordings and claims support — a genuine partnership.

🌍

Fronting support

Underpins fronting and MGA arrangements where the paper-issuer cedes most of the risk to the real risk-carrier.

💷

Cash flow

The ceding commission is received up front, helping fund acquisition costs on growing books.

The Economics — Ceding & Profit Commission

QS pricing is not a rate — it is a negotiation over commissions. The reinsurer's result is the ceded premium less ceded losses, ceding commission and brokerage; the levers that move it are the commissions. VER

FeatureWhat it isWhy it matters
Cession %The fixed share ceded (e.g. 30%)Sets how much premium, loss and capital transfer
Ceding commission% of ceded premium paid back to the cedantThe main negotiation — reimburses acquisition/expense; rises in soft markets
Sliding-scale commissionCeding commission that moves with the actual loss ratioAligns both parties — better result, higher commission
Profit commissionShare of the treaty's profit returned to the cedantRewards a well-performing book after a reinsurer expense margin
Loss corridorA band of loss ratio the cedant retains netLimits the reinsurer's exposure to deterioration
Event / loss limitCaps the reinsurer's share of any one eventStops a catastrophe turning a proportional treaty into unlimited cover

Quota Share vs Surplus vs XoL

StructureTypeHow cover works
Quota shareProportionalFixed % of every risk ceded — premium and loss shared in the same ratio
SurplusProportionalVariable % — cedes only the part of each risk above a retention (by "lines")
Excess of lossNon-proportionalReinsurer pays only the part of a loss above an attachment; no premium sharing
The ceding-commission cycle In a soft market cedants push ceding commissions up (35%+) as reinsurers compete for premium; in a hard market reinsurers pull them down and add loss corridors. A sliding-scale commission plus a profit commission aligns both sides — this is the heart of every proportional renewal. The Underwriting module and Rater work the numbers.
