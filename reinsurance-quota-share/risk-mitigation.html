Quota Share — Contract & Wordings

Because a quota share shares the whole loss ratio, its risk controls are not layers but clauses: the loss corridor, event limit, sliding scale and profit commission that shape exactly what the reinsurer takes.

A quota share transfers the whole loss ratio proportionally, so the reinsurer's protection against a bad year is written into the treaty terms rather than an attachment point. Five clauses do most of the work. VER

Loss corridor

A band of loss ratio (e.g. 75–85%) the cedant retains net before the reinsurer resumes its share above it — caps the reinsurer's exposure to moderate deterioration and keeps the cedant's skin in the game.

Event / occurrence limit

Caps the reinsurer's share of any single event, so a catastrophe cannot turn a proportional treaty into unlimited cat cover.

Sliding-scale commission

Ceding commission moves with the actual loss ratio between a floor and a cap — the treaty's automatic stabiliser.

Profit commission & carry-forward

Returns a share of profit to the cedant; a loss carry-forward means a bad year must be recouped before profit commission is paid again.

Follow-the-fortunes / settlements

Binds the reinsurer to the cedant's good-faith claims decisions within the treaty terms — the glue of the proportional relationship.

Cash-loss & funds withheld

Governs when large losses are paid in cash rather than via account, and whether premium is withheld by the cedant as security.

Counterparty & Collateral

Before binding

  • Check reinsurer security rating against your minimum.
  • For fronted / delegated quota shares, verify the back-end reinsurance panel and any collateral — trace letters of credit to the issuing bank (the Vesttoo lesson).
  • Agree the commission structure, loss corridor and event limit precisely.

During the period

  • Track the loss ratio against the sliding scale and loss corridor.
  • Monitor catastrophe accumulation against the event limit.
  • Watch counterparty credit — a fronting carrier's reinsurance-quality failure becomes the cedant's problem.

⚠️ The catastrophe trap in proportional cover

Without an event limit, a single catastrophe flows through a quota share at the full cession — turning a proportional treaty into unlimited cat cover. The 2005 KRW season taught the market to write event limits and loss corridors into cat-exposed proportional treaties. Always confirm the per-event cap before binding.
