Quota Share — Pricing Methodology

How a quota share is actually priced: cession economics, the ceding commission negotiation, sliding-scale and profit commissions, and the loss corridor — the numbers behind the Quota Share Rater.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

Quota share has no rate-on-line and no burning cost — it is priced by cession economics. The reinsurer takes a fixed share of premium and pays the same share of losses; because it inherits the cedant's costs it pays a ceding commission back, and its result is simply ceded premium less ceded losses, ceding commission and brokerage. Everything turns on the commissions. VER

1 · The Core Identity

Proportional — reinsurer result
Ceded Premium = GNPI × Cession %
Ceded Losses = Ceded Premium × Expected Loss Ratio
Ceding Commission = Ceded Premium × Ceding Comm %
Reinsurer Result = Ceded Premium − Ceded Losses − Ceding Commission − Brokerage
Reinsurer Combined Ratio = (Ceded Losses + Ceding Commission + Brokerage) ÷ Ceded Premium
GNPI = Gross Net Premium Income (premium net of other reinsurance)

2 · The Commission Levers

Ceding commission

A flat % of ceded premium reimbursing the cedant's acquisition and expense costs. The single biggest number in the negotiation; it rises in soft markets and falls in hard ones.

Sliding-scale commission

The ceding commission varies with the actual loss ratio — higher when the book runs well, lower when it deteriorates, between a floor and a cap. Aligns both parties' interests.

Profit commission

A share of the treaty's profit (after a reinsurer management expense, often with a loss carry-forward) returned to the cedant — rewards a genuinely profitable book.

Loss corridor

A band of loss ratio (e.g. 75–85%) the cedant retains net, capping the reinsurer's exposure to moderate deterioration before cover resumes above the band.

Sliding scale in one line A treaty might pay 35% ceding commission at a 55% loss ratio, sliding down 1-for-1 to a 25% floor at a 65% loss ratio, and up to a 40% cap at a 50% loss ratio. The scale turns the commission into a shared incentive: the cedant keeps more when the book runs well, the reinsurer keeps more when it doesn't.

Worked Example A — 40% Quota Share

40% QS of a UK motor book

Cedant GNPI £50,000,000 · Cession 40% · Expected loss ratio 62% · Ceding commission 30% · Brokerage 2.5%
Ceded premium (£50m × 40%)£20,000,000
Ceded losses (£20m × 62%)−£12,400,000
Ceding commission (£20m × 30%)−£6,000,000
Brokerage (£20m × 2.5%)−£500,000
Reinsurer technical result+£1,100,000
Reinsurer combined ratio94.5%

The reinsurer keeps £1.1m — a 5.5% margin on ceded premium. Note how sensitive this is: at a 35% ceding commission the treaty runs at a small loss, which is exactly why the commission is the negotiated pressure-point.

Worked Example B — Adding a Profit Commission

Same treaty, 20% profit commission after 5% reinsurer expense

Ceded premium £20m · Ceded losses £12.4m · Ceding commission £6.0m · Reinsurer management expense 5% of ceded premium (£1.0m)
Treaty profit before PC (£20m − £12.4m − £6.0m − £1.0m)£600,000
Profit commission to cedant (20% × £0.6m)−£120,000
Reinsurer result after profit commission£480,000

The profit commission hands 20% of the profit back to the cedant, aligning incentives at the cost of some reinsurer margin. In a bad year the profit is nil, no profit commission is paid, and any loss carry-forward reduces future profit commissions.

Pre-Bind Checklist — Quota Share

  1. Book & scope. Which classes, territories and years are in and out, and how homogeneous is the underlying portfolio?
  2. Loss ratio view. The cedant's projected loss ratio vs the reinsurer's independent view — the gap drives the commission.
  3. Commission structure. Flat vs sliding scale (floor, cap, slide), plus any profit commission and loss carry-forward.
  4. Loss corridor & event limit. What deterioration the cedant retains, and the cap on the reinsurer's share of any one event.
  5. Cash flow & funds withheld. Whether premium is paid over or withheld, and the interest terms.
  6. Aggregation. How the ceded book correlates with the reinsurer's other treaties and its retrocession.
  7. Wording. Follow-the-fortunes, sunset, cash-loss, sanctions and exclusions.
