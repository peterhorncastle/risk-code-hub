P&I vs. Lloyd's Split

The IG P&I Clubs cover the vast majority of liability arising from vessel ownership on a mutual basis (crewing, cargo claims, collision ¾, pollution). Lloyd's fills the gaps: charterers' liability, non-club operators, top-up limits beyond club coverage, fixed & floating objects, and SRL. The two markets are complementary, not competitive, for most large-tonnage risks.

Loss Intelligence Database

Landmark marine losses driving today's market

Every hub contains a filterable database of real historic losses. These four events — drawn from the platform's 1,750+ event dataset — shaped modern marine insurance structurally, legally and technically.

2012 — Marine Hull
Costa Concordia — Grounding, Giglio Island
$2.4bn
Largest single marine hull loss. 32 fatalities. Capt. Schettino convicted. Redefined wreck removal requirements; Nairobi Convention now mandates financial security for wreck removal globally. Total salvage operation took 3.5 years.
2022–ongoing — War Risks
Ukraine Black Sea — Fleet War Losses
~$1bn+ est.
JWC Listed Area (Feb 2022). Numerous vessel total losses in Odessa and port approaches. Triggered complete repricing of Black Sea hull war — rates rose from 0.025% to 5%+ overnight. Largest war risk event since the Iran-Iraq Tanker War of the 1980s.
2021 — Marine Cargo
Ever Given — Suez Canal Blockage
~$1bn+ total (cargo + delay)
Container vessel grounded for 6 days, blocking the Suez Canal entirely. ~400 vessels delayed. General average declared. Exposed cargo aggregation risk on ultra-large container vessels and supply-chain business interruption. Prompted ICC review of delay coverage.
2024 — Marine Liability
Dali — Francis Scott Key Bridge, Baltimore
$2–4bn est.
Container vessel lost propulsion and struck the Francis Scott Key Bridge; bridge collapsed killing 6 workers and closing the Port of Baltimore for weeks. Most complex US maritime liability event in decades — touching G (hull collision liability), GX (infrastructure), cargo delay, and wreck removal simultaneously.

Technical Architecture

How the platform is built

Designed for zero operational cost and maximum portability — fully static, no CMS, no database, no server.

Rating Engine
rater-engine.js + 195 JSON schemas
A shared pure-JS rating engine supports two model types: rate-on-value (property, marine) and frequency×severity (warranty, liability). Each risk code has its own JSON schema defining exposure measure, base rate, rating factors (categorical / band / numeric), fixed costs and variable loads. Output: gross premium, P&L waterfall, combined ratio.
Vanilla JSJSON SchemasNo dependencies
Programme Builder
programme-engine.js — multi-line tool
Cross-hub programme modelling: layer towers, reinsurance cessions (quota share, surplus, XL fac, XL treaty), territory splits, and net retention calculation. Designed to mirror how a Lloyd's broker structures a cross-class programme for a multinational insured.
