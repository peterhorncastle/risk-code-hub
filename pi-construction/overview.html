Lloyd's Risk Codes

E6
Construction & Engineering Consultants
Architects, structural engineers, civil engineers, M&E engineers, quantity surveyors, building surveyors, project managers, planning consultants, CDM Principal Designers
E7
Design & Build Contractors
Contractors who carry design liability under D&B or EPC contracts — bears both construction risk AND professional design risk. Includes design-led infrastructure contractors

E6 covers pure professional consultants whose only deliverable is advice, design, or supervision — no physical construction. E7 covers contractors who have contractually accepted design responsibility, creating a combined PI + contractor risk profile. E7 risks typically attract higher premiums and additional loading for fitness-for-purpose warranty risk not present in E6 consultant covers.

Coverage — What Construction PI Insures

Design Error & Negligent Specification

The core insuring clause: civil liability arising from a negligent act, error or omission in the performance of professional services. Covers claims arising from defective designs, incorrect specifications, or erroneous calculations causing loss to the client or third parties.

  • Structural calculation errors (undersized beams, inadequate foundations)
  • Specification of non-compliant materials (cladding, fire-stopping, fire doors)
  • M&E design clashes causing installation rework
  • Architectural design deficiencies requiring demolition or significant remediation

Supervision & Inspection Failures

PI covers the professional's failure to identify defects during site inspection or contract administration — liability arises not from the physical defect itself but from the failure of professional oversight to catch it.

  • Failure to identify non-compliant workmanship during inspection
  • Incorrect certification of works (architect's certificate of practical completion)
  • Failure to issue timely instructions causing delay or defect
  • Contract administration errors in valuation and payment

Cost & Programme Advice

QS and project management PI includes claims arising from inaccurate cost advice, programme failures, and project delivery failures where the professional owed a duty of care in relation to cost and time.

  • Negligent cost planning — significant underestimate inducing client to commit to project
  • Construction economics errors (incorrect procurement advice, wrong contract form)
  • Project management failure — critical path errors causing delay costs
  • Valuation errors in homebuyer or RICS Red Book valuations

Building Safety Act 2022 — New Liability

The Building Safety Act 2022 creates significant retrospective liability for construction professionals. VER

  • 30-year retrospective limitation for fire safety defects in higher risk buildings (HRBs)
  • New cause of action in tort — enables homeowners to sue designers, contractors
  • Building Liability Orders — holding company made liable for subsidiary's PI
  • Higher Risk Building regime (>18m / 7+ storeys): mandatory Gateway 1/2/3 process; Building Safety Regulator (BSR) oversight
  • Principal Designer (under BSA) — new mandatory competence requirements; separate from CDM Principal Designer role

Fire Safety & Cladding

Post-Grenfell, cladding and passive fire protection specification is the dominant risk driver for architects and fire engineers on residential and mixed-use buildings. VER

  • ACM cladding specification (Arconic Reynobond PE — Grenfell material)
  • Passive fire protection design failures (compartmentation, fire stopping)
  • Fire strategy report errors (fire engineered approach accepting non-standard design)
  • EWS1 form certification — retrospective liability for surveyors and fire engineers
  • Combustible insulation specification on HRBs

BIM & Digital Design Risk

Building Information Modelling (BIM) coordination creates new professional liability exposures — clash detection failure, data handover errors, and model coordination disputes.

  • BIM coordination failure — undetected design clashes causing site rework costs
  • COBie data handover errors — incorrect asset data causing FM lifecycle cost overruns
  • Golden Thread (BSA requirement) — digital record-keeping obligations for HRB lifecycle
  • ISO 19650 information management failures
  • Cyber risk in BIM environments — model corruption, ransomware on design software

E6 vs E7 — The Design Liability Distinction

Why E7 (Design & Build) Carries Higher Risk Than E6 (Consultants)

The single most important distinction in construction PI underwriting is the difference between consultant PI (E6) and design-and-build contractor PI (E7). It arises from a legal distinction that most clients and many professionals do not fully appreciate:

  • E6 Consultant standard — "Reasonable skill and care" (Bolam test): A design professional's duty is to exercise the skill and care of a reasonably competent member of their profession. If a design choice was within the range of reasonable professional judgment at the time, the professional is not liable even if the design fails. This is the standard under the Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 and general tort law.
  • E7 Contractor risk — "Fitness for purpose": Under many design and build contracts (particularly where the client specifies an output, not a design), the contractor implicitly warrants that the completed building will be fit for its intended purpose. This is a strict liability obligation — if the building is unfit, the contractor is liable regardless of whether the design met the professional standard. JCT DB 2016 cl. 2.17.1 attempts to limit this to "reasonable skill and care" — but only where explicitly incorporated; many D&B contracts do not include this carve-out. VER
  • E7 PI coverage gap: Standard PI policies cover "reasonable skill and care" duty — not fitness-for-purpose strict liability. Many E7 policies exclude fitness-for-purpose warranties not backed by professional skill-and-care obligations. Underwriters should carefully check the contract terms for D&B risks and ensure any fitness-for-purpose warranty is either excluded or specifically rated.
  • Implication: E7 risks carry higher base rates than equivalent E6 risks on the same fee income; fitness-for-purpose exposure requires additional rating or endorsement exclusion; D&B contractors should be advised to cap fitness-for-purpose liability to reasonable skill and care in their contracts.

Jurisdictional Coverage

UK (Primary Market)

  • Building Safety Act 2022 — dominant regulatory driver
  • Defective Premises Act 1972 (s.1) — original 6-year limitation extended to 30 years for new BSA s.135 duty VER
  • Architects Registration Board (ARB) — mandatory PI for registered architects
  • RICS Mandatory PI requirements — regulated firms
  • Claims-made standard; retroactive date critical

Middle East (Major Market)

  • UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar — large infrastructure and hospitality construction; significant UK/European consultancy involvement
  • No equivalent of UK ARB/RICS mandatory PI — contractually driven
  • FIDIC Yellow Book (D&B) and Silver Book (EPC) standard contract forms
  • Dispute resolution: ICC arbitration, DIAC, ADCCAC — international arbitration environment
  • Lloyd's PI coverage frequently purchased for international project work by UK/EU consultants

Australia

  • Strong mandatory PI regime — Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 (NSW) — registered practitioners must hold PI VER
  • Cladding combustibility legislation (each state) — mirrors UK Grenfell response
  • High claims frequency; class action risk increasing (social inflation)
  • Novation of consultant appointments from developer to D&B contractor — creates complex PI chain

Europe

  • EU Construction Products Regulation (CPR) — mandatory CE marking for construction products; performance declarations
  • Eurocodes — harmonised structural design standards (EC2, EC3, EC7 etc.)
  • France: Assurance dommages-ouvrage (DO) and Responsabilité civile décennale (RCD) — 10-year strict liability for structural defects, compulsory for contractors and some professionals VER
  • Germany: Architects' chambers (Architektenkammer) — PI minimum requirements vary by state
