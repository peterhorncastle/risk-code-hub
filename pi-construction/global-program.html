Why International Construction PI Requires Specialist Programme Design

Large engineering consultancies (Arup, Atkins, Jacobs, Mott MacDonald, WSP) operate across 50+ countries simultaneously — designing infrastructure, buildings, and industrial facilities under local law, local contract forms, and local dispute resolution regimes. A single Lloyd's policy written on UK law with a UK jurisdiction clause cannot provide adequate coverage for a claim brought in a Dubai arbitration tribunal, a Sydney court, or a Saudi adjudication panel.

Construction PI programme design for international consultancies requires: jurisdiction-specific coverage grant; coordinated retroactive dates across all group entities; run-off tail management for completed projects; and aggregation management across all jurisdictions in case of a systemic failure (design methodology error affecting multiple concurrent projects).

Run-Off Management

Run-Off — The Critical Long-Tail Issue for Construction PI

Run-off is the extension of claims-made PI cover beyond the end of active practice — to protect against claims arising from completed historic work. Construction PI run-off is more complex than most professional lines because of the extraordinary tail length.

  • Standard run-off period: 6 years, matching the original Defective Premises Act 1972 limitation period. Most PI policies provide a 6-year run-off endorsement for retired or dissolved practices.
  • Post-BSA 2022 run-off issue: The Building Safety Act 2022 extended the limitation period to 30 years for new dwellings and 15 years for existing HRBs — retroactively. This means that construction professionals who retired in 2010 with 6-year run-off cover may now face claims from HRB work done in 2000 that are still within the extended BSA limitation period, with NO INSURANCE. This is the most significant structural insurance protection gap in UK construction PI.
  • Run-off pricing: Typically 250–300% of last annual premium in year 1; 150% year 2; 100% year 3; 50% year 4; 25% years 5–6; nil thereafter under standard 6-year run-off. For HRB-exposed practices seeking extended run-off, pricing is bespoke and capacity very limited.
  • Corporate restructuring risk: D&B contractors frequently restructure through special purpose vehicles (SPVs) for individual projects. When the SPV is dissolved after project completion, any PI cover held by the SPV lapses. BSA 2022 Building Liability Orders (BLOs) can make the holding company liable for the dissolved SPV's liabilities — but only if the BLO is granted, which is not automatic. Underwriters should be aware of SPV structures in E7 submissions and assess whether building liability chain of responsibility has been properly maintained.

Project-Specific PI vs. Annual Practice Policy

Annual Practice Policy (Standard)

The standard E6/E7 policy covers all professional services rendered by the practice during the policy year — any project, any client, within the defined scope of practice.

  • Premium base: annual fee income (E6) or design fee equivalent turnover (E7)
  • Automatic cover for all projects within the definition of professional services
  • Claims-made: all claims first made during the policy year, regardless of when the work was done (subject to retroactive date)
  • Simpler to administer; no per-project declarations required
  • Sub-limits may apply: cladding, HRB, EWS1, ground investigation failures

Project-Specific PI

Large single projects — major infrastructure, nuclear, offshore — may require (or benefit from) project-specific PI in addition to or instead of the practice policy.

  • Single project defined; limit set at project value / design fee
  • Long-term policy period (project completion + tail period, up to 10 years)
  • All consultants on the project covered under one policy — avoids inter-consultant disputes
  • Premium: based on design fee income for the project; typically higher rate than annual practice PI due to concentration risk
  • Common for: nuclear (NNB GenCo/EDF projects); offshore energy (FIDIC Silver Book EPC); major PFI; national infrastructure (HS2, Crossrail)

Mandatory PI Endorsements

Some jurisdictions and sectors require specific endorsements or covers in addition to standard PI.

  • France: RCD/DO endorsement — 10-year strict liability cover (separate from standard PI)
  • Australia NSW: DBPA 2020 compliance endorsement — policy must confirm compliance with mandatory PI requirements
  • UK nuclear: Nuclear Installations Act compliance endorsement — specific nuclear liability regime
  • US work exclusion: explicit exclusion endorsement required for non-US practices with no US work — prevents inadvertent coverage creep
  • EWS1 sub-limit: specific EWS1 fire safety assessment sub-limit where assessor role accepted

Aggregation — Systemic Design Failure Risk

The most severe E6 risk scenario is a systemic design failure — where a single design error (methodology, software, specification) is replicated across multiple concurrent projects.

  • Example: structural calculation software bug produces incorrect beam sizing on 15 projects; all 15 buildings require structural remediation
  • Aggregation clause: each occurrence treated as separate claim (good for insured) vs. single occurrence (good for insurer where multiple linked claims arise from single error)
  • Underwriters should consider aggregation clauses specifically for E6 risks with standardised design processes — template designs, standardised structural systems, proprietary software reliance
  • RAAC as aggregation event: structural engineers who assessed multiple buildings using the same assessment methodology may face aggregate exposure if the methodology is found to be deficient

Worked Case Study — International Engineering Consultancy

Case Study: Mid-Size International Engineering Consultancy ILLUS

Practice Profile

  • 350-person civil and structural engineering consultancy
  • Annual fee income: £28m (65% UK/Europe; 25% Middle East; 10% Asia-Pacific)
  • Services: structural engineering, civil engineering, geotechnical, environmental
  • No residential work; no HRB exposure; no fire engineering
  • Key sectors: commercial, infrastructure (roads, bridges, rail), water, education
  • ISO 9001 certified; independent design check protocols for major structures
  • 5-year claims history: 2 closed claims (£85k and £210k); no open claims
  • Retroactive date: 2001 (22 years prior acts)

Programme Structure

  • Layer 1 (SIR): £100,000 per claim, self-insured
  • Layer 2 (Primary): £5m xs £100k SIR — Lloyd's lead syndicate
  • Layer 3 (XS1): £10m xs £5m — Lloyd's co-subscription
  • Layer 4 (XS2): £10m xs £15m — London market (AIG)
  • Total limit: £25m each claim
  • Basis: claims-made; worldwide jurisdiction excl. USA/Canada
  • Territory endorsements: France (RCD/DO compliance); Australia (DBPA 2020); Middle East (FIDIC FfP endorsement - excluded)
  • USA/Canada: nil exposure; explicit exclusion confirmed

Premium Calculation ILLUS

  • Fee income: £28,000,000
  • Base rate (structural/civil engineer): 0.95%
  • Profession factor (ICE/IStructE; ISO 9001; IDC protocols): 0.88 (credit)
  • Sector factor (no HRB; no residential; infrastructure): 0.95
  • Claims loading (2 closed claims, 5 years): +12%
  • Prior acts loading (22-year retro date): +18%
  • International territory loading (25% ME, 10% APAC): +8%
  • Base calculated premium: £28m × 0.95% × 0.88 × 0.95 × 1.12 × 1.18 × 1.08
  • = £28m × 1.123% = £314,440
  • ILF (base £5m → £25m limit): ×2.20
  • Final premium: £314,440 × 2.20 = ~£692,000

Key Programme Features

  • £100k SIR funded from annual risk budget; claim handling below SIR managed by in-house legal team
  • Retroactive date 2001: full prior acts; complete 22-year project file archive maintained on SharePoint (ISO 19650-compliant CDE)
  • FIDIC fitness-for-purpose endorsement: FfP warranty excluded; Middle East contracts reviewed by programme broker before signing to ensure FfP not accepted
  • France: separate RCD policy placed via French admitted insurer (Allianz France); UK programme DIL excess only
  • Annual programme review with Howden construction PI team; broker coordinates territorial compliance audit
Programme Summary: Total limit £25m each claim; annual premium ~£692,000; effective rate on limit ~2.77%. SIR £100k. Claims-made; retro 2001; worldwide excl. USA/Canada. France RCD separate (not in premium above). Lloyd's provides primary + XS1; AIG XS2. ILLUS — for training use only.
