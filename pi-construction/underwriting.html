Underwriting — PI E&O Construction & Engineering

Rating methodology, key underwriting factors, E6 vs E7 risk differentiation, scorecard, and worked examples for construction and engineering PI.

Rating Formula

E6 Premium = Fee Income × Base Rate % × Profession Factor × Sector Factor × HRB Loading × Territory Factor × Claims Loading

E7 Premium = Design Fee Equivalent × Base Rate % × D&B Factor (1.3–2.0×) × Fitness-for-Purpose Factor × Project Type × Territory Factor × Claims Loading

Limit Factor (ILF) applied where requested limit exceeds base limit (typically £1m or £2m)

Retroactive Date Loading: Clean retro date (inception) = base rate; full prior acts coverage (back to firm founding) = +15–30% loading for long-established practices
Note: Fee income is the standard premium base for E6 professional PI. For E7 Design & Build, the "design fee equivalent" is extracted from the total contract sum — typically 8–15% of contract value for building works; 12–20% for complex/specialist structures. All figures illustrative.

Key Underwriting Factors — E6 Consultants

Profession Type

The profession is the primary underwriting differentiator within E6. Base rates vary significantly by profession and claim frequency history.

  • Architect: Highest base rate E6 sub-class post-Grenfell; residential high-rise = highest loading. Current base rate ~1.5–3.0% of fee income (range wide post-BSA)
  • Structural engineer: High; RAAC and BSA retrospective tail uncertainty. ~1.0–2.5%
  • QS/surveyor: Moderate; SAAMCO cap limits quantum; high frequency low severity. ~0.8–1.8%
  • M&E engineer: Moderate; BIM coordination risk; fire engineering = uplift. ~0.8–1.5%
  • Civil/infrastructure: Variable; major infrastructure = lower frequency but higher severity. ~0.7–1.4%
  • Project manager: Moderate; delay and cost overrun claims. ~0.9–1.8%

Building Type & HRB Exposure

The Building Safety Act 2022 creates a mandatory risk distinction between Higher Risk Buildings (HRBs, >18m / 7+ storeys residential) and all other buildings.

  • HRB residential work: +40–80% loading on base premium; some underwriters decline entirely
  • HRB mixed-use (residential element >7 storeys): +25–50% loading
  • Commercial high-rise (non-residential, no HRB definition): +10–20% loading for fire strategy work
  • Low-rise residential (<7 storeys): base rate, no HRB loading
  • Infrastructure, civil, industrial: base rate for non-residential structural work

Retroactive Date & Run-Off

Claims-made policies require careful retroactive date management — the fundamental structural issue for construction PI.

  • Inception date retro (no prior acts): cleanest risk; base rate
  • Full prior acts (retro to firm founding): highest risk; +15–30% loading; requires full claims history review
  • Run-off premium: 200–300% year 1; 150% year 2; 100% year 3; 50% year 4; 25% year 5–6; nil thereafter (6-year standard run-off; 30 years for BSA-exposed practices)
  • BSA 2022 tail: for architects and structural engineers with HRB work pre-2017, run-off tails potentially extend to 30 years — run-off pricing models being revised

Claims History

Claims-made PI underwriting relies heavily on claims history as the primary frequency predictor.

  • 5-year claim-free: −10–15% loading (rate credit)
  • 1 claim (resolved): neutral or minor uplift (+5–10%)
  • 1 open claim: +20–35% loading; may require facultative reinsurance at higher limits
  • 2+ claims in 5 years: +40–80%; possible restrictions on cover; senior underwriter authority
  • Unresolved cladding/fire claim: possible declination or exclusion endorsement
  • Never presented for PI: treat as unknown risk — apply neutral loading; request explanation

Limit & Excess Structure

Limit adequacy is a critical underwriting issue in construction PI — inadequate limits are frequently a source of dispute between client and professional.

  • Minimum limit guidance: ARB recommends limit proportionate to contract value; RICS recommends £1m minimum for most regulated activities
  • ILF schedule: £1m base; £2m ~1.4×; £5m ~2.0×; £10m ~2.8×; £25m ~4.5×; £50m ~7.0× (illustrative ILF curve)
  • Aggregate vs. per-claim: construction PI typically per-claim; aggregate sub-limits for some high-risk categories (cladding, HRB)
  • Excess: minimum £1,000–£5,000 for small firms; £25,000–£100,000 for large practices; E7 excess typically £50,000–£250,000

International Work & Jurisdiction

UK-based construction professionals frequently work on projects in Middle East, Asia-Pacific, and Europe — each carrying different PI risk profiles.

  • UK/EU only: base rate; EU professional liability law broadly similar standard
  • Middle East (UAE, Saudi, Qatar): +10–20% loading; FIDIC contracts; ICC arbitration; no equivalent professional PI mandatory requirements
  • Australia: +15–25% loading; social inflation increasing; NSW DBPA 2020 mandatory PI regime
  • USA/Canada: excluded from standard E6/E7 PI; separate E2/E3 (US PI class) required
  • Developing markets: +20–40% loading; governance and contract enforcement risk

Worked Example A — E6: Architectural Practice

Example A: Mid-Size Architectural Practice — Commercial & Education Sector ILLUS

Practice Profile

  • 12-partner architectural practice, RIBA/ARB registered
  • Annual fee income: £3,200,000
  • Work split: 60% commercial offices, 25% education (schools, universities), 15% industrial
  • No residential work; no HRB work
  • ISO 9001 certified; QMS audit 2023 passed
  • 5-year claims history: 1 resolved claim (2021, £45k settlement, subrogated architect error, roof specification)
  • Retroactive date: 2008 (15 years prior acts)
  • Territory: UK only

Rating Calculation

  • Fee income: £3,200,000
  • Base rate (architect, commercial sector): 1.2%
  • Profession factor (RIBA/ARB, ISO 9001): 0.90 (credit)
  • Sector factor (commercial/education, no HRB): 1.00
  • HRB loading: 0% (no residential/HRB)
  • Claims loading (1 resolved claim 2021): +8%
  • Prior acts loading (15-year retro date): +12%
  • Territory: UK only — no loading
  • Calculated premium: £3,200,000 × 1.2% × 0.90 × 1.00 × 1.08 × 1.12
  • = £3,200,000 × 1.313% = ~£42,000
  • Minimum premium check: satisfied
  • Requested limit: £5m each and every claim / £10m aggregate
  • ILF (base £2m → £5m/£10m): ×1.65
  • Final premium: ~£42,000 × 1.65 = ~£69,300
Indicative premium: ~£69,300 for £5m/£10m aggregate limit, £50k excess, UK territory, 1-year claims-made, retro date 2008. Minimum excess £50,000 applied. ILLUS — for training use only.

Worked Example B — E7: Design & Build Contractor

Example B: Regional D&B Contractor — Commercial & Industrial ILLUS

Contractor Profile

  • Regional D&B contractor; annual turnover £45m
  • Design work (extracted): typically 12% of contract sum = £5,400,000 design value equivalent
  • Project type: commercial warehouses (40%), industrial units (35%), retail fit-out (25%)
  • No residential; no HRB
  • In-house architect and structural engineer; external M&E consultant
  • JCT D&B 2016 contract — cl. 2.17.1 reasonable skill and care included (no FfP warranty)
  • 5-year claims history: nil
  • Retroactive date: inception (no prior acts required)
  • Territory: UK only

Rating Calculation

  • Design fee equivalent: £5,400,000
  • Base rate (D&B contractor, commercial): 1.8%
  • D&B multiplier (design 12%; R,S&C only; no FfP): 1.15
  • Fitness-for-purpose factor: 1.00 (FfP excluded in JCT cl. 2.17.1)
  • Sector factor (commercial/industrial, no HRB): 1.00
  • Claims loading (5-year nil claims): −10%
  • Retroactive date (inception only): 0% loading
  • Territory: UK only
  • Calculated premium: £5,400,000 × 1.8% × 1.15 × 1.00 × 0.90
  • = £5,400,000 × 1.863% = ~£100,600
  • Requested limit: £5m each and every claim / £10m aggregate
  • ILF (base £2m → £5m/£10m): ×1.65
  • Final premium: ~£100,600 × 1.65 = ~£166,000
Indicative premium: ~£166,000 for £5m/£10m aggregate limit, £100k excess, UK territory, 1-year claims-made, inception retro date. Fitness-for-purpose exclusion endorsement applied per JCT cl.2.17.1 confirmation. ILLUS — for training use only.

Claims-Made Basis — Critical Structural Issues for Construction PI

Construction PI is almost universally written on a claims-made basis — the policy responds to claims first made during the policy period, not to the date of the negligent act. This is essential for construction given the 15–30 year latency of building defect claims. Underwriters must be vigilant about three structural issues:

  • Retroactive date continuity: Any gap in claims-made cover creates an uninsured period for claims from work done in that gap. Professionals must maintain continuous cover from the date of their earliest unresolved work. Underwriters should request evidence of continuous cover going back to the retroactive date.
  • Run-off on firm dissolution: When a practice dissolves (merger, retirement, insolvency), the claims-made policy lapses — but claims from historic work may arrive for years or decades. Run-off cover is the solution. ARB requires continuing PI for architects for 6 years after ceasing practice. BSA 2022 implications potentially extend this requirement informally for HRB-exposed practices.
  • Circumstance notification: On claims-made policies, a circumstance that might give rise to a claim must be notified to the current insurer even if no formal claim has been received. Failure to notify a known circumstance before policy expiry = uninsured. Underwriters should include clear "circumstance notification" wording and ensure insureds understand the obligation.
