Market Cycle — E6/E7 Construction PI

2005–2012 · Soft Market

Pre-Grenfell Soft Cycle

Post-Murphy v Brentwood stability; limited major loss events; market competitive. PFI boom driving E7 volume. BIM emerging as risk but not yet material. Rate indices declining 2005–2010. Combined ratios around 90–95%.

2012–2016 · Transitional

Market Stabilises; BIM Risk Emerges

Government BIM mandate 2011; market begins pricing BIM coordination risk. CDM 2015 creates new Principal Designer exposure. Rate increases modest (+3–8% pa). Some frequency increase in D&B contractor claims as PFI projects complete. Combined ratios 88–95%.

2017–2020 · Crisis

Grenfell — Market Hardening

Grenfell Tower fire (June 2017) transforms the market. 2018–2020: capacity withdraws from HRB residential architects; cladding exclusions applied industry-wide; rates increase 25–40% pa for affected professions. Fire engineer PI becomes very difficult to place. EWS1 form creates new PI sub-class. Combined ratios 105–120%.

2021–2022 · Hard Peak

Building Safety Act — Peak Hardening

Building Safety Act enacted 2022; 30-year retrospective limitation creates maximum tail uncertainty. Market at hardest in 20 years for construction PI. Some professions (fire engineers, HRB architects) face declination or prohibitive premiums. Combined ratios 98–108% for total book (HRB exposed sub-classes much worse).

2023–2024 · Partial Stabilisation

Non-HRB Stabilises; HRB Remains Hard

Non-residential and non-HRB construction PI shows rate stabilisation and some modest competition returning as 2022 and 2023 years develop benignly. HRB architects and fire engineers remain in a hard market — RAAC crisis (2023) adds a new tail concern for structural engineers. BSA tail uncertainty prevents full softening. Combined ratios 92–102%.

2024+ · Outlook

Structural Uncertainty; AI Design Risk Emerging

Grenfell Phase 2 Report (September 2024) expected to trigger additional civil litigation waves. Criminal prosecutions may follow. AI-assisted design tools entering practice — first professional liability questions emerging. BLOs (Building Liability Orders) beginning to be issued under BSA 2022. Long-term outlook: high uncertainty; underwriters extending IBNR development assumptions to 25–30 years for HRB-exposed risks.

ROE Scenarios — Lloyd's E6/E7 Portfolio

Illustrative return-on-equity scenarios for a hypothetical £200m Lloyd's construction PI portfolio (70% E6 / 30% E7). ILLUS

Bull Scenario

+12%

Benign development of 2020–2024 policy years; BSA tail remains limited; no major new structural event; rate stability on commercial sub-classes; investment income supportive at 4.5%+.

Base Scenario

+5%

Moderate claims development on Grenfell-related tail; RAAC produces £50–100m market loss over 5 years; BSA BLOs add 10–15% to HRB sub-class reserves; modest rate increase 2025.

Bear Scenario

−15%

Large-scale Grenfell civil litigation wave post-Phase 2 Report (2025–27); criminal convictions create reputational pressure; RAAC class action; BSA tail extends to 25+ years on HRB sub-class; IBNR development significantly adverse; market exits force concentration.

ROE calculated on hypothetical £200m gross written premium portfolio. Investment return assumed 4.5% (bull), 3.8% (base), 3.2% (bear) on float. All figures ILLUS.
