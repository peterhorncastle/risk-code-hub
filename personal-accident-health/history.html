Era 1: Friendly Societies & Railway Accident1850s – 1910

The PA&H market traces its roots to Victorian mutual aid organisations. Friendly societies — such as the Oddfellows (founded 1810), Foresters and Rechabites — provided sickness and death benefits to working-class members through pooled contributions. By 1872, over four million people in England and Wales held friendly society membership. VER

Commercial personal accident insurance was pioneered by the Railway Passengers Assurance Company, established in 1849, which provided travellers with accidental death cover purchased at station ticket windows — the first mass-market PA product. Rapid industrialisation created demand for workplace accident coverage alongside the negligence-based Employers' Liability Act 1880 (UK). VER

  • 1810: Independent Order of Oddfellows founded (UK) — mutual sickness benefit
  • 1849: Railway Passengers Assurance Co — first commercial PA insurer
  • 1880: Employers' Liability Act (UK) — tort foundation for occupational injury
  • 1897: Workmen's Compensation Act — first no-fault employee injury compensation
  • Lloyd's underwriters begin writing PA slips for high-risk occupations (mining, shipping) in this period
Era 2: State Insurance & Lloyd's PA Market Development1911 – 1945

Lloyd George's National Insurance Act 1911 created compulsory state sickness and unemployment insurance for workers earning below £160/year, effectively displacing friendly societies for basic protection. This paradoxically opened the market for commercial insurers to cover the gap above state provision and for higher-income individuals. VER

Lloyd's PA market expanded through the inter-war period writing accident policies for motorists (following the Road Traffic Act 1930 which mandated third-party liability but not PA), aviation PA (reflecting the growth of commercial flying), and specialist sports and entertainment PA. The concept of the "personal accident schedule" — tabulating capital sums for specific losses (loss of right hand = 100%, loss of eye = 50% etc.) — became the market standard. VER

  • 1911: National Insurance Act — state sickness benefit (UK); commercial market fills the gap above
  • 1930: Road Traffic Act — motor PA as add-on to comprehensive motor policies
  • 1920s–30s: Lloyd's sports PA market develops (cricketers, boxers, footballers)
  • WWII: Wartime exclusions and forces PA coverage — market paused for civilian leisure travel
Era 3: NHS, Private Medical & Group Benefits1945 – 1980

The National Health Service Act 1946 (effective 5 July 1948) fundamentally reshaped UK health insurance. Free at point of use NHS care removed the mass-market medical expenses demand, creating instead a premium private medical insurance (PMI) market for those seeking speed and choice. BUPA (British United Provident Association), formed in 1947, became the dominant UK PMI provider. VER

The post-war period saw rapid growth in employer-funded group benefits as companies competed for talent in full employment economies. Group PA, group life and group income protection became standard employee benefit package components. The USA developed its own parallel market: Blue Cross / Blue Shield health plans (established 1930s) became the cornerstone of employer-sponsored US health insurance — a model that persists today. VER

  • 1947: BUPA founded — UK private medical insurance pioneer
  • 1948: NHS established — free UK healthcare fundamentally alters demand structure
  • 1960s: Package holiday boom (Freddie Laker, Thomson) → travel insurance mass market
  • 1970s: Group PMI grows as executive perk; income protection (PHI) develops as long-term product
  • HIV/AIDS (first diagnosed 1981) begins impacting underwriting in this era's closing years
Era 4: HMOs, AIDS Impact & Regulatory Foundations1980 – 2000

The HIV/AIDS epidemic from the early 1980s posed an existential challenge to life and health underwriters. Insurers responded with medical screening, HIV exclusions and limitations. Actuarial assumptions about longevity and morbidity were stress-tested severely. The epidemic demonstrated the non-diversifiable nature of pandemic/epidemic risk — a lesson partially forgotten and re-learned with COVID-19 in 2020. VER

In the USA, the Health Maintenance Organization (HMO) Act 1973 had established managed care networks; by the 1980s HMOs and Preferred Provider Organizations (PPOs) dominated US employer health. The COBRA Act 1985 required employers to offer continued health coverage to departing employees — a key consumer protection. The UK saw continued PMI growth and the emergence of payment protection insurance (PPI) as banks and lenders sold creditor life/disability as loan add-ons. VER

  • 1980s: AIDS forces complete underwriting model review; HIV screening becomes standard
  • 1985: COBRA Act (USA) — portable health coverage on job loss
  • 1990s: PPI (payment protection insurance) explosive growth in UK — later to become a major mis-selling scandal
  • 1994: UK income protection ABI model definition reforms
  • 1990s: Lloyd's A&H treaty market grows substantially; syndicates specialise in A&H reinsurance
  • Internet enables direct travel insurance distribution; annual multi-trip policies develop
Era 5: 9/11, ACA Reform & Conduct Regulation2001 – 2019

The September 11 2001 attacks generated significant Lloyd's PA claims — estimated aviation PA/life losses alone ran to several hundred million dollars across the market. The attacks accelerated exclusion of terrorism PA from standard wordings and the emergence of standalone terrorism personal accident covers. EST

The US Affordable Care Act (ACA) 2010 — colloquially "Obamacare" — extended mandatory health coverage to ~30 million previously uninsured Americans, prohibited PEC exclusions for children (and later adults), and mandated community rating. For Lloyd's this was significant as US healthcare liability and medical stop-loss programmes were affected. In the UK, the PPI mis-selling scandal resulted in over £38 billion in compensation payments — damaging consumer trust in creditor protection products and triggering the FCA's wholesale conduct regulation of retail insurance. VER

  • 2001: 9/11 — aviation PA and life market claims; terrorism PA exclusions normalised
  • 2005: Lloyd's A&H market hardening after Katrina-era recalibration
  • 2010: ACA enacted (USA) — transforms US health insurance landscape
  • 2011: FCA review of PPI; subsequent redress programme (£38bn+ total UK)
  • 2013: FCA takes over from FSA; Treating Customers Fairly (TCF) embedded in retail A&H
  • 2016: FCA IPID (Insurance Product Information Document) requirements for retail products
  • 2017: GDPR and health data — sensitive data classification affects underwriting data use
  • 2019: FCA market study into pricing practices (home/motor — extending to A&H)
PPI Mis-Selling — Scale and Implications Payment Protection Insurance sold alongside mortgages, loans and credit cards from the 1990s onwards was found to be systematically mis-sold: buyers did not understand it, could not claim under it (self-employed, PEC exclusions) or simply did not need it. The FCA's 2019 deadline for PPI complaints resulted in banks paying over £38 billion in redress — the largest financial services mis-selling compensation in UK history. The scandal permanently altered how creditor protection (KP) is sold and regulated. VER
Era 6: COVID-19, Long-COVID & Mental Health Reckoning2020 – 2024

COVID-19 (declared pandemic March 2020) was the defining event for modern A&H insurance. Travel insurance claims were enormous as trip cancellations swept the globe; the market quickly excluded pandemic/epidemic perils where not already excluded. Group PA disability claims were relatively contained initially, but the emergence of long-COVID as a disabling condition — characterised by fatigue, cognitive impairment and respiratory issues lasting months to years — created a significant new claims category that reserves were unprepared for. VER

Mental health claims accelerated dramatically post-pandemic across group income protection, group PMI and individual disability products. Anxiety, depression and burnout drove claims frequency to new highs in 2021–23. Simultaneously, the FCA's Consumer Duty (July 2023) required A&H insurers to demonstrate good outcomes for retail customers — forcing policy wordings, pricing and claims practices to be reviewed. VER

  • 2020: COVID-19 pandemic — travel industry collapses; KT (travel) claims surge; pandemic exclusions rolled out
  • 2020–21: Group PMI and group PA: relatively contained COVID mortality but long-COVID emerges
  • 2021: Long-COVID recognised as disabling condition — group IP reserves under pressure
  • 2022–23: Mental health claims frequency up ~40% vs pre-pandemic (industry estimates) EST
  • 2022: War in Ukraine — KM (maritime PA) and KX (war PA) markets recalibrate
  • 2023: FCA Consumer Duty — retail A&H product governance and outcome testing
  • 2023: FCA pricing review — travel insurance accessibility for older travellers and those with PECs
  • 2024: NHS waiting list crisis drives UK PMI demand to record levels; BUPA, AXA Health see double-digit growth
