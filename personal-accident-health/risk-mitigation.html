Policy Wording Controls — The First Line of Defence

Disability Definition — Own, Any or Suited Occupation The disability definition is the most financially consequential wording element. "Own occupation" (inability to perform the insured's specific job) is the most generous to claimants and appropriate for high-income professionals. "Any occupation" (inability to perform any job for which educated/trained) is the most restrictive. "Suited occupation" is an intermediate standard. Underwriters must match the definition to the risk profile — mismatched definitions are a primary source of disputes and claims leakage. VER
Pre-Existing Condition (PEC) Exclusions Three standard approaches: (1) Full exclusion — any PEC excluded forever, regardless of treatment. (2) Moratorium underwriting (MORI) — conditions present in the 5 years pre-inception are excluded for the first 2 years; if claimant is symptom/treatment-free for 2 years they become covered. (3) Individual Medical Underwriting (IMU) — each condition individually assessed and accepted, excluded or rated. IMU gives certainty but requires disclosure; MORI is simpler at inception but creates disputes at claim stage. VER
Pandemic / Epidemic Exclusion Clause Post-COVID, epidemic/pandemic exclusions are standard across travel (KT) and are being inserted into group A&H products where absent. Standard wording excludes losses arising from any communicable disease declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) by WHO, or any national epidemic/pandemic declaration. The scope of "arising from" is critical — direct infection vs. consequential lockdown/cancellation losses are often treated differently. VER
Mental Health Exclusions — FCA Consumer Duty Scrutiny Historical travel and income protection wordings contained blanket mental health exclusions or significant limitations. Post-Consumer Duty (2023), these exclusions require justification as providing good consumer outcomes. FCA has specifically challenged mental health exclusions in travel insurance (denying cover for trip cancellation triggered by documented mental health conditions). Insurers must document the rationale for any mental health limitation or face regulatory challenge. VER

Underwriting Controls by Sub-Class

Personal Accident (KA)

  • Occupation class verification (payslips, HMRC records)
  • Leisure/sports activity declaration at inception
  • Maximum benefit as proportion of earnings (typically 60–70%)
  • Deferred period to eliminate moral hazard
  • Aggregate stop-loss for high-sum-insured risks
  • War and terrorism exclusion (buyback available)

Group PMI / Medical Expenses (KG/KC)

  • Free cover limit (below limit: no medical evidence; above: IMU required)
  • Annual aggregate deductible / co-payment
  • Hospital approved list / guided network
  • Out-patient benefit caps and sub-limits
  • Age-banded contribution structure
  • Medical inflation annual adjustment (automatic or re-rate)
  • Cosmetic/elective exclusions defined clearly

Travel Insurance (KT)

  • FCDO travel advice trigger — cancellation only where FCDO advises against travel
  • Pre-existing medical conditions: screen and rate or exclude
  • Trip duration limits (e.g. 31 days, 60 days per trip)
  • Winter sports extension (separate premium)
  • Pandemic/epidemic exclusion
  • Alcohol / illegal substance exclusion
  • Hazardous activities exclusion list
  • Policy excess / co-payment (typically £50–£200)

Sports Disability (KS)

  • Medical examination and sports-specific fitness assessment
  • Prior injury exclusions (site-specific for previously injured joints/areas)
  • Playing position rating (props higher than backs in rugby)
  • Aggregate limit per team/squad programme
  • Waiting / elimination period (typically 26–52 weeks)
  • Rehabilitation obligation clause (failure to rehabilitate may void claim)
  • Concussion/head injury protocol — independent neurological assessment

Maritime PA / Crew K&R (KM)

  • MLC 2006 compliance check — mandatory crew medical and welfare obligations
  • War/piracy territory exclusions and war risk loading (JWC HRA areas)
  • Pre-employment medical (PEM) standard
  • K&R: silence obligation — ransom and crisis response protocols
  • Coverage coordination with P&I Club (employer liability vs. PA)
  • Repatriation plan and medical evacuation logistics confirmed

Creditor Protection (KP)

  • FCA PROD product governance — genuine customer need assessment
  • Benefit is capped at loan repayment amount (indemnity basis)
  • Employment status at inception declared (self-employed usually excluded for unemployment benefit)
  • Waiting period: typically first 30–60 days of disability not covered
  • Pre-existing conditions: 12–24 month look-back exclusion
  • Fair value assessment required under Consumer Duty

Rehabilitation Services — Reducing Disability Duration

Early intervention and active rehabilitation management is the most effective tool for reducing average disability claim duration. Industry data consistently shows that early case management reduces group income protection claim duration by 20–40%. EST

  • Early notification triggers: Group IP schemes typically require employer notification within 4–8 weeks of absence onset — enabling early intervention before the disability becomes chronic
  • Case management: Specialist occupational health nurses assess functional capacity, identify return-to-work barriers and coordinate treatment. Separate from insurer claims function to avoid conflict of interest perception
  • Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT): NICE-recommended treatment for depression, anxiety and PTSD — the fastest-growing disability categories. Fast-track CBT access (via phone/digital) significantly reduces mental health claim duration
  • Physiotherapy and MSK treatment: Rapid access physiotherapy (RAP) for musculoskeletal claims. Average MSK absence 8 weeks without intervention vs. 4 weeks with early physio EST
  • Phased return to work: Graduated return plans accommodating reduced hours or modified duties — insurer should cover pro-rata benefit during phased return to incentivise earlier resumption
  • Vocational rehabilitation: Retraining support for those unable to return to original occupation (especially relevant for manual workers with physical injury)
Long-COVID Rehabilitation Challenge Long-COVID presents a specific rehabilitation challenge: the condition is heterogeneous (symptoms vary widely between individuals), lacks a clear treatment protocol, and does not respond well to standard graded exercise therapy (GET) — which can worsen fatigue in some patients. Insurers managing long-COVID disability claims should use COVID-Specific post-COVID clinics (NHS established 90+ post-COVID clinics by 2022), paced activity management (not standard GET), neuropsychological assessment for cognitive impairment, and respiratory physiotherapy where indicated. VER

Risk Management Guidance for Insureds

For Employers (Group A&H KG)

  • Absence management policy: Structured return-to-work process, day-1 absence reporting and occupational health referral pathways reduce both claim frequency and duration
  • Employee Assistance Programme (EAP): 24/7 confidential counselling reduces mental health absence by providing early intervention before conditions become disabling
  • Annual benefit review: Group scheme benefits should be reviewed annually against salary increases, headcount changes and industry benchmarks
  • Disclose accurately: Full and accurate disclosure of occupation types, hazardous activities and international travel patterns prevents disputes at claim stage

For Individual PA Purchasers (KA/KT)

  • Read the disability definition carefully: Understand whether own-occupation, any-occupation or suited-occupation applies — this determines when you can claim
  • Declare all pre-existing conditions: Non-disclosure is the leading cause of PA claims rejection. Moratorium wordings provide eventual coverage but create uncertainty
  • Check sports activities: Contact sports, motorsport, aviation and extreme sports often require endorsement or attract loading — non-declaration can void coverage
  • Understand FCDO triggers in travel: Travel cancellation cover typically only responds when FCDO advises against all or all-but-essential travel — check the exact trigger wording
