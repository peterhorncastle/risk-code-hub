2001LOSS9/11 Attacks — Aviation PA and Life Claims
September 11 2001 generated substantial PA and life insurance claims. Lloyd's aviation PA losses were estimated at several hundred million dollars. EST The attacks accelerated the exclusion of terrorism from standard PA wordings and drove demand for standalone terrorism PA products. War/terrorism exclusion language became market standard.
2003PANDSARS — First Modern Pandemic Travel Test
Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) caused widespread travel cancellation particularly in Asia, generating significant travel insurance (KT) cancellation claims. SARS alerted the A&H market to pandemic/epidemic exclusion inadequacies. Epidemic exclusions were tightened across travel wordings post-SARS — though enforcement varied by syndicate. VER
2004–06MKTLloyd's A&H Market Hardening Post-2001
Following 9/11 and SARS, the Lloyd's A&H market underwent significant hardening. Terms, conditions and exclusions tightened; capacity contracted in terrorism-exposed PA lines. The period saw increased use of aggregate deductibles in group schemes and the growth of specialist A&H MGAs writing under Lloyd's binder arrangements. EST
2005SPORTProfessional Rugby — Career-Ending Disability Claims Rise
The professionalism of rugby union (1995) created a new high-value sports disability market (KS). By the mid-2000s, career-ending spinal injury claims from professional rugby were generating significant Lloyd's losses. The case of Daniel James (paralysed playing for Leeds Rhinos, 2007) and other high-profile cases prompted market review of rugby PA pricing, aggregate limits and medical screening requirements. EST
2007–09MKTGlobal Financial Crisis — Creditor Protection (KP) Demand Spike
The 2008–09 financial crisis drove mortgage default fears, briefly increasing interest in creditor protection insurance. However the simultaneous PPI mis-selling investigation (formally launched by the OFT in 2005, followed by FSA action from 2010) suppressed creditor protection (KP) distribution. The Supreme Court's landmark PPI ruling in Plevin v Paragon [2014] further shaped redress. VER
2010REGUS Affordable Care Act (ACA) — Market Transformation
The ACA (signed 23 March 2010) extended healthcare coverage to approximately 30 million previously uninsured Americans, prohibited pre-existing condition exclusions, introduced community rating and mandated essential health benefits. For Lloyd's syndicates writing US health-related excess-of-loss and medical stop-loss, ACA fundamentally altered the risk landscape. UK insurers monitored closely given potential reform implications for NHS / PMI balance. VER
2011REGFCA/FSA PPI Redress Programme Begins
The FSA formally required banks and insurers to review and compensate PPI mis-selling. The programme became the largest consumer financial redress exercise in UK history — total payments exceeding £38 billion by the FCA's August 2019 complaints deadline. VER The scandal permanently damaged consumer confidence in creditor protection (KP) and led to the FCA's product intervention powers under PROD sourcebook.
2012TRAVELOlympic Games & Major Event Cancellation — Market Test
The London 2012 Olympics highlighted the scale of event cancellation coverage (KC) required for mega-events. While London 2012 proceeded without incident, the insurance towers protecting against cancellation (terrorism, natural disaster) established the template for subsequent Olympic, FIFA World Cup and major sports event coverage. Sports cancellation and PA combined programmes became a specialist Lloyd's sub-market. EST
2013REGFCA Authorisation — Retail A&H Conduct Regulation Intensified
The Financial Conduct Authority replaced the FSA on 1 April 2013 with a strengthened retail conduct remit. A&H products — sold directly to consumers through travel agents, banks and MGAs — came under heightened scrutiny. The FCA's Treating Customers Fairly (TCF) principles were embedded in product design, distribution and claims handling. Group schemes faced added governance requirements under the Employers' liability framework. VER
2014PANDEbola Outbreak (West Africa) — Travel & Medical PA Claims
The 2014–16 West Africa Ebola epidemic infected over 28,600 people and killed 11,325, primarily in Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone. Travel insurers saw claims for trip cancellations in affected regions. The outbreak reinforced the case for epidemic exclusion clauses in travel (KT) and medical expenses products and demonstrated the speed at which an infectious disease could disrupt travel markets. VER
2015–17MKTMGA & InsurTech Boom — A&H Distribution Disrupted
The rise of specialist A&H MGAs and InsurTech platforms fundamentally changed distribution of travel (KT), group PA (KG) and sports (KS) products. Direct-to-consumer aggregator distribution matured for travel; parametric health and travel products (instant pay on flight delay, automatic baggage claim) began to emerge. Lloyd's binder (KB) volume grew substantially as syndicates delegated distribution to technology-enabled MGAs. EST
2016PANDZika Virus — Travel Insurance Disruption (Americas)
The 2016 Zika epidemic in Latin America and the Caribbean caused widespread cancellation of travel, particularly affecting tourists concerned about the virus's link to microcephaly in foetuses. Pregnant travellers and those planning pregnancy sought refunds on bookings to affected destinations. The event tested the boundaries of FCDO travel advice triggers in UK travel policies. VER
2017REGInsurance Act 2015 — Fair Presentation Impacts A&H
Although effective from August 2016 for commercial lines, the Insurance Act 2015 embedded fair presentation duties affecting group A&H (KG) and individual PA (KA) underwriting. The duty to disclose every material circumstance the insured knows or ought to know — replacing the old marine-derived duty of utmost good faith — impacted how A&H declarations around pre-existing conditions, sports activities and occupational risk were managed. VER
2020PANDCOVID-19 Pandemic — Travel Market Collapses; Long-COVID Emerges
COVID-19 was the most significant event in A&H insurance history in decades. Travel insurance (KT) bore enormous cancellation claims as global aviation halted; Lloyd's travel claims in 2020 were historically elevated. EST Pandemic exclusions were rapidly deployed across renewals. By late 2020, long-COVID had been clinically recognised — a condition with profound long-term disability implications for group income protection (KG) reserves. VER
2021LOSSLong-COVID: A New Disability Category Enters the Market
Long-COVID — symptoms persisting beyond 12 weeks post-infection — was formally recognised by NICE and NHS England in 2021. Estimated UK prevalence reached ~2 million people at peak. VER Group income protection claims featuring long-COVID diagnoses rose significantly; average claim duration extended. Insurers faced questions around classification (physical vs. mental illness; whether standard definitions of disability applied). Reserve releases from 2020 were reversed for some carriers as duration estimates proved optimistic. EST
2022REGFCA Pricing Reform — Travel Insurance Fairness for Older/PEC Travellers
The FCA's general insurance pricing reforms (effective January 2022) prohibited the "loyalty penalty" — charging existing customers more than new customers at renewal — applying to home, motor and travel insurance. Simultaneously the FCA focused on travel insurance accessibility for older travellers and those with pre-existing conditions, which were often declined or quoted prohibitively. Signposting requirements to specialist providers were strengthened under PROD rules. VER
2022–23LOSSMental Health Claims Surge — Group IP & PMI Under Pressure
Mental health claims — anxiety, depression, burnout and PTSD — reached record levels in group income protection and group PMI portfolios in 2022–23. Industry bodies estimated mental health claims frequency increased ~40% against 2019 baselines, driven by post-pandemic wellbeing impacts and NHS waiting list delays prompting private claims. EST Rehabilitation service costs increased; average disability duration extended. Re-rating of group schemes became necessary across the market.
2023REGFCA Consumer Duty — A&H Product Governance Overhaul
The FCA Consumer Duty (effective 31 July 2023 for new products; 31 July 2024 for closed book products) set a new standard requiring retail A&H insurers to demonstrate good customer outcomes across four pillars: products & services, price & value, consumer understanding and consumer support. A&H wordings, exclusion clarity, claims communication and renewal pricing all required review. The FCA conducted targeted work on travel insurance (particularly mental health exclusions and FCDO advice triggers). VER
2023–24MKTUK PMI Market — Record Demand Driven by NHS Waiting Lists
Record NHS elective waiting lists (7+ million patients in England by 2024) drove private medical insurance (PMI) demand to levels not seen in decades. BUPA, AXA Health, Vitality and Aviva all reported double-digit PMI growth. Group PMI — driven by employers competing for staff in a tight labour market — was the fastest-growing segment. For Lloyd's reinsurers and specialist syndicates writing UK medical excess of loss, this represented a meaningful change in underlying exposure. EST
2024MKTA&H Market Hardening — Rates, Mental Health & Medical Inflation
By 2024, the Lloyd's A&H market had entered a hardening cycle: premium rates increasing across group PMI, group IP and travel lines in response to sustained claims inflation (medical 8–10% p.a. UK EST), mental health claims frequency, long-COVID duration uncertainty and rebuilding margins after COVID losses. Parametric travel products and embedded insurance (travel add-ons through credit cards, banks) continued rapid growth. The market debate on pandemic exclusion permanence versus future coverage frameworks remained unresolved.
