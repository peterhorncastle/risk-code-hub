1992Hurricane Andrew ▸Florida~$15.5bnHurricaneCAT
The defining cat XoL event: proved cat risk was under-priced, launched cat modelling as standard practice and the Bermuda "Class of 1993". Attachment points and rate-on-line rose sharply market-wide.
1994Northridge earthquake ▸California~$15bnEarthquakeCAT
Costliest US quake to date; reset seismic cat XoL pricing and led to the California Earthquake Authority to take residential quake off insurer balance sheets.
2004Florida four-hurricane season ▸Florida~$30bnHurricaneCAT
Four landfalls in one season stress-tested reinstatement provisions and the per-occurrence vs aggregate distinction, foreshadowing 2005.
2005Hurricanes Katrina, Rita & Wilma ▸US Gulf~$80bnHurricaneCAT
Katrina alone ~$65bn — exposed flood/surge modelling gaps and demand surge. Birthed the sidecar, accelerated cat bonds, launched the "Class of 2005" and hardened cat XoL sharply.
2010–11Canterbury / Christchurch quakes ▸New Zealand~$30bn (series)EarthquakeCAT
A multi-event sequence that created severe hours-clause and event-definition disputes and years of loss creep — a lasting lesson in occurrence wording for cat XoL.
2011Tōhoku earthquake & tsunami ▸Japan~$35–40bnEarthquakeCAT
Part of the costliest cat year to that point; combined with Thai floods to reprice Asia-Pacific cat XoL and highlight correlated global exposures.
2011Thailand floods ▸Thailand~$16bnFloodMAJ
Contingent business-interruption losses rippled through global supply chains — hidden accumulation few cat models captured, and a wake-up call on non-physical aggregation.
2012Superstorm Sandy ▸US Northeast~$30bnHurricaneCAT
Storm-surge flooding in a dense, high-value corridor sharpened flood-vs-wind wording and drove demand for better surge modelling in cat XoL.
2017Hurricanes Harvey, Irma & Maria ▸US / Caribbean~$92bnHurricaneCAT
Three storms in weeks trapped ILS collateral, ended the long soft market and began years of secondary-peril attrition and loss creep on cat XoL.
2018California wildfires (Camp, Woolsey) ▸California~$25bn (series)WildfireMAJ
Wildfire moved from "secondary" to a modelled peak peril; cat XoL aggregate covers repriced and attachment points rose to exclude attritional wildfire.
2021Hurricane Ida & European floods ▸USA / Europe~$36bn + ~$13bnHurricaneCAT
Ida plus the July "Bernd" floods reminded cat XoL underwriters that flood and severe convective storm can produce peak-peril losses far from Florida.
2022Hurricane Ian ▸Florida~$50–60bnHurricaneCAT
The loss that tipped the market: triggered the January 2023 reset — sharply higher rate-on-line and attachment points and withdrawal of aggregate cover.
2023Türkiye–Syria earthquakes ▸Türkiye / Syria~$5–6bn insuredEarthquakeCAT
Catastrophic economic loss (~$90bn) on a low insured share — a protection-gap example and a live test of the Turkish quake pool's cat XoL.
2024Hurricanes Helene & Milton ▸US Southeast~$35–50bnHurricaneCAT
Large but within budget for well-capitalised reinsurers; the higher post-2023 attachments meant much of the loss stayed with primary carriers — validating the restructured cat XoL market.
Reading the database Notice the pattern: a catastrophe destroys capital → cat XoL rate-on-line and attachment points rise → new capital (Bermuda class or ILS) enters → the market softens until the next shock. This is the cat cycle, and it is the single most important dynamic to understand before pricing any cat layer.
