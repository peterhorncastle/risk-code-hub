Catastrophe XoL — Timeline

Twenty events that repriced catastrophe XoL capacity, pricing and structure, from Hurricane Andrew to the softening market of 2026.

Catastrophe models arrive (AIR, RMS)
Modelling Probabilistic simulation begins to replace judgement in cat pricing. VER
1992
Hurricane Andrew
Catastrophe ~$15.5bn insured, 11 insurer insolvencies; launched the Bermuda "Class of 1993" and made cat modelling essential. $15.5bn VER
1994
Northridge earthquake
Catastrophe ~$15bn insured; reset US quake pricing and cat XoL attachment for seismic peril. ~$15bn VER
1997
First modern catastrophe bonds
Capital USAA's Residential Re and peers open capital-markets access to cat XoL risk. VER
2004
Florida four-hurricane season
Catastrophe Charley, Frances, Ivan, Jeanne stress-tested reinstatements and per-occurrence vs aggregate structures. ~$30bn VER
2005
Hurricanes Katrina, Rita & Wilma
Catastrophe ~$80bn (KRW); birthed the sidecar, accelerated cat bonds and the "Class of 2005". ~$80bn VER
2011
Tōhoku, NZ quakes & Thai floods
Catastrophe Costliest cat year to that point; Canterbury sequence created severe hours-clause and event-definition disputes. ~$120bn (yr) VER
2012
Superstorm Sandy
Catastrophe Storm-surge flooding in a dense corridor; sharpened flood-vs-wind wording. ~$30bn VER
2013
Pension capital floods into ILS
Capital Low rates drive institutional money into cat bonds and collateralised re; "convergence" softens the traditional market. EST
2016
Solvency II in force
Market Formalises the 1-in-200 capital calibration that anchors cat programme design and top-layer buying. VER
2017
Hurricanes Harvey, Irma & Maria
Catastrophe ~$92bn from three storms; trapped ILS collateral and ended the soft market's momentum. ~$92bn (HIM) VER
2017–20
Secondary perils & loss creep
Market Wildfire, convective storm and adverse development drove re-underwriting and rising attachments. VER
2018
California wildfires (Camp, Woolsey)
Catastrophe Wildfire moves from "secondary" to modelled peak peril; aggregate covers repriced. ~$25bn (series) VER
2021
Hurricane Ida & European floods
Catastrophe Ida plus the July "Bernd" floods showed flood and SCS can reach peak-peril scale far from Florida. ~$49bn VER
2022
Hurricane Ian
Catastrophe ~$50–60bn; the loss that tipped the market into the hardest cat renewal in a generation. ~$50–60bn VER
2023
The January hard-market reset
Market Cat XoL rate-on-line jumped, attachments rose sharply, aggregate cover withdrew — a structural reset. VER
2023
Türkiye–Syria earthquakes
Catastrophe ~$5–6bn insured on ~$90bn economic — a stark protection-gap and quake-pool test. ~$5–6bn VER
2024
Hurricanes Helene & Milton
Catastrophe Large but within budget; higher attachments kept much of the loss with primary carriers, validating the reset. ~$35–50bn VER
2025
Record capital, record cat-bond market
Capital Cat bonds outstanding grow 24% to $61.3bn on record $25.6bn issuance; capacity abundant. VER
2026
Softening: property-cat RoL −12%
Market The weight of capital softens cat XoL rate-on-line ~12% — sharpest risk-adjusted decline since 2014; attachments largely hold. VER
