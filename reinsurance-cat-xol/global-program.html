Catastrophe XoL — Programme Structuring

How a cedant builds a catastrophe tower — from net retention through working and top layers to the reinsurer's own retrocession — and the placement workflow that gets it bound.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

A catastrophe programme is a stack of XoL layers sized to a single target: keep the cedant's modelled 1-in-100 to 1-in-250 net loss inside its capital appetite. The cedant keeps a net retention, then buys successive layers that transfer progressively larger, rarer events; the reinsurers writing those layers buy retrocession on top. VER

The Placement Workflow

1

Exposure & model preparation

Cedant and broker geocode the portfolio and run the cat model into a submission with AAL, PML and layer loss costs.

2

Structure & strategy

Set the retention against capital appetite, choose layer boundaries around the PML curve, and decide per-occurrence vs any aggregate.

3

Marketing the placement

The broker takes the submission to lead reinsurers and ILS markets to set the rate-on-line for each layer.

4

Firm order terms & signing

A lead quote sets the price; each layer is subscribed to 100% across traditional and collateralised markets and signed down.

5

Contract & wording

Slip and treaty wording finalised — hours clause, reinstatements, UNL, cascade, exclusions and collateral (see Contract & Wordings).

6

In-force management & renewal

Track erosion, reinstatements and counterparty security through the season; re-underwrite at renewal against the updated model.

Case Study — A Coastal Property Insurer

A regional insurer with £180m GWP wants to grow into a hurricane-exposed coastal territory without letting a single storm threaten its balance sheet.

The problem

Coastal expansion concentrates hurricane accumulation the insurer cannot retain net; its 1-in-200 modelled loss would exceed its capital appetite.

The programme

A four-layer cat tower attaching at £35m (its net retention) up to £250m — a working layer plus mid and top layers, with one reinstatement each and no aggregate after the 2023 reset.

The economics

Most of the budget is spent on the working and mid layers, where each pound of premium most reduces net volatility; the remote top layer is thin but cheap.

The outcome

The insurer grows into the territory with a modelled 1-in-200 net loss inside its capital appetite and a rating-agency-acceptable PML.

Programme design is a portfolio decision Each layer is priced individually, but the cedant buys the whole tower to shape one number: the net loss after the worst modelled season. The art is spending the cat budget where it most reduces net volatility per pound — usually the working and mid layers, not the remote top.
