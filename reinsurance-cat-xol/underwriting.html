Catastrophe XoL — Pricing Methodology

How a catastrophe layer is actually priced: from the cat model's event set to expected loss, loss-on-line, rate-on-line, payback and reinstatements — the numbers behind the Cat Layer Rater.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

Cat layers cannot be burnt from loss history — a 1-in-100 layer has, by definition, almost no experience. Instead they are exposure-rated with a catastrophe model: a stochastic simulation of tens of thousands of synthetic years across a peril region, from which the expected loss to any layer is read directly. The underwriter's job is to judge the model, load its output for risk and expense, and sense-check the resulting rate-on-line. VER

1 · The Cat Model Output

Commercial models (Verisk/AIR, Moody's RMS, CoreLogic) run the cedant's exposure through an event catalogue and return a small set of metrics. Four drive layer pricing. VER

AAL — Average Annual Loss

The modelled expected annual loss over the full event set. The AAL falling into a layer is that layer's expected loss — the actuarial premium before loadings.

PML — Probable Maximum Loss

Modelled loss at a stated return period (1-in-100, 1-in-200, 1-in-250). Sets where the tower is built and where each layer attaches.

OEP vs AEP

Occurrence vs Aggregate Exceedance Probability — single largest event vs the sum of events in a year. Per-occurrence layers price off OEP; aggregate layers off AEP.

TVaR / CTE

Expected loss given the loss exceeds the PML point — the tail severity beyond the point estimate. Increasingly the Solvency II / ORSA standard.

2 · From Model to Layer Price

Cat layer pricing
Expected Loss to Layer = AALlayer = modelled event losses capped to [attachment, attachment + limit], averaged over all simulated years
Loss on Line (LoL) = Expected Loss to Layer ÷ Layer Limit
Technical Premium = Expected Loss × (1 + Risk Load) ÷ (1 − Expenses % − Brokerage %)
Rate on Line (RoL) = Layer Premium ÷ Layer Limit
Payback Period = 1 ÷ RoL ≈ Limit ÷ Premium
Reinstatement premium reloads the layer after a loss — pro-rata to time and/or amount
The margin over expected loss is the whole game Rate-on-line minus loss-on-line is the margin the layer earns for capital and volatility. A working layer might run 20% RoL on a 12% LoL; a remote top layer 3% RoL on a 1.5% LoL. The higher the layer, the thinner the margin per unit of limit — but the lower the probability of paying, so the capital charge differs too.

3 · Reinstatements

A cat layer that pays a loss is exhausted unless it reinstates. A "1 reinstatement at 100%" layer refills once, for a reinstatement premium equal to the original layer premium (pro-rated for limit used and time remaining), leaving the reinsurer exposed to a second full-limit loss in the year. Reinstatements cap the reinsurer's annual exposure and partly fund the second event.

Worked Example — A Cat XoL Layer

£40m xs £35m US wind layer

Layer limit £40,000,000 · Attachment £35,000,000 · Modelled AAL to layer £2,400,000 · Risk load 35% · Expenses 5% · Brokerage 10% · 1 reinstatement at 100%
Expected loss to layer (modelled AAL)£2,400,000
Loss on line (£2.4m ÷ £40m)6.0%
Loaded for risk (×1.35)£3,240,000
Grossed up for expenses + brokerage (÷ 0.85)£3,812,000
Rate on line (£3.81m ÷ £40m)9.5%
Payback period (1 ÷ RoL)~10.5 years
Margin over expected loss£1,412,000
Max annual exposure (incl. 1 reinstatement)£80,000,000

A 6% loss-on-line priced at ~9.5% rate-on-line earns ~£1.4m of margin per limit for capital and volatility, a ~10.5-year payback. The single reinstatement means the reinsurer can face two full-limit losses in one year for one up-front premium — the reinstatement premium partially funds the second.

Pre-Bind Checklist — Cat XoL

  1. Model & version. Which vendor model and version, which event set, and how complete and geocoded is the underlying exposure data?
  2. Layer definition. Limit, attachment, per-occurrence vs aggregate, and how the hours clause defines "one event".
  3. Reinstatements. How many, at what cost, pro-rata to time and/or amount — and the maximum annual exposure.
  4. RoL vs LoL. Does the rate-on-line carry adequate margin over the modelled loss-on-line for capital and volatility?
  5. Non-modelled & loss creep. Demand surge, secondary perils, storm surge/flood, and social-inflation loss creep the model may understate.
  6. Aggregation with retro. How the layer correlates with the reinsurer's own retrocession and the rest of its cat book.
  7. Wording. Ultimate net loss, occurrence definition, cascading, hours clause, and cyber / terror / communicable-disease exclusions.
