Reinsurance — Catastrophe Excess of Loss

Catastrophe XoL — Overview

The structure that caps a cedant's balance-sheet exposure to a single catastrophe. Catastrophe excess-of-loss (cat XoL) is non-proportional, per-occurrence reinsurance that responds once accumulated losses from one event breach a retention — the core of nearly every property insurer's outwards programme, and the segment where the reinsurance cycle bites hardest.

A cat XoL layer is written as "£limit xs £attachment" — the reinsurer pays up to the limit for the part of a single event's loss above the attachment. Unlike per-risk XoL (which protects one large risk) or proportional treaty (which shares every risk), cat XoL responds to accumulation: the many individual policies a hurricane, earthquake or windstorm hits at once. It is priced by rate-on-line against a modelled loss distribution, and it is the single largest use of both traditional reinsurance capital and ILS. VER

What Cat XoL Does

🌀

Caps single-event loss

Limits the balance-sheet hit from one hurricane, quake or windstorm accumulating across a whole book.

🏦

Protects capital & rating

Keeps the modelled 1-in-100/200 net loss within the cedant's capital appetite and rating-agency tolerance.

📊

Priced by cat model

Expected loss to each layer comes from a stochastic cat model (Verisk, Moody's RMS, CoreLogic), not loss history.

🔁

Reinstates after a loss

Reinstatement premium refills the layer for a second event in the same year — capping annual exposure.

🕐

Defines "one event"

The hours clause binds losses within a time window into a single occurrence — the most contested wording.

💠

Sits alongside ILS

Cat bonds, sidecars and collateralised re compete directly with traditional cat XoL capacity in the same layers.

Where Cat XoL Sits in the Tower

Cat XoL is the stack of layers above the cedant's net retention. A working layer near the retention is hit relatively often and priced for frequency; higher layers are remote and thinly priced. Above the cedant's programme, the reinsurer buys retrocession to protect its own net.

The pricing sense-check Every cat layer must pass one test: does the rate-on-line (premium ÷ limit) carry adequate margin over the modelled loss-on-line (expected loss ÷ limit) for capital and volatility? A 6% loss-on-line priced at 10% rate-on-line gives a ~10-year payback with margin; the same layer at 9% rate-on-line is barely priced for its expected loss. The Underwriting and Rater modules build this out.

Key Perils

🌀

US hurricane

The peak global peril; Florida and Gulf wind dominate industry PMLs and cat-bond issuance.

🌍

Earthquake

California, Japan, New Zealand — low frequency, extreme severity, long loss development.

🌬️

European windstorm

Winter storms (e.g. "Bernd"); the main European accumulation peril with its own hours clause.

🔥

Secondary perils

Wildfire, severe convective storm and flood — now modelled as peak-scale after 2017–24 losses.
