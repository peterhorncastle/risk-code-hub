Catastrophe XoL — Contract & Wordings

The clauses that decide who pays what after a catastrophe: the hours clause, occurrence definition, reinstatements, ultimate net loss, cascading and exclusions. For cat XoL, a single ambiguous word can turn one claim into several.

Cat XoL responds to accumulation, so its wording is dominated by one question: what binds many individual losses into a single "occurrence" that pierces the layer? The answer decides the recovery — sometimes by multiples of the limit. VER

Core Clauses

Hours clause

Defines the time window within which losses count as one occurrence — e.g. 72 hours for a hurricane, 168 for a windstorm, often longer for earthquake. Controls how a rolling catastrophe aggregates into the layer.

Occurrence / event definition

What binds separate losses into a single event. The Canterbury quake sequence showed how contested this is, and how many millions ride on the wording.

Reinstatements

How many times the layer refills after a loss, and at what cost. "One reinstatement at 100%" means one more full limit, paid for by a reinstatement premium pro-rata to time and amount.

Ultimate Net Loss (UNL)

Defines exactly which loss amounts the reinsurer indemnifies — including or excluding loss-adjustment expense — after all other recoveries. The base on which the layer attaches.

Cascading / drop-down

Features that let an exhausted lower layer's cover "cascade" down or a higher layer drop down after a first event, keeping protection in place for a second.

Franchise / sublimits

Peril or region sublimits and franchise deductibles that shape exactly which parts of an event the layer pays.

Reinstatement Mechanics — Worked Illustration

£40m xs £35m, one reinstatement at 100% A first event costs the layer its full £40m. The layer reinstates, and the cedant pays a reinstatement premium equal to 100% of the original layer premium, pro-rated for the fraction of limit used (here all of it) and the time remaining. The reinsurer is now exposed to a second full £40m for the rest of the year. After the second loss, with no further reinstatements, the layer is exhausted.

Counterparty & Collateral

Before binding

  • Check reinsurer / ILS-vehicle security against your minimum rating.
  • For collateralised and cat-bond capacity, verify the trust, the trustee and the issuing bank — do not rely on the broker.
  • Confirm the hours clause and occurrence definition match across every layer of the tower.
  • Model how the layer correlates with the rest of the cat book and existing retro.

During the period & at claim

  • Document the event against the hours clause promptly.
  • Track cumulative erosion of each layer and reinstatements used.
  • Watch how one event erodes multiple programmes at once.
  • Monitor counterparty credit — a downgrade or trapped ILS collateral mid-season is itself an exposure.

⚠️ The event-definition trap

Ambiguous occurrence and hours wording is the single most litigated feature of cat XoL. Before binding, be able to answer precisely: if two storms make landfall three days apart, or a quake sequence spans months, is that one event or several? The answer changes the recovery by multiples of the limit — the Canterbury sequence is the cautionary case.
