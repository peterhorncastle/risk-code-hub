ILS / Alternative Capital — Overview

Where reinsurance meets the capital markets. Insurance-linked securities turn catastrophe risk into an investable asset — cat bonds, collateralised reinsurance, sidecars and industry loss warranties — bringing pension- and hedge-fund money to bear the peak of the world's catastrophe exposure.

Insurance-linked securities (ILS) are financial instruments whose return depends on insurance — almost always catastrophe — losses. They let capital-markets investors take reinsurance risk directly, and let (re)insurers access a deep, fully-collateralised source of capacity. Alternative capital now funds a large share of the world's peak catastrophe risk, and this hub completes the Reinsurance group by explaining how. VER

The ILS Toolkit

Catastrophe bonds

144A securities issued through a special-purpose vehicle; investors' principal is at risk if a defined catastrophe trigger is breached, in exchange for a spread over a money-market yield. Multi-year and tradable.

Collateralised reinsurance

Traditional reinsurance contracts fully collateralised in a trust, so the cover is backed by cash rather than a rated balance sheet — the largest ILS segment.

Sidecars

Special-purpose vehicles that take a quota share of a (re)insurer's cat book, letting investors follow its underwriting for a share of the result.

Industry loss warranties

Binary covers paying on a market-wide industry-loss index — simple, liquid, index-based (also covered in the Retrocession hub).

How a Cat Bond Works

StepWhat happens
1 · Sponsor cedes riskA (re)insurer cedes a defined cat layer to a special-purpose vehicle (SPV)
2 · SPV issues notesThe SPV sells notes to investors and holds the proceeds as collateral in trust
3 · Investors earn a couponCollateral yield + a risk spread, paid while no trigger event occurs
4 · Trigger testIf the defined event (indemnity / industry / parametric) occurs, collateral pays the sponsor
5 · MaturityIf untriggered, principal returns to investors at maturity

Trigger Types

Indemnity

Pays the sponsor's actual loss — no basis risk for the sponsor, but investors need detailed exposure data.

Industry loss

Pays on a market-wide industry-loss index (e.g. PCS) — simple, but the sponsor carries basis risk.

Parametric

Pays on a physical measure (wind speed, quake magnitude/location) — fast to settle, with the most basis risk.

Modelled loss

Pays on a model's calculated loss for the event — a middle ground between indemnity and index.

Why Investors and Sponsors Use ILS

Diversifying returns

Catastrophe risk is largely uncorrelated with equities and bonds, so ILS offers portfolio diversification and a real risk premium.

Deep, cheap capacity

For sponsors, ILS provides multi-year, fully-collateralised capacity that competes with and complements traditional reinsurance.

No credit risk

Because cover is collateralised, the sponsor is not exposed to a reinsurer's default — the counterparty risk is pre-funded.
