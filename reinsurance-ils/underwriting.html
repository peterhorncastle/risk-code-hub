Module 05 — Underwriting & Pricing

ILS — Underwriting & Pricing

ILS pricing is catastrophe pricing expressed in the language of the capital markets: expected loss, spread, and the multiple that investors demand per unit of modelled risk.

A cat bond is priced from the same modelled expected loss as a reinsurance layer, but the output is a bond spread rather than a rate-on-line. The key ratio is the multiple — the spread divided by the expected loss — which tells you how much reward investors demand for the risk. VER

Cat-Bond Pricing

Expected loss (EL) = modelled annual probability-weighted loss to the bond layer, as % of principal.

Investor coupon = reference (collateral) yield + risk spread.

Multiple = risk spread ÷ expected loss.

Expected excess return = risk spread − expected loss (the risk premium over risk-free).

The Key Concepts

Expected loss

The cat model's annualised expected loss to the specific layer — the anchor of the whole price. Rendered by the modelling firms and scrutinised by investors.

The multiple

Spread ÷ EL. Remote layers (low EL) trade at high multiples; working layers (high EL) at low multiples. It is the ILS market's headline risk-reward gauge and moves with the cycle.

Attachment & exhaustion probability

The chance the layer is touched and the chance it is wiped out — the shape of the risk investors are paid to take.

Basis risk

For index and parametric triggers, the gap between the trigger payout and the sponsor's real loss — cheaper and faster, but the sponsor bears the mismatch.

Worked Example 1 — Catastrophe Bond

Setup: £300m principal; modelled expected loss 2.0%; risk spread 7.5%; reference / collateral yield 4.0%. ILLUS

  • Multiple = 7.5% ÷ 2.0% = 3.75× — investors want 3.75× the modelled loss in spread.
  • Investor coupon = 4.0% + 7.5% = 11.5% (£34.5m a year while untriggered).
  • Expected excess return over risk-free = 7.5% − 2.0% = 5.5% — the risk premium.
  • Sponsor's annual risk cost = the 7.5% spread (£22.5m), plus fees.

Worked Example 2 — Collateralised Sidecar

Setup: £100m of collateral (= max loss); £45m ceded premium; 55% expected loss ratio; 28% ceding commission + expenses; 4% yield on collateral. ILLUS

  • Expected losses = 55% × £45m = £24.75m; costs = 28% × £45m = £12.6m.
  • Expected underwriting result = £45m − £24.75m − £12.6m = £7.65m (combined ratio ~83%).
  • Investment income on collateral = 4% × £100m = £4.0m.
  • Total expected return = £11.65m ÷ £100m = ~11.7% on capital — underwriting plus yield.

Reading the Multiple Through the Cycle

After a big loss year, capital is scarce and multiples widen — investors demand more spread per unit of expected loss. As capital rebuilds, multiples compress. The multiple, not the raw spread, is how the ILS market compares risk across bonds and through time. VER

Pre-Bind Checklist

Scrutinise the modelled expected loss and the modelling firm's assumptions.
Compare the multiple against the market for similar peril / EL.
Understand the trigger and, for index / parametric, quantify basis risk.
Confirm collateral, reset and extension-spread terms.
Check diversification against the rest of the ILS portfolio.
