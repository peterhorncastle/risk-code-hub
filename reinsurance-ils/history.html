Module 02 — History

ILS — History

Insurance-linked securities grew from a post-Andrew experiment into a structural pillar of the global reinsurance market.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

ILS was born from a capital shortage: after Hurricane Andrew showed that a single event could overwhelm traditional reinsurance capital, the industry turned to the far larger capital markets. Four decades on, alternative capital is a permanent feature of how catastrophe risk is financed. VER

Era 1 — Born of a capital gap (1992–1997)

Hurricane Andrew (1992) exposed how thin reinsurance capital was against peak US-wind risk. The first catastrophe bonds and risk-linked securities followed in the mid-1990s, tapping capital-markets money to fill the gap. VER

Era 2 — Standardisation & growth (2000s)

The cat-bond structure standardised — 144A notes, SPVs, collateral trusts, indemnity and index triggers. Dedicated ILS funds formed, and the market grew steadily through the decade, proving the model across live cat seasons. EST

Era 3 — The convergence boom (2010s)

Low interest rates sent pension and hedge-fund money into ILS in search of uncorrelated yield. Collateralised reinsurance overtook cat bonds as the largest segment, sidecars proliferated, and alternative capital grew to roughly a fifth of global reinsurance capital. VER

Era 4 — Stress, trapped capital & maturity (2017–present)

The 2017–19 hurricane and typhoon years, loss creep and trapped collateral tested investors' resolve, and some capital retreated. The 2023 Vesttoo collateral fraud was a further shock. But higher yields and a hard market drew record cat-bond issuance back by 2024–26 — ILS is now a mature, structural part of the market. VER

Key Ideas That Endure

Uncorrelated risk

The founding investment case — catastrophe risk that diversifies a financial portfolio.

Full collateralisation

Pre-funding the limit removes credit risk and is the structural core of ILS.

Convergence

ILS is where reinsurance and the capital markets permanently meet.
