ILS — Structure & Documentation

ILS wraps reinsurance risk in a securities structure, so its "wordings" are the trigger, the collateral trust, and the terms that govern when investors' capital is released or trapped.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

An ILS transaction is a piece of financial engineering as much as a reinsurance contract. Six structural features define how it behaves — and, after Vesttoo, how well its collateral can be trusted. VER

Special-purpose vehicle (SPV)

A bankruptcy-remote entity issues the notes and holds the collateral, isolating investors from the sponsor's other business.

Collateral trust

Note proceeds sit in a trust (typically in high-quality money-market assets), so the cover is fully funded — the structural heart of ILS.

Trigger definition

Indemnity, industry-loss, parametric or modelled — the precise trigger and its reporting agent decide when investors pay and how much basis risk the sponsor bears.

Reset & extension

Annual resets re-strike the trigger to hold the expected loss constant; extension spreads compensate investors if maturity is extended while a loss develops.

Loss-development & trapping

Terms governing how long collateral is held while a loss is quantified — the trapped-collateral problem investors watch closely.

Collateral verification

Confirming the collateral is genuine and the letters of credit real — the direct lesson of the Vesttoo fraud.

Investor & Sponsor Discipline

For the investor

  • Scrutinise the modelled expected loss and the trigger's basis risk.
  • Understand reset, extension and trapping terms before the cat season.
  • Verify the collateral and its custodian independently.

For the sponsor

  • Weigh basis risk (index / parametric) against price and speed.
  • Confirm the cover meshes with the traditional reinsurance programme.
  • Plan for multi-year capacity and reset economics.

⚠️ Basis risk & trapped collateral

The two structural risks unique to ILS: an index or parametric cover may pay differently from the sponsor's real loss (basis risk), and after an event a bond's collateral can be trapped — held past maturity while the loss is quantified, denying investors their capital. Clear trigger definitions, extension spreads and loss-development terms are how both are managed; verified collateral is how the Vesttoo failure is avoided.
