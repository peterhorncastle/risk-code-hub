Module 05 — Rates & ROE

Facultative — Rates & ROE

Facultative pricing tracks the direct market it sits above — and, for excess, the same exposure curve that governs per-risk XoL. Its economics turn on winning good individual risks at an adequate rate.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Facultative rates move with the underlying direct market — when primary property rates harden, facultative follows. For fac-excess, the share of a risk that falls in a layer is governed by the exposure curve, so higher attachments cost far less. The Facultative Economics in Words

Proportional fac

Follows the original

The reinsurer inherits the direct rate and terms, so its result depends on how well the original risk was priced — selection and rate adequacy are everything.

Excess fac — low layer

Exposed, higher rate

A working facultative layer near the EML carries a real exposure factor and a meaningful rate-on-line.

Excess fac — high layer

Thin, cheap tail

A layer well above the EML has a tiny exposure factor and a very low rate — genuine catastrophe-of-the-risk cover.

Reinsurer Return — What Drives It

Facultative return is won risk by risk: adequate rates, good selection and disciplined EML judgement, against the higher per-risk transaction cost. ILLUS

Adequate rates

Typical
Follows the market

Soft & adverse
Thin rates, a loss
