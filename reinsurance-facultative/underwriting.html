Facultative — Underwriting & Pricing

Facultative is the one place in reinsurance where the reinsurer underwrites the individual risk — reading the survey, setting the EML and pricing the layer or share on its own merits.

Because a facultative reinsurer accepts one risk at a time, it prices that risk directly — not a portfolio average. The proportional form follows the original economics; the excess form is exposure-rated off the risk's value and profile. VER

The Two Pricing Routes

Facultative proportional: Ceded premium = Original premium × cession; net = ceded premium − ceding commission − brokerage.

Facultative excess: Expected losses to layer = Original premium × expected loss ratio × exposure factor.

Layer premium = Expected losses to layer × (1 + risk load) ÷ (1 − expenses − brokerage).

Rate-on-line = Layer premium ÷ Limit  ·  Payback = 1 ÷ Rate-on-line.

The Key Concepts

EML / PML

The estimated (or probable) maximum loss — how much of the sum insured a realistic worst case would actually destroy. It drives how much of a layer is genuinely exposed.

Exposure factor

The share of the risk's expected loss that falls in the layer, read off an exposure curve applied to the sum insured or EML — the heart of fac-excess pricing.

Original economics

For fac-proportional, the reinsurer inherits the original rate and terms, so its result depends on how well the direct risk was priced.

Individual survey

Facultative underwriting reads the risk survey, construction, occupancy and protections — information a treaty reinsurer never sees per risk.

Worked Example 1 — Facultative Proportional

Setup: A £20m property risk; original premium £80,000; the cedant cedes 50% facultatively; 25% ceding commission; 10% brokerage. ILLUS

  • Ceded SI = 50% × £20m = £10m exposure.
  • Ceded premium = 50% × £80,000 = £40,000.
  • Less ceding commission £10,000 and brokerage £4,000 → net premium to reinsurer £26,000.
  • The reinsurer's result then tracks the original risk's loss experience on its 50% share.

Worked Example 2 — Facultative Excess

Setup: Same £20m risk; a £10m xs £10m facultative layer; original premium £80,000; expected loss ratio 55%; exposure factor 18% (share of expected loss in the layer); risk load 35%; expenses 5%; brokerage 10%. ILLUS

  • Original expected losses = 55% × £80,000 = £44,000.
  • Expected losses to the layer = 18% × £44,000 ≈ £7,920.
  • Loaded ×1.35 = £10,692; ÷ 0.85 → layer premium ≈ £12,580.
  • Rate-on-line = £12,580 ÷ £10m ≈ 0.13% — a thin top layer, rarely exposed on this EML.

Pre-Bind Checklist

Read the individual risk survey — construction, occupancy, protections, EML.
For fac-proportional, judge whether the original rate is adequate before following it.
For fac-excess, build the exposure factor from a curve on the sum insured / EML.
Confirm the wording follows or departs from the original policy terms.
Check how the facultative cession interacts with the treaty (inuring order).
