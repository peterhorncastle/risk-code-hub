Facultative — History

Facultative is the oldest form of reinsurance — a single risk shared between insurers long before the automatic treaty was invented.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Before treaties existed, an insurer with a risk too large to keep simply asked another insurer to take a share of that one risk. That single-risk sharing — facultative — is the origin of all reinsurance, and it survives because some risks will always need individual attention. VER

Era 1 — The original reinsurance (14th–19th century)

The earliest reinsurance transactions were facultative: a marine or fire underwriter laid off part of a single voyage or building to another. Each was a one-off negotiation — the faculty to accept or decline sat with the reinsurer. EST

Era 2 — Treaties overtake the bulk (late 19th century)

The professional reinsurers of the 1800s automated the routine with treaties, moving the bulk of business onto obligatory cessions. Facultative narrowed to what treaties could not efficiently handle — the large, the unusual, the excess. EST

Era 3 — The specialist single-risk market (20th century)

Facultative became a distinct market with its own underwriters, brokers and pricing — exposure curves, EML analysis and risk surveys — serving large property, energy, marine and specialty risks that exceeded or fell outside treaty terms. VER

Era 4 — Efficiency, MGAs & automation (2000s–present)

Facultative faced pressure from higher treaty capacity and cost scrutiny, and responded with facilities, semi-automatic "fac-oblig" arrangements and digital placement platforms. It remains essential for the largest and most complex individual risks. EST

Key Ideas That Endure

The faculty

The reinsurer's option to accept or decline each risk — the defining feature that gives the class its name.

Individual underwriting

Facultative is the only reinsurance where the reinsurer truly underwrites each risk itself.

The treaty's partner

Facultative has always existed alongside treaties, handling exactly what the automatic covers cannot.
