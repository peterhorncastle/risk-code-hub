Facultative Reinsurance — Overview

The single-risk market. Where a treaty covers a whole book automatically, facultative reinsures one specific risk, negotiated and priced on its own merits. It is how the market handles risks too big, too unusual or outside the terms of the treaties.

"Facultative" means the reinsurer has the faculty — the option — to accept or decline each individual risk. Unlike a treaty, which obliges both sides to cede and accept a whole class automatically, facultative is transacted one risk at a time: the reinsurer sees the risk, underwrites it and prices it. It is the market's scalpel, used where the treaty's broad brush will not do. VER

The Two Forms of Facultative

Facultative proportional

The reinsurer takes a share (say 50%) of one risk — its premium and losses in the same ratio, less a ceding commission. Simple, and common for large property risks.

Facultative excess of loss

The reinsurer writes a layer on one risk — a limit excess of the cedant's retention — priced by exposure rating off the risk's value and profile.

Facultative obligatory ("fac-oblig")

A hybrid: the cedant may cede risks at its option but the reinsurer must accept them — a bridge between fac and treaty for a defined flow of risks.

Why Cedants Buy Facultative

Capacity top-up

A risk whose sum insured exceeds the treaty's capacity is placed facultatively for the excess.

Out-of-terms risks

A risk excluded from the treaty — an unusual occupancy, territory or peril — is reinsured individually.

Protecting the treaty

Ceding a single volatile risk facultatively keeps it out of the treaty result, protecting the cedant's treaty terms at renewal.

Expertise & sharing

Facultative brings a specialist reinsurer's underwriting view — and capacity — to a complex individual risk.
