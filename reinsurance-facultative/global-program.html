Module 07 — Programme Structuring

Facultative — Programme Structuring

Facultative is the top-up and the safety valve of a reinsurance programme — it fills what the treaties cannot reach and takes out the risks that would spoil their terms.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

The treaties do the routine work automatically; facultative handles the exceptions. A risk that exceeds the treaty's capacity is topped up facultatively; a risk outside the treaty's terms is placed on its own; and a single volatile risk can be ceded facultatively to keep it out of the treaty result. VER

Three Roles in the Programme

RoleHow facultative is used
Capacity top-upA risk larger than the treaty's per-risk capacity is placed facultatively for the excess above the treaty
Out-of-terms coverA risk the treaty excludes (occupancy, territory, peril) is reinsured individually
Treaty protectionA single volatile risk is ceded facultatively so it does not deteriorate the treaty result and terms

Facultative and the Treaty — Order of Cover

Inuring to the treaty

If the facultative reduces what the treaty sees, the treaty attaches on the risk net of the facultative — cheaper treaty, but the fac must be reliable.

Outside the treaty

If the facultative sits outside, the treaty responds on the gross risk and the fac is an extra, parallel recovery — the order must be explicit.

Avoiding gaps

The facultative attachment and limit must line up with the treaty's capacity so there is no uncovered slice between them.

The Placement Workflow

1

Identify the risk

A risk exceeds treaty capacity, falls outside its terms, or is too volatile to leave in the treaty.

2

Prepare the submission

The broker assembles the survey, EML, values and original terms for the individual risk.

3

Underwrite & quote

Facultative reinsurers underwrite the risk and quote — proportional share or excess layer.

4

Place & document

The broker completes the placement; wording, following-form and inuring order are set.

5

Manage the risk

Monitor material changes and claims; account for the fac against the treaty programme.

Case Study — A £60m Property Risk on a £40m Treaty

An insurer writes a £60m single-site property risk but its surplus/XoL treaty programme only gives it £40m of per-risk capacity.

The problem

£20m of the risk sits above the treaty's capacity — unprotected unless placed elsewhere.

The structure

A £20m xs £40m facultative layer tops up the risk to its full £60m value, sitting above the treaty tower.

The terms

Following-form wording, an EML basis, a reinstatement on the fac layer, and an explicit inuring order against the treaty.

The result

The insurer writes the whole £60m risk with full reinsurance protection, without disturbing its treaty terms.
