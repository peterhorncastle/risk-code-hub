Facultative — Contract & Wordings

Facultative is a single-risk contract, so its wording is about matching the original policy, defining the exposure, and getting the order right where it meets the treaty.

Because each facultative cession is a bespoke single-risk contract, six wording points do most of the work of making sure it responds exactly as intended and sits correctly against the rest of the programme. VER

Follow-the-original / following form

The facultative wording usually mirrors the original policy's terms and conditions, so the reinsurer covers what the cedant covers — no more, no less.

Sum insured / EML basis

Whether the cession is on the full sum insured or the estimated maximum loss changes how much of a layer is really exposed and what the reinsurer should charge.

Inuring / order of cover

Whether the facultative inures to the benefit of the treaty (reduces what the treaty sees) or sits outside it must be explicit — it changes both parties' exposure.

Claims control & cooperation

On large single risks the reinsurer often wants claims-cooperation or claims-control rights, given how material one loss is.

Reinstatement (excess fac)

A facultative excess layer may include a reinstatement so it can respond again after a partial loss on the same risk.

Exclusions & warranties

Risk-specific exclusions and warranties (protections, maintenance) are common on individually underwritten risks.

Placement Discipline

Before binding

  • Underwrite the individual risk — survey, construction, occupancy, EML.
  • Confirm following-form vs bespoke terms and any risk-specific exclusions.
  • Settle the inuring order against the treaty so cover does not gap or double-count.

During the risk

  • Monitor material changes to the risk and any warranties.
  • Exercise claims-cooperation rights on large notifications.
  • Track exposure against the treaty programme for accumulation.

⚠️ The inuring trap

If a facultative cession is meant to protect the treaty but the inuring order is left ambiguous, the same loss can be presented to both the fac and the treaty, or fall between them. Always state explicitly whether the facultative inures to the benefit of the treaty — it decides who pays first and how much the treaty really sees.
