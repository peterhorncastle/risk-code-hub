Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    VER Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) 2002 — US Federal Backstop

    Pub.L. 107-297, 116 Stat. 2322 (2002). Creates a federal programme of shared public and private compensation for insured commercial property and casualty losses from certified acts of terrorism. Key provisions: make-available mandate (insurers must offer terrorism cover); Secretary of Treasury certification of terrorism events; $100bn programme year cap; mandatory insurer retentions as % of direct earned premium. Extended by TRIEA 2005 (Pub.L. 109-144) and TRIPRA 2007 (Pub.L. 110-160). Available at: treas.gov/TRIP.

  • [L2]
    VER TRIA Reauthorization Act 2019 — Extended to December 2027

    Terrorism Risk Insurance Program Reauthorization Act of 2019 (Pub.L. 116-94, Division F). Extends the TRIA programme to 31 December 2027. Confirms certification process (Treasury Secretary + Attorney General + Secretary of Homeland Security, acting jointly). Certification threshold: aggregate loss of $5m minimum. Programme cap: $100bn per programme year. Congressional Budget Office score: negligible budgetary effect in absence of certified act. Provides long-term certainty for US terrorism insurance market participants.

  • [L3]
    VER Reinsurance (Acts of Terrorism) Act 1993 — Pool Re Statutory Basis

    Reinsurance (Acts of Terrorism) Act 1993 (c. 18). Statutory basis for Pool Re — the UK government-backed terrorism reinsurance pool. Enacted following the Baltic Exchange (1992) and Bishopsgate (1993) IRA bombings. Provides that the UK government may reinsure Pool Re, effectively acting as reinsurer of last resort. Pool Re was reformed in 2002 (all-risks coverage), 2017 (NBCR added) and 2019 (SI cap removed). Available at: poolre.co.uk. Administered by HM Treasury.

  • [L4]
    VER Anti-Terrorism, Crime & Security Act 2001 (UK) — Post-9/11 Response

    Anti-terrorism, Crime and Security Act 2001 (c. 24). Enacted in the immediate aftermath of 9/11 — received Royal Assent 14 December 2001. Extended police powers to freeze assets of suspected terrorists; introduced new offences of inciting terrorism abroad; amended extradition provisions. Insurance significance: confirms UK government's commitment to robust counter-terrorism legal framework supporting the Pool Re architecture. Superseded in many respects by the Terrorism Act 2000 and later Counter-Terrorism legislation.

  • [L5]
    VER Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015 — Protect Duty / Prevent

    Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015 (c. 6). Introduces the Prevent duty — requiring specified authorities (universities, schools, local authorities, NHS trusts) to have "due regard to the need to prevent people from being drawn into terrorism." Significant for risk management: the Act's underlying framework (alongside the Terrorism Act 2000 and the government's CONTEST strategy) establishes the legal context for UK terrorism threat assessment and corporate duty of care obligations in respect of protecting staff and customers.

  • [L6]
    VER Terrorism Act 2000 — UK Statutory Definition of Terrorism

    Terrorism Act 2000 (c. 11). Section 1 provides the authoritative UK statutory definition of terrorism: an action that involves serious violence against a person, serious damage to property, endangering life, creating a serious risk to public health or safety, or seriously interfering with an electronic system; AND the action or threat is designed to influence a government or international governmental organisation or to intimidate the public or a section of the public; AND the action or threat is made for the purpose of advancing a political, religious, racial or ideological cause. This definition is the foundation of UK terrorism insurance policy wording trigger clauses.

  • [L7]
    VER UN Security Council Resolution 1373 (2001) — Global Counter-Terrorism Framework

    UNSC Res. 1373, adopted 28 September 2001 (post-9/11). Obliged all UN member states to: criminalise the financing of terrorism; freeze terrorist assets; prevent recruitment; deny safe haven to those who finance, plan, support or commit terrorist acts. Established the Counter-Terrorism Committee (CTC). Insurance significance: Resolution 1373 created the international legal framework for sanctions screening — a critical compliance obligation for all terrorism and political risk insurers dealing with cross-border transactions.

  • [L8]
    VER EU Directive 2017/541 — Combating Terrorism (Harmonised EU Definition)

    Directive (EU) 2017/541 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 March 2017 on combating terrorism. Harmonises the definition of terrorist offences, offences related to a terrorist group, and offences related to terrorist activities across EU member states. Replaces the 2002 Framework Decision on Combating Terrorism. Insurance significance: provides a harmonised EU definition of terrorism for policy wording consistency across EU-sited risks, particularly relevant for multinational terrorism programmes covering France, Belgium, Germany, Spain, Netherlands simultaneously.

  • [L9]
    VER Insurance Act 2015 (UK) — Fair Presentation of Risk

    Insurance Act 2015 (c. 4). Replaces the Marine Insurance Act 1906 for non-consumer commercial insurance. Terrorism insureds are subject to the duty of fair presentation: every material circumstance the insured knows or ought to know must be disclosed — including known security threats, previous terrorism-related incidents on or near the insured premises, and any specific intelligence about threats to the risk. Proportionate remedies for breach. Particularly relevant for terrorism underwriting where the insured may hold information (from security services, CPNI, threat intelligence providers) that is material to the risk.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    EST Swiss Re sigma 1/2002 — 9/11 Insured Loss Analysis

    Swiss Re, sigma No. 1/2002: Natural Catastrophes and Man-Made Disasters in 2001 — Man-Made Losses at an All-Time High. Swiss Reinsurance Company, Zurich (2002). Definitive analysis of 9/11 insured losses: total $40.1bn (2001 values), comprising property damage ($11.7bn), business interruption ($11.0bn), aviation ($4.5bn), workers' compensation ($1.8bn), liability ($6.5bn), life/health ($3.7bn). Lloyd's market share: ~$4.8bn. Fundamental reference for 9/11 loss data. Available at: swissre.com/sigma.

  • [I2]
    VER Pool Re — Annual Report & Market Statistics 2023

    Pool Re (Pool Reinsurance Company Limited), Annual Report 2023. Pool Re, London (2024). Reports participating member premium volumes, aggregate exposure by postcode sector, Cat model comparison methodology, and Pool Re claims history since 1993. Key data: Bishopsgate 1993 (£350m Pool Re payout), Canary Wharf 1996 (£185m), Manchester 2017 (Pool Re claim confirmed). NBCR capacity data. Available at: poolre.co.uk. The definitive source for UK terrorism insurance market statistics.

  • [I3]
    EST Marsh — Terrorism Insurance Market Report 2024

    Marsh Ltd, Terrorism & Political Violence Market Report 2024. Marsh LLC, London/New York (2024). Published annually. Covers: Lloyd's terrorism capacity (TO/TU/TW) trends; active assailant market development; political violence premium growth in EM markets; country-level rate movement; TRIA renewal analysis. Provides broker-perspective market intelligence including capacity estimates and rate index data. Available to Marsh clients; summary data available at marsh.com.

  • [I4]
    VER OECD — Terrorism Insurance in OECD Countries: Government Schemes 2021

    OECD, Terrorism Risk Insurance 2021: Comparative Analysis of Government Schemes. OECD Publishing, Paris (2021). Comprehensive comparative analysis of government terrorism insurance pools across OECD member states: TRIA (US), Pool Re (UK), GAREAT (France), Extremus (Germany), Consorcio (Spain), ARPC (Australia), CCR (France), NHT (Netherlands), TRIP (Belgium), SASRIA (South Africa). Analyses backstop trigger thresholds, capacity limits, premium structures and claims histories. Available at: oecd.org/finance/insurance. The authoritative academic reference for government pool comparison.

  • [I5]
    VER Lloyd's — Terrorism Market Bulletin & Capacity Guide

    Lloyd's of London, Market Bulletins on Terrorism and Political Violence (various, 2002–2025). Key bulletins: Y2529 (terrorism exclusions post-9/11), Y3765 (active assailant wording guidance), Y5101 (political violence coverage notes), Y5482 (war on land — Ukraine considerations 2022). Lloyd's also publishes aggregate exposure data for terrorism accumulation management. Available to Lloyd's market participants at: lloyds.com/market-resources. Lloyd's terrorism market capacity: estimated $800m–1bn GWP (est., not publicly reported at sub-class level).

  • [I6]
    EST Verisk Maplecroft — Political Risk Atlas 2024

    Verisk Maplecroft, Political Risk Atlas 2024. Verisk Analytics / Maplecroft, Bath UK (2024). Published annually. Provides country-level terrorism, political violence, civil unrest, war and geopolitical risk indices for 198 countries. Key 2024 findings: Ukraine remains 'extreme risk' (WL); Palestine, Myanmar, Sudan 'extreme'; rising risk in West Africa (Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger); geopolitical risk premium increasing in Southeast Asia (Taiwan Strait). Used by Lloyd's underwriters for country risk zone assignment and pricing. Available at: maplecroft.com.

  • [I7]
    EST Aon — Terrorism Risk in Commercial Real Estate 2024

    Aon plc, Terrorism Risk in Commercial Real Estate: 2024 Market Perspective. Aon Risk Solutions, London/New York (2024). Analyses terrorism exposure for commercial real estate portfolios: landmark loading methodology, blast radius modelling for city-centre assets, PML scenario analysis for London CBD, New York Midtown, Paris La Défense. Provides indicative rate ranges by asset type and country zone. Key data source for underwriting benchmarks used in this hub. Available to Aon clients; summary at aon.com.

  • [I8]
    EST RMS / Moody's — Terrorism Catastrophe Model Methodology 2023

    RMS (Risk Management Solutions, a Moody's company), RMS Terrorism Model: Methodology and Validation 2023. RMS, Newark CA (2023). The leading catastrophe model for terrorism loss estimation. Model covers: vehicle bomb, VBIED, suicide bomb, active assailant, NBCR, and cyberterrorism attack scenarios. Used by Lloyd's syndicates for PML calculation, aggregation management and capital modelling. Key model scenarios: City of London VBIED (benchmark £1bn+ industry loss), New York Midtown, Chicago Loop, Los Angeles CBD. Available at: rms.com. Subscription model required for full access.

  • [I9]
    EST CPNI (Centre for Protection of National Infrastructure) — Crowded Places Guidance

    Centre for Protection of National Infrastructure (CPNI) / National Counter Terrorism Security Office (NaCTSO), Crowded Places: Guidance for Venues and Events (UK Home Office, 2024 update). Provides detailed physical security guidance for retail centres, sports stadia, hotels, transport hubs, entertainment venues and public spaces — including hostile vehicle mitigation specifications, blast-resistant glazing standards, CCTV requirements, access control, staff training and emergency planning. Available free at: protectuk.police.uk. The authoritative UK source for terrorism physical security standards, referenced in underwriting as the benchmark for risk management credit assessment.

  • [I10]
    VER Geneva Association — Insuring the Climate and Terrorism Nexus 2023

    The Geneva Association, Insuring the Climate-Terrorism Nexus: Emerging Compound Risks for the Insurance Industry. The Geneva Association, Zurich (2023). Examines the interaction between climate-related events and terrorism/political violence — including: climate displacement driving political instability and civil unrest; critical infrastructure dual exposure (flood AND terrorism); climate-motivated extremism as an emerging terrorism vector. Raises questions about whether compound events (flood + political violence) are covered by current terrorism and property wordings. Available at: genevaassociation.org.
