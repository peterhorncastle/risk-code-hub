Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Location — City Centre vs Landmark

City-centre commercial property in major urban financial districts (London EC2/EC3, New York Midtown, Paris 1er) attracts significant loadings over suburban or rural risk. Iconic or symbolic buildings — landmarks, stock exchanges, government buildings — attract further "target" loadings. Distance from a potential vehicle bomb detonation point is modelled using blast radius analysis. EST

Loading: +30% (city centre) to +100%+ (landmark iconic target)
High Impact

Country Risk Rating (Zone A–E)

The sovereign and political risk profile of the country of location is the primary rate driver for international terrorism. Underwriters use proprietary country risk models (Maplecroft, Jane's, Verisk) to assign each country to a risk zone (A–E). Zone A countries (stable democracies) attract low base rates; Zone E (active conflict, failed states) attracts maximum rates or declination. EST

Base rate range: 0.002% SI (Zone A) to 0.50%+ SI (Zone E est.)
High Impact

Construction & Occupancy (Soft Target vs Hardened)

Glass curtain-wall buildings with no vehicle perimeter protection are "soft targets" — significantly more vulnerable to vehicle bomb and active assailant attacks than blast-hardened, access-controlled facilities. Occupancy type also matters: hospitality, retail and public-access venues face higher active assailant/vehicle attack exposure than restricted-access corporate headquarters. EST

Soft target loading: +25–50%; hardened facility credit: −15–25%
High Impact

Sum Insured & PML Modelling

Terrorism losses can be total losses — 9/11 proved that even the largest commercial buildings can be fully destroyed. PML modelling is essential for large sums insured. Catastrophe models (RMS, AIR, Verisk) are used to estimate probable maximum loss on a risk and portfolio basis. Aggregation management across the portfolio is a key underwriting control — correlated losses can exceed individual risk capacity. EST

PML modelling mandatory for risks >£100m SI; aggregate exposure managed at syndicate level
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

NBCR Sublimit or Exclusion

Whether NBCR (Nuclear, Biological, Chemical, Radiological) perils are included, excluded or sublimited significantly affects risk profile. Most standard terrorism policies exclude NBCR; specialist Lloyd's syndicates offer NBCR endorsements with sublimits of $25–50m. Government backstops (Pool Re post-2017, TRIA) may provide partial NBCR cover in specific jurisdictions. VER

NBCR endorsement: +15–30% of terrorism premium; sublimits $25–50m typically
Medium Impact

Business Interruption Period & Indemnity

BI indemnity period of 12–36 months is standard for commercial property. Longer BI periods increase exposure significantly — a 36-month period for a major London office block could represent £20–50m+ in BI losses alone. Access denial BI (inability to access premises even without direct damage) is a critical underwriting consideration for urban risks following a nearby incident. EST

36-month BI vs 12-month: +20–35% BI element premium; access denial: +10–15%
Medium Impact

Active Assailant Extension

Standalone active assailant cover or AA endorsement on a terrorism policy significantly broadens exposure to mass casualty events that produce minimal property damage — vehicle attacks, knife attacks, active shooter scenarios. AA cover includes BI, crisis management costs, medical expenses and third-party liability. Premium loading reflects the broad trigger and increasing frequency of AA events. EST

AA endorsement: +10–20% of terrorism base premium depending on occupancy type
Medium Impact

Aggregation & Portfolio Concentration

Terrorism losses are highly correlated — a single large bomb in a city financial district can simultaneously damage many policyholders. Portfolio aggregation analysis (mapping all insured locations and their proximity to each other) is an essential underwriting discipline. Lloyd's syndicates manage aggregation through scenario-based PML limits and peer comparison against Lloyd's market aggregate exposures. VER

High-concentration portfolios attract additional loading; aggregation management limits written premium capacity
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Physical Security Measures

Blast-resistant glazing, vehicle barriers (bollards, hostile vehicle mitigation), access control, CCTV and security guard presence all reduce physical damage severity in the event of an attack. CPNI (Centre for Protection of National Infrastructure) compliant security plans attract modest rate credits from Lloyd's underwriters. VER

Lower Impact

Tenant Mix

High-profile government tenants or politically sensitive occupiers (embassies, defence contractors, high-profile media) increase target value and attract loadings. Generic commercial tenants in diversified multi-tenanted buildings are generally more attractive than single-tenant trophy buildings with politically symbolic occupants.

Lower Impact

Crisis Management Retainer

Pre-agreed crisis management retainer (with a firm such as Control Risks, NYA, ASB) demonstrates proactive risk management and enables rapid incident response — reducing BI period post-event. Underwriters regard a crisis management retainer as a positive risk management signal, supporting modest premium credits.

Lower Impact

Event Cancellation Interface

Venues and event spaces face terrorism exposure across both property and event cancellation/abandonment policies. Coordination of terrorism definitions between the property terrorism policy and the event cancellation policy avoids coverage gaps and ensures BI continuity. Underwriters should confirm both policies use consistent terrorism definitions.

Aggregation Warning: Terrorism is the most correlated catastrophe peril in the insurance market. A single device detonated in a major city financial district can simultaneously damage multiple policyholders held by the same syndicate. Unlike natural catastrophe, terrorism aggregation can produce losses concentrated in a small geographic area that nevertheless represent the syndicate's maximum aggregate exposure. Underwriters must conduct scenario-based PML analysis — particularly a City of London/Canary Wharf scenario, a New York Midtown scenario and a Paris CBD scenario — before committing capacity to any individual risk in these zones.
