Tail Risk Caveat: Terrorism is the most extreme tail-risk insurance class. A single 9/11-scale event would produce losses orders of magnitude larger than the annual premium income of even the largest Lloyd's terrorism book. Government backstops (TRIA, Pool Re, GAREAT) are essential to market sustainability — without them, the class would be unwritable at any commercially viable premium. ROE scenarios above assume backstops function as intended. Bear scenario does not contemplate an event approaching 9/11 scale, which would be a market-level catastrophe rather than a portfolio-level loss. [I1]

Reserving & IBNR Considerations
FactorImpact on Reserves
Catastrophic event tailA major terrorism event produces loss development over 5–10+ years — liability claims, BI disputes, coverage litigation, subrogation. IBNR loading must reflect this extended development tail
War on land (WL) developmentUkraine WL losses are still developing as of 2026 — physical damage assessments remain outstanding in active conflict zones; IBNR for WL must reflect uncertainty about when and how claims can be assessed
Government backstop recoveryClaims ceded to Pool Re, TRIA or GAREAT must be tracked separately — gross reserves should be maintained; reinsurance recovery recognised as receivable rather than netted from reserves
Active assailant frequency developmentAA losses typically develop quickly (property and casualty claims) but liability claims (duty of care to protect against terrorism) can take 3–5 years to resolve — particularly following landmark duty of care judgments such as Manchester Arena
Aggregation scenario stress-testingActuaries should model a London-CBD terrorism scenario and a New York-Midtown scenario annually to confirm IBNR and reserves are adequate to withstand a major event; compare against Lloyd's market aggregate guidance
