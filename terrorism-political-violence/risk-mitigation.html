PREVENT — Physical Security Controls
PREVENT

Physical Security Assessment (CPNI-Aligned)

A formal Hostile Environment and Threat Assessment (HEAT) carried out by a qualified security consultant — ideally CPNI-aligned — evaluates a building's vulnerability to vehicle bomb, active assailant and package-delivered CBRN attack. Assessment covers site perimeter, access points, glazing specification, standoff distances and evacuation routes. [I9]

  • Annual review for Zone C/D locations
  • Post-incident review after any nearby terrorism event
  • Assessment report should be retained and shared with insurers
PREVENT

Hostile Vehicle Mitigation (HVM)

Vehicle barriers (security bollards, HVM planters, rising arm barriers, concrete blocks) create a standoff distance between the vehicle threat and the building. The standoff distance is the single most effective physical measure for reducing blast damage from a vehicle-borne IED (VBIED). CPNI's Crowded Places guidance specifies minimum standoff distances by building risk level. [I9]

  • Certified bollards to PAS 68 / IWA 14-1 standards
  • Minimum 5m standoff significantly reduces structural damage
  • HVM perimeter should be reviewed after Nice/Berlin vehicle attack model
PREVENT

Blast-Resistant Glazing

Standard architectural glass generates lethal shards in an explosion. Blast-resistant or laminated safety glazing holds together under blast pressure, dramatically reducing secondary fragmentation casualties. Retrospective glazing film can be applied to existing glass at lower cost. The 1996 Canary Wharf and 2005 7/7 experience confirmed glazing quality as a key casualty reduction factor. [I2]

PREVENT

Access Control & Visitor Management

Layered access control — reception screening, card/biometric access to upper floors, visitor badge and escort procedures — reduces active assailant dwell time in a building and prevents package bombs being placed inside the perimeter. For high-profile tenants, mail room screening (X-ray, explosive trace detection) is standard. [I9]

PREVENT

Staff Hostile Environment & Security Awareness Training

UK Home Office ACT (Action Counters Terrorism) Awareness eLearning is recommended for all building occupants. Staff trained to recognise suspicious behaviour, vehicles, packages and individuals. Training should cover: reporting channels (Action Counters Terrorism Hotline 0800 789 321), suspicious activity recognition, and building-specific security protocols. [L5]

PREVENT

Cyber-Physical Security Convergence

Increasingly, physical security systems (CCTV, access control, building management systems) are networked and potentially vulnerable to cyber attack. A cyber attack disabling a building's access control system could facilitate a subsequent physical attack. Organisations should ensure physical security systems are on isolated network segments and subject to cybersecurity review. [I6]

DETECT — Threat Intelligence & Monitoring
DETECT

Threat Intelligence Subscription

Professional threat intelligence services (Jane's IHS, Healix International, Verisk Maplecroft, Control Risks, NYA International) provide real-time country and city-level terrorism and political violence threat assessments. For multinational organisations, threat intelligence feeds should be integrated into travel security and crisis response procedures. [I6]

  • Country risk ratings updated in real time
  • City-level tactical intelligence for major urban locations
  • Pre-travel risk briefings for staff to high-risk countries
DETECT

Suspicious Activity Reporting — "See Something, Say Something"

The UK government's "See Something, Say Something" campaign and Action Counters Terrorism (ACT) programme encourage staff and the public to report suspicious activity, unattended packages or suspicious individuals. A clear internal suspicious activity reporting protocol — who to call, how to report, escalation procedure — should be maintained and practised. [L5]

DETECT

Real-Time Incident Monitoring

Crisis management firms and threat intelligence providers offer 24/7 real-time incident monitoring for terrorism and political violence events globally. For multinational organisations with operations in Zone D/E countries, a real-time monitoring subscription enables rapid staff notification, travel restriction and asset protection decisions within minutes of an incident. [I6]

DETECT

Supply Chain Vulnerability Assessment

Terrorism attacks increasingly target supply chains as a mechanism to maximise economic disruption. A supply chain security review should identify critical suppliers in high-risk territories, assess their physical security posture, and confirm whether their business interruption would trigger a material loss for the insured's operations. Particularly relevant for manufacturing and logistics-dependent businesses. [I4]

Active Assailant Response Checklist — Run / Hide / Tell

UK Home Office "Run / Hide / Tell" Active Assailant Response Protocol [L5]

RUN: If there is a safe route, escape immediately. Leave belongings behind. Encourage others to leave but do not let them slow you down. Prevent others from entering the area. Call 999 when safe. Do not assume the alarm or others will alert the police.
HIDE: If escape is not possible, find a secure area away from the attacker. Lock/barricade doors. Move away from windows. Be quiet and turn phones to silent. Turn off lights. Do not open the door for anyone — wait for police instruction.
TELL: Call 999 as soon as possible. Remain calm and provide: your location, number and type of attackers, description of attackers, direction of travel, number of victims. Do not put yourself at risk to make the call.
Lockdown protocol: Building lockdown should be triggered by a designated security/facilities authority — not by individual staff. Lockdown protocol should be practised in annual exercises. Electronic door control (remote lockdown from security control room) should be tested quarterly.
Evacuation routes: Multiple evacuation routes should be identified — primary (away from main entrance) and secondary (rooftop, rear access, basement car park). Routes should be clearly signed and regularly walked by building staff. Muster points should be away from the building and clearly marked.
Communication tree: A pre-defined communications cascade (security team → senior management → all staff) should be tested bi-annually. WhatsApp/mass notification app for rapid staff alert. Agree a "safe word" for real incident vs drill.
Staff training frequency: Active assailant awareness training — Run/Hide/Tell — should be delivered to all staff annually (online) and floor wardens/security staff in full tabletop exercise format bi-annually. Training records should be maintained to evidence duty of care compliance. [L5]
Business continuity trigger: The active assailant protocol should automatically trigger the Business Continuity Plan (BCP). Pre-designate an alternative operating site; confirm remote working capability for all staff within 2 hours of incident. Crisis management retainer firm (Control Risks / NYA) should be on speed-dial for immediate activation.
Protect UK / CPNI (Centre for Protection of National Infrastructure): The UK government's National Counter Terrorism Security Office (NaCTSO) — operating as Protect UK — provides free, authoritative physical security guidance for UK businesses and venue operators. Key publications include: Crowded Places Guidance (for retail, hospitality, events and public space operators), Hostile Vehicle Mitigation (HVM specification and bollard standards), Protective Security for People, and the ACT Awareness eLearning. All UK commercial property owners and venue operators should engage with Protect UK / CPNI guidance as a minimum standard of due diligence. Available at: protectuk.police.uk and cpni.gov.uk. [I9]
Emerging Threat — Drone / UAV Attacks: Small unmanned aircraft (drones / UAVs) have emerged as a delivery mechanism for explosive payloads, CBRN dispersal agents and reconnaissance in conflict zones (Ukraine) and are being considered as a terrorism vector in civilian contexts. Standard terrorism insurance wordings are being reviewed to confirm or clarify coverage for drone-delivered attacks. Key questions: is a drone an "aircraft" (triggering the standard war/aviation exclusion) or a "weapon"? Organisations in Zone C/D should include drone threat in their security assessments. Lloyd's market is developing drone-specific endorsement wording as of 2024–25. [I6]

01
Commission a Security Assessment

CPNI-aligned physical security review — the foundation of all terrorism risk management

02
Install HVM Perimeter

Certified bollards/planters create standoff — the single most effective physical damage reduction measure

03
Train All Staff — Run/Hide/Tell

Annual Run/Hide/Tell training is a legal duty of care obligation and a premium credit factor

04
Subscribe to Threat Intelligence

Real-time country and city-level intelligence enables rapid incident response and travel restrictions

05
Retain a Crisis Management Firm

Pre-agreed retainer enables 24/7 incident response, reducing BI period and casualty management costs

06
Test the BCP for Terrorism Scenarios

Business continuity plans should be tested against a terrorism-specific scenario annually

07
Review Policy Wording for Coverage Gaps

Confirm BI period, access denial, active assailant and NBCR status in policy wording annually
