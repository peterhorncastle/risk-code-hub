Prevention Controls (PREVENT)
Prevent

Clinical Governance Framework

Compliance with NICE guidelines, Royal College standards and NHS England clinical frameworks. Regular internal clinical audit comparing outcomes to national benchmarks. Clinical governance committee with non-executive oversight. The foundation of all risk reduction in healthcare settings. VER

Underwriting credit: −10 to −20% for documented governance framework
Prevent

Informed Consent Documentation (Post-Montgomery)

Post-Montgomery v Lanarkshire [2015], consent must be patient-centred and documented. Material risks must be disclosed in patient-understandable terms. Alternatives must be offered. Dedicated consent clinics for complex procedures (spinal, obstetric, oncological) are best practice. Generic consent forms are no longer adequate for high-risk procedures. VER

Underwriting credit: −8 to −15% for documented post-Montgomery consent process
Prevent

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) — Mandatory

Mandatory serious incident reporting and RCA for all Never Events, patient deaths and serious harms (HSIB/PSIRF framework from October 2023). RCA must identify contributing factors, systemic failures and learning. Actions must be tracked to completion. External RCA review for complex cases. Absence of RCA culture is a significant loading factor. VER

Underwriting credit: −5 to −10% for evidenced RCA programme
Prevent

WHO Surgical Safety Checklist

The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist (three-phase: sign in, time out, sign out) has been shown to reduce post-operative complications by 36% and deaths by 47% in controlled studies. Mandatory in NHS surgical settings. Private hospitals and overseas facilities should demonstrate compliance as a standard underwriting requirement. VER

Underwriting credit: −5% for verified checklist implementation with audit trail
Prevent

Peer Review & Appraisal

Annual medical appraisal (GMC requirement for revalidation), regular peer review of practice by a senior colleague, and participation in specialty-specific audit (e.g. National Surgical Register for surgeons, MBRRACE-UK for maternity). For consultants in private practice, hospital-arranged appraisal or MDO-facilitated peer review is necessary. VER

Underwriting credit: −5 to −8% for completed annual appraisal and revalidation
Prevent

Telemedicine Safety Protocols

Post-COVID telemedicine requires specific clinical protocols: safe patient identification, technology failure backup plan, criteria for safe remote versus in-person assessment, documentation of remote consultation limitations, and clear referral thresholds for remote presentations requiring physical examination. Absence of telemedicine protocols is a material exposure for GM and GQ risks. VER

Underwriting information requirement: % of consultations conducted remotely
Detection Controls (DETECT)
Detect

Incident Reporting System (Datix / PSIRS)

NHS Patient Safety Incident Report System (PSIRS, replacing National Reporting and Learning System from 2024) and Datix clinical incident management system. High near-miss reporting rates (relative to patient volume) are a positive safety culture indicator — organisations that report more incidents detect and correct errors earlier. Under-reporting is a red flag. EST

Underwriting indicator: near-miss reports per 1,000 patient episodes (benchmarked)
Detect

Patient Complaints First-Response

Formal patient complaints are early warning signals for potential clinical negligence claims. NHS Duty of Candour (Health & Social Care Act 2008 / Regulation 20) requires hospitals to notify patients of unexpected harm and apologise. A complaints management system with defined response timelines (and evidence that complaints lead to improvement actions) reduces claim likelihood. VER

Underwriting indicator: complaint resolution rate and escalation to PHSO
Detect

Coroner Liaison Protocol

Timely reporting of patient deaths to HM Coroner where required (Rule 28 / Coroners and Justice Act 2009), proactive engagement with Prevention of Future Deaths reports (Regulation 28), and legal team engagement at inquest. Prevention of Future Deaths reports that result in systemic change reduce future liability. Late or inadequate coroner liaison is an aggravating factor in claims. EST

Underwriting information: any open Regulation 28 Prevention of Future Deaths reports
Detect

Early Notification to Insurer / MDO

Early notification on adverse clinical events — before a formal claim is made — allows insurers to deploy risk management support, facilitate early resolution (where appropriate) and manage potential claims more cost-effectively. Late notification (notifying only when a letter before action arrives) is a policy condition breach risk and worsens claims economics. EST

Underwriting requirement: early notification protocol evidenced as part of risk management

Informed Consent Checklist — Post-Montgomery v Lanarkshire [2015] UKSC 11 VER

  • Risks explained in patient-understandable terms — avoid medical jargon; use plain language; check patient understanding by asking them to explain risks back (teach-back method)
  • Material risks individually identified and disclosed — both common (even if minor) and uncommon (if serious) risks relevant to this specific patient, not generic risks only
  • Alternative treatment options discussed — including non-operative management, watchful waiting, and alternative specialist referral pathways
  • Adequate time allowed for consideration — not immediately before surgery; allow reflection period especially for elective procedures; avoid coercive urgency unless clinically justified
  • Consent documented in patient-specific terms — dated, signed, with the specific risks discussed noted on the consent form (not just generic form text)
  • Witnessed consent for high-risk / irreversible procedures — third party witness for major surgical procedures; independent witness for procedures with capacity concerns
  • Consent revisited if material new information arises — significant interval between consent and procedure, or new clinical information, requires re-consent
  • Interpreter / translation provided if needed — patient's first language; professional interpreter (not family member); interpretation documented
Never Events — Automatic Regulatory Report & Maximum Underwriting Concern: The NHS Never Events list (NHS England / NRLS) defines a set of serious, largely preventable patient safety events that should never occur if established preventive measures are in place. Key Never Events in MedMal context include: wrong-site surgery (operating on wrong body part or wrong patient), retained foreign object post-surgery (surgical swab, instrument), wrong implant/prosthesis, wrong blood in wrong patient, overdose of methotrexate (regular vs daily dosing error), and intrathecal injection of wrong medication. Any Never Event triggers: (1) immediate duty of candour to patient; (2) NHS PSIRS mandatory report; (3) CQC notification; (4) HM Coroner referral if death; (5) immediate early notification to insurer/MDO. Never Events are also reportable to NHS Resolution under CNST and to Lloyd's syndicates under policy notification conditions. Never Events should be disclosed in underwriting submissions — their occurrence indicates significant systemic failure beyond the individual incident. VER VER
AI Diagnostics Liability — Emerging Risk Area: AI-powered diagnostic tools (radiology AI for chest X-ray analysis, pathology AI, sepsis prediction algorithms, AI-assisted ECG interpretation) are increasingly deployed in NHS and private healthcare settings. The regulatory framework is developing: MHRA classifies qualifying AI tools as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), requiring UKCA marking and post-market surveillance. Liability when an AI diagnostic tool contributes to a missed or delayed diagnosis is contested: is the clinician negligent for relying on the AI output without independent review? Is the hospital (or NHS trust) liable for deploying an inadequately validated algorithm? Is the manufacturer liable under the Consumer Protection Act 1987 / EU AI Act? Commercial MedMal underwriters are currently reviewing policy wordings to clarify AI diagnostics coverage — some are introducing exclusions pending framework clarity, others are retaining coverage with enhanced information requirements. Underwriters should ask whether insured practices deploy AI diagnostic tools, what validation has been performed, and what clinical oversight exists for AI-generated recommendations. VER
