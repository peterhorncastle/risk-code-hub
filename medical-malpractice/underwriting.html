Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Medical Speciality & Procedure Mix

The single most important rating factor. Obstetrics carries the highest liability exposure due to catastrophic cerebral palsy claims (up to £20–30m). Neurosurgery, spinal surgery and cardiac surgery follow. GP/primary care is lower frequency but post-Montgomery consent claims are rising. Aesthetics carries high frequency. EST

Speciality multiplier range: 0.8× (aesthetics) to 6.0× (obstetrics)
High Impact

Jurisdiction — UK vs USA vs Other

The most dramatic rating differentiator internationally. US MedMal is 10–15× UK rate equivalent: jury trials, contingency fees, punitive damages and higher life/care cost awards make US obstetrics and surgery risks in an entirely different severity class. Middle East private hospitals typically 2–4× UK. Australian rates are 3–5× UK for equivalent specialities. EST

USA multiplier: 10–15× UK equivalent; AUS: 3–5×; ME: 2–4×
High Impact

Claims History (3–5 Years, Severity-Weighted)

Prior clinical negligence claims — particularly large obstetric or surgical claims — are the strongest single predictor of future loss. Frequency must be interpreted relative to case volume (claims per 1,000 patient contacts). A pattern of consent-related claims post-Montgomery indicates systemic consent practice failure requiring loading. VER

Loading: +40% to +150% for adverse prior loss pattern; −10% for 5-year clean
High Impact

Bed Count / Procedure Volume / Patient Throughput

Hospital risk scales with volume: bed count and annual procedure volume (particularly surgical episodes and deliveries) drive exposure. For GP/primary care, list size and annual consultation volume are the proxies. For nursing homes, registered bed count and resident acuity level are key. Volume-based rating is the standard approach for institutional risks. EST

Base rate per bed (hospital): £300–£2,500 depending on speciality mix and jurisdiction
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Retroactive Date (Claims-Made)

The retroactive date on a claims-made policy defines the earliest date of alleged negligence that is covered. A long retroactive date (e.g. unlimited / full prior acts) substantially increases insurer exposure for long-latency specialities like obstetrics. Underwriters typically charge an additional premium for retroactive coverage beyond 5 years. EST

Unlimited retroactive vs 3-year: approximately +40 to +80% additional premium for obstetrics
Medium Impact

Extended Reporting Period (Tail Cover)

On retirement or career break, practitioners require tail cover — an ERP allowing claims arising from past treatments to be notified post-policy expiry. Tail pricing reflects the speciality-specific latency distribution of claims. Obstetric ERPs may run 10–15 years given cerebral palsy claim latency. ERP is typically priced at 150–200% of the final year's premium. EST

ERP pricing: 100–250% of annual premium depending on speciality and term
Medium Impact

Team Qualifications & Appraisal Status

GMC registration status, revalidation compliance, annual appraisal completion, and CPD record are all positive risk indicators. Practitioners with lapsed revalidation, regulatory conditions or prior fitness-to-practise proceedings attract significant loading. For nursing homes, CQC rating is the primary quality proxy. VER

CQC 'Inadequate' rating: +50% to +100% loading vs 'Good'/'Outstanding'
Medium Impact

Incident Reporting Culture & Governance

Participation in NHS patient safety incident reporting (PSIRS), Datix or equivalent clinical incident management system, and evidence of root cause analysis (RCA) following adverse events indicate a positive safety culture. Organisations with high near-miss reporting relative to size generally have better loss experience than those that under-report. EST

Best-practice governance credit: −10 to −20% on base premium
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Peer Review Participation

Participation in collegiate peer review, morbidity and mortality (M&M) meetings and clinical audit cycles is a positive quality indicator. Surgeons and physicians who participate actively in peer review demonstrate clinical governance engagement. Absence of peer review in smaller private practices is a mild adverse factor. VER

Lower Impact

Consent Documentation Standards

Post-Montgomery [2015], robust written consent documentation covering material risks, alternatives, and patient understanding is a clinical and legal imperative. Absence of documented consent, or use of pre-printed generic consent forms without patient-specific risk discussion, increases consent-based claims exposure materially. VER

Lower Impact

Litigation Management Quality

Prompt notification to insurer on adverse event, engagement with MDO/solicitors early, and a proactive early resolution culture (supported by the NHS Duty of Candour requirement) reduce claim development. Organisations with formal early notification protocols and legal panel relationships have demonstrably better claims outcomes. VER

Lower Impact

Telemedicine Exposure (Post-COVID)

Post-pandemic telemedicine and remote consultation now represent a significant proportion of GP and outpatient encounters. Remote consultation limitations (inability to physically examine, technology failures, patient digital literacy) create a new clinical negligence exposure. Underwriters should capture telemedicine volume as a % of total consultations. EST

Premium Rating Formula
// Medical Malpractice GH/GM/GN/GO/GQ/GT — indicative rating formula
Base Premium = Bed Count (or Annual Procedures / List Size)
              × Base Rate per Unit (£/bed/year or £/procedure)

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Speciality Hazard Multiplier // 0.6× (physio) → 6.0× (obstetrics)
  × Jurisdiction Multiplier // 1.0× (UK) → 10–15× (USA)
  × Claims History Loading // −10% (5yr clean) → +150% (adverse pattern)
  × Retroactive Date Loading // unlimited retro: +40–80% for obstetrics
  × Limit of Indemnity Factor // ILF from base £1m limit
  × Governance / CQC Rating Credit // −20% (Outstanding) → +50% (Inadequate)
  × Deductible / SIR Credit // −5% to −30% depending on level
+ ERP Loading (if applicable) // 100–250% of annual premium

Warning — Retroactive Date and Tail Cover Gap: The most common coverage gap in MedMal is failure to arrange adequate tail cover (ERP) at career change or retirement, or failure to negotiate an adequate retroactive date at inception. A GP who has practised for 20 years and switches insurer will leave a substantial historic exposure gap if the new insurer's retroactive date does not cover the prior practice period. Underwriters and brokers must verify continuity of cover, particularly for obstetric consultants, and ensure ERP terms are agreed in advance of any practice change. VER
