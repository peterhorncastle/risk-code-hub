Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    VER Life Assurance Act 1774 (UK) — Insurable Interest

    14 Geo. 3, c. 48. The "Gambling Act" — prohibits the issue of life insurance policies where the policyholder has no insurable interest in the life of the insured. Requires the insured's name to appear on the policy. Remains the foundational statute for insurable interest in life assurance in England and Wales. Confirmed and supplemented by the Insurable Interest Act 2015 (Scotland). Life Assurance Act 1774 applies to individual life; group life insurable interest established by employment relationship (employees are insured lives; employer has insurable interest via business disruption).

  • [L2]
    VER Insurance Act 2015 (UK) — Duty of Fair Presentation

    Insurance Act 2015 (c. 4). Replaces the Marine Insurance Act 1906 duty of disclosure for commercial non-consumer insurance contracts. Introduces proportionate remedies for breach of duty of fair presentation (s.8–11): avoidance for deliberate/reckless non-disclosure; proportionate reduction of claim for innocent non-disclosure. Applies to group life and group IP contracts placed by employers as commercial clients. Individual consumer life insurance is regulated under FCA ICOBS (ICOBS 5A) — different regime.

  • [L3]
    VER ERISA 1974 — US Group Life & Health Plan Requirements

    Employee Retirement Income Security Act 1974, 29 U.S.C. §1001 et seq. Governs employer-sponsored group life and health benefit plans in the United States. Key provisions: fiduciary duty requirements (s.404); reporting and disclosure (summary plan descriptions, Form 5500); claims and appeals procedures (s.503); COBRA continuation coverage (group health); FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act 1993 — unpaid leave, separate statute) interface. ERISA preempts state insurance law for self-insured employer plans but not for fully insured group policies. Lloyd's TL must comply with ERISA requirements for any US group life/IP coverage through US licensed fronting arrangements.

  • [L4]
    VER ABI Concordat & Moratorium on Genetics & Insurance 2018 (UK)

    Association of British Insurers, Concordat and Moratorium on Genetics and Insurance, revised 2018, extended to November 2025. Voluntary industry moratorium: insurers agree not to require or use predictive genetic test results in underwriting life, critical illness or income protection insurance, with the exception of Huntington's disease test results for policies with a sum assured over £500,000. Applicable to all ABI member insurers and Lloyd's of London syndicates writing UK life business. The moratorium does not apply to diagnostic genetic tests or family history. Post-2025 framework under review — potential statutory replacement if voluntary moratorium lapses.

  • [L5]
    VER Pensions Act 2008 — Auto-Enrolment (Life Assurance Interface)

    Pensions Act 2008 (c. 30). Requires UK employers to automatically enrol eligible workers into a qualifying workplace pension scheme. Employers must contribute minimum 3% of qualifying earnings. Many group life schemes are provided within or alongside the auto-enrolment pension — employees enrolled in the pension are automatically covered under the group life scheme (death-in-service). Interaction: Pension Act 2008 auto-enrolment drives growth in workforce pension participation, increasing the number of lives covered under employer group life schemes. Also: IHTA 1984 s.11 — group life benefits written in discretionary trust are free of inheritance tax on the insured member's death (key tax advantage of employer group life).

  • [L6]
    VER Equality Act 2010 — Group Life & IP Non-Discrimination

    Equality Act 2010 (c. 15). Prohibits discrimination on the basis of protected characteristics (disability, age, sex, race, religion, pregnancy etc.) in the provision of goods and services including insurance. Relevance to group life and IP: (1) Age-based premium differences in group life are permissible if actuarially justified (age is a legitimate risk factor — EqA 2010, Schedule 3, para 20A). (2) Disability discrimination in group IP: an employer cannot exclude a disabled employee from group IP without objective justification. (3) EU Gender Directive (2012 Test-Achats ruling): sex-based pricing prohibited for new retail contracts — unisex rates required for individual life sold after December 2012.

  • [L7]
    VER FCA Consumer Duty 2023 — Fair Value in Protection Products

    FCA Policy Statement PS22/9, A new Consumer Duty, effective 31 July 2023 for new products and open books; 31 July 2024 for closed books. Requires manufacturers and distributors of retail financial products — including individual term life, critical illness and income protection — to act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers. Key outcome: fair value (benefits must be proportionate to price paid); consumer understanding; consumer support. FCA Consumer Duty does not directly apply to B2B group life/IP contracts sold to employers, but applies to any protection products sold to individual consumers. Implementation reviewed through PROD, ICOBS and ongoing FCA supervisory activity.

  • [L8]
    VER FCA ICOBS — Income Protection (PHI) Regulatory Framework

    FCA Insurance: Conduct of Business Sourcebook (ICOBS), Chapter 5A — Long-term care insurance and income protection insurance. Specific conduct requirements for IP/PHI sales including: demands and needs assessment; fair presentation of policy terms (especially definition of incapacity — "own occupation" vs "any occupation" distinction must be clearly explained at point of sale); claims handling standards. ICOBS 8 (claims handling): insurer must handle IP claims promptly and fairly; must not unreasonably reject a claim; treating the claimant with appropriate sensitivity especially for mental health or long-COVID claims.

  • [L9]
    VER EU Solvency II Directive 2009/138/EC — Life Insurance Reserving

    Directive 2009/138/EC on the taking-up and pursuit of the business of Insurance and Reinsurance (Solvency II). Applies to Lloyd's of London as a whole; Lloyd's TL syndicates writing life business must comply with Solvency II technical provisions for long-term insurance (Article 77: best estimate + risk margin). Long-term life insurance reserves are calculated on a market-consistent present value basis, discounted at risk-free rate plus credit adjustment. Mental health IP IBNR and long-COVID reserve uncertainty are significant Solvency II valuation challenges for TL books. Post-Brexit: UK implemented domestic equivalent (Solvency UK / Prudential Regulation Authority rules) from 2024 with some divergences from EU Solvency II.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    EST Swiss Re — UK Protection Gap Report 2023

    Swiss Re Institute, Mind the Gap: Addressing the UK Protection Shortfall (2023). Estimates the UK protection gap — the difference between the financial support required if a main earner dies or suffers long-term disability and the cover actually held — at £2.6 trillion (lump-sum shortfall, 2023 estimate). Report documents that only 10% of the UK working-age population holds income protection insurance. The report is the definitive reference for UK protection gap analysis and is widely cited by life insurers, advisers and policymakers. Available at swissre.com.

  • [I2]
    EST Swiss Re Sigma — Life & Health Insurance Market Trends 2023

    Swiss Re sigma No. 3/2023, World insurance: global resilience in a hard market. Provides global life and health insurance premium data, market trends and forecasts. Key findings for TL market: global life insurance premiums grew 5% in real terms in 2022; health insurance the fastest-growing life/health segment globally; COVID mortality shock has accelerated protection awareness and demand, particularly in Asia-Pacific. Swiss Re sigma is the authoritative global insurance market statistics source. Available at swissre.com.

  • [I3]
    VER ABI / CMAP — UK Protection Market Statistics 2023

    Association of British Insurers (ABI) and CMAP (Claims Management and Analysis Platform), UK Life and Health Insurance Statistics (2023). Key UK market data: individual protection GWP approximately £3.5bn (term life ~£1.5bn, CI ~£1.4bn, IP ~£0.6bn est.); group life GWP approximately £2.2bn; group IP approximately £0.9bn. ABI also publishes annual claims statistics — in 2023, UK life insurers paid approximately £3.8bn in protection claims (all product types). ABI data is the primary source for UK protection market sizing. Available at abi.org.uk.

  • [I4]
    VER CMI (Continuous Mortality Investigation) — Longevity & Mortality Tables 2023

    Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA), Continuous Mortality Investigation, CMI 2023 Working Paper. Annual mortality improvement model update. CMI 2023 shows: partial recovery of pre-COVID longevity improvement trend; post-COVID excess cardiovascular mortality persists; long-COVID morbidity impact remains uncertain. CMI tables are the standard mortality improvement basis for UK life insurance pricing and reserving. CMI S3 series (assured lives) used for term life/CI pricing; SAPS series (Self Administered Pension Scheme) for group life/IP. Available to IFoA members at actuaries.org.uk.

  • [I5]
    VER Grid (Group Risk Development) — UK Group Risk Market Statistics 2023

    Group Risk Development (Grid), Grid Employer Research: Group Risk Annual Statistics (2023). UK group risk market data: group life GWP approx. £2.2bn, covering approximately 9.9m employee lives; group IP GWP approx. £0.9bn, covering approximately 3.4m employee lives; group CI GWP approx. £0.12bn. Grid research confirms mental health is the leading cause of long-term group IP claims by duration (>50% of new claims). Grid annual report is the definitive UK group risk market source. Available at grouprisk.org.uk.

  • [I6]
    EST Unum / Zurich — Mental Health in the Workplace: Claims Data 2023

    Unum UK, Mental Health Claims Statistics (2023); Zurich UK, Group Risk Claims Report (2023). Both reports confirm: mental health (depression, anxiety, burnout, PTSD) has become the leading cause of new long-term income protection claims by duration, accounting for more than 50% of new IP claim entries in the group market in 2023. Average mental health IP claim duration: 3–4 years (extended from 2–3 years pre-COVID). Early intervention reduces claim duration by approximately 30%. EAP access at point of notification significantly improves return-to-work outcomes. Both reports available at unum.co.uk and zurich.co.uk.

  • [I7]
    EST MHP / Reassure — Impaired Life Underwriting Market Survey 2023

    Medical & Health Products (MHP) and Reassure Ltd, Impaired Life Underwriting Survey (2023). Annual survey of UK life office underwriting practice for non-standard lives. Key findings: EPM ratings for common impairments (diabetes, cardiac conditions, cancer survivors) remain broadly consistent across the market; Lloyd's specialist market consistently offers more competitive terms for complex impairments that mainstream offices decline; digital CMR access has reduced application-to-offer timescales by 40–60% for substandard lives. The definitive UK reference for impaired life underwriting practice.

  • [I8]
    VER COVID-19 Actuaries Response Group — Excess Mortality Analysis UK 2020–22

    COVID-19 Actuaries Response Group (C19ARG), UK Excess Mortality During the COVID-19 Pandemic (2022). Published by IFoA working group. Key findings: approximately 200,000 UK excess deaths in 2020–21; significant variation by age band, geography and socioeconomic group; excess cardiovascular mortality persisting into 2022; long-COVID morbidity creating ongoing IP claim pressure. Report widely used by TL underwriters for COVID impact assessment and reserve development. Available at actuaries.org.uk.

  • [I9]
    VER Lloyd's — TL Market Statistics Bulletin

    Lloyd's of London, Market Statistics Bulletin (annual). Provides GWP data by class of business at aggregate Lloyd's market level. TL (Temporary Life and Permanent Health) is a distinct risk code within Lloyd's reporting structure. Lloyd's TL market GWP estimated at £300–500m (estimate; not separately disclosed at sub-class level in public statistics). Key Lloyd's TL players: specialist life managing agents, international group benefit syndicate underwriters. Lloyd's TL market is primarily: (1) substandard individual lives declined by standard market; (2) very high sum assured individual life; (3) international group life/IP for multinational employers; (4) facultative reinsurance of life portfolio excess. Available at lloyds.com.

  • [I10]
    VER Equitable Life Working Party — Lessons from the Crisis (IFoA 2001)

    Institute of Actuaries, The Equitable Life Assurance Society: Report of the Equitable Life Working Party (2001). Comprehensive actuarial analysis of the Equitable Life crisis — management of guaranteed annuity rate (GAR) liabilities, bonus smoothing, regulatory failings. Key lessons for TL market: (1) long-term life insurance guarantee obligations require robust asset-liability matching; (2) with-profits discretionary participation is not a substitute for contractual guarantees; (3) actuarial independence and challenge of management assumptions is essential for life office solvency. The working party report remains the definitive actuarial analysis of the Equitable Life failure. Available via IFoA library (actuaries.org.uk).
