Underwriting Controls
Underwriting Control

Full Medical Disclosure at Inception — CMR Access

Full disclosure of medical history via declaratory application form, supplemented by Customer Medical Records (CMR) access with insured consent for higher sum assureds. CMR provides GP medical history electronically, replacing slow and incomplete GP report requests. Reduces non-disclosure risk — the primary source of disputed life claims. EST

Underwriting Control

Attending Physician Statement (APS)

APS obtained from the insured's own doctor for lives with significant medical history or above the full medical limit (typically £500k+). APS provides specialist clinical commentary on the impairment, prognosis and treatment compliance. Essential for accurate EPM rating of substandard lives and for avoiding adverse selection at high sum assureds. Cost borne by insurer.

Underwriting Control

Financial Underwriting — Insurable Interest & Income Multiples

For term life: total sum assured across all policies must not exceed 25–30× earnings (income multiple test). For IP/PHI: total benefit must not exceed 50–65% of pre-disability income (anti-over-insurance). For group life: employer must demonstrate insurable interest (employment relationship). Prevents moral hazard of over-insurance. VER

Underwriting Control

Non-Disclosure Investigation at Claim

All material non-disclosures at inception trigger claims investigation under Insurance Act 2015 proportionate remedy framework. Death claims within 12–18 months of inception receive enhanced scrutiny. Life insurers retain right to avoid or reduce claims on proportionate basis for deliberate or reckless non-disclosure. CMR records at inception date preserved as baseline. VER

Underwriting Control

Reinsurance Treaty Limits & Facultative Review

Lloyd's TL syndicates operate within reinsurance treaty automatic binding limits — typically £250k–£500k net retention per life. Lives above the automatic binding authority require facultative reinsurance placement. Treaty exclusions (war, aviation, certain impairments) must be respected; cedants review treaty scope annually. Catastrophe XL for group life required for concentrations of 500+ lives per location.

Underwriting Control

Group Scheme Financial Controls

For group life and IP: employer financial health review — insolvent employers cannot sustain premium payments, and redundancy schemes may produce adverse mortality/morbidity selection (healthier employees leave voluntarily; unhealthy employees remain). Annual census of insured employees required; mid-year additions and deletions must be notified within contractual period. EST

Claims Management Controls
Claims Management

Early Intervention & Rehabilitation — Key to IP Cost Reduction

For IP/PHI, the most significant claims cost driver is claim duration. Early intervention — contacting the claimant within the first weeks of absence (even during the deferred period) — significantly increases return-to-work likelihood. Access to physiotherapy, counselling and occupational health assessments during the deferred period prevents conditions escalating to long-term disability. Early intervention is the single most cost-effective IP risk management measure. EST

Claims Management

Independent Medical Examination (IME)

For disputed or complex IP claims (particularly mental health, chronic fatigue, long-COVID), an Independent Medical Examination by a specialist instructed by the insurer provides an objective clinical opinion on the claimant's functional capacity and prognosis. IME reports are key evidence in functional assessments and return-to-work planning. Legal representation management is essential when claimants have solicitor involvement.

Claims Management

Fraud Indicator Monitoring

Life and health fraud indicators include: claim shortly after policy inception with undisclosed pre-existing condition (non-disclosure fraud); multiple IP claims across different insurers simultaneously; GP shopping (attending multiple GPs to obtain overlapping sick notes); inconsistency between claimant's reported functional limitations and observed activity on social media. ABI Life Insurance Fraud Database (LIFD) provides shared intelligence.

Claims Management

Vocational Rehabilitation Programme

Structured vocational rehabilitation — occupational therapy, job modification assessment, retraining and supported return-to-work — is the most effective long-term IP cost management intervention. IP insurers may fund vocational rehabilitation as an investment to reduce ongoing claim duration. Return-to-work incentives (partial benefit payments during graduated return) are standard in Lloyd's group IP policies. VER

Mental Health IP Claims Management Checklist

8-Point Mental Health IP Claim Review Checklist

1

Appropriate Deferred Period Review: Confirm claimed incapacity commenced after the policy deferred period — not before (back-dated sick notes are a fraud indicator). Verify incapacity date with employer payroll and GP records.

2

EAP (Employee Assistance Programme) Workplace Programme: Confirm whether employer had an EAP in place at the time of claim. EAP involvement (counselling referral, occupational health assessment) prior to IP claim is evidence of good-faith employee support; absence may indicate workplace factors contributing to claim.

3

Therapist-Led Rehabilitation Pathway: Has the claimant been referred to a qualified therapist (IAPT pathway, CBT specialist, clinical psychologist)? A clear treatment pathway with progress milestones is essential. Absence of active treatment is a case management red flag.

4

Return-to-Work Graduated Plan: Has a phased return-to-work plan been agreed with the employer? Graduated return (e.g., 2 days per week, building to full time over 8–12 weeks) is the most effective mental health return-to-work strategy. IP partial payment during graduated return should be available under policy terms.

5

Treating Psychiatrist Engagement: For claims lasting more than 6 months, direct engagement with the treating psychiatrist (with claimant consent) provides clinical progress assessment. Psychiatric opinion on functional capacity and prognosis is essential for reserve setting and claim strategy.

6

Functional Assessment (not purely diagnostic): IP claim assessment should focus on functional capacity (what the claimant can and cannot do) rather than solely diagnostic label. A depression or anxiety diagnosis does not necessarily preclude all work activity. Functional occupational assessment by occupational therapist provides objective evidence.

7

Legal Representation Management: If the claimant engages solicitors, this signals a contentious claim. Claims handlers should ensure all communications are legally appropriate; claims managers and legal counsel should be briefed. Solicitor-represented claimants often pursue claims to maximum benefit period; active management and early settlement negotiation is preferable to protracted litigation.

8

Reserve Adequacy Review: Mental health IP claims are notoriously difficult to reserve accurately. Initial reserves often understate ultimate claim duration — particularly post-COVID long-COVID overlap. Reserves should be reviewed at 3-month intervals for the first two years; periodic stochastic reserve modelling recommended for large individual mental health claims exceeding £100,000 in estimated total benefit.

Genetic Testing Moratorium — ABI Concordat 2018 (extended to 2025): Underwriters MUST NOT require or use predictive genetic test results in assessing any application for life assurance, critical illness or income protection — with ONE exception: policies with sum assured or benefit equivalent over £500,000 may request disclosure of a confirmed Huntington's disease predictive test result only. This moratorium is legally voluntary but industry-wide. It is in force until November 2025. CMR review processes must ensure genetic test results held in GP records are not inadvertently used in underwriting decisions. Post-2025 framework is under government and ABI review — legislative replacement possible. Any change to the moratorium framework would significantly affect TL underwriting of high-value lives and impaired life books. VER
7 Top Risk Management Actions for Group Life & IP Purchasers (Employers)
ACTION 01
Implement EAP Programme

An Employee Assistance Programme with confidential counselling access reduces mental health claim frequency and demonstrates duty of care. Most group IP insurers will offer premium credit.

ACTION 02
Early Absence Notification

Notify the group IP insurer of absences likely to exceed the deferred period as early as possible — early intervention within the deferred period is the most effective claim duration reducer.

ACTION 03
Mental Health First Aid Training

Train line managers in mental health awareness — early identification and non-stigmatising response to mental health challenges is the primary prevention for long-term IP claims from mental health conditions.

ACTION 04
Maintain Accurate Employee Census

Keep the group life and IP insurer's employee schedule accurate — notify additions, deletions and salary changes within contractual timescales. Inaccurate census data leads to coverage gaps and claim disputes.

ACTION 05
Review Group Life Concentrations

If a significant proportion of insured employees work in the same building, review catastrophe XL cover adequacy. Post-9/11 and post-COVID, group life catastrophe concentration risk is a standard Lloyd's underwriting concern.

ACTION 06
Check International Compliance

For multinational groups: verify that statutory death-in-service and disability benefit requirements in each operating country are met — either through the Lloyd's international master scheme or local admitted policies.

ACTION 07
Review Trust Deed (Group Life)

Group life benefits must be written under a discretionary trust to benefit from inheritance tax exemption. Ensure the trust deed is up to date and trustees are properly constituted; a 10-year review is minimum recommended frequency. VER
