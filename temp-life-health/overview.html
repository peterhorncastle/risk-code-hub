Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

TL — Temporary Life & Permanent Health

The Lloyd's TL code encompasses temporary life assurance (term life), permanent health insurance (PHI / income protection), critical illness insurance, group life, and group income protection schemes. At Lloyd's, TL business is primarily specialist, substandard or non-standard risk — impaired lives, high-value lives, international and expatriate schemes, and complex group programmes that fall outside standard life insurer appetite. VER

Why Lloyd's for Life & Health?

Lloyd's provides capacity for risks the standard life market declines or rates heavily: lives with significant medical impairments (diabetes, cancer history, cardiac conditions), very high sum assured (£5m+), unusual occupations (military, offshore energy, expeditions), international/expatriate group schemes crossing multiple jurisdictions, and large group concentrations requiring catastrophe XL protection. VER

"Permanent" Health — What It Means

Permanent health insurance (PHI) does not mean the cover lasts for life. "Permanent" means the insurer cannot cancel or decline to renew the policy once issued, regardless of subsequent deterioration in the insured's health. The benefit pays monthly income if the policyholder cannot work due to illness or injury, typically until recovery or normal retirement age. This distinguishes PHI from accident & sickness (A&S) policies, which can be cancelled. VER

Coverage Taxonomy

Term Life Assurance

Pays a lump sum death benefit if the insured dies within a fixed term (e.g., 10, 20, or 25 years). Level term (fixed sum assured) or decreasing term (mortgage protection). At Lloyd's — typically impaired lives, very high value, or international. No investment element; pure protection. VER

Permanent Health / Income Protection

Monthly benefit paid if the insured is unable to work due to illness or injury, after an agreed deferred (waiting) period. Benefit continues until the insured recovers, reaches retirement age, or dies. "Own occupation" vs "any occupation" definition determines claim threshold. Key Lloyd's focus: large individual IP, long deferred periods, international schemes. VER

Critical Illness Insurance

Pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a specified serious illness — typically cancer, heart attack, stroke, with additional conditions listed in the policy schedule. Invented by cardiac surgeon Dr Marius Barnard (South Africa) in 1983; launched in UK in 1986. Often combined with term life assurance. VER

Group Life (Death-in-Service)

Employer-sponsored lump sum benefit paid to dependants of an employee who dies while employed. Typically a multiple of salary (2×–6× salary). Employers use group life as a tax-efficient employee benefit (premiums deductible; benefit paid free of inheritance tax via discretionary trust). Lloyd's underwrites large, international and complex group life schemes. VER

Group Income Protection

Employer-sponsored income protection scheme providing monthly benefit to employees unable to work through illness or injury. Benefit is paid to the employer (who continues to pay the absent employee) or directly to the employee. Lloyd's focus: large multi-country schemes, high-concentration occupational groups, mental health loading. VER

Substandard / Impaired Lives

Lives with elevated mortality or morbidity risk due to medical history — diabetes, previous cancer, cardiac conditions, HIV, obesity, mental health history. Standard insurers decline or rate prohibitively. Lloyd's specialist underwriters apply extra mortality per mille (EPM) or percentage rating to price the additional risk. A core Lloyd's TL competency. VER

UK Protection Gap — £2.6 Trillion: Swiss Re estimates the UK protection gap — the difference between the financial support families would need if the main earner died or became permanently disabled, and the cover they actually hold — at £2.6 trillion as of 2023. EST Only 10% of the working-age population holds income protection insurance. COVID-19's excess mortality shock (689,000 excess UK deaths 2020–21 est.) accelerated awareness of underinsurance. This represents both a systemic risk for the life/health market and a significant long-term market opportunity.
Regulatory Classification (UK): Life assurance (including term life and group life) is regulated under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 as long-term insurance business (Schedule 1, Part II). PHI/income protection is regulated as Class I long-term business. Lloyd's TL syndicates must comply with Solvency II reserving requirements for life business. VER FCA Consumer Duty (2023) applies to protection products sold to retail consumers; group life and IP sold to employers as employee benefits is subject to Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation duties.
