Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Age

The primary mortality and morbidity driver. Mortality rates approximately double every 8 years of age (Gompertz law). For term life, premium increases sharply from age 40 onward. For IP/PHI, both claim frequency and duration increase with age; benefit-to-retirement-age policies are priced substantially higher for older entrants. At Lloyd's: substandard lives at older ages require careful EPM assessment.[I4]

Age 25 to 45 male non-smoker, £500k term: approximately 5–6× premium increase
High Impact

Medical History — Impaired Lives

Existing or past medical conditions are the key differentiator for Lloyd's TL business. Standard market declines or rates prohibitively for conditions including: cancer (within 5 years), cardiac conditions, poorly controlled diabetes, HIV+, significant mental health history. Lloyd's specialist underwriters use EPM (extra mortality per mille) or percentage ratings to price the additional risk. CMR (Customer Medical Records) access is standard for lives above threshold sum assured.[I7]

Well-controlled Type 2 diabetes: +50–200% extra premium; previous cancer (5yr+): +100–300%
High Impact

Sum Assured / Benefit Level

Financial underwriting (insurable interest and income multiple checks) required at high sum assureds. For term life: income multiple typically capped at 25–30× earnings. For IP: benefit typically capped at 50–65% of pre-disability income. Very high sum assureds (£2m+ individual; £10m+ group) require additional medical evidence (full medical, ECG, blood chemistry) and reinsurance treaty approval at Lloyd's.

Non-medical limit: typically £350–500k (term life, under age 50); full medical above this
High Impact

Term / Benefit Period

Longer terms increase mortality exposure. For IP/PHI: "benefit to age 65" policies carry substantially higher risk than policies with a 2 or 5-year benefit period. Own-occupation IP to 65 is the most comprehensive (and expensive) benefit; any-occupation definition significantly reduces claim likelihood. Short deferred periods (4 weeks) add to premium versus longer deferred periods (52 weeks/104 weeks).[I3]

Own occupation to 65 vs 2-year any occupation: 4–7× premium differential typically
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Occupation

Occupation significantly affects IP/PHI pricing. Class 1 (professional/sedentary) has lowest morbidity; Class 4 (heavy manual, hazardous) is most expensive. For term life, hazardous occupations (offshore, military, mining, deep-sea diving) attract loadings or exclusions. Occupation determines both claim frequency (accident risk) and claim definition applicability (own vs any occupation).

IP Class 1 (professional) vs Class 4 (heavy manual): 3–5× premium multiple
Medium Impact

Smoker / Non-Smoker Status

Smoking is a major independent mortality and morbidity risk factor — associated with increased mortality from cardiovascular disease, COPD, and cancer. Standard market rates for smokers are 20–40% higher than non-smokers on term life, and proportionately more for CI (cancer prevalence significantly higher). Smoker definition: any tobacco use in preceding 12 months. Ex-smokers may qualify for non-smoker rates after 12 months abstinence (varies by insurer).[I2]

Smoker loading: typically +25–40% on term life premium vs equivalent non-smoker
Medium Impact

BMI / Weight

Obesity (BMI >30) is associated with increased mortality from cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes, and with higher IP claim rates (musculoskeletal, mobility, mental health). BMI 30–35: typically rated. BMI >40: significant loading or declination depending on other factors. At Lloyd's: morbid obesity (BMI >40) with comorbidities is a substandard life requiring EPM assessment.

BMI 30–35 loading: +25–75% term life; BMI 35–40: +100–200%; BMI 40+: case by case
Medium Impact

Mental Health History (IP)

Mental health history is the most significant IP-specific underwriting consideration. Single episode of depression, well-treated: typically excludable with specific exclusion endorsement or modest loading. Recurrent depression, bipolar disorder, personality disorders: potential decline or full mental health exclusion. Mental health claims are now the dominant IP claim cause by duration; underwriters must balance inclusivity with anti-selection risk.[I6]

Specific mental health exclusion or 20–100% loading depending on history severity
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Family History

Parental death from cardiovascular disease or cancer before age 60 increases the insured's mortality rating. Relevant particularly for CI (inherited cancer predisposition, familial hypercholesterolaemia). Family history of hereditary conditions (Huntington's, BRCA1/2 carriers who have tested) is subject to ABI genetic testing moratorium rules until 2025.

Lower Impact

Geographic Residence / Foreign Travel

Residence in high-mortality countries (Sub-Saharan Africa, certain Asian markets) attracts loadings on international schemes. Frequent travel to high-risk regions (war zones, tropical disease regions) requires assessment. Expatriate lives — away from home country — often have enhanced healthcare access but elevated accidental death and tropical disease risk.

Lower Impact

Hazardous Activities

Participation in high-risk sports or hobbies (mountaineering, SCUBA diving, motorsport, skydiving, private aviation) attracts exclusions or premium loadings on term life and CI. IP policies typically exclude disability arising from hazardous activities. At Lloyd's: specialist hazardous life cover written on named-activity basis with appropriate loadings.

Lower Impact

Sex

Historically females paid lower life insurance premiums (lower mortality) and higher IP premiums (higher morbidity). EU Gender Directive (Test-Achats ruling, 2012) prohibited sex-based pricing in new retail contracts. Group life and IP schemes are typically priced on unisex rates. Individual policies post-2012 use unisex tables; legacy policies may retain sex-based pricing where permitted.

Substandard Life Rating Methodology — Extra Mortality Per Mille (EPM)
EPM (Extra Mortality Per Mille): The premium for a substandard life is calculated by adding an EPM to the standard mortality rate. EPM represents the additional expected deaths per 1,000 lives per year attributable to the impairment. A life with an EPM of 5 is expected to experience 5 additional deaths per 1,000 per year above the standard table rate. EPM ratings are derived from actuarial studies of impaired life mortality experience — published by UK and US life office research organisations.[I7]
// TL — Substandard Life Rating: Two Main Approaches

// Approach 1: Flat Extra (EPM) — adds £X per £1,000 sum assured per year
Total Premium = Standard Premium + (EPM × Sum Assured ÷ 1,000)

// Approach 2: Percentage Rating — treats life as older than actual age
// e.g., +50% mortality rating on 45yr male treated as 52yr male actuarially
Rated Premium = Standard Premium × Percentage Rating Multiple

// For IP/PHI: percentage extra morbidity (PEM) applied to claim incidence rate
IP Premium = Standard IP Premium × (1 + PEM/100)

// Lloyd's: large substandard lives often require RI approval above treaty line

Genetic Testing Moratorium (ABI Concordat 2018, extended to 2025): Underwriters must NOT require disclosure of, or take into account, predictive genetic test results when underwriting life, critical illness or income protection insurance — with one exception: policies with a sum assured over £500,000 may require disclosure of a positive Huntington's disease predictive test result. This moratorium is currently in force until November 2025. Post-2025 framework is under government review. Underwriters must ensure their CMR access processes do not inadvertently capture genetic test results held on medical records.[L4]
