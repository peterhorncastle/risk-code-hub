International Group Life / IP Programme Architecture

4-Layer Structure — Multinational Group Life & Income Protection

LAYER 4
RI / CAT XL

Catastrophe XL Reinsurance

Catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance protecting against mass mortality events — terrorism, pandemic, natural catastrophe — affecting multiple employees simultaneously. Trigger: more than X lives in a single event. Essential for groups with large employee concentrations in single locations. Lloyd's life syndicates typically purchase CAT XL above their group life retention. Post-COVID, pandemic language in CAT XL treaties has been revised significantly.

LAYER 3
MASTER POLICY

Lloyd's International Master Group Life + IP Policy

Lloyd's syndicate issues the international master policy covering all employees globally — providing difference-in-conditions (DIC) and difference-in-limits (DIL) coverage above local admitted policies, and direct coverage where no local policy is required or where a local policy would not be admitted. The master policy is the primary instrument for expatriate (non-national) employees and for standardising benefit levels across jurisdictions. EST

LAYER 2
LOCAL ADMITTED

Local Admitted Life / Health Policies (Where Compulsory)

In jurisdictions requiring locally admitted insurance (Australia, Germany, USA for ERISA purposes, UAE for certain categories), local life/health policies issued by admitted insurers satisfy statutory requirements. Local policies are coordinated with the Lloyd's master policy through a network of local insurance partners (often under a pooling arrangement: IGP network, AXA XL, Prudential etc.). Local policies pay up to their benefit level; excess is covered by the master policy.

LAYER 1
RETENTION / SIR

Employer Self-Insurance Retention (SIR)

Large multinational employers may retain a deductible or self-insured retention (SIR) — typically covering the first X months of group IP benefit before the Lloyd's policy pays, or a per-life deductible on group life. SIR reduces premium and aligns employer incentives with employee wellbeing. The SIR employer typically funds ongoing salary for the retained period through payroll rather than insurance payment.

Death-in-Service & IP Statutory Requirements by Jurisdiction
JurisdictionDeath Benefit Statutory RequirementIP / Disability RequirementTax TreatmentLloyd's Relevance
UK No statutory minimum death-in-service. Voluntary; typically 2×–4× salary employer discretionary. Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) only (£109/week, 28 weeks). Private group IP entirely voluntary. Employer premiums: deductible business expense. Benefit: free of IHT if written in discretionary trust (IHTA 1984 s.11). Lloyd's TL writes UK group life/IP for large complex or high-concentration schemes; also excess over standard market capacity.
USA No federal minimum group life (unless ERISA plan). ERISA plans must comply with reporting, disclosure and fiduciary requirements. Typical employer-provided: 1×–2× salary. No federal private disability mandate (except federal employees). State SDI (CA, NY, NJ, HI, RI, WA). FMLA provides unpaid leave protection only. Employer premiums deductible. Employee premiums for >$50k group life: imputed income. Benefits: generally income tax-free to beneficiary (term life). Lloyd's TL writes US excess group life (above ERISA primary plans), high-value key person life, and international group life for US multinationals.
Germany Betriebliche Altersversorgung (bAV) occupational pension includes death benefit where collectively agreed. No universal minimum death-in-service. Statutory disability pension (Erwerbsminderungsrente) from statutory pension fund. Private group IP supplements state benefit above statutory minimum income. bAV employer contributions tax-deductible. Deferred salary contributions (Entgeltumwandlung) tax and social security advantaged up to 4% of contribution ceiling. Lloyd's TL provides international master policy cover for German-headquartered multinationals; local bAV carrier handles German admitted portion.
Australia Superannuation funds required to provide default life insurance to members (Superannuation Guarantee employer contribution 11% of salary). Default life: typically 1×–2× salary in industry super funds. Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) insurance mandatory within super fund. Income Protection (IP) available as optional super fund benefit or standalone. Super fund contributions: tax-deductible to employer. Life/IP premium within super: deductible from super fund taxed at 15% rate. Benefits: complex tax treatment on death vs TPD vs IP. Lloyd's TL writes group life/IP excess layers for large Australian employers above compulsory super fund default cover, and for expatriate employees not covered by Australian super.
UAE Gratuity (end-of-service payment) is the primary statutory benefit for termination/death — calculated as 21 days salary per year for first 5 years. DIFC and ADGM have separate frameworks; some employers replace gratuity with group life schemes. No statutory group income protection requirement. Some free zone employers (DIFC, ADGM) provide private group health including disability provisions. Takaful (Islamic insurance) products available for local national employees. Insurance proceeds: no income tax in UAE (no personal income tax). Employer premiums: deductible for corporate tax purposes (introduced 2023). Lloyd's TL writes UAE expatriate group life and IP for international employers. Takaful compliant structures available for Shariah-sensitive clients. DIFC/ADGM regulatory environment is more permissive for Lloyd's placement.
Singapore CPF (Central Provident Fund) includes Dependants' Protection Scheme (DPS) — automatic term life for CPF members, $70k SGD cover. Employers may provide additional group life above DPS. MediShield Life: compulsory health insurance for hospitalisation. CPF contributions include Medisave for medical expenses. Private group disability/IP supplements CPF provisions for professional and international employees. CPF employer contributions not deductible (treated as benefit-in-kind). Employer group life/IP premiums deductible. Benefits: death/disability benefits not subject to Singapore income tax. Lloyd's TL writes Singapore international group life/IP for multinational employers and high-value individual life for high-net-worth Singapore residents.
Case Study — UK Pharmaceutical Company: International Group Life & IP Restructure

Scenario: 2,000-Employee Global Pharma — 12 Countries, Lloyd's International Master Programme

ElementDetail
Employer ProfileUK-headquartered pharmaceutical company; 2,000 employees across UK (900), USA (400), Germany (250), Australia (150), UAE (100), Singapore (100), other (100)
Previous StructureUK group life/IP through UK insurer; US employees on local group life; other markets: patchwork of local providers with inconsistent benefit levels and no coordination
ProblemInconsistent benefit levels creating employee equity issues; claim disputes in UAE and Australia; US ERISA group life not coordinated with international scheme; no catastrophe XL on group life concentrations
Benefit DesignDeath-in-service: 4× salary (all employees, globally harmonised). Group IP: 2-year deferred period; own occupation; benefit to age 65; 75% of salary. Mental health coverage: included with EAP requirement.
Lloyd's Master PolicyLloyd's TL syndicate issues international master group life + IP policy. DIC/DIL coverage: responds where local policy does not exist or is insufficient. Excess of local policy limits for jurisdictions with admitted local policies.
Local Admitted PoliciesAustralia: super fund trustee life/TPD compliant policy issued locally. USA: ERISA-compliant group life (1× salary base; excess to 4× via Lloyd's master). Germany: bAV carrier for German nationals; Lloyd's master for expatriates. Singapore: DPS supplement via CPF-registered insurer; excess via Lloyd's master.
UAE StructureDIFC-regulated group life policy (Takaful option for local nationals); Lloyd's master provides excess and IP coverage (not available under local Takaful product).
Catastrophe XLGroup life catastrophe XL: triggers on death of 3+ employees in a single event; covers up to £20m aggregate. Lloyd's CAT XL reinsurance purchased by writing syndicate. Particularly relevant to UK head office (900 employees in one location).
Programme PremiumTotal programme GWP est. £1.2m p.a. (group life and IP combined). Split: 45% UK, 25% USA, 15% Germany, 15% other international.
OutcomeHarmonised benefit levels globally; ERISA compliance achieved; DIFC/super fund compliance resolved; CAT XL in place for HQ concentration; single point of contact for programme management; £180k annual premium saving vs previous patchwork structure.
Group Life Catastrophe Accumulation — Key Underwriting Risk: Group life policies covering employees in the same building or campus create dangerous accumulation risk — a single catastrophic event (terrorism, fire, structural collapse) could trigger multiple simultaneous death claims up to the aggregate group life limit. Lloyd's TL underwriters must: (1) identify concentrations above 50 lives per location; (2) ensure catastrophe XL is in place above the retention; (3) aggregate monitor across their TL portfolio for geographical concentration (e.g., multiple group life risks covering employees in Canary Wharf or the City of London). Post-9/11 and post-COVID pandemic, CAT XL treaty language for group life has been significantly tightened by reinsurers. VER
International Pooling Arrangements: Large multinational employers may participate in an international pooling network (IGP — International Group Program, AXA Global Benefits, MetLife Global Employee Benefits) that pools group life and health premiums across all countries into a single dividend structure. Pooling rewards good claims experience across the global portfolio with a dividend reinvestment or reduced premium in following years. Lloyd's TL syndicates participate in pooling arrangements through network partner relationships, underwriting the excess layers above local admitted policies within the pool. Pooling is most advantageous for employers with 1,000+ employees in 5+ countries with favourable claims experience. EST
