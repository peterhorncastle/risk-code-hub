Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Territory / Jurisdiction

The single most important factor. Determines the MTPL minimum limit floor, the legal environment for injury claims (tort vs no-fault), medical cost inflation, court award levels, and the strength of the fraud/litigation environment. High-severity jurisdictions (South Africa RAF, USA excess, certain LatAm markets) attract significant surcharges vs European/GCC markets. [L1]

Territory loading range: 0.7× (low-litigation GCC) → 3.5× (high-litigation LatAm)
High Impact

Fleet Size and Vehicle Type Mix

Fleet size determines frequency exposure; vehicle type mix drives severity. HGV fleets have higher per-incident third-party liability severity. Private car fleets have higher frequency. Construction plant and specialised vehicles require bespoke rating. EV proportion within a fleet increasingly rated as a severity uplift factor. [I9]

HGV fleet surcharge: +40–80% vs equivalent private car fleet
High Impact

Driver Profile — Age, Experience & Territory

Young and inexperienced drivers (under 25) attract significant loadings in most territories. Named driver vs any qualified driver policies have different frequency profiles. International drivers operating in unfamiliar jurisdictions face elevated accident risk. Fleet driver risk scores (from telematics or HR records) are increasingly used as a rating input. [I3]

Young driver loading: +30–60%; any driver vs named: +15–25%
High Impact

3–5 Year Loss Ratio Experience

Prior loss experience on the programme or fleet is the strongest predictor of future results. Binder/coverholder programmes require rolling loss ratio analysis — underwriters should look at paid + IBNR gross loss ratio, not just reported claims. Frequency trends (not just severity) are critical: rising frequency in a stable environment indicates deteriorating underlying risk, not just claims inflation. [I7]

Adverse LR loading: +25–100% for 3yr LR above 85%; credit: −10% for 3yr LR below 55%
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Vehicle Value and OEM Parts Inflation

Sum insured basis (agreed value vs market value) and the proportion of high-value or specialist vehicles affects severity. ADAS-equipped vehicles have disproportionately high repair costs for relatively minor impacts — sensors in bumpers, windscreens and mirrors require calibration after any replacement. OEM parts vs aftermarket parts policy affects average repair cost. [I2]

ADAS high-spec vehicle surcharge: +10–20% on repair severity estimates
Medium Impact

MGA / Coverholder Quality

The quality of the binding authority holder is the primary underwriting control for delegated motor business. Key factors: financial strength, years in market, claims handling infrastructure, IT systems (policy admin and MIS), regulatory standing, anti-fraud procedures, and track record with Lloyd's. Annual coverholder due diligence audits (per Lloyd's DA minimum standards) are mandatory. [I7]

New/unproven coverholder: +15–25% initial margin loading
Medium Impact

Retention / Franchise Structure

Fleet excess/deductible (the insured retains attritional losses) significantly affects the exposure profile for the reinsurer or the Lloyd's syndicate at the top of the programme. Sliding scale deductibles (linked to fleet loss ratio) create alignment of interest. Per-occurrence vs aggregate retentions have different risk transfer profiles. [I9]

£5,000 fleet deductible credit: −8 to −12%; aggregate deductible: −15 to −25%
Medium Impact

Telematics Data Quality

For UBI (usage-based insurance) programmes, the quality and completeness of telematics data is a significant rating input. High-quality telematics (real-time speed, harsh braking, fatigue monitoring, GPS tracking) allows risk-differentiated rating and post-accident investigation. Poor data quality (incomplete records, device tampering) is a negative signal for programme underwriting. [I3]

Verified telematics credit: −5 to −15% for demonstrably low-risk driving scores
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Anti-Theft Devices

Immobilisers, tracking devices (Tracker, LoJack), steering column locks, and GPS fleet monitoring all reduce the probability of vehicle theft. In high-theft territories (South Africa, parts of LatAm) these may be policy conditions rather than rating credits.

Lower Impact

Named vs Any Qualified Driver

Named driver policies allow actuarial selection by driver risk profile. Any qualified driver policies cannot exclude high-risk individuals — relevant particularly for hire car and short-term rental programmes. Named driver restriction typically achieves a 10–20% rate discount.

Lower Impact

Claims Handling Jurisdiction

Where claims are handled affects the legal costs, settlement speed and subrogation recovery prospects. In-territory claims handling (local adjusters, approved repairers) generally produces better outcomes than remote handling. Coverholder claims authority limits (FNOL, reserve, authority) must be reviewed in the binder agreement.

Lower Impact

Approved Repairer Network

Access to an approved repairer network provides cost control (agreed labour rates, parts discounts), quality control (repair standards), and subrogation opportunity (proper evidence preservation). Particularly important for ADAS-equipped vehicles requiring sensor calibration. Repairers without ADAS calibration equipment produce inadequate repairs.

Premium Rating Formula
// International Motor — Indicative rating formula (fleet binder programme)
Base Premium = Fleet Vehicle Count × Base Rate per Vehicle (by territory/class)

Adjusted Premium = Base Premium
  × Territory Loading Factor // 0.7× (GCC) → 3.5× (high-severity EM)
  × Vehicle Type Mix Factor // HGV weighting, EV proportion surcharge
  × Driver Profile Factor // young driver %, any-driver vs named
  × Experience Loading (3–5yr LR) // −10% (clean) → +100% (adverse)
  × Coverholder Quality Credit // −5% to −15% for top-tier coverholder
  × Telematics Credit // −5 to −15% for verified UBI programme
  × Deductible / Retention Credit // −8% to −25% depending on structure
  × NatCat Aggregate Loading // +10–30% for hail/flood-exposed territory

EV Battery Fire Exclusion Watch: Some international motor wordings use fire exclusion sub-clauses that may inadvertently exclude EV thermal runaway events — particularly wording referencing "spontaneous combustion" or "electrical fault" exclusions originating from older wordings. Underwriters placing EV fleet programmes should verify that thermal runaway battery fires are explicitly covered and that salvage handling protocols for lithium-ion batteries (which can reignite for up to 24 hours) are reflected in the policy conditions and the approved repairer network requirements.
