Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    LEGAL EU Motor Insurance Directive 2009/103/EC (Consolidated)

    Directive 2009/103/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 September 2009 relating to insurance against civil liability in respect of the use of motor vehicles and the enforcement of the obligation to insure against such liability. OJ L 263, 7.10.2009. Consolidates the five preceding EU Motor Insurance Directives (72/166/EEC, 84/5/EEC, 90/232/EEC, 2000/26/EC, 2005/14/EC) into a single instrument. The principal legislative basis for MTPL across all EU/EEA member states.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL EU Motor Insurance Directive Amendment 2021 — Increased Minimum Limits

    Directive (EU) 2021/2118 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 November 2021 amending Directive 2009/103/EC. Key changes: minimum MTPL limits raised to EUR 6.45m (BI per incident) and EUR 1.22m (PD per incident); explicit AV coverage obligation under compulsory MTPL; insolvency protection for victims. Member state implementation deadline: December 2023. Available at EUR-Lex: eur-lex.europa.eu

  • [L3]
    LEGAL UK Road Traffic Act 1988 — Compulsory Third-Party Insurance

    Road Traffic Act 1988 (c. 52), Part VI — Third-Party Liabilities. Section 143: requirement for users of motor vehicles on roads to be insured against third-party liabilities. Section 151: insurer's liability to pay judgment even where insurer has grounds for avoidance or cancellation. Section 152: exceptions. Supplemented by the Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) Regulations 2003 (SI 2003/37). The UK Automated and Electric Vehicles Act 2018 supplements the RTA for autonomous vehicle insurance liability.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL Green Card System — Council of Bureaux Regulations, Geneva 1953

    The international motor insurance Green Card system is governed by the Internal Regulations of the Council of Bureaux (CoB), adopted under the auspices of the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE). The system was established by UNECE Recommendation No. 5 (1949) and the CoB was constituted in 1953. The Consolidated Resolution on the Facilitation of Road Transport (Recommendation No. 5) remains the international legal basis. Council of Bureaux secretariat: cobx.org. 48 member bureaux as at 2024.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL UAE Federal Law No. 21 of 1995 — Motor Vehicle Insurance

    UAE Federal Law No. 21 of 1995 concerning the Compulsory Insurance against Civil Liability arising from Motor Vehicle Accidents. Establishes the mandatory third-party liability motor insurance requirement across all UAE emirates. Supplemented by the UAE Insurance Authority (now Financial Services Regulatory Authority — FSRA) regulations on minimum limits, policy conditions and approved insurer lists. Comprehensive motor cover remains voluntary and commercially competitive.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Australian CTP Legislation — State-by-State

    Australian Compulsory Third-Party (CTP) insurance operates under individual state legislation. Key statutes: Motor Accidents Injuries Act 2017 (NSW); Transport Accident Act 1986 (Victoria, TAC); Motor Accident Insurance Act 1994 (Queensland, MAIC); Motor Vehicle (Third Party Insurance) Act 1943 (WA); Motor Vehicles Act 1959 (SA). Each state sets its own premium rates, benefit entitlements and managed care arrangements. National Heavy Vehicle Regulator (NHVR) oversees HGV safety nationally.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL South Africa Road Accident Fund Act 56 of 1996

    Road Accident Fund Act 56 of 1996 (South Africa). Establishes the Road Accident Fund (RAF) as the compulsory no-fault bodily injury compensation scheme for road accident victims in South Africa. Funded by a fuel levy collected from all petroleum products. RAF provides compensation for death, bodily injury and medical costs arising from road accidents, regardless of fault. RAF Amendment Act 19 of 2005 modified benefit entitlements and set a ceiling on general damages. RAF's unfunded liability exceeds ZAR 200bn as at 2024.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL Vnuk v Zavarovalnica Triglav — C-162/13, ECJ (2014)

    Damijan Vnuk v Zavarovalnica Triglav d.d., Case C-162/13, EU:C:2014:2146 (ECJ, 4 September 2014). The European Court of Justice held that the concept of "use of vehicles" in EU Motor Directive 72/166/EEC extends to any use of a vehicle consistent with its function as a vehicle, including use on private land and non-public roads. This ruling potentially extends mandatory MTPL to agricultural tractors, forklift trucks and other off-road vehicles. Subsequent referrals (Rodrigues de Andrade C-514/16; Torreiro C-334/16) refined the Vnuk principles. Legislative clarification sought through Directive Amendment 2021.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL UK Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB) Agreements — Uninsured and Untraced Drivers

    Motor Insurers' Bureau, Uninsured Drivers Agreement 2017 and Untraced Drivers Agreement 2017. The MIB is the UK's guarantee fund body, established 1946 as a company of motor insurers, administering compensation for victims of uninsured and untraced (hit-and-run) drivers. All motor insurers transacting motor business in the UK must be MIB members and contribute to the MIB levy. Post-Brexit, the UK MIB operates under its own bilateral arrangements with EU national bureaux for cross-border incidents. mibi.ie (ROI equivalent).

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY Swiss Re — Global Motor Insurance Outlook 2024

    Swiss Re Institute, Global Motor Insurance: Navigating the EV Transition, Repair Cost Inflation and NatCat Accumulation (2024). Analyses the global motor insurance premium market (~$800bn GWP), key regional trends, EV impact on severity, ADAS frequency effects, and the emerging NatCat accumulation challenge in motor portfolios (hail, flood). Key reference for international motor market sizing and trend analysis. Available at swissre.com/institute.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY Thatcham Research — ADAS Impact on Motor Claims Frequency and Severity 2023

    Thatcham Research, Advanced Driver Assistance Systems: Insurance Implications — Frequency Reduction vs Severity Inflation (2023). Documents the dual effect of ADAS on motor insurance: 27% reduction in injury accidents for AEB-equipped vehicles; 18–35% increase in average repair cost for ADAS-equipped vehicles due to sensor replacement and recalibration requirements. Essential reading for international motor underwriters assessing fleet ADAS penetration. Available at thatcham.org.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY KPMG — Future of Motor Insurance Report 2024

    KPMG, The Future of Motor Insurance: Autonomous Vehicles, EV Transition and the Telematics Revolution (2024). Analyses the strategic challenges facing motor insurers globally: AV liability allocation, EV underwriting, UBI programme design, and the changing role of the MGA in international motor distribution. Includes survey data from 150 senior insurance executives across 20 countries. Available at kpmg.com/motorinsurance2024.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY LexisNexis Risk Solutions — International Motor Fraud Trends 2023

    LexisNexis Risk Solutions, International Motor Insurance Fraud: Detection, Analytics and the Role of Data Sharing (2023). Documents motor fraud trends across key international markets: South Africa (staged accidents, vehicle substitution), MENA (workshop inflation, phantom passengers), and Eastern Europe (cross-border organised fraud rings). Reviews analytics tools and data-sharing frameworks for motor fraud detection. Available at risk.lexisnexis.com.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY Insurance Europe — European Motor Insurance Statistics 2023

    Insurance Europe, European Motor Insurance Key Statistics 2023. Annual compendium of motor insurance premium volumes, claims ratios, vehicle parc data and market structure across 35 European markets. Key data: EU motor premium ~EUR 150bn; average combined ratio 96–102% (2021–23 claims inflation); MTPL as proportion of premium varies from 35% (UK) to 70% (some Eastern European markets). Available at insuranceeurope.eu.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY McKinsey — EV Insurance Implications for Carriers 2024

    McKinsey & Company, Charging Ahead: How Electric Vehicles Are Reshaping Motor Insurance Economics (2024). Analyses EV-specific insurance dynamics: battery fire thermal runaway severity, OEM data sharing barriers, repair cost inflation for high-voltage systems, and the loss of telematics capability through OEM app ecosystems replacing traditional black-box telematics. Includes battery fire cost modelling and insurance product design implications. Available at mckinsey.com/insurance.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY IUA — London Market International Motor Binder Statistics

    International Underwriting Association of London (IUA), London Motor Market: International Delegated Authority Business — Annual Statistical Return. Annual IUA survey of Lloyd's and company market international motor binder GWP, loss ratio trends, territory mix and coverholder count. Estimated total Lloyd's international motor GWP (MF/MG/MH/MI/MP combined): USD 1.5–2bn (est., not publicly disaggregated). Available to IUA members at iua.co.uk.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY RMIIA — South African Motor Insurance Outlook 2024

    Risk Management and Insurance Institute of Africa (RMIIA) / South African Insurance Association (SAIA), South African Short-Term Insurance Market: Motor Sector Outlook 2024. Documents South African motor market dynamics: theft frequency, Road Accident Fund structural issues, minibus taxi sector, hail and flood NatCat exposure, and the impact of load-shedding (power cuts) on vehicle theft and vandalism claims. Key reference for Lloyd's South Africa motor binder underwriters. Available at saia.co.za.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY Aon — Fleet Risk Management Benchmarking 2023

    Aon, Global Fleet Risk Management Benchmark Report 2023. Survey of 450 fleet risk managers across 28 countries covering: fleet safety policy maturity, telematics adoption rates, driver risk scoring methodologies, EV fleet transition challenges, and insurance programme structures. Key findings: fleets with telematics programmes achieve 18–25% lower combined ratios; 38% of global fleets include EV vehicles in 2023 (vs 8% in 2020). Available at aon.com/fleetrisk.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's Motor Market Bulletin

    Lloyd's of London, Market Bulletins relating to Motor Insurance (international classes). Relevant bulletins include: Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y5398 — minimum standards for motor delegated authorities (2022); Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y5502 — EV underwriting guidance for Lloyd's syndicates (2023); Lloyd's Performance Management Directorate (PMD) — motor class guidance notes for MF/MG/MH/MI/MP reporting. Available to Lloyd's market participants via the Lloyd's Market Bulletin portal (lloyds.com/bulletin).
