Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

MF — Motor Fleet International

International motor fleet programmes, predominantly written on a delegated authority basis through specialist MGAs. Covers multi-vehicle fleets operated by corporations, rental companies, logistics operators and NGOs outside the UK. Third-party liability, comprehensive, and fleet excess of loss structures are all common. [I7]

MG — Motor International General

Catch-all code for international motor business that does not fit neatly into other sub-classes. Includes specialist classes such as motor trade road risks, agricultural vehicles, special types, and miscellaneous international motor business written on a direct or facultative basis.

MH — Motor International Heavy

Heavy goods vehicles (HGV), truck fleets, articulated lorries, buses and coaches written on an international basis. High severity/moderate frequency profile. Cargo liability exposure on HGV adds complexity. CMR Convention governs international road haulage liability across Europe and Middle East. [L3]

MI — Motor International

Broad international personal lines motor programmes — typically binder business covering private car, light commercial vehicles and motorcycles in international markets. Lloyd's syndicates underwrite the risk through a coverholder in the local market (e.g. South Africa, Middle East, Australia).

MP — Motor International Private

Private car delegated authority programmes specifically — pure passenger vehicle books written via international MGAs. Includes telematics/UBI (usage-based insurance) programmes which are increasingly common in MENA, Latin America and South Africa. Performance monitoring of the coverholder is critical. [I3]

UK Motor is a Separate Hub: Lloyd's UK motor business (codes M2–M6) is covered in the separate Motor UK hub. These MF/MG/MH/MI/MP codes exclusively cover non-UK international business — i.e. risks where the vehicle is not registered and principally garaged in Great Britain. Post-Brexit, GB has its own International Motor Insurance Bureau arrangement distinct from the EU Green Card system. [L9]
Coverage Taxonomy

Third-Party Liability (MTPL)

Mandatory in virtually all jurisdictions worldwide. Covers bodily injury and property damage caused to third parties by the insured vehicle. Minimum limits vary enormously — from minimal GCC levels to the harmonised EU minimum (currently EUR 6.45m BI / EUR 1.22m PD per EU Directive 2021). [L1]

Accidental Damage (Comprehensive)

Covers physical damage to the insured's own vehicle from accident, collision, overturning or impact. The cornerstone of most comprehensive motor programmes. Sum insured basis (agreed value vs market value) is a critical underwriting decision — particularly relevant in high-inflation or depreciation-volatile markets. [I1]

Theft, Fire & Natural Perils

Theft and attempted theft of the vehicle; fire including spontaneous combustion; storm, flood, hail and earthquake damage. NatCat exposure in motor portfolios is significant — Australian hail events and North American flood total losses are major aggregate exposures. EV battery fires present a new variant of this peril. [I1]

Personal Accident (Driver & Passengers)

Fixed benefit or medical expenses cover for driver and passenger occupants injured in an accident. Mandatory in several jurisdictions (e.g. Singapore, Malaysia). Often an add-on to fleet programmes; some markets (South Africa RAF) run compulsory no-fault government schemes alongside the commercial motor market.

Legal Expenses

Defence costs and pursuit of uninsured driver claims. Valuable in litigious jurisdictions. In EU markets, motor legal expenses is a regulated class in its own right (Insurance Distribution Directive). Typically sublimited; covers lawyer fees, court costs and enforcement of judgements against uninsured third parties.

Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist

Covers the insured's own BI/PD loss where caused by an uninsured or untraced driver. Essential in markets with poor compulsory insurance compliance — South Africa, parts of LatAm, some APAC markets. In the UK, the Motor Insurers' Bureau administers the untraced and uninsured driver agreements. [L9]

Delegated Authority / MGA Model
The dominant distribution model for Lloyd's international motor is delegated authority (binder business). A Lloyd's syndicate grants a binding authority to a specialist MGA or coverholder, typically located in the territory of risk, authorising them to bind risks on behalf of the syndicate within agreed parameters (risk criteria, territory, vehicle type, sum insured limits, policy conditions). The coverholder manages distribution, policy issuance, premium collection and often first-line claims handling. The syndicate audits the coverholder quarterly against the binder terms, monitoring loss ratios, premium volume and aggregate exposure. Quality of coverholder due diligence (Lloyd's minimum standards for delegated authority — DAs) is the primary underwriting control. [I7]
EV Severity Emerging Risk: Electric vehicle batteries introduce a new claims dynamic: thermal runaway fires are dramatically harder to extinguish than conventional fires (requiring thousands of litres of water), repair costs for high-voltage battery systems can exceed the vehicle's market value, and OEM parts supply constraints cause extended off-road periods. Lloyd's international motor underwriters should explicitly assess the proportion of EVs within a fleet or programme book and model severity uplift accordingly. Telematics data from EV fleets can assist (regenerative braking, speed profile) but battery state-of-health data sharing from OEMs remains limited. [I6]
