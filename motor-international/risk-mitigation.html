PREVENT Controls (6)
PREVENT

Fleet Safety Policy — Speed Limiters & Dashcams

A documented fleet safety policy covering maximum speeds (speed limiters mandatory for HGVs in most jurisdictions), dashcam requirements for all vehicles, and consequence management for violations is the foundation of a well-managed fleet. Speed limiter compliance data from telematics is increasingly a rating input for Lloyd's international motor binders. VER

PREVENT

Driver Telematics Programme

Real-time telematics monitoring of harsh braking, rapid acceleration, speeding, cornering forces and time-of-day driving provides the data to identify and coach high-risk drivers before an incident occurs. UBI programmes in MENA and South Africa have demonstrated 15–25% frequency reductions among drivers who receive coaching based on telematics scores. EST

PREVENT

Young and High-Risk Driver Management

Young drivers (under 25) represent a disproportionate accident frequency risk in virtually every international market. Fleet policies should include a formal young driver programme: increased supervision during first 12 months, restricted vehicle types (no high-performance), mandatory telematics monitoring, and enhanced licence checks. Insurers may require young driver restrictions as a policy condition for fleet discounts. VER

PREVENT

Vehicle Maintenance Schedule

Regular preventive maintenance (manufacturer service intervals, tyre inspections, brake assessments, lighting checks) reduces the probability of mechanical failure contributing to accidents. For HGV fleets, vehicle defect reporting and pre-use inspection records are legally required in most EU jurisdictions and form part of DVLA (UK) or equivalent operator licence conditions. VER

PREVENT

Journey Risk Management — Fatigue Prevention

Driver fatigue is a leading cause of serious and fatal road accidents, particularly in long-haul HGV operations. EU Drivers' Hours Regulations mandate rest periods; similar rules apply in Australia (NHVL) and across GCC countries. Fleet operators should implement journey planning tools, route risk assessment for high-risk roads, and fatigue monitoring via in-cab camera systems. EST

PREVENT

Pre-Trip Inspection Protocols

Formal, documented pre-trip vehicle inspections (WALKAROUND check: tyres, lights, mirrors, windscreen, fuel/fluids, load security) reduce the risk of mechanical failure contributing to accidents. Digital pre-trip inspection apps linked to the fleet management system provide an audit trail and trigger immediate maintenance requests for defects identified pre-journey. EST

DETECT Controls (4)
DETECT

Dashcam Footage Review Post-Incident

Front and rear dashcam footage is the most effective post-incident evidence tool available to fleet operators. It provides objective evidence of fault, speed, road conditions, and driver behaviour in the moments before impact — transforming disputed liability cases and significantly improving fraud detection. Lloyd's international motor underwriters should require dashcam installation for all fleet risks above a minimum threshold. VER

DETECT

GPS Tracking — Vehicle Theft Recovery

GPS tracking devices (Tracker, CalAmp, Continental VDO) enable real-time vehicle location and geofencing alerts. Stolen vehicles with active tracking are recovered significantly faster and with lower repair costs. In high-theft territories (South Africa, parts of LatAm), GPS tracking may be a policy condition and insurers may offer rate credits of 5–15% for verified active tracking. EST

DETECT

Fraud Indicator Monitoring — SIU Referral Triggers

Systematic motor insurance fraud (staged accidents, whiplash farming, vehicle substitution, phantom passengers) requires proactive detection protocols. Key referral triggers to SIU (Special Investigations Unit): multiple claims from the same vehicle/driver within 12 months; claims reported through the same solicitor/claims farmer; incident time and location inconsistent with telematics data; damage inconsistent with reported mechanism. EST

DETECT

FNOL Process — First Notice of Loss Quality

High-quality FNOL (First Notice of Loss) capture within 24 hours of an incident is critical: third-party details, police report number, dashcam footage preservation, witness statements, and medical referral for any injury. Poor FNOL quality leads to evidence degradation, increased liability exposure, and fraudulent claim inflation. Coverholders should be assessed on FNOL process quality as part of annual audit. EST

Fleet Manager Risk Control Checklist

DVLA Licence Check Programme: Implement systematic driver licence validation at least annually (monthly for high-mileage or commercial drivers). Use electronic DVLA Sharepoint access or equivalent international licence bureau. Undetected disqualified drivers void commercial motor cover in most jurisdictions.

Driver Risk Score Monitoring: Use telematics-derived driver risk scores to identify high-risk individuals (top 10% by score) for coaching intervention. Target a 20% reduction in harsh events within 3 months of first coaching session. Re-score monthly. Remove persistently high-risk drivers from high-value or HGV vehicles.

High-Mileage Driver Fatigue Management: Identify drivers accumulating above 35,000 miles/year (or equivalent in km). Require quarterly medical declarations for fatigue indicators. Apply EU Drivers' Hours or equivalent rest period rules even where not legally mandated for the vehicle class.

Vehicle Defect Reporting: Implement a digital defect reporting system accessible to all drivers via mobile app. Require vehicle off-road status for safety-critical defects (brake warning, tyre pressure warning, EV battery fault). Track defect-to-repair time KPI — target under 48 hours for safety-critical items.

Accident Reporting Within 24 Hours: Mandatory 24-hour FNOL to the insurer/coverholder for all incidents, regardless of apparent severity. Even apparently minor incidents can develop into substantial BI claims months later. Late FNOL is a leading cause of increased claims costs and coverage disputes.

Approved Repairer Network: Require all vehicle repairs to be authorised through the insurer's approved repairer network. Non-approved repairs risk inadequate ADAS sensor recalibration, which can create product liability exposure for the fleet operator and void manufacturer warranty on safety systems.

TNC / Gig Driver Endorsement Check: For fleets that include ride-sharing or courier drivers (Uber Eats, Deliveroo, DHL Flex), verify that the commercial motor policy includes an explicit TNC endorsement covering the "Period 1" gap (app open, no passenger). Standard fleet policies may exclude commercial use by gig economy drivers.

EV Charging Safety Protocols: For EV fleets, implement depot charging safety procedures: visual inspection of charging cables before each use, monthly thermal imaging of charging bay electrical panels, segregation of damaged/suspect EV batteries from charging points, and emergency response plan for battery fire including foam and immersion cooling capability pre-positioned at main depots.

EV-Specific Risk Management

Electric vehicles introduce a distinct risk profile that requires specific mitigation measures beyond standard fleet practices. The Lloyd's international motor market is actively reviewing EV-specific policy wordings and risk criteria as the EV proportion of insured fleets rises.

Battery Fire Risk

Lithium-ion thermal runaway fires burn at 2,000°C+ and cannot be extinguished with conventional foam. They require immersion in water (30,000–80,000L) or specialist containment systems. Pre-position thermal imaging cameras in EV charging depots and establish emergency services communication protocols.

Range Anxiety Accidents

Drivers distracted by low battery range or deviating from routes to find charging points face elevated accident risk. Fleet management systems should provide in-vehicle real-time range-to-charge alerts and routing to approved charge points to reduce driver distraction and decision stress.

Charging Station Incidents

Third-party liability exposure at public charging stations (cables as trip hazards, connector damage, electrical surge incidents) is a growing exposure not covered by all standard motor policies. Verify that public charging liability is included in fleet MTPL and confirm electrical infrastructure liability coverage at owned/operated depot charge points.

OEM Data Sharing for Claims

EV manufacturers hold critical post-accident battery telemetry data (state of charge, temperature, pre-accident energy flows) that can determine fault in accidents and confirm or exclude battery defect as a contributing factor. Fleet operators should ensure their vehicle purchase/lease agreements include data access rights for insurers and accident investigators.

7 Top Risk Actions for International Motor Insureds

1

Deploy telematics on 100% of fleet vehicles and use driving score data actively for driver coaching — not just for underwriting evidence.

2

Implement mandatory dashcam recording (front and rear) for all vehicles — essential for fraud defence and disputed liability resolution.

3

Run quarterly driver licence validation for all fleet drivers and remove any disqualified drivers from fleet vehicles immediately.

4

Establish a 24-hour FNOL reporting culture — communicating to all drivers that immediate incident reporting protects both the driver and the business.

5

For EV fleets: invest in depot charging infrastructure safety (thermal imaging, segregation protocols, emergency response plan) before any thermal incident occurs.

6

Ensure all ADAS-equipped vehicles are repaired only by certified repairers with ADAS calibration capability — uncalibrated ADAS systems following repair are a liability waiting to happen.

7

Review fleet motor policy wording annually against the evolving risk landscape: TNC endorsement, EV battery fire coverage, ADAS sensor replacement cover, and territorial limit extensions for international operations. EST
