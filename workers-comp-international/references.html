Legal & Statutory Sources
  • [L1]
    VER Defence Base Act — 42 U.S.C. § 1651 et seq.

    The Defence Base Act (1941, as amended) extends Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (LHWCA) benefits to employees of US government contractors and subcontractors working overseas on military bases and public works contracts. Key provisions: §1651 (coverage extension), §1652 (carrier authorisation requirement), §1654 (WHCA coordination). Administered by US Department of Labor, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP). The foundational statute for the US international WC / W4 DBA market. Available at dol.gov/agencies/owcp/dlhwc/dba.

  • [L2]
    VER Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act — 33 U.S.C. § 901 et seq.

    The LHWCA (1927, as amended) provides federal WC for maritime and dock workers on navigable waters of the United States and adjoining areas. The DBA incorporates the LHWCA benefit schedule and claims administration procedures by reference — DBA benefits are calculated per LHWCA. Key provisions: §908 (compensation schedule), §910 (wage basis), §933 (third-party actions). The LHWCA benefit schedule's 1927 origins create tension with modern compensation expectations. Available at dol.gov/agencies/owcp/dlhwc.

  • [L3]
    VER War Hazards Compensation Act — 42 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq.

    The War Hazards Compensation Act (1942, as amended) provides that the US government reimburses DBA insurers and employers for compensation and medical costs paid to workers injured or killed as a result of a war hazard — including hostile fire, enemy action, and terrorism attributable to war. The WHCA backstop is what makes DBA commercially insurable in active war zones. Filing: DBA carrier must submit WHCA reimbursement claim to DOL/OWCP DFEC following war hazard payment. Reimbursement process: 12–36 months typically. Available at dol.gov/agencies/owcp/dlhwc/whca.

  • [L4]
    VER UK Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — Extraterritorial Scope

    The Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 (c. 57) requires UK employers to maintain EL insurance for employees "who are based in Great Britain." The Act's extraterritorial scope is limited — it applies to UK-based employees working temporarily outside Great Britain (e.g. on short assignments) but does not extend to employees permanently based overseas. UK employers with overseas-based employees rely on W4 for EL cover in those territories. Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) supervises EL insurance provision; minimum £5m limit per occurrence. Available at legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L5]
    VER UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 (Labour Law) — Worker Compensation Obligations

    UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 (Labour Law) and subsequent decrees establish occupational injury and disease compensation obligations for UAE employers. Replaced in part by UAE Labour Law 2021 (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021). Employers must maintain WC cover through UAE Insurance Authority-licensed insurers. Benefit schedule: end-of-service gratuity, medical treatment, death benefits. UAE requires locally admitted insurance — Lloyd's W4 placed through UAE-licensed fronter with LOC arrangements. Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE) supervises compliance.

  • [L6]
    VER Philippine POEA Standard Employment Contract — Compulsory Insurance Provisions

    Philippine Overseas Employment Administration (POEA) Standard Employment Contract mandates compulsory insurance for all Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs). Republic Act 10022 (2010 — Migrant Workers Act amendment) requires employers to maintain insurance providing: (a) accidental death benefit of PHP 200,000; (b) natural death benefit of PHP 100,000; (c) permanent total disablement benefit up to PHP 200,000; (d) repatriation costs; (e) subsistence allowance. Coverage is in addition to employer's W4 programme benefits. POEA issues insurance compliance certificate. Available at poea.gov.ph.

  • [L7]
    VER ILO Convention C102 — Social Security (Minimum Standards) 1952

    International Labour Organization Convention No. 102 (1952) establishes minimum standards for social security, including workers' compensation (Part VI) and employment injury benefit (Part VI). Ratified by 60 ILO member states. Establishes the principle that workers deployed abroad retain social security rights. The Convention's minimum benefit standards — covering medical care, temporary disability, permanent disability, and survivors' benefits — underpin the argument that TCN workers have rights to compensation regardless of the generosity of host-country statutory WC schemes. Available at ilo.org.

  • [L8]
    VER UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights 2011 — Supply Chain Duty of Care

    UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (Ruggie Principles, 2011 — UN Human Rights Council Resolution A/HRC/17/31). Principle 13: Business enterprises must avoid causing or contributing to adverse human rights impacts through their own activities, including operations. Principle 17: Human rights due diligence (HRDD) including impact assessment. W4 relevance: multinationals have a responsibility to ensure that TCN and local national workers in their supply chains receive adequate WC protection — not merely the legal minimum of the host state. Several jurisdictions (UK MHRCA, France Duty of Vigilance Law) are translating the Ruggie Principles into mandatory requirements. Available at ohchr.org.

  • [L9]
    VER US DOL / OWCP — DBA Filing Procedures; Form LS-202

    US Department of Labor, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP), Division of Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation. Form LS-202 (Employer's First Report of Injury or Occupational Illness): must be filed within 10 days of employer's knowledge of a reportable work-related injury or disease. Penalty for late filing: up to $37,000 per violation (Civil Monetary Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act). DBA district offices: Baltimore, New Orleans, San Francisco, Seattle, Jacksonville, Boston, New York. Available at dol.gov/agencies/owcp/dlhwc/dba; electronic filing via DOL's OWCP Data Interchange portal.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    VER US DOL / OWCP — DBA Program Statistics and Annual Reports 2023

    US Department of Labor, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs, Division of Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation, Annual Report to Congress (published annually). Contains DBA-specific data: number of cases, compensation paid, medical costs, WHCA reimbursements, DBA carrier authorisations. Contractor deaths and injuries in Iraq and Afghanistan documented in OWCP special reports 2003–2021. Available at dol.gov/agencies/owcp/dlhwc/statistics. The authoritative primary source for DBA claims volume and cost data.

  • [I2]
    EST Control Risks — Global Risk Map 2024; Country Risk Ratings

    Control Risks Group, RiskMap 2024. Annual country-by-country security risk assessment used by multinational employers, insurers and Lloyd's underwriters as a primary reference for W4 territory zone classification. Rates countries on political risk, security risk, operational risk and travel risk dimensions. Provides the framework against which W4 zone A–E classifications are calibrated. Available at controlrisks.com. Supplementary reference: Healix International country risk ratings (healix.com) used specifically in international assistance and medevac planning.

  • [I3]
    EST Healix International — Expat Health and Security Incident Data 2023

    Healix International, Global Health and Security Risk Report 2023. Covers medical evacuation statistics, average medevac costs by region, security incident data for expat workers, and health risk ratings by country. Average medical repatriation cost data referenced in this hub ($85,000+ per event) derived from Healix published benchmarks. Healix manages international medical assistance and security response for over 10 million global travellers. Available at healix.com.

  • [I4]
    EST International SOS (ISOS) — Duty of Care Report 2024

    International SOS, Duty of Care Report 2024: The Rising Cost of People Risk. Annual benchmark survey of multinational employer duty of care practices, international travel risk management, medical assistance costs, and wellness programme effectiveness. Includes data on PTSD and mental health trends in high-risk assignments, COVID repatriation costs, and AI-enabled risk monitoring. ISOS serves over 75% of Fortune 500 companies' international travel assistance needs. Available at internationalsos.com. ISOS Assistance App and 24/7 medical advice line referenced in W4 pre-deployment checklist.

  • [I5]
    VER Lloyd's — W4 / DBA Market Bulletin and Capacity Guide

    Lloyd's of London market bulletins and Realistic Disaster Scenario (RDS) framework guidance relating to international WC, DBA, and hostile zone accumulation risk. Lloyd's Accident and Health / Casualty divisions provide annual W4 market guidance to managing agents. DBA concentration in Iraq and Afghanistan has been modelled as an RDS event. Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) Casualty Working Group publications on international WC and DBA coverage issues. Note: W4 GWP at Lloyd's is not publicly disclosed at sub-class level; $400–600m DBA segment estimate is a market estimate (EST). Available at lloyds.com/market-resources.

  • [I6]
    EST AON — Global Employer Survey: Expatriate Benefits and Risk 2023

    Aon plc, 2023 Global Employer Survey: International Mobility, Expatriate Benefits and Risk Management. Survey of 1,200+ multinational employers covering: expat benefit design, WC/EL programme structure, duty of care practices, assignment risk ratings, and international insurance spend. Key finding: 34% of multinationals have experienced a W4 claim in the past 3 years; medevac is the most commonly triggered benefit. Available at aon.com. Relevant benchmark data for W4 underwriting: average expat assignment cost, typical employee benefit structure, and regional headcount trends.

  • [I7]
    EST Mercer — International Mobility and Benefits Benchmarking 2023

    Mercer, 2023 Worldwide Benefit and Employment Guidelines. Comprehensive reference for international employee benefit design across 150+ countries — covering statutory WC/EL benefit requirements, typical employer-provided supplemental benefits, and compensation structures for expat, TCN and local national employees. Used by W4 underwriters to assess benefit adequacy and identify statutory compliance gaps in multinational programmes. Available at imercer.com. Also relevant: Mercer's Expatriate Management Survey covering assignment costs and mobility trends.

  • [I8]
    EST ICRC — Humanitarian Worker Safety and Security Data 2023

    International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), Aid Worker Security Report 2023 (compiled with Humanitarian Outcomes). Documents attacks on humanitarian aid workers globally: number of killed, wounded and kidnapped by organisation, country and incident type. Key 2023 data: 116 aid workers killed, 98 wounded, 87 kidnapped globally. Gaza, Sudan, DRC, Somalia and Myanmar are highest-risk operating environments. Used by W4 underwriters as primary reference for NGO/humanitarian sector Zone D/E loss frequency data. Available at icrc.org and humanitarianoutcomes.org.

  • [I9]
    VER CNBC / Reuters — Ukraine Conflict Contractor Withdrawal Reporting 2022

    Reuters and CNBC reporting on multinational corporate withdrawals from Ukraine following the February 2022 Russian invasion. Key coverage: contractor workforce evacuation timelines, injury and death reports for non-Ukrainian contractor personnel, and insurance implications. Supplemented by: UK Department for Business and Trade briefings on UK companies withdrawing from Ukraine; EU sanctions and force majeure guidance for Ukrainian contract performance. The Ukraine conflict is the most significant European W4 market event since the 1990s Balkans conflicts. Available at reuters.com; cnbc.com; ukraine-economy.gov.ua.

  • [I10]
    VER OECD — Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises: Worker Protection Chapter

    Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD), Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct (2023 update). Chapter V: Employment and Industrial Relations — establishes that MNEs should provide working conditions and safety standards that comply with host-country law but should also meet ILO minimum standards where host-country law falls short. Chapter VI: Environmental — applies to TCN workers' conditions in the supply chain. The OECD Guidelines are the primary international soft-law framework governing multinational employer obligations to their global workforces, including WC/EL adequacy. Available at mneguidelines.oecd.org. National Contact Points in 50+ countries handle complaints under the Guidelines.
