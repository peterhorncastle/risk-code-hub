Lloyd's Risk Code — W4

W4 — Workers Compensation / EL International

W4 covers workers compensation and employers liability for multinational companies' employees working outside their home country. The class encompasses statutory WC requirements in host countries, common-law employers liability suits, expatriate personal accident and medical cover, and the Defence Base Act (DBA) — the US statutory WC scheme for American government contractors working overseas. Programme structuring across 50+ jurisdictions is the defining complexity. VER

W4 vs W3 vs W5/W6 — Key Distinctions

W3 (UK EL) covers employers liability for employees based in the UK under the Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. W5/W6 covers US workers compensation for domestic US employees under individual state schemes. W4 is the international and multinational class — it responds where employees are deployed outside their home jurisdiction and statutory WC requirements must be addressed across multiple countries simultaneously. DBA (US overseas contractor) is a W4 speciality. VER

Employee Classification — Critical for W4

Three key employee categories each carry different W4 exposures: Expatriates (Expats) — home-country nationals deployed overseas, typically highest-cost per head; Third-Country Nationals (TCNs) — workers from a third country deployed in a host country (e.g. Filipino workers in Iraq on a US contractor project); Local Nationals (LNs) — host-country citizens employed locally, often covered by mandatory local WC schemes. DBA applies regardless of nationality when working on a US government contract. VER

Coverage Taxonomy — 6 Key Covers

Statutory Workers Compensation

Coverage for host-country statutory WC obligations — benefit schedules, medical treatment and wage replacement as required by the law of the jurisdiction of employment. Complexity arises because benefit structures differ enormously by country and may require locally-admitted policies in some territories. VER

Employers Liability — Common Law

Coverage for civil suits brought by employees alleging negligence by the employer. Particularly important in jurisdictions that maintain common-law EL alongside statutory WC (UK, Australia, Ireland). Limits typically USD 5m–25m any one occurrence for international programmes. VER

Expatriate Personal Accident & Medical

PA and medical evacuation cover for expat employees — typically layered over statutory benefits. Covers emergency medical treatment, medical repatriation (average cost $85,000+ est.), accidental death and disability, and crisis response. Often structured through Healix, ISOS or similar specialist medevac providers. EST

Defence Base Act (DBA)

US statutory WC for US government contractors and subcontractors working overseas on US military bases, embassy projects, and USAID/USACE contracts. Mandatory under 42 U.S.C. § 1651; incorporates LHWCA benefit schedule. Must be placed with a DBA-authorised carrier. DOL/OWCP administers. VER

USL&H Extension — Maritime

US Longshore and Harbor Workers Compensation Act extension for maritime workers — stevedores, harbour workers, vessel repair personnel and offshore platform workers operating on navigable waters. Interfaces with DBA for overseas maritime operations (e.g. contract vessel crew on US Navy resupply). VER

War Risk / Hostile Environment Extension

Extension covering injury, disability and death arising in war zones and hostile environments — Iraq, Afghanistan, Libya, Ukraine, and other conflict-affected territories. War risk is a standard exclusion in most WC policies; the endorsement or War Hazards Compensation Act (WHCA) backstop restores cover for DBA-insured USG contractors. VER

International Compliance Challenge — Admitted Insurance Requirements: The admitted insurance requirement is the central compliance challenge in W4 programme structuring. Some countries (e.g. UAE, Saudi Arabia, Brazil) require WC/EL cover to be placed with a locally licensed and admitted insurer — Lloyd's direct paper is not accepted. In these jurisdictions, a fronting arrangement is required: a locally-admitted insurer issues the statutory policy and reinsures the risk (often 100%) to Lloyd's. Other countries accept a Lloyd's overseas policy. Conflict zones frequently have no viable local admitted market, making Lloyd's direct W4 paper the only practical option. Failure to maintain the required admitted cover is a criminal or civil offence in several jurisdictions. VER
