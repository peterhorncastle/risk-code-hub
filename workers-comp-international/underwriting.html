Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Territory of Operations

The single most important W4 rating factor. Zone A to Zone E classification drives rate multipliers of 1× to 12×+. Underwriters review Control Risks and Healix country risk ratings alongside Lloyd's own territory rating guidance. Mixed-territory programmes require payroll allocation by territory. [I2]

Rate multiplier: 1.0× (Zone A) → 12.0×+ (Zone E war zone)
High Impact

Employee Type — Expat / TCN / LN

Expatriates carry the highest per-head benefit costs (salary continuation, medical evacuation, home-country statutory obligations). Third-Country Nationals (TCNs) present complex multi-jurisdiction benefit calculations. Local nationals typically covered by host-country statutory WC. Employee mix fundamentally affects expected average cost per head. [L5]

Expat: 2–4× rate vs LN; TCN: 1.5–2.5× rate vs LN (wage differential + benefit complexity)
High Impact

Industry / Activity Class

Oil & gas and construction in high-risk territories carry the highest accident frequency and severity. NGO/humanitarian workers in conflict zones face kidnap and hostile-act risk. Military support contractors (DBA) face combat-adjacent casualties. Clerical/office expats in Zone A countries present minimal hazard. Activity matters as much as geography. [I5]

Office/clerical Zone A: 30–50% LR expected. Oil & gas Zone D/E: 80–150%+ LR expected.
High Impact

DBA Applicability

If the US government contract requires DBA coverage (confirmed by contract review), the DBA insuring agreement must be included and the carrier must be DOL-authorised for DBA. DBA benefit schedule (LHWCA-based, uncapped medical) is significantly more generous than most private WC schedules. Average DBA serious injury claim significantly exceeds equivalent non-DBA claim. [L1]

DBA loading: +40–100% vs equivalent non-DBA international WC programme
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Headcount and Payroll by Territory

Premium base: number of employees × territory rate × average wage (for wage replacement benefit calculation). Payroll declaration accuracy is critical — underestimation is the most common W4 presentation error. End-of-year payroll adjustment clauses are standard for fluctuating workforce sizes. [I6]

Premium = Headcount × Rate per head per territory (adjusted to payroll basis)
Medium Impact

War / Hostile Environment Sublimit

War risk extension limit setting is a key coverage negotiation. Full limits apply for DBA (backed by WHCA for war hazard deaths); for non-DBA W4 covers, war risk sublimits of $1m–$5m any one person are common, with aggregate event limits of $10m–$50m+ depending on programme size and zone E exposure. [L3]

War risk extension: +15–40% of base W4 premium depending on zone E/D exposure
Medium Impact

Repatriation Benefit Structuring

Medical repatriation benefit (average $85,000+ per event est.) — cost of air ambulance or scheduled medical evacuation, escort medical personnel, receiving hospital costs, and family travel. Benefit limit and provider quality (ISOS, Healix, Europ Assistance) significantly affects both cost and recovery outcomes. [I3]

Medevac per event limit: typically $250k–$1m for W4 programmes
Medium Impact

Fronting Jurisdiction Requirements

Where admitted insurance is required (UAE, Saudi, Brazil, France), fronting arrangements add cost (fronting fee 2–8% of local ceded premium) and complexity. Underwriters assess fronting availability and letter of credit requirements by territory. Fronting failure in a required-admission jurisdiction is an uninsured loss. [I5]

Fronting cost: 2–8% of locally ceded premium; LOC cost: 0.5–1.5% of limit
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Security Protocols and Site Hardening

Documented site security standards — blast walls, T-barriers, convoy procedures, armed escort, buddy system check-ins — provide rate credit for Zone D/E exposures. Absence of documented security protocols is a moderate negative factor. Underwriters review employer's HEAT training programme and security risk assessment.

Lower Impact

Medical Evacuation Provider Quality

SLA in place with ISOS, Healix, Europ Assistance or comparable provider — response time commitments, aircraft availability, network quality — positively influences W4 risk quality assessment. Evacuation capability materially affects outcome severity. Remote operations without evacuation plan are a loading factor.

Lower Impact

Claims Notification Procedures

DBA: DOL Form LS-202 must be filed within 10 days of employer knowledge of injury ($37,000+ penalty for late filing). Clear internal claims reporting procedures, designated DBA claims contact and 24/7 OWCP notification capability are underwriting quality indicators for DBA accounts. [L9]

Lower Impact

Host Country WC Statutory Benefit Level

Host country statutory WC benefit generosity (high in UK/Germany/Australia, very low in some African and Asian jurisdictions) determines how much the W4 policy needs to add above statutory minimums. Low-benefit jurisdictions increase the W4 policy's net exposure; this is factored into expected cost modelling by territory.

Key Exclusions
ExclusionRationaleEndorsement / Workaround
War / Hostile Act (standard)Standard WC policy exclusion — war risk is not covered unless specifically endorsedWar risk extension required for Zone D/E; DBA WHCA backstop for USG contracts
Pre-existing conditions (PA element)PA cover excludes pre-existing medical conditions from accidental death / disability benefitsPre-deployment medical clearance mitigates; some policies have buy-back options
Suicide / self-inflicted injuryStandard WC exclusion; some jurisdictions require cover for mental health / PTSD-related eventsTerritory-specific PTSD and mental health benefit extensions available
Contractual liability assumedLiability assumed under contract beyond what tort law would impose is not standard EL coverExpress contractual liability extension required where employer has assumed enhanced liability
Nuclear / radiologicalStandard exclusion across all WC/EL productsSpecial nuclear facility rider required for Fukushima-type environments (specialist market)
Chemical / biological (non-war)Occupational chemical exposure typically addressed via EL; weaponised CBRN excluded in war riskOccupational disease extension covers industrial chemical exposure; CBRN remains war risk
Independent contractorsWC/EL covers employees only — independent contractors are not employees at lawLabour-only subcontractor endorsement; principal employer extension where required by statute
Wilful misconductEmployer intentional exposure of employees to known risk without mitigationNot available — underwriters review safety management systems to assess moral hazard
DBA Compliance Warning — DOL Filing Deadlines: Under the Defence Base Act, the employer (insured) is required to file Form LS-202 (Employer's First Report of Injury) with the US Department of Labor / Office of Workers' Compensation Programs (OWCP) within 10 days of knowledge of a reportable injury. Failure to file results in a penalty of up to $37,000 per violation. Late filing also prejudices the WHCA reimbursement claim for war hazard injuries. DBA underwriters must confirm that the insured has a robust claims reporting procedure and designated DBA claims contact. [L9]
