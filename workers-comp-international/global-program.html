International WC / EL Multinational Programme — 6-Layer Tower

Illustrative W4 Programme Tower — Top-Down View (highest priority layer first)

1
XL Aggregate Reinsurance

Catastrophe excess of loss and aggregate stop-loss protecting the Lloyd's W4 portfolio against accumulation of Zone D/E losses. WHCA backstop provides supplementary government indemnification for war hazard injuries on DBA contracts. Typically arranged at syndicate level rather than per-programme.

2
Lloyd's Excess EL — Master Policy

Lloyd's W4 master policy providing excess employers liability (common law civil suits) above statutory WC benefits. Typically USD 5m–25m any one occurrence; worldwide coverage. Responds for expat and TCN workers across all territories. Central master policy issued from Lloyd's London; local admitted policies issued separately where required.

3
Defence Base Act (DBA) — US Government Contract Requirement

Mandatory statutory WC cover for US government contractors. Must be placed with a DBA-authorised carrier; most Lloyd's DBA paper placed through US domestics (AIG, Zurich, Chubb) with Lloyd's taking reinsurance. LHWCA benefit schedule applies. DOL/OWCP administers. WHCA backstop for war hazard injuries. Form LS-202 filing within 10 days. VER

4
Local Admitted WC — Host Country Statutory

Locally admitted workers compensation policies in jurisdictions that require them (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Brazil, France, etc.). Issued by locally licensed insurer; reinsured (often 100%) to Lloyd's master programme. Fronting insurer issues the statutory policy certificate required for local regulatory compliance. Fronting fee: 2–8% of ceded premium. VER

5
War / Hostile Environment Endorsement

Endorsement extending the master W4 policy to cover injuries, death and disability arising from war, hostile acts, terrorism, civil commotion and kidnap in designated Zone D/E territories. Subject to aggregate event limit and per-territory sublimits. War risk endorsement is a requirement for any Zone D/E deployment — standard WC policy excludes war. VER

6
Self-Insured Retention (SIR)

Employer's self-insured retention for attritional WC/EL losses below the insurance layer. Typical SIR: USD 50,000–$500,000 per occurrence for mid-to-large multinationals. SIR management requires internal claims administration capability for routine minor injuries and occupational health matters. SIR adequacy is reviewed at programme inception and renewal.

Jurisdiction Profiles — 6 Key Territories
Zone A–B

United Kingdom — W3 Domestic + W4 for Expats

UK domestic EL for UK-based employees is covered under W3 (Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969). W4 applies to UK-domiciled employers whose employees work outside the UK. UK EL Act has limited extraterritorial scope; UK employees working temporarily overseas retain UK EL Act protection. Lloyd's W4 master policy supplements UK statutory EL for overseas deployments. VER

Fronting: Not required for Lloyd's W4 for overseas-deployed UK employees. UK EL Act policy required for UK-based workers.
Zone E — War Zone

Iraq / Afghanistan — DBA Required, War Risk Extension Mandatory

All workers on US government contracts in Iraq and Afghanistan require DBA coverage — mandatory per 42 U.S.C. § 1651. War risk extension is mandatory; WHCA backstop reduces commercial war hazard exposure. DOL/OWCP Form LS-202 must be filed within 10 days of injury. TCN benefit calculations require specialist DBA claims management. No viable local admitted WC market in active conflict zones — Lloyd's direct or US domestic DBA carrier. VER

Fronting: Not applicable — Lloyd's direct or US DBA carrier. WHCA backstop for war hazard deaths essential.
Zone B — GCC

UAE — Local Admitted Required; DIEDC Fronting

UAE Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 and subsequent labour legislation require employers to maintain local WC cover. UAE Insurance Authority requires locally licensed insurers. Lloyd's W4 is placed through a DIEDC (Dubai International Financial Centre) admitted insurer or an onshore UAE insurer fronting the risk, with 100% reinsurance to Lloyd's. Letters of Credit (typically 25–50% of annual premium) are required by the fronting insurer. VER

Fronting: Mandatory. DIEDC fronter or UAE-licensed insurer. LOC required. Fronting fee: 3–6% of ceded premium.
Zone C

Nigeria — NAICOM Admits Lloyd's on Referral; NLNG/Shell Precedents

Nigeria's National Insurance Commission (NAICOM) generally requires local WC placement with NAICOM-licensed insurers (Workmen's Compensation Act / Employees Compensation Act 2010). However, Lloyd's has been admitted on referral for large multinational programmes (Shell NLNG precedent) where local market capacity is insufficient. Nigerian ECA 2010 benefits are materially lower than Lloyd's W4 scope — gap cover is a key added value. VER

Fronting: Often required — NAICOM-licensed fronter. Lloyd's admitted on referral for large accounts. LOC situation varies.
Zone E — War Zone

Ukraine — Conflict Zone W4 Direct; War Exclusion Carve-Out Required

Ukraine's conflict status since 2022 has made Lloyd's W4 direct paper the standard approach — no viable local admitted market for war risk WC. War exclusion carve-out is required in the W4 policy to restore coverage for injury or death arising from the Russian-Ukrainian conflict. Territorial definitions require careful drafting to distinguish between occupied and non-occupied territories. Reconstruction contractors entering Ukraine from 2023 onwards require bespoke Zone E underwriting. EST

Fronting: Not applicable — Lloyd's direct W4. War carve-out endorsement mandatory. Aggregate Zone E sublimit required.
Zone B

Philippines — OFW System; POEA Compulsory Insurance; Separate Structure

Philippine Overseas Employment Administration (POEA) mandates compulsory insurance for all Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs) under the POEA Standard Employment Contract. The compulsory POEA insurance (minimum P100,000 death benefit) is separate from and in addition to any W4 cover under the overseas employer's programme. Large Philippine TCN employers use a layered structure: POEA compulsory base + employer W4 supplement. VER

Structure: POEA compulsory base (mandatory) + W4 supplement via employer's international programme. Two separate policies required.

Case Study: UK Oil Services Company — 600 Employees, Multi-Jurisdictional W4 Programme Restructure

A UK-headquartered oil services company with 600 employees across 5 countries had been operating with fragmented local WC/EL policies arranged independently in each jurisdiction — no consolidated Lloyd's master programme. Programme review revealed significant coverage gaps, compliance failures, and premium inefficiency. The restructure consolidated to a Lloyd's W4 master with local admitted fronting where required.

US Department of Labor — DBA Filing Requirements Summary:

Form LS-202 (Employer's First Report of Injury or Occupational Illness): Must be filed by the employer with DOL/OWCP within 10 days of knowledge of a work-related injury or occupational disease under DBA. Filed electronically via DOL's OWCP Data Interchange or by mail to the applicable OWCP district office.

Penalty for late filing: Up to $37,000 per violation (adjusted periodically for inflation under the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act).

Form LS-203 (Employee's Claim for Compensation): Filed by the injured worker within 1 year of injury (2 years for occupational disease) to preserve DBA rights.

WHCA Reimbursement Claim: Filed by the DBA carrier following payment of a war hazard claim — government reimburses. Separate from the LS-202 process; filed with DOL OWCP Division of Federal Employees' Compensation (DFEC). VER
