## v5.9 — July 2026 — Stop-Loss / Aggregate hub (6th Reinsurance structure hub) — hub #58

Sixth hub of the Reinsurance group and the first **whole-account aggregate** structure: full 9-module hub + aggregate stop-loss rater at `reinsurance-stop-loss/`. Amber/bronze theme (`--hub-primary:#9a6a1e`).

- **The distinguishing idea:** stop-loss answers a bad *year*, not one loss (per-risk) or one event (cat). When the whole-account loss ratio breaches an attachment (e.g. 90%) the reinsurer pays the excess up to an exhaustion point (e.g. 110%), capping any mix of frequency and severity. The programme's top backstop.
- **Modules:** Overview (whole-year trigger, attachment/exhaustion as loss-ratio points, co-participation, annual cap, crop/volatile-line home), History (4 eras — agricultural origins → aggregate pricing science → volatility/capital management → Solvency II & new lines), Timeline (10 events incl. 2012 US drought, COVID-19), Loss Database (bad-year *types* — drought, multiple mid-size cats, attrition, systemic COVID, new-line mispricing, reserve strengthening), Underwriting (stop-loss expected-cost formula, empirical/fitted/compound aggregate modelling, moral-hazard discussion, worked 90–110% layer), Rates & ROE (2 Chart.js charts — illustrative loss-ratio distribution with shaded tail + attachment-probability curve; attachment trade-off), Risk Mitigation/Contract & Wordings (co-participation, attachment above expected, annual cap, loss-ratio definition, risk-transfer test; the moral-hazard trap), Global Programme (stop-loss as the backstop above proportional + per-risk + cat; mutual-new-class case study), References (source categories incl. collective risk theory, 12-row confidence audit, data gaps).
- **Stop-Loss Rater** (`rater.html`): models the whole-year loss ratio as a normal distribution and prices the layer with the stop-loss formula E[(L−a)⁺]−E[(L−b)⁺], net of co-participation, then loads and grosses up. Returns expected recovery, expected loss cost (% of SP), layer premium, rate-on-line, payback and the probability of attaching. Implements erf/Φ/φ in-browser. ## v5.7 — July 2026 — Surplus Treaty hub (4th Reinsurance structure hub) — hub #56

Fourth hub of the Reinsurance group and the second **proportional** structure: full 9-module hub + surplus-cession rater at `reinsurance-surplus/`. Teal theme (`--hub-primary:#1f6f78`).

- **The distinguishing idea:** unlike quota share's fixed percentage, a surplus treaty cedes a *variable* share — the cedant keeps a retention (a "line") on every risk and cedes only the surplus above it, up to N lines. Small risks stay fully retained; large risks are shared; the net account is homogenised.
- **Modules:** Overview (variable cession, the line, table of lines, where surplus sits among QS/XoL/fac), History (5 eras — birth of the treaty → property workhorse → XoL competes → capital & specialisation → specialist niche), Timeline (10 events 1846–2026), Loss Database (accumulation-focused market/loss episodes — Andrew, KRW, Thai floods, HIM, Bernd, Vesttoo), Underwriting (surplus cession formula: ceded SI = min(SI−retention, lines×retention); two worked examples incl. a risk beyond capacity → facultative), Rates & ROE (2 Chart.js charts — ceding-commission cycle + retention-vs-cession-ratio), Risk Mitigation/Contract & Wordings (table of lines, event limit, sliding scale, profit commission, follow-the-fortunes; wordings table; counterparty/collateral), Global Programme (surplus in the property tower beneath XoL and above QS; heterogeneous-commercial-property case study), References (source categories, 12-row confidence audit, data gaps).
- **Surplus Rater** (`rater.html`): two panels — Panel A computes the single-risk cession (SI, retention, lines → ceded SI, cession ratio, ceded premium, reinsurer share of loss, above-capacity slice); Panel B prices the treaty result (ceded premium, loss ratio, ceding/profit commission, brokerage → combined ratio and result). Reuses `shared/rater-styles.css`.

## v5.3.6 — July 2026 — Cycle Modeller: de-cluttered rate dynamics (removed double-counting)

Peter flagged the rate/ROE path as too volatile and suspected double-counting between loss inflation, the rate decline and the capital-softening term. He was right:

- The soft-market rate rule applied a fixed −4%/yr decline **plus** the excess-capital softening, and neither was measured against loss-cost inflation — so rates fell ~8% while losses rose ~4%, eroding adequacy ~12%/yr, ballooning COR and tripping the aggressive hardening. The fixed drift and the capital term partly double-counted the same "excess capital → soften" force.
- **Fix:** nominal rate change now **tracks loss-cost inflation at equilibrium** (`rateChange = lossTrend + structuralDrift − softSens×(coverage − target)`), so an adequately-capitalised market holds a flat COR. Softening only occurs *below* inflation to the extent the market is over-capitalised; hardening still fires on margin erosion / capital destruction. Each factor does one job, netted against the loss trend rather than stacked as three separate cuts.
- Defaults recalibrated: "Soft-market rate drift −4%" → **"Structural rate drift −1%"** (competitive drift on top of inflation tracking); hardening aggressiveness 2.5× → **2.0×**. Result: a gentler, slower, more realistic cycle — COR drifts up over ~5–6 years then corrects, instead of a violent 4-year blow-out-and-snap. Cycle-engine explainer updated to describe the no-double-count design.
- Added a **real rate change** line (nominal − loss-cost trend) to the rate/ROE chart and a row to the table, so both the headline nominal rate move and the true inflation-adjusted adequacy move are visible — a soft market often shows only a small nominal cut but a clearly negative real move. Nominal = solid purple, real = dashed red.
