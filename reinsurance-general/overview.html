Reinsurance — Overview

The gateway module to the Reinsurance group: what reinsurance is, why cedants buy it, and how the market splits into proportional and non-proportional structures. This hub sits above the structure-specific hubs (Quota Share, Surplus, Per-Risk XoL, Catastrophe XoL, Stop-Loss, Facultative, Retrocession, Structured/Finite and ILS) and frames the vocabulary an underwriter needs to read any reinsurance placement.

Reinsurance is insurance for insurers: a contract under which a reinsurer indemnifies a cedant (the ceding insurer) against part of the risk the cedant has assumed from its own policyholders. It is the mechanism that lets a single Lloyd's syndicate write a $500m energy platform or a Florida homeowners book without holding the full loss on its own balance sheet. Reinsurance transfers volatility, releases capital, stabilises results and expands underwriting capacity — and it is itself one of the largest and most capital-intensive segments of the global insurance market. Why Cedants Buy Reinsurance

🛡️

Capacity

Write larger limits and bigger single risks than the cedant's own capital and risk appetite would otherwise permit.

📉

Volatility Reduction

Smooth loss ratios year-to-year by ceding the peaks — catastrophe, large single losses and adverse aggregations.

🏦

Capital Relief

Reduce required regulatory capital (Solvency II SCR, Lloyd's capital) by transferring risk to a rated counterparty.

🌍

Catastrophe Protection

Cap the balance-sheet impact of a single hurricane, earthquake or systemic event across a whole portfolio.

🚀

Market Entry / Growth

Support entry into a new line or territory by sharing early-years uncertainty until the book matures.

🎓

Expertise & Services

Access reinsurer pricing, exposure modelling, wordings and claims expertise — particularly through proportional treaties.

The Two Great Divides

Every reinsurance contract can be located on two axes. The first is facultative vs treaty — whether cover is negotiated risk-by-risk or across a whole book. The second is proportional vs non-proportional — whether the reinsurer shares premium and losses in a fixed ratio, or responds only once losses breach an agreed threshold. Almost every named structure in this hub is a combination of these two axes.

How reinsurance differs from the primary hubs The other Risk Code Hubs are lines of business rated by exposure (rate-on-value). Reinsurance is a structure layered on top of any line, priced by rate-on-line, burning cost or cession economics rather than rate-on-value. That is why the Reinsurance rater uses a bespoke layer-pricing engine rather than the standard rate-on-value model.
