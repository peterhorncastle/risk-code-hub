Reinsurance — Timeline

Twenty events that reshaped reinsurance capacity, pricing and structure, from the founding of Cologne Re to the softening market of 2026.

1846
Cologne Re founded
Company The first company created solely to write reinsurance, introducing the professional reinsurer to the market. VER
1863
Swiss Re founded after the Glarus fire
Company A devastating 1861 town fire prompted the creation of Swiss Re; Munich Re followed in 1880. VER
1906
San Francisco earthquake & fire
Catastrophe The first modern catastrophe test; Munich Re's payments and Cuthbert Heath's "pay all" defined claims-paying reputations. ~$500m (1906 $) VER
1960s–70s
Bermuda emerges as a reinsurance domicile
Company Favourable regulation and tax established Bermuda as a captive and reinsurance hub, later the engine of post-crisis capacity. EST
1987–88
Catastrophe models arrive (AIR, RMS)
Market Probabilistic simulation began to replace judgement in catastrophe pricing — the single biggest change in reinsurance underwriting. VER
1992
Hurricane Andrew
Catastrophe ~$15.5bn insured, eleven insurer insolvencies; exposed systemic cat under-pricing and launched the Bermuda "Class of 1993". $15.5bn VER
1994
Northridge earthquake
Catastrophe ~$15bn insured; reshaped US quake pricing and led to the California Earthquake Authority. ~$15bn VER
1997
First modern catastrophe bonds
Capital USAA's Residential Re and others opened capital-markets access to cat risk, seeding the ILS market. VER
2001
11 September attacks
Catastrophe ~$47bn insured across multiple lines from one event; triggered the Bermuda "Class of 2001" and terror exclusions. ~$47bn VER
2005
Hurricanes Katrina, Rita & Wilma
Catastrophe Katrina alone ~$65bn insured; birthed the sidecar, accelerated ILS and the "Class of 2005". ~$80bn (KRW) VER
2008
Global financial crisis
Market Reinsurers' asset side hit; underscored the appeal of ILS as returns uncorrelated with financial markets. VER
2011
Tōhoku, NZ quakes & Thai floods
Catastrophe The costliest catastrophe year to that point; Thai floods exposed hidden global supply-chain / CBI aggregation. ~$120bn (yr) VER
2013
Pension-fund capital floods in
Capital Low interest rates drove institutional money into ILS; "convergence" capital began softening the traditional market. EST
2016
Solvency II in force
Regulatory Formalised reinsurance as an SCR-reducing risk-mitigation technique across the EU and UK. VER
2017
Hurricanes Harvey, Irma & Maria
Catastrophe ~$92bn insured from three storms; trapped ILS collateral and ended the long soft market's momentum. ~$92bn (HIM) VER
2018–20
Secondary perils & loss creep
Market Wildfire, convective storm and typhoon losses plus adverse development drove disciplined re-underwriting and retention increases. VER
2022
Hurricane Ian
Catastrophe ~$50–60bn insured; the loss that tipped the market into the hardest renewal in a generation. ~$50–60bn VER
2023
The January hard-market renewal
Market Property-cat rate-on-line jumped sharply, attachment points rose and aggregate covers withdrew — a structural reset. VER
2025
Record capital, record cat-bond market
Capital Dedicated reinsurance capital reached $648bn; cat bonds outstanding grew 24% to $61.3bn on record $25.6bn issuance. VER
2026
Softening: property-cat RoL −12%
Market The weight of capital reversed the cycle; the sharpest risk-adjusted property-cat decline since 2014. VER
