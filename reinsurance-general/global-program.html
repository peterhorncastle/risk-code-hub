Reinsurance — Programme Structuring

How a cedant assembles a full reinsurance programme — from net retention through working and catastrophe layers to retrocession — and the placement workflow that gets it bound.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

A cedant rarely buys one reinsurance contract. It builds a programme — a stack of complementary structures that together shape the net result: a proportional treaty to release capital and share the book, per-risk cover for large single losses, a catastrophe tower for accumulation, and sometimes retrocession on top. Understanding how the pieces fit is what turns a list of treaties into a coherent risk-transfer strategy. VER

The Placement Workflow

1

Exposure & data preparation

The cedant and broker assemble loss history, portfolio data and cat-model output into a submission.

2

Structure & strategy

Decide retention, which layers to buy, proportional vs non-proportional mix, and how much risk to keep net.

3

Marketing the placement

The broker takes the submission to lead reinsurers to set terms and price, then builds a following market.

4

Firm order terms & signing

A lead quote sets the price; the layer is subscribed by multiple reinsurers to 100% and signed down.

5

Contract & wording

Slip and treaty wording finalised — reinstatements, hours, exclusions, collateral (see Contract & Wordings).

6

In-force management & renewal

Track erosion, reinstatements and counterparty security through the year; re-underwrite at renewal.

Case Study — A Mid-Size Property Insurer

A regional property insurer with £180m GWP wants to grow into a new coastal territory without overloading its capital or exposing the balance sheet to a single hurricane.

The problem

Rapid growth strains regulatory capital, and coastal expansion concentrates catastrophe exposure the insurer cannot retain net.

The programme

A 30% quota share releases capital and shares the growing book; a per-risk XoL caps single-risk severity; a three-layer cat tower (£10m attaching) protects against accumulation.

The economics

The quota-share ceding commission offsets much of the cat-programme cost; net retention is set at a level the capital base can absorb in a bad year.

The outcome

The insurer grows into the new territory with a stable net loss ratio and a modelled 1-in-200 net loss within its capital appetite.

Programme design is a portfolio decision Each layer is priced individually, but the cedant buys the whole tower to shape one number: the net result after a bad year. The art is spending the reinsurance budget where it most reduces net volatility per pound — usually the working and mid cat layers, not the remote top of the tower.
