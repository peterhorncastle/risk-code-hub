Reinsurance is a cyclical business. Rate-on-line rises after major losses destroy capital and falls when capital rebuilds — the pattern the Guy Carpenter Rate-on-Line indices have tracked since 1990. The Reinsurance Cycle in Four Phases

Hard · 2023

The generational repricing

After Ian and years of loss creep, the January 2023 renewal delivered the sharpest property-cat increase in decades — higher rates, sharply higher attachment points, and the withdrawal of aggregate and low-layer cover. Reinsurers reset the terms of trade in their favour.

Peak margin · 2024

Discipline holds, profits surge

Benign large-loss experience relative to the reset attachment points produced exceptional reinsurer returns. Capital rebuilt rapidly through retained earnings while structures stayed disciplined.

Softening · 2025–26

The weight of capital

Record $648bn capital and a 24% larger cat-bond market chased a finite amount of risk. January 2026 property-cat rate-on-line fell ~12% (Howden Re risk-adjusted −14.7%, retro −16.5%) — the largest decline since 2014, though attachment-point discipline largely held.

Watch · forward

What turns it back

A single large catastrophe, a casualty-reserve shock, or an ILS pull-back could reverse the softening quickly — the cycle's defining feature is how fast it turns.

What an analyst reads here Munich Re and Hannover Re benefit from the same hard-market window but position differently: Munich Re runs a larger NatCat budget and a lower headline combined ratio with a bigger, more diversified balance sheet; Hannover Re accepts higher net retention (thinner retrocession) for faster bottom-line growth and a best-in-class ~21% ROE — which would bite harder in a future major-cat year. Both sit well above their Solvency II targets (Munich Re 175–220%, Hannover Re >200%), which is the basis for their buybacks and rising dividends. The persistent reserve releases at Beazley and Hiscox are a late-cycle signal — prior-vintage redundancy released even as the current accident year is stressed by cat. VER
