1906San Francisco earthquake & fire ▸USA~$500m (1906$)EarthquakeCAT
First modern global catastrophe test. Munich Re's full payment and Cuthbert Heath's "pay all" instruction proved the value of geographic diversification and cemented London's claims-paying reputation. Pushed the market toward excess-of-loss reinsurance.
1989Hurricane Hugo ▸Caribbean / US SE~$4.2bnHurricaneMAJ
One of the first billion-dollar US hurricanes; an early warning that catastrophe exposures were growing faster than pricing tools. A precursor to the Andrew shock three years later.
1992Hurricane Andrew ▸Florida, USA~$15.5bnHurricaneCAT
The defining reinsurance event of the modern era: eleven insurer insolvencies and proof that cat risk was systematically under-priced. Response: catastrophe modelling became central to pricing and the Bermuda "Class of 1993" recapitalised the market.
1994Northridge earthquake ▸California, USA~$15bnEarthquakeCAT
Costliest US quake to that date; insurers withdrew from residential quake cover, prompting the state-backed California Earthquake Authority and a permanent repricing of quake reinsurance.
1995Kobe earthquake ▸Japan~$3bn insuredEarthquakeMAJ
Enormous economic loss (~$100bn) but modest insured share revealed a vast protection gap in Japanese quake cover — a structural theme reinsurers still price around today.
200111 September attacks ▸USA~$47bnMan-madeCAT
A single event triggering property, aviation, liability, life and workers'-comp claims simultaneously — the archetypal clash loss. Drove terror exclusions, government terror pools (TRIA, Pool Re) and the Bermuda "Class of 2001".
2004Florida four-hurricane season ▸Florida, USA~$30bnHurricaneCAT
Charley, Frances, Ivan and Jeanne in one season stress-tested reinstatement provisions and per-occurrence vs aggregate structures, foreshadowing 2005.
2005Hurricanes Katrina, Rita & Wilma ▸US Gulf Coast~$80bn (KRW)HurricaneCAT
Katrina alone ~$65bn — the costliest single storm to that point. Exposed flood/surge modelling gaps and demand surge; birthed the sidecar, accelerated cat bonds and launched the "Class of 2005".
2010–11Canterbury / Christchurch quakes ▸New Zealand~$30bn (series)EarthquakeCAT
A multi-event sequence that created severe "hours clause" and event-definition disputes and years of loss creep — a lasting lesson in aggregation wording for reinsurers.
2011Tōhoku earthquake & tsunami ▸Japan~$35–40bn insuredEarthquakeCAT
Part of the costliest catastrophe year to that point (~$120bn). Combined with Thai floods to reprice Asia-Pacific reinsurance and highlight correlated global exposures.
2011Thailand floods ▸Thailand~$16bn insuredFloodMAJ
Contingent business-interruption losses rippled through global electronics and auto supply chains, revealing hidden CBI aggregation few reinsurers had modelled — a wake-up call on non-physical accumulation.
2012Superstorm Sandy ▸US Northeast~$30bnHurricaneCAT
Storm-surge flooding in a dense, high-value corridor; sharpened flood-vs-wind wording and drove demand for named-storm and surge modelling improvements.
2017Hurricanes Harvey, Irma & Maria ▸US / Caribbean~$92bn (HIM)HurricaneCAT
Three major storms in weeks; trapped ILS collateral, ended the long soft market's momentum and began several years of secondary-peril attrition and loss creep.
2017–18California wildfires (Tubbs, Camp) ▸California, USA~$25bn (series)Man-madeMAJ
Wildfire moved from "secondary" to a modelled peak peril. Reinsurers repriced aggregate covers and pushed attachment points higher to exclude attritional wildfire loss.
2020COVID-19 pandemic ▸Global~$40–80bn (est.)SystemicCAT
Business-interruption, event-cancellation and credit lines hit simultaneously worldwide. Litigation over BI wordings drove sweeping communicable-disease exclusions across reinsurance treaties.
2021Hurricane Ida & European floods ▸USA / Europe~$36bn + ~$13bnHurricaneCAT
Ida plus the July European floods ("Bernd") reminded reinsurers that flood and severe convective storm can produce peak-peril-scale losses far from Florida.
2022Hurricane Ian ▸Florida, USA~$50–60bnHurricaneCAT
The loss that tipped the market: it triggered the January 2023 hard renewal, the sharpest property-cat repricing in a generation, with higher attachments and withdrawal of aggregate cover.
2023Türkiye–Syria earthquakes ▸Türkiye / Syria~$5–6bn insuredEarthquakeCAT
Catastrophic humanitarian and economic loss (~$90bn) with a low insured share — a stark protection-gap example and a live test of Turkish quake-pool reinsurance.
2019–24US casualty "social inflation" ▸USAReserve creep, multi-$bnCasualtyMAJ
Nuclear jury verdicts, litigation funding and adverse development on liability lines drove casualty reinsurance repricing and tighter treaty terms — a slower-burning but structural pressure alongside cat.
2024Hurricanes Helene & Milton ▸US Southeast~$35–50bn (combined)HurricaneCAT
Large but within budget for well-capitalised reinsurers; higher post-2023 attachment points meant much of the loss stayed with primary carriers, validating the restructured market.
Reading the database as an underwriter Notice the pattern: a catastrophe overwhelms capacity → capital is destroyed → rates harden and structures tighten → new capital enters → the market softens until the next shock. This is the reinsurance cycle, and it is the single most important dynamic to understand before pricing any treaty.
