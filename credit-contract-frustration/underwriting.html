Credit & Contract Frustration — Underwriting

Rating methodology, key factors, worked examples, pre-bind checklist, and automatic referral triggers for CR single-risk, WT whole turnover, and CF contract frustration lines.

Whole Turnover (WT) Premium Calculation

// WT Annual Premium = Turnover × Premium Rate × Adjustments
Annual Premium = Estimated Annual Insured Turnover
                 × Base Premium Rate
                 × Country Risk Loading
                 × Sector Risk Factor
                 × Buyer Concentration Factor
                 ÷ (1 – First-Loss Percentage)
                 × Deductible Credit

// Base rate: 0.10–0.35% of annual insured turnover (domestic); 0.25–0.80% (export including emerging markets)
// Indemnity percentage typically 85–95%; first-loss deductible typically 10–20% of any claim

Single-Risk Credit (CR) Premium Calculation

// CR Single-Risk = Credit Limit × RoL × Risk Adjustments
Annual Premium = Credit Limit (Sum Insured)
                 × Base Rate-on-Line
                 × Buyer Credit Quality Factor
                 × Country Risk Factor
                 × Tenor Factor
                 × Waiting Period Credit

// Base RoL: 0.5–3.0% for investment-grade buyers in OECD markets; 2–8% for sub-IG or emerging market buyers
// Tenor factor: increases for medium-term (2–7 year) contracts vs short-term (90–180 day)

Contract Frustration (CF) Premium Calculation

// CF = Insured Contract Value × CF Rate × Political Risk Adjustments
Annual Premium = Insured Contract Value
                 × Base CF Rate
                 × Political Risk Country Factor
                 × Buyer Type Factor (private vs SOE vs sovereign)
                 × Contract Duration Factor
                 × Frustration Cause Factor

// CF Base rate: 0.25–1.5% for investment-grade OECD sovereign; 1.5–5.0% for high-risk country SOE buyer
// Frustration causes: import prohibition, contract cancellation by government, force majeure declaration, CI/TR

Whole Turnover (WT) Rating Factors

Buyer Portfolio Quality

Average credit quality of the policyholder's debtor book — proportion of buyers with external credit ratings, trade payment histories, financial accounts availability. Blue-chip domestic buyers attract lowest rates; SME and private buyer books attract loading.

WT base: ×0.7–1.5 on rate

Geographic Mix — Export Destinations

Export WT rates vary significantly by destination market. OECD Western Europe: lowest; Eastern Europe/Russia: +50–100%; MENA: +30–80%; Sub-Saharan Africa: +80–200%; High-risk countries (Venezuela, Iran, Belarus): typically excluded or sublimited.

Export loading: ×1.2–3.0 vs domestic base

Sector / Industry Risk

Sectors with higher insolvency rates or cyclical vulnerability carry rate loading. Construction, retail, and print media have historically higher credit loss rates. Pharmaceuticals, food/FMCG, and utilities carry lower risk. Credit insurers maintain sector-level pricing adjustments based on claims experience.

Sector loading: ×0.8–1.8

Buyer Concentration

Highly concentrated debtor books — where the top 5 buyers represent >50% of insured turnover — carry significant loading or single-buyer sub-limits. Diversified books of 50+ buyers carry credits. Concentration limits per buyer typically capped at 15–20% of aggregate insured turnover.

Concentration loading: +10–50%

Policy Deductible / First Loss

Standard WT deductible is 15–20% of any individual claim (first-loss deductible). Some policies use an annual aggregate deductible (first X% of annual losses absorbed by insured). Higher deductibles attract significant premium credits — often the primary lever for premium reduction in renewal negotiations.

Deductible credit: −10 to −35%

Waiting Period

Protracted default trigger requires non-payment for a specified period (typically 90–180 days) before a claim can be filed. Longer waiting periods reduce premium — the insured absorbs short-term payment delays before insurance triggers. Standard waiting period for domestic WT: 90 days; export: 120–180 days.

180-day vs 90-day credit: −10 to −20%

Policyholder Loss History (APH)

Actual bad debt history (typically 3–5 years) is the primary rating input — comparable to APH in crop insurance. Policyholders with stable, low bad debt experience attract claims-free discounts. Policyholders with recent large losses face mandatory loading or reduced indemnification percentage.

Claims-free credit: −5 to −25%; loss loading: +20–80%

Credit Management Quality

Policyholders with documented credit management procedures — formal buyer credit assessment processes, trade reference checking, credit limit monitoring systems (D&B, Experian), legal action protocols — attract underwriting credit. Poor credit management is the primary cause of avoidable WT losses.

Credit management credit: −5 to −15%

Single-Risk Credit (CR) & CF Rating Factors

Buyer Credit Quality (CR)

External credit rating (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) or internal credit assessment. Investment-grade buyers (BBB- and above): base rate. Sub-investment-grade buyers: significant loading. Unrated private buyers: assessed on financials, years in business, sector, payment track record.

IG: 0.5–1.5% RoL; Sub-IG: 2–6% RoL; High-risk: 5–10%+

Country Risk — Political & Transfer (CR & CF)

Country risk category (1–7, per OECD country risk classification) is the primary rating driver for export CR and CF. Category 0–2 (OECD low-risk): minimal loading. Category 5–7 (high-risk emerging market): rates 3–5× OECD base. Countries under sanction: typically excluded.

OECD cat 0–2: base; cat 5–7: ×3–5; sanctioned: excluded

Buyer Type — State vs Private (CF)

Sovereign buyers (Ministry, government entity): CF triggered by government action — lower commercial default risk but political frustration risk. State-owned enterprise (SOE): intermediate; political frustration and commercial default both possible. Private buyer: commercial default risk primary; CF triggered only by government action preventing payment.

Sovereign: lowest CF rate; SOE: +25–60%; private: highest commercial rate

Contract Tenor / Credit Period

Short-term credit (up to 180 days): lowest rate — least time exposure. Medium-term (1–3 years): moderate loading. Capital goods / project (3–7 years): significant loading for political and country risk. Longer tenors compound the probability of adverse political or economic events intervening.

Tenor factor: ×1.0 (90 days) → ×2.5–4.0 (5+ years)

CF Cause Coverage (CF)

Breadth of CF peril coverage: import/export prohibition (most common); contract cancellation by government; government-imposed force majeure; currency inconvertibility/transfer restriction (CI/TR); war and civil disturbance frustration. Broader cause coverage = higher rate. Narrow-cause policies (import prohibition only) cheaper.

Narrow cause: base; full CF suite: +20–40%

OECD Arrangement Compliance (Medium-Term CR)

For medium-term export credit structured within the OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits, minimum premium rates (MPRs) apply per country risk category. Lloyd's market operates above MPR for private-market-only risk; below MPR products require ECA backing. Arrangement compliance simplifies premium benchmarking for 5–7 year capital goods contracts.

OECD MPR: benchmark floor for medium-term CR/CF

Collateral & Security (CR)

Presence of collateral — letter of credit (LC), bank guarantee, pledged receivables, insurance assignment — significantly reduces the net insured loss in the event of buyer default. LC-backed transactions may be excluded from CR cover (LC cancellation risk is a separate product). Partial security reduces premium proportionally.

Full LC: typically excluded; partial security: −15 to −40% rate

Indemnity Percentage (CR & WT)

Standard indemnity: 85–95% of net loss (invoice value less recoveries). The 5–15% non-insured portion acts as a moral hazard control — the insured retains skin-in-the-game. Higher indemnity percentages (95%) are available at premium loading and may be required by banks using credit insurance as a collateral enhancement.

85% indemnity: standard; 95%: +8–15% premium loading

Pre-Bind Underwriting Checklist

  • REQUIRED Insured's trading name, registered address, and company registration — confirm legal entity to be insured matches the contracting entity extending credit.
  • REQUIRED For WT: 3 years' audited accounts for the policyholder and 3-year bad debt history (actual write-offs by year). For CR: buyer financials (2 years' accounts) or credit agency report.
  • REQUIRED Proposed credit limits — individual buyer limits (WT) or single contract sum insured (CR/CF). Confirm sum insured does not exceed buyer's capacity to absorb.
  • REQUIRED Payment terms and credit period — must match proposed waiting period. 90-day waiting period is inappropriate if the insured extends 180-day payment terms to buyers.
  • REQUIRED Country list and geographic mix of insured buyers. Confirm no sanctioned country exposure (OFAC, EU, UK sanctions) included in scope without explicit exclusion endorsement.
  • PREFERRED Buyer credit report (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian, Creditsafe) for any buyer limit >£250,000. For sub-IG or unrated buyers, obtain full trade reference and payment history.
  • PREFERRED Description of the insured's internal credit management process: how are credit limits set, reviewed, and monitored? Who approves limits above £100k? Credit committee documentation preferred.
  • PREFERRED For CF: copy of the underlying contract to be insured, including payment terms, force majeure clause, jurisdiction and governing law.
  • PREFERRED Claims history (5 years) including claims made, paid, and reserved. Any large single-buyer loss events. Any current overdue debts above policy waiting period threshold.
  • INFO Sector breakdown of insured debtor book — proportion by industry. Flag if >40% concentrated in any single sector. Construction, retail, and hospitality require additional scrutiny.
  • INFO For medium-term CR: details of goods/services being exported, expected shipment date, full contract value, drawdown schedule (if staged payments), and any ECA co-insurance involvement.
  • INFO Existing credit risk management tools: letters of credit, bank guarantees, pledged security, factoring/invoice discounting facilities. Confirm whether existing third-party credit protections are in scope or excluded from WT coverage.

Automatic Referral Triggers

Single buyer >20% of insured turnover

Buyer concentration at this level requires senior underwriter review and potential individual buyer sub-limit. Adverse selection risk — insured may seek credit insurance specifically because of concerns about this buyer.

Country risk category 6 or 7 buyer exposure

OECD category 6–7 or Lloyd's excluded country list exposure requires referral. Include any sanctioned country buyer (OFAC/EU/UK sanctions list) — automatic exclusion unless special facility.

Any single credit limit >£500k on unrated buyer

Limits of this size on buyers without external credit rating or D&B assessment require additional financial information and senior review. Adverse selection concern: why is this buyer unable to obtain a credit rating?

Loss ratio >80% in prior 3 years

Policyholder with 3-year loss ratio above 80% requires mandatory loading, coverage restructure (higher deductible), or declination. Credit management concerns must be addressed before renewal.

CF on state enterprise buyer in high-risk country

Contract frustration on a state-owned enterprise buyer in an OECD category 5–7 country requires dedicated PR/CF underwriter input. Country risk analysis and government financial position review mandatory.

Fintech / SCF platform distribution

Any credit insurance submission from a supply chain finance platform, invoice marketplace, or fintech intermediary requires enhanced due diligence on the underlying receivables, originator credit quality, and risk of concentration to a single platform buyer.

Sum insured >£5m single-risk CR

Large single-risk CR transactions above £5m require senior underwriter approval and possible co-insurance or ECA co-participation analysis. Consider whether ECA/UKEF involvement reduces the private market exposure.

Any exposure to current distressed sector

Significant exposure to sectors under current acute stress (retail closures, construction insolvency wave, tech sector layoffs, etc.) — requires real-time monitoring of limit portfolio for that sector and potential proactive limit adjustment discussion with policyholder.
Key Credit Insurance Principle — Indemnification Not Speculation: Credit insurance is designed to indemnify genuine trade creditors against genuine buyer default — not to enable speculative credit extension to buyers the insured would not have traded with on open account absent the insurance. Underwriters must be alert to adverse selection: the insured applying for cover on specific buyers because of suspected creditworthiness concern. Credit insurance does not replace sound credit management — it complements it.
