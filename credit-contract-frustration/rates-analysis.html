Credit insurance pricing is driven by a fundamentally different dynamic from most insurance lines — premium rates are closely tied to the economic cycle, and the market has a structural tendency toward pro-cyclical behaviour. Rates fall as economies expand and loss ratios improve; they rise sharply — and often too late — as recessions crystallise credit losses. This makes credit insurance one of the more technically challenging lines to underwrite counter-cyclically. EST

Four-Era Market Cycle

Era 1 · 2010–2012

Post-GFC Recovery & Rate Hardening

Post-GFC credit insurance market entered 2010 in a state of hard pricing following 2008–2009 losses. Combined ratios had exceeded 110% in 2009. Rates rose 20–40% on WT lines; single-risk CR remained elevated. Government guarantee schemes in UK and Europe wound down gradually 2010–2011, returning risk to private market. Index rises from 100 to 125–130 by 2012.

Era 2 · 2012–2018

Benign Cycle & Rate Softening

Extended period of economic expansion in OECD markets produces low attritional loss ratios in the WT book. Competition between Allianz Trade, Atradius, Coface, and the Lloyd's market intensifies. Rates soften 5–15% cumulatively from 2012–2018. CF rates remain more stable, driven by MENA Arab Spring losses 2011–2012 and ongoing political risk volatility. Index retreats to 95–105 range.

Era 3 · 2018–2021

Retail Insolvencies, COVID Shock & Hardening

UK retail insolvency wave (BHS, Carillion, Thomas Cook, Debenhams) and global COVID-19 disruption (2020) cause sharp rate hardening. WT rates rise 15–30% 2020–2021. Government guarantee schemes (UK TCR, Schutzschirm) contain net losses but also reduce private market premium volume as some risk transfers to government. Greensill collapse 2021 shocks fintech-facing credit line. Index climbs to 135–150.

Era 4 · 2021–2024

Russia/Ukraine CF Wave & Emerging Market Stress

Russia/Ukraine war (2022) triggers largest CF event since Argentina 2001. CF rates harden 20–40%. WT rates firm further 10–20% as rising insolvency rates in energy-intensive industries and supply chain disruption generate claims. Multiple emerging market sovereign defaults (Sri Lanka, Zambia) keep country risk categories elevated. Index peaks at ~165–175 by 2024 before modest softening.

Pro-Cyclicality Warning: Credit insurance is inherently pro-cyclical. Rates fall during economic expansions as loss experience improves, attracting new capacity and intensifying competition. When a recession strikes, credit losses rise rapidly and simultaneously across whole portfolios — generating combined ratios well above 100%. Underwriters who have priced to a benign cycle experience will find technical reserves inadequate in stress years. The 2009, 2012 (Greek restructuring), and 2020 (COVID) years all illustrate this dynamic. Prudent credit insurance portfolios maintain explicit economic cycle reserves and avoid growing rapidly into late-cycle environments.
