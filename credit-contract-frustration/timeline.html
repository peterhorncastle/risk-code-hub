1982
Sovereign Default
Mexican Debt Default — Latin American Crisis Begins
Mexico announces inability to service $80 billion external debt in August 1982, triggering the Latin American debt crisis. Sixteen countries reschedule debt 1982–1985. Credit insurers with LA export exposure face severe CF and CR losses; limit withdrawal accelerates. ECAs absorb much of the medium-term loss. VER
1988
Market Structure
OECD Arrangement Formalized — ECA Premium Minimums
The OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits formalises minimum premium rates (MPR) and maximum repayment terms for ECA-backed export credit, preventing subsidy competition between ECAs. This creates a clearer boundary between ECA and private market roles — private insurers compete on price and terms within the Arrangement's constraints. VER
1991–1992
Sovereign / Country
Soviet Union Collapse — Former USSR Country Risk Reset
Dissolution of the USSR forces credit insurers to reassess country risk for 15 new sovereign states. Russia, Ukraine, and the Baltic states open as new credit markets — initially high-risk, gradually normalising through the 1990s. ECAs (HERMES, COFACE, ECGD) begin writing Russian buyer credit. Private market cautious. VER
1997–1998
Sovereign / Country
Asian Financial Crisis — Korean & Indonesian Buyer Defaults
Thai baht devaluation triggers cascade of currency collapses. Korean chaebol insolvencies follow (Daewoo 1999: largest Korean corporate failure). Indonesian rupiah loses 80% of value — import financing collapses. Significant CR/WT losses for exporters to Korea, Indonesia, and Thailand. ECAs and private market collectively claim billions in losses. VER
1998
Sovereign Default
Russian Sovereign Default — August 1998
Russia declares moratorium on GKO (rouble treasury bonds) and suspends foreign debt payments following oil price collapse and capital flight. Rouble devalues 75%. Trade credit to Russian buyers — particularly for capital goods and consumer goods — suffers immediate loss crystallisation. CF claims filed on frustrated export contracts. VER
1999
Buyer Collapse
Daewoo Group Collapse — Korea's Largest Corporate Failure
Daewoo Group files for restructuring with $78 billion in debt — the largest corporate failure in Korean history at the time. Suppliers across Asia, Europe, and North America with open-account credit to Daewoo entities file CR claims. Demonstrates concentration risk in single-buyer exposures for WT policyholders. VER
2001–2002
Contract Frustration
Argentine Sovereign Default — $93bn & Currency Freeze
Argentina's December 2001 default — largest in history at $93 billion — triggers the corralito (banking freeze) and corralón (currency restrictions). Peso convertibility is suspended; state-owned buyer contracts are frustrated. CF and CR insurance claims exceed $2 billion industry-wide. Argentine market closes to open-account trade for several years. VER
2002
Buyer Collapse
Enron Collapse & Energy Sector Credit Losses
Enron's December 2001 bankruptcy — with $63 billion in assets — generates credit insurance losses for energy sector suppliers who had open-account credit exposure to Enron affiliates. Credit insurers' energy sector limit concentrations come under scrutiny. Demonstrates that investment-grade rated buyers can default rapidly and without warning. VER
2004
Market Development
Atradius Created — Market Consolidation Completes
Gerling NCM Credit Insurance and Atradius merge to form Atradius N.V. — completing the consolidation of the global specialist credit insurance market into three dominant carriers: Allianz Trade (Euler Hermes), Atradius, and Coface. This oligopoly structure raises concerns about market concentration and the systemic implications of all three simultaneously withdrawing limits during a crisis. VER
2008–2009
Systemic Credit Event
Global Financial Crisis — Credit Insurance Under Extreme Stress
Lehman Brothers' September 2008 failure triggers global trade finance freeze. Credit insurers face surging claims as buyer insolvencies spike in 2009; specialist insurers withdraw or reduce credit limits across portfolios, raising accusations of pro-cyclical behaviour at the worst moment for policyholders. UK ECGD/UKEF expands guarantees. Combined loss ratios in WT exceed 100% for the first time in the modern era. VER
2009
Regulation
Solvency II Development — Credit Risk Capital Treatment
EU Solvency II framework development introduces specific capital treatment for credit risk in insurance portfolios — distinguishing between trade credit, reinsurance credit risk, and investment credit risk. Credit insurers must hold capital against concentrated exposures, incentivising diversification and limiting single-buyer limits. Implementation 2016. VER
2011–2012
Contract Frustration
Arab Spring CF Wave — Libya, Egypt, Syria
Arab Spring uprisings frustrate export contracts to state-owned buyers in Libya (civil war), Egypt, Syria, and Tunisia. Libyan government contracts are particularly affected — infrastructure projects in progress are abandoned, equipment is destroyed, and counterparty governments collapse. CF claims filed for government-imposed force majeure on construction and infrastructure export contracts. VER
2012
Sovereign / Country
Greek Debt Restructuring — Eurozone Credit Stress
Greek PSI (Private Sector Involvement) haircut of ~53% on €206 billion of government bonds — the largest sovereign debt restructuring in history. Greek corporate buyer defaults surge; Greek private sector suppliers struggle to access trade credit. COFACE, Atradius, and Allianz Trade tighten Greece credit limits significantly. ECA programmes limited to new Greek-government-guaranteed transactions. VER
2016
Regulation
Solvency II Implementation — Credit Insurer Capital Requirements
Full Solvency II implementation for EU credit insurers. Capital requirements for concentrated credit exposures force portfolio diversification. Specialist credit insurers hold material capital buffers against systemic events. The framework's pro-cyclical nature — requiring more capital precisely when losses rise — remains a structural tension in the credit insurance market. VER
2018
Finance / Fintech
Supply Chain Finance Expansion — Insurance Enablement
Rapid growth in supply chain finance (reverse factoring) platforms uses credit insurance as an enabler. Platforms such as Greensill Capital, Taulia, and C2FO integrate credit insurance limits into their receivables purchasing frameworks, allowing banks to purchase supplier receivables with insurance protection. Lloyd's CR capacity flows to SCF platforms via delegated authorities. EST
2020
Government Intervention
COVID-19 — European Government Credit Guarantee Schemes
As COVID lockdowns trigger mass buyer distress, credit insurers reduce limits. UK government launches £10bn Trade Credit Reinsurance scheme (May 2020); Germany, France, and Netherlands create Schutzschirm and similar schemes. Total EU government guarantee exposure peaks ~€180 billion. Private and state capacity work in parallel to preserve trade credit flow. VER
2021
Finance / Fintech
Greensill Capital Collapse — $5bn Credit Insurance Shock
Greensill Capital files for insolvency in March 2021. Tokio Marine HCC — which had underwritten credit insurance on Greensill's supply chain finance receivables — faces an estimated $5 billion exposure claim. The episode raises fundamental questions about the use of credit insurance to support non-traditional financial structures, the role of insurance in enabling leverage, and concentration risk in single-programme credit facilities. VER
2022
Contract Frustration
Russia/Ukraine War — CF & Sanctions-Driven Frustration
February 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine triggers largest CF event since Argentina 2001. SWIFT exclusion of Russian banks, asset freeze, and export prohibition frustrate thousands of export contracts. Wheat and commodity trade from Ukraine halted. Credit insurers with Russian whole turnover exposure report significant CF and protracted default losses across multiple European markets. VER
2022–2023
Buyer Collapse
Emerging Market Stress — Sri Lanka, Zambia, Pakistan Defaults
Sri Lanka sovereign default (April 2022) — first in its history; Zambia restructures debt; Pakistan IMF bailout. Multiple low-income country debt distress cases follow post-COVID fiscal deterioration and commodity price inflation. ECA and private CR/CF exposures to state-owned buyers in affected markets generate claims. G20 Common Framework debt restructuring process proves slow and contested. VER
2023–2024
Market Development
InsurTech Credit — API-Embedded Trade Credit for SMEs
InsurTech platforms Hokodo, Nimbla, and Tinubu Square embed trade credit insurance directly into B2B e-commerce and invoice financing platforms via API. For the first time, SME sellers can obtain per-invoice credit insurance at point of sale. Lloyd's credit syndicates provide capacity via MGA/binding authority structures. Fundamentally disrupts traditional annual whole turnover policy model for SME segment. EST
