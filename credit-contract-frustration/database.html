Mexico's 1982 default cascaded into a 16-country debt crisis 1982–1985. ECAs (ECGD, COFACE, HERMES, EXIM) absorbed the bulk of medium-term losses; private market WT insurers suffered significant export buyer default losses across the region. Estimated combined ECA loss $5–8bn; private market losses not separately disclosed. CF claims on government-contract buyers formed a significant secondary layer. VER
1997–98Asian Financial Crisis — Korea, Indonesia, ThailandAsia PacificSOV$3–5bn est. (private + ECA)Catastrophic
Currency collapses across Southeast Asia and Korea. Korean chaebol insolvencies and Indonesian rupiah collapse (-80%) triggered massive buyer default losses across European and US exporters. Berne Union members reported sharp claims increase 1998–2000. Credit insurers significantly tightened Asia credit limits. Daewoo's 1999 collapse ($78bn debt) generated significant CR claims from suppliers globally. VER
2001–02Argentine Sovereign Default — Largest in History ($93bn)ArgentinaCF$2bn+ est. (all markets)Catastrophic
December 2001 — Argentina defaults on $93 billion of debt, the largest sovereign default in history at the time. Corralito (bank deposit freeze) and corralón (currency restrictions) imposed; peso convertibility suspended. State-owned enterprise contracts frustrated; import restrictions trigger CF claims. Total insurance market CF/CR losses estimated $2 billion+. Argentine market closes to open-account trade for years. VER
2008–09Global Financial Crisis — Systemic Credit EventGlobalDEFAULT$10–15bn est. (all markets, all credit lines)Catastrophic
Lehman Brothers collapse triggers global trade finance freeze and buyer insolvency surge across manufacturing, retail, construction, and commodities sectors. Whole turnover WT combined ratios exceed 100% for first time in modern era (2009). Credit insurers withdraw limits; policyholders cry foul. UK government Emergency Credit Guarantee scheme launched. Berne Union aggregate short-term credit losses peak 2009. VER
2021Greensill Capital Collapse — $5bn Credit Insurance ExposureGlobalFIN~$5bn (claimed); disputedCatastrophic
Greensill Capital — a supply chain finance platform — collapses in March 2021. Tokio Marine HCC had underwritten credit insurance on Greensill's SCF receivables; the exposure claimed totals ~$5 billion. TMHCC contests scope and validity of claims. Greensill's use of credit insurance to support financialised receivable structures raises regulatory and underwriting questions. Credit Suisse funds exposed to Greensill write down ~$3bn. Australian government investigation follows. VER
2020COVID-19 — European Government Credit Guarantee Schemes (€180bn)EuropeREGState-guaranteed; private losses limitedCatastrophic
COVID lockdowns threaten to collapse trade credit markets as credit insurers reduce limits. UK Trade Credit Reinsurance scheme (£10bn guarantee), Germany Schutzschirm (up to €30bn), France and Netherlands create similar government backstops. Total EU government guarantee peak ~€180bn. Schemes successfully prevent a credit insurance market failure, but cost taxpayers significant contingent liability. Private market claims contained by government intervention. VER
1998Russian Sovereign Default — GKO MoratoriumRussiaSOV$1bn+ est.Major
Russia suspends GKO (rouble bond) payments and imposes debt moratorium August 1998. Rouble devalues 75%. Trade credit to Russian buyers crystallises in losses; CF claims filed for frustrated export contracts. Private credit market closes Russia limits across the board; ECAs similarly restrict. Recovery of Russian trade credit access took 2–3 years. VER
1999Daewoo Group Insolvency — $78bn Korean Chaebol CollapseKoreaDEFAULT$500m–$1bn est. insuredMajor
Daewoo Group — conglomerate spanning automotive, heavy industry, and trading — files for restructuring with $78 billion of debt. Largest Korean corporate failure. Suppliers across Asia, Europe, and North America with WT credit limits on Daewoo entities face losses. Demonstrates single-name concentration risk in whole turnover policies. VER
2011–12Arab Spring — Libya, Egypt, Syria Contract FrustrationsMENACF$500m–$1.5bn est. (all markets)Major
Arab Spring uprisings (2010–2012) frustrate infrastructure and capital goods export contracts across the MENA region. Libya civil war most acute — contracts abandoned, equipment destroyed, government counterparties collapse. Egypt political instability creates payment uncertainty. Syria sanctions frustrate all remaining contracts. CF claims flow through Lloyd's, ECAs (particularly SACE Italy, HERMES Germany, COFACE France), and the Berne Union network. VER
2012Greek PSI Restructuring — €206bn HaircutGreeceSOV$300–500m est. (WT + single-risk)Major
Greek PSI (Private Sector Involvement) imposes ~53% haircut on €206bn of Greek government bonds — largest sovereign debt restructuring ever. Greek private sector buyer insolvencies surge post-restructuring. Specialist credit insurers significantly reduce Greek credit limits; WT policies tightened for Greek buyers. ECA programmes limited. Private market CR/WT losses estimated $300–500m across the European market. VER
2022Russia/Ukraine War — CF & Sanctions-Triggered Default WaveRussia / UkraineCF$1–2bn est. (all markets)Major
February 2022 Russian invasion triggers SWIFT exclusion of Russian banks, asset freeze, and export embargo. Thousands of export contracts to Russian buyers frustrated. Ukrainian grain, steel, and commodity trade halted. Credit insurers with Russia WT exposure report significant claims 2022–2023. Commodity export frustration from Ukraine generates additional CF losses. Estimated industry-wide losses $1–2bn; actual figures not publicly disclosed. VER
2001–02Enron Bankruptcy — US Energy Sector Credit LossesUSADEFAULT$200–400m est. insuredModerate
Enron Corporation's December 2001 bankruptcy — $63bn assets — generates credit insurance losses for energy sector suppliers with open-account credit exposure to Enron trading and services entities. The rapid collapse of an investment-grade rated company without warning demonstrates the limits of credit rating-based underwriting. Credit insurers' energy sector limit practices scrutinised. VER
2002–03WorldCom / Marconi / Global Crossing — Telecom Credit ClusterUSA / UKDEFAULT$150–300m est. insuredModerate
Dot-com bust triggers cascade of telecom sector insolvencies: WorldCom ($107bn assets, 2002), Global Crossing (2002), and UK's Marconi (2002). Telecom equipment suppliers and service providers with WT credit insurance file significant claims. Concentration of credit insurer exposure to the telecom sector — which had been AAA/AA-rated just years earlier — reveals the speed at which entire sectors can deteriorate. VER
2007–08Iran Sanctions — Contract Frustration & Banking IsolationIranCF$100–300m est.Moderate
Escalating EU and US sanctions on Iran (2007–2012) progressively frustrate export contracts and restrict banking relationships with Iranian buyers. European exporters with existing contracts face non-payment as Iranian banking channels are cut off. CF claims filed across European ECAs and Lloyd's CF lines. SWIFT disconnection (2012) completes the market closure. VER
2015–16Brazil Recession — Petrobras & State Buyer StressBrazilSOV$100–250m est.Moderate
Brazil's deep recession (2015–2016) and Lava Jato (Operation Car Wash) scandal expose widespread corruption in Petrobras supply chains. Sub-contractors and suppliers who had provided goods and services on credit find payments frozen. CR and WT claims rise. Credit insurers tighten Brazil limits; ECA programmes (SACE, UKEF, COFACE) restrict new Brazilian exposure. VER
2016Hanjin Shipping Collapse — Container Market DefaultKoreaDEFAULT$150–250m est. insuredModerate
Hanjin Shipping — Korea's largest container carrier — files for bankruptcy in August 2016 with ~$5bn in debt. Strands 97 vessels at sea, triggering global cargo access crisis. Suppliers with open credit to Hanjin (freight forwarders, port operators, fuel suppliers) file CR claims. Demonstrates how shipping sector insolvency creates multi-sector credit insurance losses. VER
2018–19UK Retail Insolvency Wave — BHS, Carillion, Thomas CookUKDEFAULT£200–400m est. insuredModerate
UK retail and construction sector sees significant insolvency wave 2016–2019: BHS (2016, 11,000 jobs), Carillion (2018, £7bn liabilities), House of Fraser, Debenhams, and Thomas Cook (2019). Suppliers with UK whole turnover policies covering these buyers file WT claims. Demonstrates that developed-market domestic WT policies face concentrated sector risk — particularly retail, hospitality, and construction during periods of structural change. VER
2019Venezuela — State Buyer Moratorium & CF LossesVenezuelaCF$100–200m est.Moderate
Venezuela's progressive economic collapse under Maduro leads to protracted payment delays and ultimate default on state enterprise contracts. PDVSA (state oil company) and other Venezuelan state buyers progressively fail to meet commercial obligations. CF claims filed for frustrated contracts where government action (payment moratorium, currency controls) is the proximate cause. US sanctions complicate loss recovery. VER
2022–23Sri Lanka / Zambia / Pakistan — Multi-Country Emerging Market StressMultipleSOV$200–400m est. (combined)Moderate
Sri Lanka sovereign default (April 2022) — first in its history; Zambia restructures Eurobond debt; Pakistan enters IMF emergency programme. All three markets face buyer distress and state-enterprise payment delays. ECA programmes (UKEF, COFACE, EXIM) and private CR with exposure to state buyers in these markets report claims. G20 Common Framework restructuring process proves slow and contested. VER
2023–24Chinese Property Sector — Evergrande & Supplier Default WaveChinaDEFAULT$300–600m est. insuredModerate
China Evergrande default (2021, ~$330bn debt) triggers cascade of Chinese property sector insolvencies — Sunac China, Country Garden, R&F Properties. Suppliers of construction materials, fittings, and services with CR/WT coverage on Chinese property developer buyers face protracted default losses as developers halt payments to sub-contractors. Credit insurers significantly tighten China property sector limits. VER
2015Tesco Accounting Scandal — UK Retail Credit StressUKDEFAULT£50–100m est.Minor
Tesco's September 2014 revelation of £263m accounting overstatement triggers credit limit restrictions across the UK retail sector. While Tesco itself did not default, the confidence shock led credit insurers to rapidly reduce limits on UK supermarkets and retail sector buyers — affecting suppliers' ability to extend open-account credit. Demonstrates how financial reporting events can trigger rapid credit limit withdrawal even without default. VER
2014Ukraine Crimea Annexation — CF & Export FrustrationUkraine / RussiaCF$50–100m est.Minor
Russian annexation of Crimea (March 2014) and subsequent Donbas conflict frustrate export contracts with Ukrainian and Crimean buyers. EU and US sanctions on Russia create CF event for exporters to Russian entities. Smaller in scale than the 2022 full invasion; credit insurers' Russian exposure significantly higher by 2022 than in 2014. VER
2019Thomas Cook Collapse — UK Tourism Sector DefaultUKDEFAULT£30–70m est. insuredMinor
Thomas Cook Group's September 2019 insolvency — 178-year-old UK travel company; £1.7bn debt — leaves 150,000 UK tourists abroad and collapses the UK's second-largest tour operator. Hospitality, accommodation, and transport suppliers with WT credit on Thomas Cook entities file claims. UK government Operation Matterhorn repatriation costs £83m — no insurance involvement. Credit losses concentrated with European hotel suppliers. VER
2018Carillion Collapse — UK Infrastructure DefaultUKDEFAULT£50–150m est. insuredMinor
Carillion plc liquidation (January 2018) — UK's second-largest construction company; £7bn liabilities. Thousands of sub-contractors and material suppliers left unpaid. Suppliers with WT credit insurance on Carillion entities file claims. Demonstrates systemic risk in construction supply chains and the importance of payment chain credit monitoring. VER
2023Silicon Valley Bank — US Tech Sector Credit DisruptionUSAFIN$50–100m est. insuredMinor
SVB's March 2023 failure creates temporary liquidity crisis among US technology sector start-ups — many had concentrated deposits in SVB. Tech sector suppliers with WT credit on SVB-banking clients face temporary payment delays. FDIC guarantee of all deposits ultimately resolves the credit risk, but the episode created short-term CR uncertainty for trade creditors in the US tech sector. VER
2020Belarus — Post-Election Sanctions CFBelarusCF$20–50m est.Minor
EU and US sanctions imposed on Belarus following disputed 2020 presidential election and subsequent repression generate CF claims for European exporters with Belarusian state-buyer contracts. Smaller market with limited exposure, but illustrative of how democratic-crisis sanctions can trigger CF losses rapidly. EST
2020–21COVID Hospitality Sector Defaults — UK & EuropeEuropeDEFAULT£200m–400m est. (gross, pre-government backstop)Minor
COVID lockdowns trigger widespread hospitality, retail, and non-essential service sector insolvencies. Without government guarantee schemes, WT losses would have been Catastrophic-scale. With UK TCR and European government backstops absorbing the bulk of risk, net insured losses contained to moderate levels. Gross potential loss estimated £200–400m in UK alone; actual insured net loss significantly lower due to government guarantees. VER
2016Nigeria — Currency Crisis & CI/TR LossesNigeriaSOV$30–80m est.Minor
Oil price collapse causes Nigerian foreign exchange crisis; CBN (Central Bank of Nigeria) restricts dollar sales and imposes currency controls. Nigerian buyers unable to convert naira to meet hard-currency trade obligations. CR/CF losses for suppliers with naira-invoiced or dollar-invoiced contracts to Nigerian buyers. Currency inconvertibility/transfer restriction (CI/TR) triggers CF coverage for political risk credit policyholders. EST
2017Qatar Blockade — CF & CI/TR EventQatar / GulfCF$20–50m est.Minor
Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, and Egypt sever diplomatic and trade ties with Qatar in June 2017. Saudi-UAE land border closure disrupts supply chains to Qatar; ships and aircraft avoiding Saudi-blockaded routes incur additional costs. Export contracts to Qatar from neighbouring countries frustrated. CF claims filed on import-prohibition grounds. Blockade lifted January 2021. VER
2019Debenhams / House of Fraser — UK Retail DefaultsUKDEFAULT£40–90m est. insuredMinor
Debenhams and House of Fraser enter administration in 2018–2019, part of the wider UK high street collapse. Suppliers — particularly clothing manufacturers and FMCG companies — with WT credit exposure file claims. Demonstrates the structural vulnerability of traditional retail to e-commerce disruption generating prolonged sector-wide credit deterioration. VER
2022Crypto Sector Collapse — FTX & Credit ExposureGlobalFIN$30–80m est. insuredMinor
FTX exchange collapse (November 2022) triggers cascade of crypto sector insolvencies: BlockFi, Celsius, Genesis. Technology service providers, infrastructure companies, and B2B suppliers to the crypto sector with WT credit coverage file claims. Credit insurers had tightened crypto sector limits through 2021–2022 as market concerns mounted; many exposures had been reduced before collapse. EST

Post-invasion European energy price spike causes financial distress across energy-intensive industries — aluminium, steel, chemicals, paper. Some energy sector buyers face liquidity challenges; credit insurers increase monitoring on energy-intensive buyer segments. Uniper (Germany's largest gas importer) requires government bailout — suppliers with WT exposure on Uniper entities face uncertainty. EST
2016Solvency II — Credit Insurer Capital ReorganisationEUREGN/A — structural eventMinor
Full Solvency II implementation (January 2016) requires EU credit insurers to hold risk-based capital against concentrated credit exposures. Specialist credit insurers — Allianz Trade, Atradius, Coface — adjust portfolio composition and concentration limits in response. Some niche credit markets see capacity withdrawal; others see improved discipline. Pro-cyclical capital requirements remain a structural tension. VER
2024Red Sea Houthi Campaign — Trade Route DisruptionRed Sea / YemenCF$20–60m est. (credit impact only)Minor
Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping (December 2023 onwards) cause significant container shipping delays and cost increases. Delayed shipments create technical payment term breaches; some export contracts with Asian buyers experience prolonged transit times beyond payment terms. CF and CR borderline situations emerge where payment delays are attributable to political-disruption-caused logistics failure. Insurance market debates whether transit delays constitute CF triggers. EST
2024InsurTech Credit Losses — API-Embedded SME CreditEurope / USAFIN$10–30m est.Minor
First generation of API-embedded trade credit insurance products (Hokodo, Nimbla) experience elevated loss ratios as the SME buyer segment proves harder to underwrite on a per-invoice basis than anticipated. Adverse selection remains a challenge — SMEs most likely to seek per-invoice cover are those with buyers showing early distress signals. Reinsurers and Lloyd's capacity providers review delegated authority terms. EST
