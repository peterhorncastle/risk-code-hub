Module 07 — Global Programme

Credit & Contract Frustration — Global Programme

Multinational credit insurance structures, ECA co-insurance frameworks, admitted requirements by jurisdiction, 8-step placement workflow, and an illustrative case study.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

Global credit insurance programmes serve multinational companies with export sales across dozens of countries — requiring a master policy structure covering the parent's global debtor book, with local admitted fronting where required by law, and ECA co-insurance where private market capacity is insufficient. The architecture differs fundamentally from property or liability programmes: credit insurance does not typically require local admitted policies for coverage to be valid — the insured party (the exporter) is in the exporting country, not the buyer's country. Complexity arises from sanctions screening, country-by-country credit limit management, and ECA interaction. EST

CR/CF/WT Global Programme Structures

Global Whole Turnover Master Policy

A single master WT policy covering the multinational exporter's global debtor book — all export sales from all originating entities in all exporting countries. Single insurer (or co-insurance panel) manages all credit limits globally. Country risk limits applied centrally. Premium consolidated at group level. Most efficient structure for large multinational exporters — single renewal, single policy document, central credit management reporting. EST

Coordinated Local Policies

Each country of operation has its own local WT policy (typically with a local affiliate of Allianz Trade, Atradius, or Coface — which all have global networks). Group co-ordination ensures consistent buyer credit limits across all subsidiaries; premium consolidated via master treaty. Useful where local regulatory requirements mandate local policy issuance, or where subsidiaries have distinct debtor books that benefit from separate management. EST

ECA Co-Insurance Structure

For high-risk-country single-risk CR or medium-term capital goods export credit: private market (Lloyd's, Allianz Trade, Atradius, Coface) co-insures alongside the relevant ECA (UKEF, COFACE, HERMES, EXIM). ECA typically takes a proportional share (50–70%) of the risk with private market covering the balance. ECA participation lowers the effective political risk for the private market; private market brings commercial credit assessment expertise. VER

Bank Receivables / SCF Facility

Financial institutions (banks, SCF platforms) purchase trade receivables from exporters; credit insurance underwrites the bank's credit risk on the purchased receivable portfolio. Structure: bank (policyholder) purchases receivables from multiple sellers; credit insurer covers the bank against buyer default on purchased receivables. Lloyd's CR capacity participates via binder/facility with delegated authority to an MGA. EST

8-Step Global Credit Insurance Placement Workflow

  1. Global Debtor Book Analysis: Compile comprehensive receivables data — by buyer name, country of domicile, credit terms, annual sales volume, outstanding balance, and payment history. Segment by geography, sector, and credit quality. Identify top-20 buyers by exposure. Flag buyers in OECD category 4+ countries, buyers with external credit ratings, and buyers where ECA involvement is anticipated.
  2. Sanctions Screening: Screen all buyer entities against OFAC SDN list, EU Consolidated Sanctions List, and OFSI (UK) Financial Sanctions Register. Any match must be excluded from coverage. Flag any buyers in countries subject to comprehensive sanctions regimes (Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Syria, Belarus). No credit insurance can be placed for buyers in sanctioned countries without specific OFAC/HMT authorisation.
  3. Country Risk Assessment: Map buyer portfolio against OECD country risk categories. Identify countries where private market capacity may be insufficient (cat 6–7). Determine where ECA co-insurance or government-backed guarantee is required. Assess ENSO/political risk factor for key markets (identify whether any buyer markets are experiencing IMF programme, FX crisis, or political instability).
  4. Programme Structure Decision: Select between global WT master policy, coordinated local policies, or hybrid. Determine ECA co-insurance requirements for medium-term CR transactions. Agree deductible structure (per-buyer first-loss, annual aggregate, or XL excess). Confirm whether bank receivables facility structure applies if the policyholder uses invoice discounting or factoring.
  5. Market Submission: Prepare risk submission for lead credit insurer or Lloyd's specialty market: debtor book analysis, 3-year bad debt history, credit management procedures documentation, key buyer profiles for top-10 exposures, country breakdown, and previous policy terms. Approach specialist credit insurers (Allianz Trade, Atradius, Coface) for WT lead; Lloyd's for CR/CF/XL layers. Obtain indicative terms from 2–3 markets.
  6. ECA Engagement (if required): For medium-term CR on cat 4+ country buyers: approach UKEF (UK Export Finance) for Buyer Credit or Supplier Credit Guarantee eligibility assessment. Complete UKEF transaction application including buyer information, contract details, and exporter financial information. UKEF due diligence includes anti-corruption assessment (FCPA/UKBA compliance) and environmental/social risk assessment for transactions above OECD threshold values.
  7. Programme Negotiation & Binding: Negotiate final policy terms with lead market: credit limits by buyer, country sub-limits, waiting period, indemnity percentage, premium rate, maximum credit period, discretionary credit limit (DCL), and any special conditions. Confirm XL/stop-loss structure with separate Lloyd's market or reinsurer. Issue policy documentation — master policy, schedule of approved limits, and any endorsements for excluded buyers/countries.
  8. Ongoing Management: Establish quarterly review cycle with insurer — updated buyer credit reports, debtor ageing analysis, new credit limit applications, and any material changes to buyer/country risk profile. Monitor insurer limit decisions — any reduction in credit limits is actionable intelligence. Review premium adjustment at year end based on actual insured turnover vs estimate. Annual renewal: full credit submission update; early start (4–6 months before expiry) for complex global programmes.
Illustrative Case Study

Meridian Engineering Group — Global CR/CF Programme Rebuild After Argentina & Turkey Losses

A UK-headquartered capital goods manufacturer with £220m annual export turnover across 32 countries (35% Western Europe, 25% Asia Pacific, 20% MENA, 10% Latin America, 10% Sub-Saharan Africa) approaches the market for renewal following two significant losses: a £4.2m protracted default on a Turkish private buyer (2022, post-lira devaluation) and a £2.1m CF claim on an Argentine SOE buyer (2023, peso convertibility restrictions). The incumbent credit insurer declined to renew the Argentine and Turkish buyer limits. EST

Programme Challenge Two losses in 24 months (£6.3m gross; £5.1m net of deductible) had produced a 3-year loss ratio of 212% for the incumbent. Turkish buyer limits withdrawn; Argentine market effectively closed to private market insurance. £18m of annual turnover to emerging market SOE buyers in cat 4–5 countries had no replacement coverage. Medium-term capital goods contracts to Vietnamese and Egyptian SOE buyers (£12m combined) also needed CR cover.
Solution Structure Split programme: (1) Western Europe and Asia-Pacific WT placed with Allianz Trade at +28% premium increase — clean book, low loss ratio. (2) MENA and LatAm emerging market exposure placed with Lloyd's specialty CR underwriter — higher rate (+45%), sublimited to £1.5m per buyer, 120-day waiting period. (3) Vietnamese and Egyptian SOE medium-term CR co-insured: UKEF (65%) + Lloyd's CR (35%) per UKEF Buyer Credit Guarantee scheme.
Argentine & Turkish Replacement Argentine SOE exposure: not reinstated by private market — UKEF only market willing to consider at +180% rate. Meridian reduced Argentine credit exposure by 70% (cash-in-advance terms only). Turkey: Atradius reinstated limited Turkish buyer limits at 2.5× previous rate with 15% first-loss deductible increase. Meridian implemented enhanced buyer financial monitoring for Turkish buyers (quarterly financial review vs annual previously).
Outcome & Lessons Total programme premium: £1.85m p.a. vs £0.78m expiring — +137%. Coverage limits on EM buyers reduced by 40% vs pre-loss programme. ECA co-insurance on medium-term CR added £210k p.a. (UKEF premium). Meridian also implemented a standalone CF programme for MENA SOE contracts at Lloyd's — £45k p.a. for £8m of contract value coverage. Key lesson: geographic diversification of export credit exposure and pre-emptive use of ECA cover for high-risk-country buyers significantly reduces dependence on private market during country crisis events.
