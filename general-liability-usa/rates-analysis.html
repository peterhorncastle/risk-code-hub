US GL Market Rate Cycle

US General Liability experienced a dramatic shift from a prolonged soft market to sustained hardening from 2019 onwards, driven by social inflation, nuclear verdict proliferation, PFAS/mass tort reserve strengthening, and withdrawal of capacity from high-hazard segments. Lloyd's E&S market hardened earlier and more sharply than the admitted market, given its disproportionate exposure to the most hazardous risks. EST

2010–2016 — Soft Market

Benign Loss Environment, Competitive Pricing

Low interest rates drove capacity expansion. Social inflation not yet measurable. Rates flat to −5% annually. Lloyd's E&S market competed aggressively on price, particularly for premises and products GL. PCOH underpriced for latent development.

2017–2018 — Early Inflection

Social Inflation Emerges; Rates Begin to Rise

Nuclear verdicts >$10m begin appearing in Florida, Georgia, Texas. Trucking GL losses accelerate. Lloyd's begins non-renewing high-hazard hospitality and construction risks. Rates +5–15% for nuclear verdict classes. Market still broadly competitive for low-hazard risks.

2019–2022 — Hard Market

Capacity Withdrawal, Disciplined Pricing

PFAS mass tort reserves increase sharply. Opioid litigation settles for $50bn+. SAM lookback windows open in major states. Construction GL (especially NY §240) becomes effectively uninsurable in admitted market. Lloyd's E&S rates +20–60% across nuclear verdict segments. High-hazard classes see +80–120%.

2023–2024 — Selective Stabilisation

Rate Adequacy in Some Segments; Sustained Pressure in Others

Low-hazard accounts (professional offices, retail — non-nuclear states): rates flat to +5%. High-hazard (nuclear verdict states, construction, healthcare, trucking): continued +10–25%. Mass tort exclusions fully embedded. E&S capacity still limited for highest-hazard classes. Social inflation trend shows no sign of reversal.

⚠ Pro-Cyclicality Warning: US GL loss development is heavily right-skewed and socially inflated. Reported loss ratios in 2020–2022 appeared manageable in many segments, but IBNR development on nuclear verdict years (particularly 2018–2021) has continued to develop adversely through 2024. Underwriters relying solely on reported loss ratios without IBNR and social inflation development loads are materially under-reserving.

ROE Scenario Analysis — Illustrative £250m US GL Portfolio

Three scenarios modelled for an illustrative Lloyd's syndicate US GL portfolio ($250m GWP, approximately 55% UA / 30% UC / 10% UR / 5% US by premium). Based on 2024 market conditions. ILLUS

Bull Scenario
+14.8%

Loss ratio 58%. Social inflation stabilises. Nuclear verdict frequency plateaus. No new mass tort category emerges. Rate +5% on renewal. IBNR development benign.

Base Scenario
+7.2%

Loss ratio 68%. Social inflation continues at 8% p.a. trend. Nuclear verdicts elevated but not escalating. 2–3 significant mass tort reserve development events. Rate flat to +3%.

Bear Scenario
−12.5%

Loss ratio 88%. New mass tort category emerges (e.g. AI product liability, microplastics). Nuclear verdict frequency accelerates. SAM lookback window expansion triggers major IBNR development. Rate inadequate relative to loss cost.

The base scenario ROE of +7.2% is adequate but below Lloyd's target return of ~12–15% for a class with US GL's loss volatility and long-tail development. This explains continued market discipline on high-hazard segments and capacity restriction in nuclear verdict states. The bear scenario reflects the genuine tail risk of a new mass tort category — which historically has materialised approximately once per decade in the US GL market. EST
