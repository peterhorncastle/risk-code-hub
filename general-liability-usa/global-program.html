US GL Programme Structures

US General Liability programme structure differs materially from other jurisdictions because of the US's state-based insurance regulatory system, mandatory admitted/non-admitted rules, and the sheer scale of potential exposures that drive complex layered placements. Lloyd's participates primarily in the E&S (excess and surplus lines) segment, but also writes admitted US GL through Lloyd's licensed subsidiaries. VER

Standard US Admitted CGL (small-mid market)

Primary CGL $1m/$2m with admitted US carrier. All state filings and regulatory compliance handled by admitted carrier. Lloyd's typically not involved at this layer unless risk declined by admitted market. Umbrella excess layers may involve Lloyd's UC placement.

Lloyd's E&S Primary (US)

Risk declined by admitted market — filed through state-licensed surplus lines broker (Stamford-licensed in most states). Lloyd's writes non-admitted primary CGL on its own form or endorsed ISO CG 00 01. Surplus lines premium tax applies in state of risk domicile (2–5% depending on state). Diligent search documentation required.

Layered Umbrella / Excess Programme

Large commercial risks ($100m+ revenue) typically have primary layer ($1m–$5m) placed with admitted carrier plus umbrella/excess layers. Lloyd's UC participates as follow or lead on excess layers. Structure: primary → umbrella → excess → excess of excess. Each layer may have different carrier(s) and forms.

Multinational DIC/DIL Programme

Difference-in-conditions/difference-in-limits (DIC/DIL) for multinationals with US and international operations. Master policy issued by Lloyd's (or non-admitted carrier) wraps around local admitted policies to provide consistent worldwide cover. Critical for PFAS, products, and completed operations exposures with unclear geography of loss.

Programme Placement Workflow

Eight-step process for placing a complex US GL programme through Lloyd's E&S market. EST

1
Risk Assessment & Operations Analysis — Complete ACORD 125/126, SIC/NAICS code confirmation, state-by-state revenue/payroll breakdown, 5-year loss runs. Identify all referral triggers (PFAS, SAM, NY §240, nuclear verdict states).
2
Admitted Market Diligent Search — Approach admitted market carriers (typically 2–3 minimum). Document declinations or restrictions. Diligent search affidavit required in most states before E&S placement is lawful. Some states have exempt insured lists that bypass this requirement.
3
Programme Structure Design — Determine primary limit, SIR/deductible level, umbrella and excess tower required. State-specific limit requirements reviewed (e.g. some states require minimum limits for liquor licence). TRIA decision documented. NY §240 coverage decision confirmed.
4
Lloyd's E&S Market Submission — Submit MRC (market reform contract) slip to Lloyd's casualty market. Specify UA (primary) and/or UC (umbrella/excess) codes. PFAS exclusion, SAM standalone decision, social inflation load all specified in slip conditions. TPA appointment if high-frequency risk.
5
Lead Underwriter Negotiation — Lloyd's lead syndicate sets terms and conditions. Line of 40–60% typical for lead on primary; 20–30% for excess layers. Coverage counsel review of any non-standard exclusions or endorsements proposed by lead. TRIA offer documented in policy.
6
Following Market / Co-Insurance Placement — Additional lines placed with following syndicates to reach 100%. Excess layers placed with admitted US carriers where available and appropriate. Co-insurance agreement and lead/follow protocol confirmed for large loss notification.
7
Surplus Lines Filing & Tax — Surplus lines broker files policy in state(s) of risk domicile within state-mandated deadline (typically 30–60 days post-bind). Surplus lines premium tax collected and remitted by broker. Multi-state allocation by premium or exposure method.
8
Ongoing Programme Management — Annual mid-term review: new state operations, product changes, subcontractor programme compliance. Quarterly large loss notification to excess carriers. Annual renewal submission: updated loss runs, exposure changes, social inflation development discussion with underwriters.
