US GL Risk Control Framework

US GL risk mitigation is more complex than any other jurisdiction because it must operate across 50 distinct state legal systems, a plaintiff bar that actively develops new loss theories, and a litigation funding industry that provides resources to pursue even marginal claims to trial. The controls below represent best-practice standards for insureds and for underwriters evaluating risk quality. EST

⚠ Nuclear Verdict Mitigation Priority: The single most impactful risk control for high-hazard US GL is venue management — understanding which states and counties carry the highest nuclear verdict risk, and structuring operations, contracts, and litigation strategy to minimise exposure in those venues. No loss control programme eliminates nuclear verdict risk, but jurisdictional strategy can materially reduce frequency.
PREVENT — Loss Prevention Controls

Premises Safety Programme

Written slip-and-fall prevention programme: wet floor protocols, CCTV coverage, incident log, lighting inspection schedule. Third-party premises audit annually for high-footfall retail and hospitality. CCTV footage retention 90 days minimum. Reduces attritional GL frequency by 20–35%.

Product Safety & Quality Control

ISO 9001 or equivalent QMS for all manufactured products. Pre-market product testing documentation. CPSC compliance review for consumer products. Supply chain PFAS and materials audit. Labelling and warning adequacy review (learned intermediary doctrine for B2B products). FDA/USDA compliance for food/pharma products.

Subcontractor Qualification Programme

Mandatory for all construction GC and general contractor risks. Require: certificate of insurance with GC as named AI (CG 20 10/20 37), minimum GL limits of $1m/$2m, annual recertification. Maintain active ACORD certificate tracking system. Unqualified subcontractors: primary exposure falls to GC's GL.

Contractual Liability Management

Review all client, vendor, and property contracts for indemnity provisions that expand GL exposure beyond operations (blanket contractual liability). Identify and cap unlimited indemnity obligations. Construction contracts: Texas and Florida anti-indemnity statutes limit some contractor-to-owner indemnities — separate legal review required.

PFAS / Toxic Chemical Declaration

Annual PFAS material use survey for all manufacturing and industrial operations. Any use of fluorosurfactants, PTFE coatings, AFFF fire suppression, or stain repellents triggers specialist environmental GL review. Full supply chain disclosure to underwriters — concealment of PFAS use may void coverage.

SAM Prevention Programme (Youth-Serving Orgs)

Two-adult rule for all interactions with minors. Background check for all staff and volunteers (FBI/CJIS database). Mandatory reporting policy (all 50 states require reporting to state authorities). Suspicious behaviour reporting hotline. Historical incident review — any known prior incidents must be disclosed at placement.

DETECT — Early Warning & Monitoring

Incident Reporting & Near-Miss Tracking

All incidents reported within 24 hours to risk manager. Near-miss reporting encouraged (no-blame culture). Monthly incident review with operations team. OSHA 300 log maintained (300+ employee businesses). Near-miss frequency is a leading indicator of future GL claims — track trends quarterly.

Product Complaint & Recall Monitoring

Real-time CPSC SaferProducts.gov and FDA MedWatch complaint monitoring for consumer product manufacturers. >3 similar complaints triggers internal product safety review. CPSC voluntary recall protocol — rapid response reduces punitive damages exposure and demonstrates corporate responsibility.

Litigation Funding Intelligence

Monitor litigation funding activity in your industry sector — funded plaintiff campaigns often target specific industries/companies before claims surge. Maintain external legal intelligence service for nuclear verdict states. Identify venue patterns — certain counties (Jefferson County AL, Cook County IL, Broward County FL) have historically elevated verdict rates.

Claims FNOL & Reservation of Rights Monitoring

First notice of loss (FNOL) within 24 hours of any potentially covered event. Large loss notification threshold defined — typically $50k for primary GL. Reservation of rights (ROR) letter issued promptly if coverage question exists. ROR protects insurer's right to later deny coverage — failure to issue timely ROR may waive coverage defences.

RESPOND — Claims Management

Early Intervention & Investigation

Preserve evidence immediately — CCTV footage, product samples, incident scene photographs, witness statements. Litigation hold notice issued on receipt of any demand letter or claim. Scene investigation within 48 hours for serious injuries. Do not admit liability or make unauthorised payments — all statements coordinated with defence counsel.

Defence Counsel Selection (Nuclear Verdict States)

Use specialist defence counsel with nuclear verdict defence experience in relevant jurisdiction. Reptile theory counter-strategy requires specific defence counsel training. Venue analysis — file for change of venue in plaintiff-favourable counties where legally possible. Jury selection expertise critical in high-hazard jurisdictions.

Coverage Counsel & ROR Management

Complex GL coverage disputes (PFAS, SAM, cyber GL, BIPA, NY §240) require specialist coverage counsel separate from defence counsel. Cumis counsel (independent defence counsel at insurer expense) required in many states when insurer has reserved rights. Timely communication between coverage and defence teams essential.

Early Neutral Evaluation / Mediation

Most US GL claims settle before trial — median settlement stage is after discovery but before trial. Early neutral evaluation (ENE) as alternative to full discovery can reduce defence costs 30–50%. Mediation mandatory in many federal courts for cases over $75k. Settlement authority framework must be pre-agreed between insurer and insured.

RECOVER — Post-Loss Actions

Subrogation — Products Liability

US GL subrogation rights against product manufacturers, distributors, and component suppliers can be substantial. Preserve subrogation rights: no unilateral release of third parties, document evidence of product defect, obtain cooperation agreement from insured. Products liability subrogation recovery rate materially higher than premises liability.

Post-Claim Operational Review

Root cause analysis within 30 days of claim resolution — what control failure allowed the loss? Update risk management programme to close identified gaps. Review AI endorsements and subcontractor programmes following AI claims. Share learnings with risk management team without creating privilege issues.

Structured Settlement / Medicare Set-Aside

For serious BI claims involving future medical costs, structured settlements using annuities reduce insurer's total settlement cost. Medicare Set-Aside (MSA) accounts mandatory for settlements involving Medicare-eligible claimants — failure to account for MSA can create federal liability. Engage structured settlement specialist for claims >$500k future medical.

Reputation & Stakeholder Management

Major GL events (product injury, premises catastrophe, nuclear verdict) require coordinated PR response. Silence is not an option — plaintiff bar will own the narrative. Designate crisis communications lead. Distinguish legal admissions (avoid) from empathy (acceptable). Consumer product recalls: proactive communication reduces punitive damages and future litigation volume.

Before / During / After Claim Guidance

Practical guidance for US GL insureds and their brokers. US GL claims management is materially more adversarial and technical than equivalent processes in other jurisdictions. EST

Before a Claim

  1. Maintain complete, current loss runs with closed/open/reserved breakdowns — gaps raise red flags at renewal
  2. Verify AI endorsement language matches contractual obligations — mismatched AI wording is the most common GL coverage dispute
  3. Confirm TRIA offer/rejection documentation is current and on file
  4. Review products list annually — new products require underwriter notification within 60 days
  5. Ensure subcontractor COI tracker is active — lapsed subcontractor insurance = uninsured GC exposure
  6. Review excess/umbrella schedule — gaps between primary and umbrella layers are the most dangerous coverage deficiency
  7. Document all PFAS and SAM disclosure decisions in underwriting file

During a Claim

  1. Notify insurer within policy-required FNOL period (typically "as soon as practicable" — treat as 24–48 hours for serious BI)
  2. Do not admit liability, make statements, or offer payments without insurer consent — violation of cooperation clause may void coverage
  3. Preserve all evidence — CCTV, products, documentation, communications (litigation hold if demand letter received)
  4. Respond to insurer's ROR letter within stated deadline — failure to respond may waive your right to contest
  5. If insurer appoints defence counsel, understand that counsel's primary obligation is to you (the insured) under most state bar rules
  6. Track defence costs separately — under most GL policies, defence costs are outside limits but count toward aggregate in some forms
  7. Notify any umbrella/excess carriers when primary limits are materially eroded (>50% reserved)
  8. Consider early mediation — US GL trials are expensive, unpredictable, and generate publicity
⚠ TRIA Offer Requirement: All Lloyd's US GL policies must contain a formal Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) offer. Insureds who decline TRIA coverage must sign a written rejection. Lloyd's syndicates are required to comply with TRIA as a condition of accessing the US market. TRIA covers certified acts of terrorism — uncertified acts (domestic terrorism, hate crimes) remain on standard GL coverage subject to normal terms.
