1963
Regulatory

Greenman v Yuba Power — Strict Products Liability Established

California Supreme Court (Justice Traynor) establishes strict liability for defective products — manufacturer liable even without negligence. Adopted across most US states by 1970s. Fundamentally expands products GL exposure beyond negligence standard. VER

1966
Regulatory / Market

ISO Modern CGL Form Introduced — Occurrence Basis Standardised

ISO releases standardised CGL form with occurrence-basis trigger, separation of premises/operations from products/completed operations. Becomes the universal US GL template. Lloyd's syndicates increase US GL reinsurance participation as form standardisation improves portfolio management. VER

1973
Environmental

Borel v Fibreboard — First Major Asbestos Verdict; Asbestos Litigation Begins

5th Circuit upholds asbestos personal injury verdict based on failure to warn. Triggers the avalanche of asbestos litigation that would ultimately cost US GL carriers an estimated $70–100bn. No other single tort event has generated comparable insured losses in US history. VER

1982
Environmental

Johns-Manville Chapter 11 — Asbestos Bankruptcy Trust Model Created

Largest asbestos manufacturer files Chapter 11; establishes the asbestos bankruptcy trust model (§524(g) trust) which pays current and future claimants from a funded trust. Template replicated by 100+ asbestos defendants and later by mass tort defendants (opioids, talc, BSA). Lloyd's London absorbs major RI losses. VER

1986
Regulatory

ISO CG 00 01 (1986) — Absolute Pollution Exclusion and Dual Aggregates

New CGL form: absolute pollution exclusion replaces qualified; two separate aggregates (general aggregate + PCOH aggregate); per-occurrence limit. Still the structural basis for most US CGL programmes. The absolute pollution exclusion generates decades of coverage litigation over scope. VER

1994
Products Liability

Liebeck v McDonald's ("Hot Coffee") — Nuclear Verdict Enters Public Consciousness

$2.86m initial verdict (reduced to ~$640k) for scalding hot coffee burn; galvanises tort reform debate; symbolises both the US litigation lottery and the genuine risk that products GL claims can massively exceed expected damages. Motivates state-level punitive damages caps. VER

1998
Mass Tort

Tobacco MSA — $206bn State AG Template Established

All 50 US states settle with four major tobacco manufacturers for $206bn over 25 years — the largest civil litigation settlement in history. Establishes state AG-driven mass tort resolution outside traditional class action. Template directly applied to opioids ($26bn), PFAS, and climate/carbon litigation. VER

2004
Products Liability

Vioxx Withdrawal — $4.85bn Merck GL Settlement

Merck voluntarily withdraws Vioxx (rofecoxib) after cardiovascular risk confirmed; approximately 27,000 lawsuits; $4.85bn settlement (2007). Largest pharmaceutical product liability settlement at that time. Triggers heightened scrutiny of pharmaceutical products GL and post-market surveillance obligations. VER

2006
Market Development

US E&S Market Expansion — Lloyd's Grows Non-Admitted Portfolio

Post-Katrina, admitted carriers restrict high-hazard GL; US E&S market grows to fill capacity gap. Lloyd's expands US GL presence via wholesale broker network (AmWINS, Ryan Specialty); Lloyd's USA platform formalised as coordinating body. US surplus lines GWP grows from ~$30bn to ~$50bn 2005–2010. EST

2010
Environmental

Deepwater Horizon — $65bn Total Loss; Largest US GL/Pollution Event

Macondo well blowout kills 11; $65bn total cost to BP (cleanup, claims, fines). Largest combined GL/pollution loss in history. Tests occurrence trigger for ongoing pollution; establishes contractor/operator GL liability framework for offshore operations. GL and umbrella carriers to BP's contractor chain also impacted. VER

2012
Construction Defect

Chinese Drywall Construction Defect — Multi-State GL Claims Wave

Chinese-manufactured drywall installed 2001–2007 in 100,000+ homes emits hydrogen sulfide; corrodes HVAC and wiring; causes health complaints. Litigation in Florida, Virginia, Alabama. Builder/installer GL and umbrella policies triggered. Illustrates long-tail construction defect exposure and supply chain GL liability. VER

2016
Sexual Abuse / SAM

USA Gymnastics / Michigan State — SAM GL Coverage Tests

Larry Nassar abuse of ~265 female athletes; USA Gymnastics settles for $380m; Michigan State University $500m. Tests GL coverage for institutional SAM: does a CGL policy cover claims against an institution for failing to prevent employee abuse? Coverage litigation turns on expected/intended exclusion and definition of "occurrence." Triggers SAM-specific endorsements and E&S wordings. VER

2018
Mass Tort

Roundup / Bayer-Monsanto — $11.6bn Glyphosate Settlement; Social Inflation Milestone

First Roundup/glyphosate verdict ($289m, later reduced); Bayer acquires Monsanto; faces 160,000+ claims; settles $11.6bn (2020). Tests GL coverage for products with alleged latent disease causation (non-Hodgkin lymphoma). Demonstrates how a single product line can generate catastrophic GL exposure aggregating across hundreds of separate occurrence-year policies. VER

2018
Market Development

Third-Party Litigation Funding (TPLF) Becomes Systemic

US litigation finance market reaches $10bn+; Burford Capital, Fortress, IMF Bentham compete for mass tort dockets. TPLF allows plaintiffs' firms to fund large-scale GL litigation without bearing case costs, dramatically increasing the volume and persistence of mass tort proceedings. Directly correlated with nuclear verdict frequency increase post-2018. EST

2019
Mass Tort

Opioid MDL — $26bn+ National Settlement; Public Nuisance GL Theory

AmerisourceBergen, McKesson, Cardinal Health + J&J national settlement $21bn; Purdue Pharma bankruptcy $6bn Sackler contribution; total opioid industry settlements $50bn+. State AGs successfully employ "public nuisance" as GL liability theory — expanding potential GL trigger to any product causing widespread societal harm. Sets template for climate, PFAS, and future mass tort GL claims. VER

2021
Sexual Abuse / SAM

Boy Scouts of America Bankruptcy — $2.46bn SAM Settlement

BSA files Chapter 11; resolves 82,000+ sexual abuse claims through §524(g) trust for $2.46bn — largest SAM settlement in US history. Establishes that institutional SAM liability can reach bankruptcy-triggering levels. Followed by diocese-level Catholic Church bankruptcies (e.g. Archdiocese of New Orleans $90m). SAM GL exposure becomes a defining E&S underwriting challenge. VER

2022
Environmental / PFAS

3M PFAS Settlement $10.3bn — Largest Environmental GL Settlement

3M settles with US public water systems for $10.3bn (2023 finalisation); DuPont/Chemours $1.185bn; additional state AG cases pending. PFAS testing in public drinking water confirms near-universal contamination. GL insurers begin introducing PFAS exclusions; admitted market largely excludes PFAS; Lloyd's/E&S market faces pricing and accumulation decisions. VER

2023
Mass Tort

J&J Talc — $8.9bn Bankruptcy Plan; "Texas Two-Step" Contested

J&J's LTL Management subsidiary (talc liabilities) bankruptcy plan of $8.9bn for mesothelioma and ovarian cancer claims. Bankruptcy court ultimately rejects plan (3rd Cir. 2021; re-filed 2023). Texas Two-Step divisive merger bankruptcy strategy faces sustained legal challenge from plaintiff bar backed by TPLF. Outcome will determine availability of mass tort resolution via corporate restructuring. VER

2024
Market Development

US E&S Market Surpasses $100bn GWP — Nuclear Verdict Frequency Record High

US excess and surplus lines GWP exceeds $100bn (AM Best data). Swiss Re reports nuclear verdict ($10m+) frequency up 27% 2020–2023 vs prior period; average nuclear verdict $35m+. Lloyd's US UA/UC/US volumes grow; admitted carriers further restrict high-hazard GL classes; E&S market absorbs displaced risks. Social inflation formally recognised as structural cost driver by major carriers. VER

2025
Regulatory

PFAS EPA Maximum Contaminant Levels — Regulatory GL Trigger Activated

EPA finalises maximum contaminant levels (MCLs) for PFOA/PFOS/PFAS in drinking water (April 2024; effective 2026). Creates mandatory compliance remediation obligations for water utilities and PFAS manufacturers with direct GL liability implications. Estimated compliance cost $1.5–3.5bn per year nationally. GL insurers accelerating PFAS exclusion adoption across both admitted and E&S markets. VER
