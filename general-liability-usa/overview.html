What Is US General Liability?

US Commercial General Liability (CGL) is one of the most significant and complex insurance classes in the world. It covers businesses operating in the United States against third-party claims for bodily injury, property damage, personal injury, and advertising injury arising from their operations, products, and premises. The United States tort system — characterised by jury trials, punitive damages, class actions, and plaintiff-friendly litigation funding — makes US GL materially more expensive and volatile than equivalent GL coverage in any other jurisdiction.

Lloyd's occupies a critical niche in the US GL market as an excess and surplus (E&S) lines carrier: when US-admitted insurers decline or are unable to cover a risk, Lloyd's writes coverage on a non-admitted basis in most states. This makes Lloyd's disproportionately exposed to the hardest-to-place risks — emerging exposures (PFAS, cannabis, sexual abuse and molestation), high-hazard industries, and unusual or complex risk profiles that the admitted market cannot accommodate.

UA — General Liability USA (Direct)

Primary commercial general liability (CGL) on ISO CG 00 01 form or equivalent. Covers bodily injury / property damage (Coverage A), personal and advertising injury (Coverage B), and medical payments (Coverage C). Occurrence basis. Includes products-completed operations hazard (PCOH). Written on admitted or E&S surplus lines basis depending on risk profile.

UC — Umbrella & Excess (USA)

Commercial umbrella liability (broad form, drops down when underlying exhausts) and excess liability (follow-form, attaches above underlying limits only). Umbrella extends coverage beyond the underlying CGL, auto, and employer's liability policies. Key distinction: umbrella provides first-dollar coverage for uncovered claims; excess does not. Both codes sit in the UC family.

UR — Reinsurance (USA GL)

Facultative and treaty reinsurance of US GL portfolios. Includes: per-risk facultative XOL for large individual risks; quota share treaties for admitted GL portfolios; aggregate stop-loss; casualty catastrophe (litigation funding / mass tort / class action XOL) treaties. Lloyd's syndicates are significant treaty reinsurance capacity providers to US admitted GL carriers.

US — Surplus Lines (E&S, USA)

Non-admitted excess and surplus lines GL for risks ineligible for the admitted market: high-hazard manufacturers, cannabis operators, PFAS-exposed entities, sexual abuse and molestation (SAM) risks, pyrotechnics, firearms, and other risks the admitted market declines. Lloyd's is the dominant non-admitted E&S carrier in the US via individual state surplus lines licensure.

Social inflation is distinct from economic inflation — it represents a systematic increase in claims severity beyond what economic conditions alone would explain. Lloyd's US GL underwriters must price for social inflation in addition to standard loss trend.

ISO CGL Form Structure (CG 00 01)

The Insurance Services Office (ISO) CGL form is the standard template for US GL. Lloyd's and admitted carriers may use ISO forms, proprietary forms, or manuscript forms for complex risks. All share the following principal coverage sections:

Coverage A

Bodily Injury & Property Damage Liability

  • BI/PD caused by an "occurrence" during the policy period
  • Occurrence = accident, including continuous/repeated exposure
  • Two aggregates: general aggregate + PCOH aggregate
  • Per-occurrence limit applies
  • Expected or intended exclusion (not accidental)
  • Contractual liability exclusion (with exception for insured contracts)
Coverage B

Personal & Advertising Injury Liability

  • False arrest, malicious prosecution, wrongful eviction
  • Libel, slander, defamation of character
  • Invasion of privacy
  • Copyright infringement in advertising
  • Claims-made trigger (despite occurrence-basis Coverage A)
  • Key exclusion: knowing violation, criminal acts
Coverage C

Medical Payments

  • No-fault medical expense payment for BI on insured premises
  • Typical limit: $5,000–$10,000 per person
  • Does not require proof of negligence
  • Claim settlement tool — reduces litigation frequency
PCOH

Products-Completed Operations Hazard

  • Products: BI/PD from goods manufactured/sold/distributed
  • Completed operations: work already performed at other locations
  • Separate aggregate limit from general aggregate
  • Key exposure for manufacturers, contractors, food/beverage
  • Long-tail: claims can arise years after product/work delivery
Umbrella / Excess

UC — Umbrella & Excess Layer (UA/UC distinction)

  • Sits above CGL, auto, and employer's liability
  • True umbrella: broad form with drop-down for uncovered claims
  • Excess: follow-form, no drop-down
  • Limits: $5m–$100m+ for major corporates
  • Critical for managing nuclear verdict exposure
  • Self-insured retention (SIR) replaces deductible in many US programmes
E&S / Surplus Lines

US — Surplus Lines Mechanism

  • Available when admitted market declines (diligent search required)
  • Non-admitted; state-by-state surplus lines licensure
  • Lloyd's: largest single E&S capacity provider in the US
  • Stamping offices: SLSO (California), FSLSO (Florida) etc.
  • No guaranty fund protection for E&S risks
  • Premium tax payable to insured's home state

What Makes US GL Unique

Jury Trials & Punitive Damages

The 7th Amendment right to jury trial in civil cases means US GL claims are determined by lay juries, not judges. Juries award punitive damages in addition to compensatory damages — often multiples of actual loss — to "punish" defendant conduct. No equivalent mechanism exists in most non-US GL markets. Punitive damages are often not insurable in some states. VER

Class Actions & MDL (Multi-District Litigation)

Rule 23 class actions and MDL consolidation allow plaintiffs' attorneys to aggregate thousands of individual claims against a single defendant into one proceeding. This fundamentally changes the economics of GL claims: rather than individual frequency losses, the US system produces occasional catastrophic-severity events. PFAS, opioid, and talc MDLs are current examples. VER

Discovery & Pre-Trial Costs

US civil procedure (FRCP) allows extensive pre-trial discovery — depositions, document production, expert witnesses. Defence costs alone on a significant US GL claim can reach $1–5m before trial. This incentivises settlements (even where the defendant has a strong defence) and drives loss severity above the claimed damages. ALAE (allocated loss adjustment expense) is a major component of US GL loss costs. VER

E&S / Non-Admitted Access

US insurance regulation operates state-by-state. The "diligent search" requirement (attempting admitted market placement first) unlocks the surplus lines market. Lloyd's does not require a state insurance licence — it operates via the Surplus Lines Law Reform Act (SLARA) compliance and individual state approval. No guaranty fund protection applies to E&S risks. VER
