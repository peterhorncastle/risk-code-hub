DIC/DIL Control Framework — PREVENT
Prevent

NatCat PML Modelling (AIR/RMS)

Annual probabilistic NatCat PML modelling using AIR Worldwide Touchstone or RMS Risk Intelligence across all global TIV locations. Map PML by peril against local admitted sublimits to quantify the DIC gap at 1-in-100, 1-in-250 and 1-in-500 return periods. Thailand 2011 was catastrophic for programmes without this analysis. [I10]

Prevent

Annual Local Admitted Policy Review

Review all local admitted policies annually at renewal to identify: new exclusions added since last review, sublimit changes, warranty/condition additions, and any coverage gaps that the DIC must fill. DIC is only as good as the accuracy of its gap analysis. [I2]

Prevent

BEPS Arm's-Length Pricing Documentation

Prepare and maintain transfer pricing documentation for all DIC/DIL premium allocations between group entities. Documentation must demonstrate that premium rates charged to subsidiaries are arm's-length (i.e., consistent with what an unrelated party would pay). KPMG/Deloitte transfer pricing specialists typically engaged. [L4]

Prevent

Admitted Policy Renewal Coordination

Coordinate renewal of local admitted policies and DIC master policy to ensure continuous coverage with no gap period. DIC must attach correctly to the local admitted policy at all times — a gap in local admitted coverage (e.g., lapse on renewal) may leave the DIC without valid primary below it, creating admitted rules compliance and coverage disputes. [I4]

Prevent

Currency Matching — Local vs Master

Align the currency of local admitted policies with master DIC/DIL policy where possible. FX mismatch creates uncertainty at claims stage — local policy pays in local currency, but DIC trigger calculation uses master currency exchange rate on loss date. Consider FX exposure reserves or hedging in programmes with material EM currency exposure.

Prevent

Annual Regulatory Compliance Audit

Conduct annual country-by-country review of admitted insurance rules in all programme jurisdictions. Rules change — particularly in emerging markets. Brazil, India, China, Mexico and Saudi Arabia have issued new non-admitted restrictions in recent years. Non-compliance voids local policy coverage and potentially DIC master trigger. [I3]

DIC/DIL Control Framework — DETECT
Detect

Local Loss Early Notification Protocol

Establish a formal early notification chain for large losses at local level: local risk manager → regional risk coordinator → group risk manager → DIC programme broker → Lloyd's DIC underwriter. DIC claims cannot be quantified until local claim is settled, but early notification enables DIC underwriter to reserve appropriately and appoint DIC-specific adjusters. [I9]

Detect

Catastrophe Response Team Activation

Large multinationals maintain a global emergency response organisation (ERO) that activates on major NatCat events. ERO should include insurance programme notification triggers — when a facility is affected by a NatCat event in a DIC-exposed jurisdiction, automatic DIC programme notification should occur within 72 hours regardless of estimated loss quantum.

Detect

Local Claims Adjuster Appointment

Appoint a bilingual local loss adjuster who is familiar with both the local admitted policy and the master DIC programme. Dual appointment (local + international adjuster) ensures that the DIC gap calculation (what local paid vs what master covers) is performed correctly and documented for DIC claim presentation. [I9]

Detect

DIC/DIL Claims Quantification

Once local adjuster establishes local policy settlement, quantify DIC claim: (a) DIC trigger — what did local policy NOT cover? (b) DIL trigger — what did local policy cover up to sublimit? (c) Master programme standard — what would full coverage provide? The difference is the DIC/DIL claim. Present to Lloyd's DIC underwriter with supporting documentation from local claim file. [I9]

Multinational Programme Annual Review Checklist — 8 Key Items

All local admitted policies listed and reviewed: Complete schedule of local admitted policies in all programme jurisdictions — carrier name, policy number, period, TIV, sublimits, exclusions. Compare each to master DIC standard to identify gaps.

NatCat sublimits vs exposure analysis: For each NatCat-exposed jurisdiction (flood, earthquake, wind), compare local admitted sublimit to AIR/RMS modelled PML at 1-in-100 and 1-in-250. Gaps = DIL top-up requirement.

BI indemnity period matches supply chain recovery time: Review maximum indemnity period (MIP) in local admitted BI policies vs estimated supply chain recovery time. Thailand/Japan showed 6-month MIPs wholly inadequate for 12–24 month recovery. BI DIC top-up required for extended periods.

D&O DIC/DIL terms broader than local: Confirm master D&O DIC/DIL provides US securities coverage, broader entity coverage, and Side C where required for US-listed entities. Check local D&O policies do not contain conditions that would prevent DIC trigger.

Premium allocation BEPS-compliant: Verify that DIC/DIL premium allocations to subsidiaries are documented as arm's-length. Transfer pricing benchmark analysis updated annually. Local tax counsel in major jurisdictions reviewed the allocation methodology.

Claims cooperation clause in master and all locals: Verify that all local admitted policies contain a claims cooperation clause requiring the local insurer to cooperate with the DIC master insurer's adjusters. "Follow-the-settlements" clause in master policy confirmed. [I4]

Currency risk addressed: FX exposure between local policy currency and master policy currency reviewed. Material mismatches flagged to risk manager and DIC underwriter. FX hedging or currency endorsement considered for programmes with significant EM currency exposure.

Renewal date alignment: All local admitted policies aligned to single renewal date where possible, coordinated with master DIC renewal. Avoid situations where local policies lapse before master renewal, creating gaps in the primary layer below DIC.

BEPS Warning — Transfer Pricing for Intra-Group Insurance: OECD Base Erosion & Profit Shifting (BEPS) Actions 8–10 require that all intra-group insurance transactions — including DIC/DIL premium charged by the parent to operating subsidiaries — be priced at arm's length. Tax authorities in France, Germany, Australia, and others have challenged premium deductions where the arm's-length basis is not adequately documented. Consequences of non-compliance include: premium deduction disallowance (corporate tax cost), penalties, and potential structural disruption of the programme. Engage specialist transfer pricing counsel annually for multinational DIC/DIL programmes exceeding $1m total premium. [L4]

Non-Admitted Insurance Restrictions — Key DIC/DIL Markets

Markets where DIC/DIL master policy cannot be placed directly with Lloyd's non-admitted — fronting or locally-admitted structure required:

Brazil
Highly restrictive — SUSEP approval required; local fronting mandatory. IRB reinsurance via fronter. [L9]
China
Strict — PICC/Ping An admitted required; Lloyd's only via RI or Shanghai FTZ. [L8]
India
GIC Re involvement; fronting required; Lloyd's India office limited access.
Mexico
CNSF restricts non-admitted; Mexican admitted fronter required for direct risks.
Argentina
SSN prohibits most non-admitted placements; local carrier mandatory.
Russia
CBR-licensed carrier required (+ sanctions/war complications post-2022).
South Africa
FSCA restricts non-admitted; SARB approval needed; Lloyd's limited access.
Saudi Arabia
SAMA-licensed insurer required; non-admitted prohibited for Saudi risks.
USA
Surplus lines non-admitted generally permitted for large/complex risks under NRRA 2010. [L2]
EU (post-Brexit)
Lloyd's Europe (Brussels) required for direct placement; non-admitted from UK complicated. [L3]
Singapore
MAS permits non-admitted for sophisticated/large commercial risks; DIFC equivalent.
UAE (DIFC)
DIFC financial zone permits Lloyd's direct access — most open Gulf market.
Australia
APRA non-admitted permitted for eligible risks; Lloyd's has strong AU market access.
Japan
FSA permits non-admitted for specific large/complex risks; Lloyd's has Japan licence.
Canada
Provincial surplus lines non-admitted generally available; OSFI oversight of Lloyd's.

Top 7 Actions for DIC/DIL Programme Insureds

1

Commission a full TIV revaluation every 3 years. TIV undervaluation is the single most common cause of DIC/DIL inadequacy. Use independent RICS/Marshall Valuation Service appraisal for all major locations — Thai and Japanese factories in particular. Inflation since 2020 has rendered many pre-COVID valuations severely inadequate.

2

Run NatCat PML modelling annually using AIR/RMS. Map your NatCat PML (1-in-100 and 1-in-250) against your local admitted sublimits country by country. The gap is your DIC/DIL requirement — quantify it before your underwriter does.

3

Extend BI indemnity period to match actual recovery time. Use supply chain recovery data from your operations team to determine realistic BI indemnity requirements. If your supply chain takes 18 months to recover from a major NatCat event, your BI DIC must provide at least 18 months.

4

Ensure follow-the-settlements clause in all master and local policies. Without this, the DIC underwriter can dispute every local settlement. With it, the DIC claim flows automatically once local claim is settled. This single clause is the most important DIC/DIL claims management tool. [I9]

5

Review BEPS transfer pricing documentation annually. Engage specialist counsel; update arm's-length benchmarks each year. Tax authority challenge of premium deductions is increasing — being audit-ready before a challenge is received is significantly less costly than responding reactively. [L4]

6

Map all jurisdictions for non-admitted compliance. Use the red/amber/green country framework to identify where fronting structures are required. Ensure fronting agreements are renewed and reinsurance treaties in place before the admitted policy is issued. Do not rely on expired fronting arrangements.

7

Appoint a dedicated multinational programme broker and review annually. Experienced multinational brokers (Marsh Global Specialties, Aon Global Broking, WTW) bring network relationships, admitted rules knowledge and claims coordination capability that generalist brokers lack. Programme complexity demands specialist expertise. [I2]
