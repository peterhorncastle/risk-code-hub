Underwriting Methodology

Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Total Insured Value (TIV) Across Global Portfolio

The primary exposure measure. DIC/DIL premium is fundamentally a function of the global TIV across all insured locations, weighted by the DIC/DIL exposure at each location (i.e., the gap between local admitted policy terms/limits and master programme standard). Accurate TIV declaration is essential — undervaluation is the most common cause of DIC/DIL inadequacy. EST

Base rate: 0.01–0.06% of global TIV for property DIC/DIL depending on NatCat and DIC scope
High Impact

NatCat Exposure — PML Modelling Essential

For property DIC/DIL, NatCat probable maximum loss (PML) modelling using AIR Worldwide or RMS is non-negotiable. Flood, earthquake and windstorm exposures by geography must be mapped against local admitted policy sublimits to quantify the DIC/DIL gap. Thailand 2011 and Harvey 2017 demonstrated the catastrophic cost of unmodelled NatCat accumulation in DIC books. EST

NatCat loading: 0.5× to 3.0× depending on concentration of TIV in high-hazard zones
High Impact

Local Admitted Policy Terms — What DIC Must Fill

The scope of DIC coverage is entirely determined by what the local admitted policies exclude or sublimit. Underwriters must review local policy terms in every jurisdiction to identify: (a) perils excluded locally but covered by master, (b) sublimits below master standard, and (c) conditions/warranties in local policies that may create additional coverage gaps. EST

DIC scope wider = higher risk; pure DIL (limit top-up only) = lower risk and lower rate
High Impact

Jurisdictions Covered — Number and Country Risk

Programme complexity and admitted rules compliance risk increase with number of jurisdictions. High-risk jurisdictions (BRIC, Southeast Asia, Latin America) require fronting or reinsurance structures that create additional friction and coverage gaps. Each country adds admitted rules analysis, local policy review, and claims coordination complexity. EST

Loading: +5–10% per high-risk jurisdiction added; regulatory compliance costs are standalone
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

DIC vs DIL Split

DIC (covering perils the local insurer declined) is inherently riskier than pure DIL (topping up a limit for a peril the local insurer accepted). A programme that is predominantly DIL (limit top-up for a well-covered peril) attracts significantly lower rates than one requiring DIC for excluded perils such as flood in Thailand or earthquake in Japan. EST

Pure DIL programme: −20 to −35% vs DIC + DIL combined
Medium Impact

BI Period and Supply Chain Complexity

Business interruption DIC/DIL is increasingly significant and materially more complex than property DIC/DIL. Thailand 2011 and Japan 2011 demonstrated that BI recovery periods of 12–24 months vastly exceed local policy indemnity period sublimits (often 6 months). Supply chain mapping is essential — the CBI DIC exposure can exceed direct property DIC exposure. EST

BI DIC/DIL: typically rated separately from property DIC at 15–25% additional premium
Medium Impact

Admitted Policy Quality — Local Carrier Strength

Where local admitted policies are placed with financially strong, reputable carriers (Zurich, AXA, Tokio Marine local subsidiaries), the DIC programme operates with confidence that local policies will pay correctly and promptly. Where local carriers are financially weak or of uncertain quality, there is risk of local policy non-performance, which could inadvertently trigger DIC claims. EST

Loading: +10–20% where local admitted carriers are non-investment-grade or state-controlled in weak markets
Medium Impact

Claims Coordination Complexity

DIC/DIL claims require precise coordination between the local admitted insurer and the DIC master underwriter. The DIC underwriter must obtain: (a) evidence of local policy response (or declination), (b) local claim quantum, (c) agreement on DIC trigger mechanism. Follow-the-settlements clauses in master policies are essential — without them, DIC litigation risk is high. VER

Claims coordination complexity loading: +5–15% for programmes without established claims protocols
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

BEPS Compliance — Transfer Pricing Documentation

Well-documented arm's-length premium allocation between parent and subsidiaries reduces tax authority challenge risk. Poor BEPS compliance does not directly affect DIC coverage but creates premium disallowance risk that may lead to mid-term programme restructuring. BEPS compliance is increasingly a broker/buyer pre-placement checklist item. VER

Lower Impact

Broker / Programme Manager Experience

Experienced multinational programme brokers (Marsh, Aon, WTW multinational divisions) bring strong local network relationships, admitted rules knowledge, and programme management capability. Less experienced brokers increase programme gaps risk — local policies may not align precisely with master terms, creating inadvertent DIC/DIL disputes at claims stage.

Lower Impact

Currency of Local vs Master Policy

Currency mismatch between local admitted policy (e.g., THB Thai baht) and master DIC/DIL policy (USD or GBP) creates FX risk at claims stage. Post-loss depreciation of local currency (common in disaster-affected emerging markets) can reduce the local policy payout in master currency terms, affecting DIL attachment calculation. Currency matching is best practice.

Lower Impact

Regulatory Inspection History

A track record of regulatory compliance — no adverse findings from local regulators, no non-admitted violations, no premium tax disputes — supports a clean programme structure. Regulatory violations in any jurisdiction can void local policy coverage, inadvertently triggering DIC claims on the master and creating insurer/insured disputes.

DIC Rate Derivation Note: Unlike most insurance classes, DIC/DIL has no independent exposure base — its exposure is entirely defined by the gap between local admitted policy terms/limits and master programme standard. This makes DIC rating inherently dependent on a full review of all local admitted policies in the programme. Underwriters who skip local policy review are pricing blindly. The "5–15% of admitted premium" heuristic for pure DIL is widely used as a first-pass estimate but should always be validated against actual sublimit gaps and PML modelling for NatCat-exposed programmes. EST
NatCat Accumulation Risk: The Thailand floods 2011 demonstrated that DIC underwriters face correlated aggregate losses — multiple policyholders with the same DIC trigger (Thai flood exclusion) triggering simultaneously. Unlike single-risk insurance, DIC accumulation across a syndicate's book can produce catastrophic aggregate losses in a single NatCat event. PML modelling at the portfolio level, not just per risk, is essential for NatCat DIC underwriting. Lloyd's requires syndicate-level NatCat DIC aggregate monitoring. VER
