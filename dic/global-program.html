Jurisdiction Case Studies — 6 Key Markets

USA — Surplus Lines Non-Admitted

The Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act 2010 (NRRA) streamlined US surplus lines placement — Lloyd's DIC master policies are placed as surplus lines in the insured's home state only, simplifying multi-state programmes. The US is the largest single market for Lloyd's DIC/DIL — flood exclusion in admitted commercial policies creates a structural DIC market. US property DIC for flood and NatCat sublimit top-up represents c.30% of the total Lloyd's DC book by premium estimate. [L2]

China — Fronting / Reinsurance Only

China's CBIRC (formerly CIRC) prohibits direct non-admitted insurance placement for Chinese domestic risks. Foreign multinationals with Chinese factories must use PICC, Ping An, CPIC or other CBIRC-licensed carriers for local admitted policies. Lloyd's can participate only as reinsurer (behind a Chinese fronter) or through the Shanghai Free Trade Zone where Lloyd's has special access permissions. Claims coordination between Chinese admitted policies and Lloyd's DIC RI is complex and slow. The Tianjin explosion (2015) exposed the structural difficulties in this market. [L8]

India — GIC-Re Involvement Required

India's IRDAI requires that most commercial insurance risks include a mandatory 5% cession to GIC-Re (General Insurance Corporation of India). Lloyd's India has a limited licence but DIC/DIL master programmes for Indian risks typically require an Indian-admitted fronter (New India Assurance, United India) with Lloyd's participating as reinsurer. The mandatory cession complicates programme structure and premium allocation. IRDAI is gradually liberalising but India remains a complex DIC/DIL market.

Brazil — SUSEP / IRB Restriction

Brazil has the most restrictive non-admitted rules in Latin America. SUSEP Circular No. 563/2017 tightened the framework for non-admitted reinsurance and fronting structures. All Brazilian risks must be insured with SUSEP-licensed admitted carriers. Lloyd's accesses Brazilian DIC/DIL risk through reinsurance agreements with Brazilian admitted fronters (e.g., Tokio Marine Seguros Brasil, Porto Seguro). IRB (Instituto de Resseguros do Brasil) historically had mandatory reinsurance involvement — this has been modified but remains a consideration. [L9]

EU — Post-Brexit Lloyd's Europe

Brexit created a structural access challenge for Lloyd's in the EU27. Prior to 2021, Lloyd's syndicates could passport into EU member states. Post-Brexit, direct non-admitted placement from London is no longer available for EU risks in the same way. Lloyd's Europe S.A. (Brussels) was established to maintain EU market access — EU DIC/DIL risks are now placed through Lloyd's Europe which reinsures to Lloyd's syndicates. Solvency II harmonisation means EU member states' rules are broadly consistent — but premium tax, local policy language and regulatory supervision requirements vary country by country. [L3]

UAE (DIFC) — Special Financial Zone Access

The Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) operates as a separate financial jurisdiction within the UAE, regulated by the Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA). Lloyd's has direct access to DIFC risks without the non-admitted restrictions that apply to onshore UAE (regulated by the UAE Insurance Authority). For Gulf-region multinationals, DIFC-based holding companies are frequently used to structure DIC/DIL master policies, providing a compliant access point for the wider Gulf region programme. Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) offers a similar structure.

Programme Structure: A global pharmaceutical manufacturer with operations in 40 countries places 35 local admitted policies (5 countries have no admitted requirement or use EU/UK policies that are adequate). Local admitted policies are placed with regional carriers — AXA, Allianz, Tokio Marine, and local national insurers in emerging markets. All local policies are reviewed annually by the multinational broker programme team against the master DIC standard.

NatCat Sublimit DIC Requirement: The pharma company has significant API manufacturing in Japan (earthquake) and significant packaging operations in Southeast Asia including Thailand (flood). NatCat PML modelling (AIR) identifies: Japan EQ 1-in-250 PML $380m vs local Jishin Hoken separate product coverage of $50m per event; Thailand flood 1-in-100 PML $120m vs local admitted flood sublimit $10m. Lloyd's DC master policy provides: Japan EQ DIC ($330m excess $50m), Thailand flood DIC ($110m excess $10m), and NatCat DIL top-up for all other markets.

BI and Supply Chain DIC: The pharma company's supply chain recovery time for a major API manufacturing site is 18–24 months (regulatory re-approval of API batches adds 6–12 months to physical rebuild). Local admitted BI policies have 6-month MIPs. The DIC master provides 18-month BI DIC extension across all markets, and CBI DIC cover for key single-source API suppliers.

Financial Lines DIC: The company is US-listed (NYSE). Its local D&O policies exclude US securities class action coverage. The Lloyd's master D&O DIC/DIL provides US securities coverage (Side C and ABC coverage), plus top-up to £100m master D&O limit above all local policy limits averaging £8m.

BEPS and Transfer Pricing: All DIC/DIL premium is allocated to subsidiaries based on a weighted TIV and exposure analysis, documented in an annual transfer pricing report prepared by Deloitte. The arm's-length benchmark uses comparable multinational programme rates from market surveys (WTW, Aon). Three EU jurisdictions have received preliminary tax authority queries on premium deductions — all resolved with documentation provided. [L4]

Claims Coordination: All local admitted policies contain a "claims cooperation and follow-the-settlements" clause requiring the local insurer to: (a) notify the DIC master broker within 72 hours of any loss exceeding $500k, (b) provide full claim file access to the DIC adjuster, and (c) cooperate with DIC-specific PD and BI quantification. In the event of a DIC trigger, the master DIC underwriter is co-appointed at the claim adjuster stage. [I9]

"Follow-the-Settlements" Clause — The Single Most Important DIC/DIL Claims Provision: A follow-the-settlements clause in the DIC/DIL master policy requires the master insurer to follow any payment made by the local admitted insurer, provided the loss is within the scope of the master policy and the local settlement is not fraudulent. Without this clause, the DIC underwriter can independently re-adjudicate the local loss — leading to protracted disputes. With it, once the local claim is settled, the DIC claim flows automatically. Ensure this clause is present in every DIC master policy and that it is reciprocally referenced in local admitted policies via the claims cooperation clause. [I4]
