Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Insurance Act 2015 (UK) — Duty of Fair Presentation

    Insurance Act 2015 (c. 4). Applies to all commercial insurance placements at Lloyd's including DIC/DIL master policies. Requires the insured to make a fair presentation of all material circumstances — particularly important for DIC/DIL where the insured must disclose the terms of all underlying local admitted policies, NatCat exposures, and prior losses. Proportionate remedies for breach replace the old "all-or-nothing" avoidance under the Marine Insurance Act 1906.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act 2010 (NRRA) — USA

    Pub. L. 111-203, Title V (Dodd-Frank Act, 2010). Streamlines US surplus lines placement by establishing the home state of the insured as the sole regulatory jurisdiction for non-admitted insurance. Applies to Lloyd's DC placements for US-domiciled multinationals — DIC/DIL master policies placed as surplus lines in the insured's home state only, eliminating the need for compliance in all states where the insured has operations. Available at: congress.gov.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL EU Solvency II Directive 2009/138/EC — Cross-Border Insurance

    Directive 2009/138/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 November 2009 on the taking-up and pursuit of the business of Insurance and Reinsurance. Harmonises insurance regulation across EU27. Post-Brexit, UK (Lloyd's) no longer benefits from EU passport — DIC/DIL master policies for EU risks must be placed through Lloyd's Europe S.A. (Brussels) or under freedom-of-services arrangements where available. EEA cross-border provisions under Articles 14–55.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL OECD BEPS Action Plan 8–10 — Arm's-Length Pricing for Related Party Transactions

    OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Project, Actions 8–10: Aligning Transfer Pricing Outcomes with Value Creation (OECD Publishing, 2015). Establishes that intra-group insurance transactions — including DIC/DIL premium charged by parent to subsidiaries — must be priced at arm's length. Requires robust transfer pricing documentation annually. Tax authorities in OECD member states increasingly audit multinational programme premium allocations. Available at: oecd.org/tax/beps.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL UK Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA) — Cross-Border Insurance Permissions

    Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (c. 8). Part IV: Permission to carry on regulated activities. Lloyd's syndicates' authorisation to carry on insurance business derives from FSMA 2000 and Lloyd's regulatory framework under Part XIX. Cross-border DIC/DIL placements at Lloyd's for non-UK risks rely on the FSMA authorisation framework and applicable local permissions in each jurisdiction.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Lloyd's Global Accounts Premium (GAP) / Overseas Market Information System (OMIS)

    Lloyd's regulatory framework for non-admitted insurance placements. The Overseas Market Information System (OMIS) provides country-by-country guidance on non-admitted insurance permissions for Lloyd's syndicates. Global Accounts Premium (GAP) rules govern the allocation of premium between Lloyd's and locally-admitted markets where applicable. Market Bulletin Y5282: Lloyd's non-admitted placement guidance. Available to Lloyd's market participants at lloyds.com/market-resources.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL EU Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) 2016/97 — Intermediary Obligations

    Directive (EU) 2016/97 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 January 2016 on insurance distribution. Applies to multinational programme brokers placing DIC/DIL business for EU-based corporate clients. Requires product oversight and governance (POG) requirements, disclosure obligations, and training standards for brokers distributing insurance products to EU corporate clients. Relevant for DIC/DIL brokers with EU multinational clients.

  • [L8]
    LEGAL Chinese Insurance Law (as amended) — CBIRC Licensing Requirements

    People's Republic of China Insurance Law (2015 revision); CBIRC (China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission — formerly CIRC) regulations on insurance company licensing. Direct insurance of Chinese domestic risks is restricted to CBIRC-licensed admitted carriers. Lloyd's participation in Chinese risk is limited to reinsurance of admitted carriers or direct placement through the Shanghai Free Trade Zone (FTZ) pilot programme under CBIRC approval. Lloyd's Shanghai branch established 2018 under FTZ rules.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL Brazilian Circular SUSEP No. 563/2017 — Reinsurance and Non-Admitted Rules

    Circular SUSEP No. 563 of 24 November 2017 (Superintendência de Seguros Privados). Updates the Brazilian regulatory framework for reinsurance and non-admitted insurance. Reinforces that all Brazilian insurance risks must be covered by SUSEP-licensed admitted insurers. Non-admitted placement prohibited for Brazilian domestic risks. Lloyd's access to Brazilian DIC/DIL risk is exclusively through SUSEP-licensed fronting carriers placing reinsurance to Lloyd's. Available at: susep.gov.br.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY AON — Global Risk Management Survey 2023

    Aon plc, Global Risk Management Survey 2023. Published biennially — major survey of risk managers at multinational corporations globally. Documents prevalence of multinational insurance programme structures including DIC/DIL: approximately 70% of Fortune 500-equivalent multinationals use a structured multinational programme with master DIC/DIL element. Key reference for multinational programme market sizing. Available at: aon.com/risk-management-survey.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY Marsh — Multinational Insurance Programme Guide 2024

    Marsh & McLennan, Multinational Insurance Programme Guide: Navigating DIC/DIL, Admitted Rules and BEPS in the Global Insurance Market (2024 edition). Marsh multinational practice publication. Covers: CMP vs fronting vs non-admitted structure comparison; BEPS transfer pricing requirements for premium allocations; country-by-country non-admitted rules summary; DIC/DIL rate derivation methodology. Regarded as the practitioner reference guide for multinational programme broking. Available to Marsh clients.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY Swiss Re — Admitted vs Non-Admitted Insurance: Global Rules 2022

    Swiss Re Institute, Admitted vs Non-Admitted Insurance: A Guide to Global Regulatory Requirements (Swiss Re, 2022). Comprehensive country-by-country survey of admitted insurance rules in 60+ markets. Documents non-admitted restrictions, premium tax implications, and fronting requirements in key DIC/DIL markets. An essential reference for multinational programme structure compliance. Available at: swissre.com/institute.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's — DC Code Market Bulletin and Multinational Programme Guide

    Lloyd's Market Association (LMA), Multinational Insurance Programme Practice Note; Lloyd's Market Bulletins on DC code risk categorisation and programme structure requirements. LMA5399: Multinational Programme Claims Cooperation and Follow-the-Settlements Guidance (2019). Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y5282: Non-admitted placement permissions and GAP rules for DC placements. Available at lloyds.com/market-resources and lma.org.uk.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY KPMG — Transfer Pricing for Insurance Captives and Related Parties 2023

    KPMG LLP, Transfer Pricing for Captive Insurance and Multinational Programme Premium Allocations: A Practical Guide (KPMG, 2023). Covers: arm's-length benchmark methodology for DIC/DIL premium allocation; documentation requirements under BEPS Action 13 Country-by-Country Reporting; tax authority challenge scenarios; best practice premium allocation models for multinational DIC/DIL programmes. Available at kpmg.com/tax/transfer-pricing.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY WillisTowersWatson — Multinational Programme Benchmarking Survey 2023

    WTW (WillisTowersWatson), Global Multinational Insurance Programme Benchmarking Survey 2023. Annual survey of multinational programme structures, premium rates, DIC/DIL scope, and market cycle position. Documents NatCat hardening 2017–2022 and its impact on DIC/DIL sublimit availability and pricing. Key rate benchmarking reference for DC underwriters. Available to WTW clients at wtwco.com/multinational.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY FERMA — European Risk Management Survey 2023

    Federation of European Risk Management Associations, European Risk Management Survey 2023. Documents multinational programme prevalence among European corporate risk managers: 62% of large European corporates (revenue >€1bn) use a structured multinational DIC/DIL programme. Hurricane Sandy, COVID BI interface, and post-Brexit Lloyd's access are identified as key programme management concerns. Available at ferma.eu/research.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY InsuResilience — Developing Country Admitted Insurance Framework Challenges

    InsuResilience Global Partnership (G7/G20), State of Disaster Risk Finance and Insurance in Developing Countries (2022). Documents the challenges of admitted insurance market adequacy in developing countries — inadequate NatCat sublimits, limited admitted market capacity, and the role of DIC/DIL master policies in filling coverage gaps for multinationals investing in emerging markets. Key reference for understanding why DIC triggers are concentrated in developing-country admitted markets.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY IUA — London Market Multinational Programme Practice Note

    International Underwriting Association (IUA), London Market Multinational Programme Practice Note (2021). Covers claims cooperation clauses, follow-the-settlements provisions, DIC/DIL trigger documentation requirements, and best practice for local adjuster appointment in DIC/DIL claims. Essential claims management reference for Lloyd's DC underwriters and multinational programme claims handlers. Available at iua.co.uk/publications.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY Thailand Floods 2011 — Swiss Re Sigma Claims Analysis

    Swiss Re, Sigma No. 2/2012: Natural Catastrophes and Man-Made Disasters in 2011. Documents the Thailand floods as the most costly flood event in insurance history to that date: $20bn+ insured losses, primarily from industrial estate flooding north of Bangkok. Analysis of DIC/DIL trigger patterns — local Thai admitted policies excluding flood, Lloyd's DIC master policies triggered en masse. Established as the defining DIC/DIL loss event and the benchmark for NatCat DIC accumulation modelling. Available at swissre.com/sigma.
