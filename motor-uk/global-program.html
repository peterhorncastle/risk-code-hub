Multinational Motor Fleet Programme Architecture

Large multinational corporations with vehicle fleets across multiple countries require coordinated motor insurance programmes. UK (M5) often forms the master programme jurisdiction where the UK fleet is the largest or the programme is placed at Lloyd's. International fleet programmes involve local admitted policies in each operating territory, with a master policy (typically non-admitted) providing difference-in-conditions/limits (DIC/DIL) cover above local policies.

Captive and Self-Insurance Structures

When Does a Captive Make Sense for Motor Fleet?

Large fleet operators — typically 500+ vehicles with stable, predictable loss histories — may benefit from a captive reinsurance structure where the frequency tier of the fleet programme is retained in a captive insurance company (typically Guernsey, Isle of Man, Cayman, Bermuda or Luxembourg for UK/European multinationals). The captive writes a reinsurance of the Lloyd's fronting policy, retaining frequency claims and ceding severe losses above the captive's retention to the external market.

  • Benefit 1 — Cost reduction: Frequency claims (high volume, low-value) are retained in-house at cost. Premium paid to external market covers only severity — lower overall cost where loss experience is favourable. ILLUS
  • Benefit 2 — Cashflow: Captive retains float on claim reserves; investment income belongs to the group rather than the insurer. For very large fleets, this is material.
  • Benefit 3 — Alignment: Captive ownership aligns fleet management incentives — the fleet operator directly bears cost of poor driving. Supports investment in telematics and training programmes.
  • Requirement: Fronting insurer (typically Lloyd's syndicate) issues admitted policy; captive reinsures. Lloyd's acting as fronting insurer for motor captive arrangements requires regulatory sign-off (PRA/FCA); Lloyd's market has established fronting protocols. VER
  • Minimum size for viability: Generally accepted that captive motor programmes become economical above ~£3m annual premium retention. Smaller fleets better served by deductible buydown and risk management. EST

Green Card System — EU/EEA Territorial Coverage

UK motor policies covering EU/EEA driving use the Green Card system — an international certificate of insurance enabling vehicles to travel and be covered for statutory third-party liability in signatory countries. Post-Brexit, UK insurers issue Green Cards separately from the UK certificate of insurance for EU travel. VER Fleet programmes regularly covering EU territory should have pre-arranged Green Card issuance protocols with their insurer — typically digital/electronic Green Cards now accepted in most EEA states. For large fleets with frequent EU cross-border operations, a blanket territorial extension to the fleet policy (covering EU under UK policy rather than requiring individual Green Cards) may be preferable and is available from Lloyd's market.
