Lloyd's Risk Codes — M2 to M6

Five Lloyd's risk codes cover UK motor insurance, ranging from personal-lines private car through commercial fleets and specialist motor trade. All classes are subject to compulsory minimum third-party liability under the Road Traffic Act 1988 and FCA regulation. Lloyd's syndicates focus on non-standard and specialist segments where standard market pricing is inadequate.

Code Class Scope Policy Basis Typical Limits
M2 Private Motor Private cars and light vehicles — personal lines, agreed/market value, classic and high-performance cars, non-standard risks Annual; comprehensive / TPFT / TPO; SDP, SDP+C, or business use Unlimited TPL (RTA); vehicle value up to £500k+ (specialist)
M3 Commercial Motor Light commercial vehicles, vans, HGV, specialist vehicles, courier fleets, ride-hail operators Annual; goods-in-transit add-on; any-driver / named-driver Unlimited TPL; vehicle/cargo values to £5m+
M4 Motorcycle Motorcycles, mopeds, scooters, e-bikes (where classified), racing and track-day bikes Annual; comprehensive / TPFT / TPO; laid-up option Unlimited TPL; bike value to £100k+
M5 Motor Fleet Corporate fleets (typically 10+ vehicles), hire/rental fleets, chauffeur fleets, corporate travel programmes Annual fleet policy; any-driver or named-driver; blanket or scheduled vehicles Unlimited TPL; fleet values to £50m+
M6 Motor Trade Motor trade road risks and combined; dealer lots, repairers, recovery operators, vehicle testing — covering vehicles in custody/control Road risks only or combined; blanket cover for vehicles in trade Unlimited TPL; premises and trade stock to £10m+

Coverage Overview

UK motor insurance is legally stratified into three coverage tiers by the Road Traffic Act 1988. Lloyd's participates primarily in comprehensive and specialist segments where technical underwriting adds value. The compulsory minimum — Third Party Only — is largely a commodity market dominated by direct insurers.

Comprehensive (Fully Comp)

  • Own damage (accidental, malicious, weather)
  • Third-party property damage and bodily injury
  • Theft and attempted theft
  • Fire (internal and external)
  • Windscreen and glass
  • Agreed/market value settlement

TPFT — Third Party Fire & Theft

  • Third-party bodily injury (unlimited)
  • Third-party property damage
  • Fire damage to own vehicle
  • Theft of own vehicle
  • No own-damage cover for accidents
  • Common on older or lower-value vehicles

TPO — Third Party Only (Minimum Legal)

  • Third-party bodily injury (unlimited by statute)
  • Third-party property damage
  • Minimum required by Road Traffic Act 1988
  • MIB backstop for uninsured/untraced drivers
  • Compulsory on all mechanically propelled vehicles used on public roads

Fleet & Commercial Extensions

  • Goods in transit (own and third-party goods)
  • Employers' liability (driving for work)
  • Breakdown and recovery
  • Replacement vehicle / loss of use
  • Any-driver up to age / licence limits
  • Territory extensions (EU, worldwide)

Classic & High-Value (M2 Specialist)

  • Agreed value (no depreciation deduction)
  • Limited mileage options
  • Club / rally participation cover
  • Track-day endorsement
  • Laid-up period discounts
  • Salvage retention rights

Motor Trade Combined (M6)

  • Road risks — vehicles in customer / dealer custody
  • Premises (fire, theft, business interruption)
  • EL and PL for trade activities
  • Tools, stock and equipment
  • Credit risk (demonstrator finance)
  • Product liability for repairs / parts fitted

Regulatory & Statutory Framework

Road Traffic Act 1988 & Compulsory Insurance Regime

All mechanically propelled vehicles used on a road or other public place in Great Britain must carry at minimum third-party liability insurance under Part VI of the Road Traffic Act 1988. Failure to comply is a criminal offence; penalties include fixed penalty notices, licence endorsement (IN10), vehicle seizure and prosecution. Key statutory machinery includes:

  • Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB): Compensates victims of uninsured and untraced drivers. Funded by levies on all UK motor insurers. Uninsured Drivers Agreement (2015) and Untraced Drivers Agreement (2017).
  • Motor Insurance Database (MID): Operated by MIB — real-time database of all UK insured vehicles used by police, DVLA and ANPR cameras for enforcement.
  • Continuous Insurance Enforcement (CIE): Since 2011, DVLA cross-references MID against registered keeper records. Uninsured vehicles receive automatic penalties unless validly SORNed.
  • FCA Regulation: Motor insurance is a regulated activity under FSMA 2000. Insurers must hold a Part 4A permission and comply with ICOBS (Insurance Conduct of Business Sourcebook) rules.
  • FCA Pricing Reform 2022: Banned "price walking" — renewal premiums cannot exceed equivalent new business price. Significant structural impact on personal lines pricing.
  • Automated Vehicles Act 2024: New statutory framework for autonomous and self-driving vehicles. Insurer liable for incidents while vehicle is self-driving; subrogation rights against manufacturer.

Key Emerging Risks

UK motor is undergoing its most significant structural shift in a century, driven by electrification, connectivity and autonomy. Lloyd's underwriters writing M2–M6 must assess these risk vectors alongside traditional frequency/severity drivers.

Electric Vehicles (EV)

  • Battery replacement costs: £5,000–£30,000+ (BEV) EST
  • Thermal runaway — uncontrolled exothermic reaction; difficult to extinguish
  • Total-loss thresholds lower due to battery write-off criteria
  • Charging infrastructure liability (public charge points)
  • EV-specialist repair networks still limited (2024) VER

Telematics & UBI

  • Usage-based insurance (UBI) via black-box or app
  • Pay-as-you-drive (PAYD) and pay-how-you-drive (PHYD)
  • Data privacy obligations under UK GDPR
  • Young driver telematics — dominant pricing tool under 25
  • Cyber risk if telematics data compromised

Autonomous Vehicles (AV)

  • Automated Vehicles Act 2024 — insurer first-response liability during self-drive
  • Right of subrogation against manufacturer for product defect
  • SAE levels 1–5 — only Level 3+ covered by AV Act regime
  • ADAS (Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems) — partial automation raises attribution questions
  • Long-term shift of premium from personal lines to product liability

Gig Economy & Ride-Hail

  • Uber, Bolt, Ola — hire & reward classification requires specific PHV cover
  • Hire or reward exclusion in standard policies
  • Delivery courier boom post-COVID — classification grey areas
  • FCA guidance on gig economy insurance gaps (2021)
  • Aggregator platform liability questions
