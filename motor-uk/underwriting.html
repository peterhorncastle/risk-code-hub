Rating Framework — Motor Insurance Pricing

UK motor rating is among the most statistically sophisticated in general insurance. The personal lines market (M2) uses generalised linear models (GLMs) and increasingly machine learning on millions of data points — policyholder characteristics, claims history, vehicle data, postcode and telematics behaviour. Lloyd's specialist motor operates on manual underwriting for non-standard risks, agreed value, fleet and motor trade, with actuarial support for portfolio management.

Indicative Motor Premium Formula

Annual Premium = Base Rate × Vehicle Factor × Driver Factor × Territory Factor × Use Factor × Claims Factor × Cover Factor × EV/AV Loading
Note: Each factor is a multiplier applied to the base rate. In personal lines, factors are derived from GLM statistical models on portfolio experience. In Lloyd's specialist motor, factors are applied judgmentally by underwriters using market benchmarks and loss history. ILLUS

Fleet Underwriting (M5) — Key Rating Variables

Fleet motor underwriting differs fundamentally from personal lines. Rating is based on fleet loss experience (own claims data), fleet composition, driver management, mileage exposure and industry sector — not individual driver characteristics. A minimum of 3 years fleet loss history is typically required; larger fleets provide greater statistical credibility.

Motor Trade Underwriting (M6)

Motor trade combined policies are among the most complex in Lloyd's motor book, combining road risks (vehicles in custody/control), premises, liability and stock/equipment in a single wording. Underwriting requires detailed assessment of the nature of trade, premises quality, stock values, employee risk and territorial exposure.

M6 Key Underwriting Considerations

  • Road risks: Who drives vehicles in trade? Any-driver broad cover increases risk vs named-driver or aged-limited covers. Key question: are customers' vehicles driven, or only dealer stock?
  • Stock valuation: Declared stock value accuracy is critical — dealers may understate for premium saving, creating underinsurance. Maximum single vehicle value and total maximum simultaneous exposure.
  • Premises security: Yard security (CCTV, perimeter, overnight lighting), key management (electronic key safes vs board key), arson exposure for bodyshop premises.
  • Business type: New car dealer (lower risk — franchise oversight) vs used car dealer (higher risk — unpredictable stock profile) vs repairers (body-in-white / paint shop fire exposure) vs recovery operators (HGV exposure).
  • Employee checks: Driving licence validation for all test drive and delivery drivers. Points and conviction checking on hire. Particularly important for high-value marques.
  • EV-specific: Battery charging protocols, lithium-ion battery storage areas (sprinkler requirements), electric vehicle specialist repair capabilities.

Specialist Private Motor — Agreed Value & Classic Car (M2)

Lloyd's specialist M2 underwriting centres on high-value vehicles, historic/classic cars, supercars and non-standard risks that standard market cannot accommodate. The key differentiator is agreed value settlement — the sum insured is agreed at inception and paid in full on total loss, without depreciation deduction or market value argument.
