Fleet Risk Management (M5)

For fleet motor underwriting, insurer risk appetite is heavily influenced by the quality of the insured's fleet risk management programme. A well-structured programme can reduce claims frequency by 20–35% versus an unmanaged equivalent fleet. EST Lloyd's underwriters should request and assess the following when quoting fleet business.

Driver Selection & Licensing

  • DVLA licence validation for all drivers at inception and annually (electronic DVLA check service)
  • Minimum licence holding requirement (typically 2+ years)
  • Maximum points threshold (typically 6 SP points; DR codes = decline)
  • Named driver vs any-driver policy — any-driver significantly broadens risk
  • Overseas licence validation for non-UK licence holders

Driver Training

  • RoSPA (Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents) Advanced Driver scheme — gold standard
  • IAM (Institute of Advanced Motorists) qualification for key drivers
  • Defensive driving courses — particularly for high-mileage, long-haul drivers
  • E-learning modules on distracted driving, fatigue management
  • Post-incident coaching — mandatory retraining after at-fault accidents
  • Young driver mentoring programmes where fleet employs under-25s

Telematics & Monitoring

  • Hardwired black-box telemetry on all fleet vehicles — speed, acceleration, braking, cornering
  • Active coaching — real-time alerts, driver scoring dashboards
  • Distracted driving detection — forward-facing camera with AI analysis
  • Fatigue monitoring — driver facing camera (regulated use under UK GDPR)
  • Geo-fencing — alert if vehicle leaves approved territory
  • Stolen vehicle tracking — immediate response capability

Vehicle Selection & Maintenance

  • Euro NCAP 5-star safety rating specified as minimum for fleet vehicles
  • ADAS features — autonomous emergency braking (AEB), lane-keeping, blind-spot detection
  • Manufacturer-approved service intervals — maintained fleet vs deferred maintenance
  • Tyre management programme — pressure monitoring (TPMS), tread depth audits
  • EV fleet: battery health monitoring, charging location safety assessments
  • HGV/LCV CLOCS (Construction Logistics and Community Safety) compliance where applicable

Grey Fleet Management

  • Grey fleet = employees using own vehicles for business purposes
  • Employer owes duty of care under Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 even for grey fleet
  • Business use endorsement on employee's personal policy is required — insurer must know
  • DVLA and MOT check process for grey fleet vehicles
  • Vehicle age and condition minimum standards
  • Mileage and fuel reimbursement records create audit trail of use

Incident Management & FNOL

  • 24/7 First Notification of Loss (FNOL) capability — immediate scene management
  • Approved repairer network for cost control and quality management
  • Replacement vehicle protocols — like-for-like, duration caps
  • Recovery and roadside assistance — rapid response reduces secondary incidents
  • Third-party capture — direct contact with third parties to prevent CMC involvement
  • Fraud indicator checklist at FNOL stage

EV-Specific Risk Management

Electric Vehicle Battery Risks — Thermal Runaway and Write-Off Protocols

Electric vehicles introduce a new risk profile that differs fundamentally from internal combustion engine vehicles. Lloyd's underwriters writing EV-heavy fleets (M5) and specialist private clients (M2) must understand and mitigate these risks:

  • Thermal runaway: Uncontrolled exothermic reaction in lithium-ion battery cells — once initiated, cannot be stopped by conventional firefighting. Fire temperature exceeds 1,000°C; water immersion or specialist containment systems required. Fire service guidance: vehicles may reignite 24–48 hours after apparent extinguishment. VER
  • Enclosed parking risk: Major fire risk in underground and multi-storey car parks. Several incidents in Europe and UK prompted insurer and fire service guidance on EV parking restrictions. Some Lloyd's market underwriters now require enclosed parking risk assessments for fleets with >20% EV. EST
  • Battery write-off criteria: Current industry practice: if battery has been in a significant impact event or shows thermal damage, most insurers write off the vehicle rather than attempt battery replacement — battery health cannot be reliably assessed post-impact. Total-loss thresholds are therefore lower for EVs than equivalent ICE vehicles. VER
  • Charging safety: Level 3 (DC fast charging) station safety — risk of equipment fault. Home charging — electrical installation certificate requirements, overload risk on older domestic wiring. Fleet charging depots — fire suppression requirements for high-density charge points. EST
  • Underwriting actions: Battery health declaration at inception; battery age and cycle count where available; parking and charging protocols as warranty conditions; thermal containment bags/blankets for individual vehicle storage; enhanced salvage protocols (damaged EV salvage requires specialist handling).

Fraud Prevention — UK Motor

Motor insurance fraud costs UK insurers an estimated £1.3bn+ annually. EST Fraud takes many forms across the M2–M6 codes. Awareness of fraud patterns is critical for Lloyd's underwriters, particularly in non-standard and specialist segments where fraud incentives are higher.

Crash for Cash (Staged Accidents)

  • Induced collision — innocent victim causes staged rear-end incident
  • Phantom passengers — non-existent occupants added to claims
  • Warning signs: same solicitor/CMC on multiple claims; unusual accident circumstances; multiple passenger injury claims from minor impact
  • Industry tool: Insurance Fraud Bureau (IFB) and CheatLine for reporting
  • Telematics data — acceleration/braking data inconsistent with claimed collision force

Ghost Broking

  • Fraudulent certificates issued using compromised or fabricated insurer details
  • Victims unknowingly driving uninsured — discovered only at claims stage or police stop
  • Predominantly targets young drivers seeking lower premiums
  • Social media distribution (Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok)
  • Detection: MID validation; ANPR police enforcement; claims repudiation triggers MIB notification

Fronting

  • Main driver (usually younger, higher risk) named as additional driver on policy held by lower-risk individual (e.g. parent)
  • Technically misrepresentation at inception — policy voidable
  • Telematics enables detection: driving pattern analysis identifies true primary driver
  • Claims repudiation risk — insurer may void policy and decline claim if fronting established
  • Insurers have legal obligation under s.151 RTA 1988 to pay third parties regardless

Motor Trade Fraud (M6)

  • Phantom vehicle insurance — non-existent stock insured and "stolen"
  • Over-valuation of stock — vehicles insured above market value
  • Keyless entry device theft — technology increasingly used to steal insured stock
  • Cyber-enabled dealer management system fraud (see claims database)
  • Prevention: physical stock audit; vehicle VIN verification; electronic key management

Top 10 Risk Mitigation Actions — Lloyd's Motor Underwriter

  1. Request 3-year loss history and validate against MID: Fleet and commercial motor risks must provide claims run-off data. Verify vehicle counts and claims against MID records to detect phantom vehicle fraud.
  2. DVLA licence check for all named drivers: Use electronic DVLA checking service (My Licence) — do not rely on photocopies. Re-check annually for fleet policies.
  3. Assess telematics deployment and data quality: For fleet (M5), prefer fully deployed telematics with active coaching. Apply pricing credit; request data reports from insured's fleet management system.
  4. EV battery health declaration: For significant EV fleets (>15% BEV), require battery health certification and parking/charging risk assessment. Confirm no enclosed multi-storey parking without adequate sprinkler suppression.
  5. Motor trade stock verification (M6): Request stock schedule or dealer management system report at inception and periodically. Physical audit for large stocks. Electronic key management as warranty condition.
  6. Verify insured use class accuracy: Personal lines (M2) — confirm intended use: courier/gig economy activity is hire or reward; business use must be declared. Commercial (M3) — confirm goods-in-transit and any any-driver exposure.
  7. Fraud indicator review at quote stage: Policy aggregator checking (IFB tool), address verification, previous claims history via Claims and Underwriting Exchange (CUE) database, anti-fronting telematics capability.
  8. Understand grey fleet exposure for corporate clients (M5): Confirm whether employer has grey fleet (employee-owned vehicles used for business) and verify those employees hold appropriate business use on their own policies.
  9. Classic/HNW agreed value — require independent valuation: For vehicles above £100k, require independent valuation from marque specialist (e.g. H&H Classics, Bonhams) at inception and every 3 years. Review after major restoration work.
  10. AV policy wording readiness: Confirm motor policy wordings address Automated Vehicles Act 2024 — insurer response during self-driving mode and subrogation rights against manufacturer. Update fleet programme wordings for EVs and AV-capable vehicles.
