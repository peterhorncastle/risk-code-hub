Retrocession — Programme Structuring

Retro sits at the very top of the risk-transfer chain, protecting the reinsurer's own net accumulation — and it is where capital-markets money most often enters the system.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

A reinsurer builds a retro programme the way a cedant builds a reinsurance programme — but on its own accumulated book. It combines indemnity cat layers, index (ILW) covers and collateralised / ILS capacity to cap the peak of its catastrophe exposure and free capital. VER

A Reinsurer's Retro Tower

ElementRole
Net cat accumulationThe reinsurer's own peak exposure after all inwards business
RetentionWhat the reinsurer keeps before its retro attaches
Indemnity cat retro layersPay the reinsurer's actual accumulated loss above the retention — no basis risk
Industry loss warrantiesIndex-based top-ups and fill-ins — simple, fast, with basis risk
Cat bonds / sidecarsMulti-year collateralised capacity from capital markets (see the ILS hub)
Aggregate / second-eventProtection against a bad cat year or a costly second event

Design Choices

Indemnity vs index

Indemnity retro matches the reinsurer's real loss but needs full exposure disclosure; ILWs are quick and capital-efficient but leave basis risk. Most programmes blend both.

Traditional vs ILS capacity

Rated reinsurers offer paper without collateral concerns; ILS offers deep, price-disciplined capacity but with collateral and trapping to manage.

Peak-zone focus

Retro spend concentrates on the peak zones (US wind, US/JP quake, Euro wind) that dominate the reinsurer's tail and its capital charge.

The Placement Workflow

1

Accumulation roll-up

The reinsurer models its net cat accumulation by peril and zone across all inwards business.

2

Programme design

Retention, indemnity layers, ILW fill-ins and any cat-bond / sidecar capacity are structured against the tail.

3

Marketing & capital

The broker places layers across rated reinsurers and ILS funds, agreeing collateral where relevant.

4

Documentation

Occurrence / clash, index triggers, collateral and commutation terms are finalised.

5

Monitoring

Track accumulation, loss development and trapped collateral through the year.

Case Study — A Global Reinsurer's Peak-Zone Retro

A reinsurer has built a large US-wind accumulation across hundreds of cedant treaties and wants to cap its 1-in-200 loss and free capital.

The problem

A single major US hurricane would drive a loss that dominates the reinsurer's result and its rating-agency capital requirement.

The structure

Indemnity cat retro layers on the net US-wind accumulation, topped up with a US-wind ILW and a multi-year cat bond for peak capacity.

The terms

Full exposure disclosure, a clear occurrence/clash definition, collateral and commutation terms for the ILS capacity.

The result

The reinsurer caps its peak US-wind loss, reduces its required capital and writes more inwards business — with capital-markets money carrying the tail.
