Module 02 — History

Retrocession — History

Retro's history is dominated by one cautionary tale — the LMX spiral — and one transformation — the arrival of capital-markets money.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Reinsurers have always laid off part of their own books, but retrocession earned its reputation — and its lessons — in the London Market Excess spiral, and was then remade by the entry of collateralised capital-markets money. VER

Era 1 — Laying off the reinsurer's book (early 20th century)

From the earliest professional reinsurers, a reinsurer that had assumed too much of one exposure retroceded part of it to a peer. Simple, sensible risk-spreading — the original purpose of retro. EST

Era 2 — The LMX spiral (1980s–early 1990s)

London Market Excess reinsurers wrote each other's retro in a tight circle. When Piper Alpha (1988) and the 1990 European windstorms struck, the same loss passed round the spiral repeatedly — each reinsurer recovering from the next — multiplying the market loss far beyond the original and bankrupting many participants. The spiral is retro's defining lesson in transparency and clash. VER

Era 3 — Modelling & discipline (1990s–2000s)

Catastrophe models, clash analysis and a hard look at underlying exposures brought discipline. Retro became a modelled cat business, and the reckless recycling of the spiral era was designed out. VER

Era 4 — Capital-markets retro (2000s–present)

Cat bonds, sidecars, collateralised reinsurance and industry loss warranties brought pension- and hedge-fund capital into retro. ILS now provides a large share of retro capacity, and events like the 2017 hurricanes and the Vesttoo collateral fraud (2023) tested how well that capital and its collateral hold up. VER

Key Ideas That Endure

Transparency

Knowing the underlying exposures — the spiral's fatal gap — is now central to retro underwriting.

Clash

Quantifying how one event cascades across cedants is the heart of retro pricing.

Alternative capital

Retro is the main bridge between the traditional market and capital-markets money.
