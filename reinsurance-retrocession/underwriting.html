Retrocession — Underwriting & Pricing

Retro pricing is catastrophe pricing at one remove: the retrocessionaire models the retrocedant's accumulated exposure across all its cedants, then prices the layer or the index trigger.

The retrocessionaire cannot see every underlying policy, so it prices off the retrocedant's modelled accumulation — the aggregate cat exposure built up across all the reinsurance the retrocedant has assumed. Two instruments dominate: an indemnity cat layer, and an index-based industry loss warranty. VER

The Two Pricing Routes

Retro cat XoL (indemnity): Layer premium = modelled AAL to layer × (1 + risk load) ÷ (1 − expenses − brokerage); rate-on-line = premium ÷ limit.

Industry loss warranty (ILW): Expected loss = limit × P(industry loss > trigger); premium = expected loss × (1 + risk load) ÷ (1 − expenses − brokerage).

Multiple = rate-on-line ÷ probability — the reward per unit of modelled risk.

The Key Concepts

Accumulation modelling

Rolling up the retrocedant's exposure by peril and zone across all its assumed business — the essential and hardest step, because the same event hits many of its treaties at once.

Clash

The risk that one event triggers losses across multiple cedants or lines simultaneously — retro is precisely the cover for clash, so quantifying it is central.

Indemnity vs index

Indemnity retro pays the retrocedant's actual loss (no basis risk, more information needed); an ILW pays on an industry index (simple, but with basis risk against the real loss).

The multiple

Retro and ILS price in "multiples" of expected loss — a 4% expected-loss ILW at a 12% rate-on-line trades at a 3× multiple. The multiple is the market's risk-reward gauge.

Worked Example 1 — Retro Cat Layer

Setup: £50m xs £150m on the reinsurer's net cat accumulation; modelled AAL to the layer £3.5m; risk load 45%; expenses 5%; brokerage 10%; one reinstatement. ILLUS

  • Loss-on-line = £3.5m ÷ £50m = 7.0%.
  • Loaded = £3.5m × 1.45 = £5.075m; ÷ 0.85 → premium ≈ £5.97m.
  • Rate-on-line ≈ £5.97m ÷ £50m ≈ 11.9%; payback ≈ 8.4 years.
  • Margin over expected loss ≈ £2.47m for capital and volatility.

Worked Example 2 — Industry Loss Warranty

Setup: £25m ILW attaching on a $30bn US-wind industry loss; modelled annual probability the industry loss exceeds $30bn = 4%; risk load 80%; expenses 4%; brokerage 8%. ILLUS

  • Expected loss = £25m × 4% = £1.0m.
  • Loaded = £1.0m × 1.80 = £1.80m; ÷ 0.88 → premium ≈ £2.05m.
  • Rate-on-line ≈ £2.05m ÷ £25m ≈ 8.2%; multiple ≈ 8.2% ÷ 4% ≈ 2.0×.
  • Simple to trade, but the retrocedant carries basis risk — the index may not match its own loss.

Pre-Bind Checklist

Model the retrocedant's accumulation by peril and zone across all assumed business.
Quantify clash — how many cedants and lines one event would hit at once.
Decide indemnity vs index (ILW) and price the basis risk of an index cover.
Trace the underlying exposures to avoid spiral-type recycling of the same risk.
Confirm collateral for any collateralised / ILS retrocessionaire.
