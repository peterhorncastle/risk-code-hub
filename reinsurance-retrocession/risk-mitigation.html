Retrocession — Contract & Wordings

Retro wordings exist to stop the two dangers unique to the top of the chain: the spiral that recycles risk, and the collateral that may not be there when a loss develops.

OverviewHistoryTimelineLoss DatabaseUnderwritingRates & ROERisk MitigationGlobal ProgrammeReferencesRater

Retro is reinsurance of reinsurers, so its wordings deal with problems that only arise at the top of the chain — recycled risk, index basis, and the security behind collateralised capacity. Six features do the work. VER

Underlying-exposure transparency

The retrocessionaire needs to see what stands behind the cover, so the same risk is not written round a circle — the anti-spiral discipline.

Clash / occurrence definition

How losses across many cedants combine into one event decides what the retro pays — the core of clash cover.

Index / trigger basis (ILW)

For an ILW, the industry-loss index and reporting agent must be defined precisely, and the retrocedant must accept the basis risk against its own loss.

Collateral & trust arrangements

For collateralised / ILS retro, funds held in trust secure the cover; the wording must specify amount, release and — post-Vesttoo — verification.

Reinstatements

Whether and how a cat retro layer is restored after a loss, and at what cost, given how a single season can exhaust it.

Loss-development & commutation

How and when collateral is released as a loss develops — the trapped-collateral problem — and terms to commute a run-off.

Counterparty & Collateral

Before binding

  • Trace the underlying exposures — never write blind into a possible spiral.
  • For collateralised retro, verify the collateral and trust to the issuing bank (the Vesttoo lesson).
  • Price the basis risk of any index / ILW cover explicitly.

During & after

  • Monitor loss development and the release schedule of any trapped collateral.
  • Track clash accumulation across cedants against the retro programme.
  • Consider commutation to close a developing run-off cleanly.

⚠️ The trapped-collateral trap

A collateralised retrocessionaire posts funds in trust; if a loss is still developing at year-end, that collateral is "trapped" — locked against a loss that may never fully materialise, starving the investor of capital to redeploy. Clear loss-development and commutation terms, and disciplined reserving, are how both sides manage it. And after Vesttoo, verifying that the collateral is genuine is non-negotiable.
