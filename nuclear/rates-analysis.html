Critical Data Note: Nuclear insurance is written through national pools with entirely non-public pricing. Unlike most commercial insurance classes, nuclear pool contribution rates are determined confidentially by pool technical committees and are not disclosed. All charts, indices, loss ratios and financial figures on this page are illustrative estimates for educational purposes only — they do not represent actual pool rates, actual loss experience, or actual pool financial performance. They are constructed to be directionally plausible based on publicly available information about nuclear market structure and major loss events. [I10]

ROE Scenarios — Illustrative Nuclear Pool PortfolioILLUSTRATIVE ONLY
Nuclear ROE Note: Nuclear pools operate on a collective co-insurance basis — individual insurer ROE from nuclear participation reflects their proportionate pool share and depends heavily on whether a major event occurs. In non-loss years, the nuclear pool is typically profitable (low frequency, adequate premiums). In a Chernobyl/Fukushima-scale event affecting a commercially insured Western plant, a single incident could overwhelm pool capacity and trigger the government backstop — representing effectively unlimited loss in the bear scenario.

IBNR & Development Considerations
FactorImpact on Nuclear Pool Reserves
Long latency of radiation-induced diseaseRadiation-induced cancers (leukemia, thyroid cancer) may not manifest for 10–40 years after exposure — one of the longest-tail liabilities in insurance. IBNR provisions for NL and NV workers' compensation must extend decades.
Catastrophe provision (CAT IBNR)Pools maintain explicit catastrophe reserves to provide capital for a major event year. The probability of a Chernobyl/Fukushima-scale event at a commercially insured Western plant in any year is very low — but the potential severity is enormous. CAT IBNR is typically separately managed from attritional IBNR.
TMI decontamination precedentTMI Unit 2 took 14 years to decontaminate and partially decommission — demonstrating that nuclear property claims develop very slowly. Long tail on NP property claims must be factored into reserves.
Regulatory-driven cost escalationPost-accident regulatory requirements (new safety systems, upgraded seismic protection) often exceed initial repair cost estimates. Davis-Besse ($143m repair) and San Onofre ($4.7bn decommissioning) illustrate regulatory cost escalation risk in NP claims.
Convention liability limit changesUpward revision of operator liability limits (Paris Protocol 2004, INES events triggering convention review) can alter the expected severity of NL pool claims for future events — IBNR must be reviewed when convention limits change.
Subrogation — limited in nuclearNuclear channelling doctrine means pools cannot subrogate against contractors or equipment suppliers for nuclear damage. Subrogation potential is more limited than in other classes.
