Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

NP — Nuclear Property

Physical damage insurance for nuclear power stations, research reactors, fuel fabrication plants, reprocessing facilities and decommissioning sites. Covers the physical structure, reactor systems, turbines, and ancillary equipment. Extremely high values at risk — a single modern PWR reactor plant may have a PML of $1–5bn+. Capacity is pool-dominated; direct commercial market capacity is very limited. [I3]

NL — Nuclear Liability

Third-party liability insurance for licensed nuclear operators, covering nuclear damage claims from members of the public — personal injury, property damage, environmental contamination and economic loss arising from a nuclear incident. Strictly governed by international conventions (Paris, Brussels, Vienna) and national legislation. The channelling doctrine means all liability flows to the licensed operator; suppliers and contractors cannot be sued directly for nuclear damage. [L1]

NV — Nuclear Various / Other

Covers nuclear exposures that do not fit neatly into NP or NL categories: transport of nuclear materials (radioactive materials in transit — fresh or spent fuel), decommissioning liability (long-tail: 50–100 year horizon), nuclear workers' compensation and radiation exposure, and peripheral contractor and engineering risks associated with nuclear sites. Includes cover for Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) during development and construction phases. [I2]

Coverage Taxonomy

Property Damage (NP)

Physical damage to nuclear plant and equipment — reactor vessel, containment structure, turbine hall, cooling systems, fuel storage. Covers sudden accidental loss including fire, explosion, mechanical breakdown, and earthquake (subject to seismic sub-limits). Pools structure capacity across national pool members. [I3]

Operator Third-Party Liability (NL)

Nuclear damage claims to third parties — personal injury (radiation exposure, latent disease), property damage, environmental contamination, economic loss in defined convention zones. Strict liability (no fault required), limited (capped at convention limits), and channelled exclusively to the licensed operator. [L1]

Nuclear Material Transport (NV)

Transit cover for radioactive materials: fresh nuclear fuel elements, spent fuel flasks, radioactive waste, isotopes for medical or research use. Transport liability follows the same channelling principles as site liability. Separate transit policies or endorsements to site cover. Route risk, container integrity and regulatory compliance are key underwriting factors. [L3]

Decommissioning Liability (NV)

Nuclear power stations have operational lives of 40–60 years but decommissioning takes decades longer — the UK's oldest stations will not be fully decommissioned until 2100+. Long-tail liability for contamination, waste management, site clean-up. Funded nationally via Nuclear Decommissioning Authority (UK NDA) / equivalent bodies. [L9]

Workers' Compensation (NV)

Radiation exposure claims from nuclear workers — acute and latent disease (cancer, leukemia with long development periods). Employers' liability/workers' comp for licensed nuclear site workers. Radiation dose monitoring is a key underwriting requirement. ICRP dose limits apply; regulatory dose records are available for underwriting review. [L7]

Contractors' Liability (NV / Limited)

Due to the channelling doctrine, contractors and suppliers working on nuclear sites cannot be sued for nuclear damage by third parties — liability runs exclusively to the licensed operator. Contractor insurance is therefore limited to non-nuclear perils (conventional property, conventional liability). This is a critical distinction from all other construction and engineering classes. [L1]

Why Nuclear Insurance Pools Exist — The Pool Structure Explained

Nuclear power stations represent some of the largest and most concentrated insured values in the world. A single reactor site may have physical values of $2–5bn and potential third-party liability running to hundreds of millions under the applicable conventions. No single insurer — and very few groups of insurers — can provide the necessary capacity alone.

National nuclear insurance pools were established from the late 1950s onwards to aggregate capacity across all willing insurers within a country under a single co-insurance arrangement. In the UK, the Nuclear Risk Insurers (NRI) pool co-ordinates capacity from Lloyd's syndicates and company market participants. Similar pools operate in France (ANP — Advance), USA (ANI — American Nuclear Insurers), Japan, Germany, and approximately 20 other countries with commercial nuclear fleets.

Layer 1: National nuclear insurance pool (e.g. NRI in UK) — primary coverage provided by pool members collectively
Layer 2: International excess layer — commercial market (Lloyd's and international reinsurers) above pool retention
Layer 3: Government indemnity / convention backstop — state provides unlimited or very large excess layer beyond commercial market capacity
State of Last Resort: For a true Chernobyl or Fukushima-scale event, the costs far exceed any insurance pool — the state (and ultimately taxpayers) bears residual liability
Regulatory Classification: Nuclear insurance is mandatory for licensed nuclear operators in all convention signatory states. The Paris Convention 1960 (as amended by the 2004 Protocol) requires operators to maintain insurance or other financial security up to the applicable liability limit — at least €700m for major states. The UK Nuclear Installations Act 1965 requires mandatory insurance via approved insurers, with the NRI pool as the designated mechanism. Uninsured nuclear operations are not permitted. [L4]
Warning — Conflict Zone Nuclear Plants: The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant (Ukraine) has been subject to military conflict since 2022 — the first time in history a large operating nuclear power station has been in an active war zone. Standard nuclear insurance policies include war exclusions. This creates an unprecedented coverage gap and reputational risk for the nuclear sector. IAEA emergency monitoring is in place. [I5]
