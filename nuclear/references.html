Legal & Regulatory Sources [L1]–[L9]
  • [L1]
    LEGAL Paris Convention on Third Party Liability in the Field of Nuclear Energy 1960 (as amended by Protocol of 2004)

    OECD Nuclear Energy Agency. Convention done at Paris, 29 July 1960; entered into force 1 April 1968. 2004 Amending Protocol: OECD/NEA, Paris 12 February 2004 (not yet in force in all states as of 2026). Establishes the fundamental principles of Western European nuclear liability: strict liability (no fault required), channelling (all liability to the licensed operator), limitation (capped amounts), and mandatory financial security (insurance). The foundational legal text for NL insurance in Western Europe. Available: oecd-nea.org/law/paris-convention.

  • [L2]
    LEGAL Brussels Supplementary Convention 1963 (as amended by 1964 Additional Protocol and 2004 Protocol)

    Convention Supplementary to the Paris Convention on Third Party Liability in the Field of Nuclear Energy 1963, done at Brussels 31 January 1963; entered into force 4 December 1974. The Brussels Convention adds a state-funded layer of compensation above the operator's liability limit under the Paris Convention, recognising that private insurance alone cannot cover catastrophic scenarios. Signatory states collectively fund the difference between the operator's limit and the Brussels ceiling. Administered by OECD/NEA. Essential for understanding the multilateral government backstop in Western European nuclear liability.

  • [L3]
    LEGAL Vienna Convention on Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage 1963 (IAEA) and 1997 Protocol

    Vienna Convention on Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage: IAEA, done at Vienna 21 May 1963; entered into force 12 November 1977. 1997 Protocol: IAEA, Vienna 12 September 1997. The Vienna Convention is the IAEA's parallel nuclear liability framework, extending similar principles (strict liability, channelling, limited liability) to non-OECD states. The 1997 Protocol significantly increases minimum liability amounts and broadens the definition of nuclear damage to include environmental damage, economic loss, and preventive measures. The Joint Protocol 1988 links the Paris and Vienna systems for cross-convention claims. Available: iaea.org.

  • [L4]
    LEGAL Nuclear Installations Act 1965 (UK) — c. 57

    Nuclear Installations Act 1965 (UK). Gives domestic effect to the Paris/Brussels Convention framework in UK law. Requires that any person wishing to operate a nuclear installation in the UK must hold a nuclear site licence granted by the Office for Nuclear Regulation (ONR, formerly Nuclear Installations Inspectorate / NII). Imposes strict liability on the licensed operator for nuclear incidents: liability is absolute (no fault required) and exclusive (no other person is liable for nuclear damage). Requires mandatory insurance through approved insurers — in practice, through the NRI pool. Sets out the Nuclear Installations Inspectorate regime. As amended by various Energy Acts. Available: legislation.gov.uk.

  • [L5]
    LEGAL Price-Anderson Nuclear Industries Indemnity Act 1957 (USA) — 42 U.S.C. § 2210 — as reauthorised 2005

    Pub. L. 85-256, 71 Stat. 576 (1957); most recently reauthorised by Energy Policy Act 2005, Pub. L. 109-58. Price-Anderson creates the US nuclear liability framework: (1) Primary insurance: each commercial nuclear operator must hold $450m of nuclear liability insurance (provided through ANI pool); (2) Secondary financial protection: all US reactor operators contribute to an industry-wide indemnity pool — currently approximately $13bn in aggregate; (3) Government indemnity: for losses exceeding the industry pool, Congress appropriates funds (unlimited in practice). Reauthorisation required every 20 years — next due 2025. Available: usnrc.gov/regulations/dev/price-anderson.

  • [L6]
    LEGAL Atomic Energy Act 1954 (USA) — 42 U.S.C. §§ 2011–2297h-13

    The Atomic Energy Act 1954 is the foundational US legislation governing all aspects of nuclear material and nuclear energy — from weapons production to civilian power. Established the Atomic Energy Commission (subsequently reorganised into NRC and DOE). The AEA provides the legal basis for NRC licensing, nuclear materials regulation, and the Price-Anderson liability framework. It defines "nuclear incident," "nuclear facility," and the scope of federal nuclear regulation. Amended many times including by the Energy Reorganisation Act 1974 (which separated regulatory from promotional functions by establishing NRC and ERDA/DOE). Available: nrc.gov.

  • [L7]
    LEGAL Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 — SI 2017/1075 (UK)

    Statutory Instrument 2017 No. 1075 (UK). Implements Council Directive 2013/59/Euratom on basic safety standards for protection against ionising radiation. Imposes duties on employers to protect workers and members of the public from ionising radiation exposures. Establishes dose limits (20mSv/yr effective dose for workers; 1mSv/yr for members of the public; 6mSv/yr for apprentices/students). Requires radiation risk assessment, designation of controlled and supervised areas, personal dosimetry for classified workers, and maintenance of dose records for 50 years post-exposure (reflecting long latency of radiation-induced disease). Available: legislation.gov.uk. Regulator: ONR (nuclear) / HSE (industrial).

  • [L8]
    LEGAL IAEA Safety Standards Series — Safety Fundamentals SF-1 (2006): Fundamental Safety Principles

    IAEA Safety Standards Series No. SF-1 (Vienna: IAEA, 2006). Establishes the fundamental safety objective — to protect people and the environment from harmful effects of ionising radiation — and the fundamental safety principles that underpin all IAEA safety standards. SF-1 is the apex of the IAEA safety standards pyramid, below which sit Safety Requirements and Safety Guides. The document establishes defence in depth as a fundamental safety principle for nuclear installations. Also referenced: INSAG-10 (Defence in Depth in Nuclear Safety, 1996, IAEA Safety Reports Series). Available: iaea.org/resources/safety-standards.

  • [L9]
    LEGAL Energy Act 2004 (UK) — c. 20 (Nuclear Decommissioning and NDA Establishment)

    Energy Act 2004 (UK), Part 1 (Nuclear Decommissioning). Establishes the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority (NDA) as the public body responsible for the decommissioning and clean-up of the UK's civil nuclear legacy — primarily Sellafield (Cumbria), Magnox and UKAEA sites. NDA is responsible for £121bn+ (estimated 2024) in nuclear decommissioning liabilities over the coming century. The Act also creates the Nuclear Liabilities Fund for post-privatisation decommissioning obligations. Part 2 covers nuclear decommissioning funding obligations for private operators. The NDA's work is the largest single nuclear insurance and liability programme in the UK. Available: legislation.gov.uk; nda.gov.uk.

Industry & Market Sources [I1]–[I10]
  • [I1]
    INDUSTRY IAEA PRIS — Power Reactor Information System: Global Reactor Statistics 2024

    International Atomic Energy Agency, Power Reactor Information System (PRIS). Real-time database of operational, under construction, and shutdown nuclear reactors worldwide. Key 2024 statistics: ~440 commercial nuclear reactors operational globally; ~60 under construction; nuclear provides approximately 10% of global electricity. PRIS is the definitive source for global reactor inventory data used in market sizing, accumulation analysis, and regional market assessment. Available: pris.iaea.org. Updated continuously.

  • [I2]
    INDUSTRY World Nuclear Association — Nuclear Insurance and Liability Overview 2023

    World Nuclear Association (WNA), Nuclear Insurance and Liability (updated 2023). Comprehensive overview of the global nuclear insurance framework, including pool structures, international convention status, national liability systems, and insurance capacity by country. WNA also publishes The Nuclear Industry: Nuclear Insurance briefing papers. Key reference for understanding how nuclear liability law translates into insurance requirements across different jurisdictions. Available: world-nuclear.org/information-library/safety-and-security/nuclear-insurance-and-liability.

  • [I3]
    INDUSTRY NRI (Nuclear Risk Insurers) — Annual Market Report and Capacity Statement

    Nuclear Risk Insurers Ltd (NRI), UK national nuclear insurance pool. NRI co-ordinates capacity from Lloyd's syndicates and company market participants for all UK licensed nuclear sites. NRI publishes annual capacity statements and market bulletins for members. NRI also manages the UK's pool-to-pool treaty participation with international pools (French ANP, US ANI, German DKVG, etc.). The NRI technical team conducts site assessments for major risks. NRI contact: nri.org.uk. Note: NRI capacity and rate data are not publicly disclosed — only available to pool members.

  • [I4]
    INDUSTRY WANO (World Association of Nuclear Operators) — Peer Review Programme Statistics 2023

    World Association of Nuclear Operators (WANO), established 1989 (post-Chernobyl). Conducts peer reviews at all member nuclear plants globally on a 4-year cycle — operators from one plant review another. WANO peer reviews identify Areas for Improvement (AFIs) and share Significant Operating Experience Reports (SOERs) across members. WANO performance statistics (peer review outcomes, AFI closure rates) are a key indicator of global nuclear safety culture trends. 2023 WANO review: positive trend in safety culture indicators post-Fukushima. Available: wano.info. WANO peer review summary should be routinely requested as part of NP/NL underwriting submissions.

  • [I5]
    INDUSTRY OECD NEA — Nuclear Insurance and Liability: Ten Years After Fukushima (2021)

    OECD Nuclear Energy Agency, Nuclear Third Party Liability: Ten Years After the Fukushima Daiichi Accident (OECD/NEA, Paris, 2021). Reviews how the Fukushima accident has affected nuclear liability law, convention implementation, and insurance capacity globally in the decade following the accident. Key topics: TEPCO compensation mechanism analysis, adequacy of existing convention limits post-Fukushima, pending 2004 Protocol implementation challenges, and lessons for nuclear liability convention reform. Available: oecd-nea.org. Essential for understanding how Fukushima changed nuclear liability thinking.

  • [I6]
    INDUSTRY Swiss Re — Nuclear Energy: Risk and Insurance (2022)

    Swiss Re Institute, Nuclear Energy: Risks and Insurability (Zurich: Swiss Re, 2022). Swiss Re is one of the major reinsurers participating in nuclear pool treaties internationally. This publication examines the insurability of nuclear risks, the adequacy of convention liability limits, the impact of Fukushima on capacity and pricing, and the emerging challenges of SMRs and new reactor designs. Swiss Re's perspective as a major international reinsurer provides market context unavailable from regulatory sources. Available: swissre.com/risk-knowledge.

  • [I7]
    INDUSTRY IAEA — The Fukushima Daiichi Accident: Report by the Director General (2015)

    International Atomic Energy Agency, The Fukushima Daiichi Accident: Report by the Director General (Vienna: IAEA, 2015). Six-volume comprehensive assessment of the Fukushima Daiichi accident — causes, progression, radiological consequences, emergency response, and lessons for nuclear safety worldwide. The definitive technical reference for the 2011 accident. Also referenced: IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 78 (Chernobyl's Legacy: Health, Environmental and Socio-Economic Impacts, 2006). Available: iaea.org/publications/10962/the-fukushima-daiichi-accident.

  • [I8]
    INDUSTRY ONR (Office for Nuclear Regulation) — UK Annual Report and Nuclear Safety 2023/24

    Office for Nuclear Regulation (ONR), Annual Report and Accounts 2023/24 (London: ONR, 2024). ONR is the UK's independent nuclear regulator, responsible for licensing nuclear sites, enforcing nuclear safety and security standards, and regulating nuclear transport. Annual report provides: performance of UK licensed nuclear sites, inspection findings, significant events, and ONR's assessment of UK nuclear safety. Also ONR publishes Nuclear Site Licence Conditions — the primary regulatory requirements with which licensed operators must comply. Available: onr.org.uk.

  • [I9]
    INDUSTRY Rolls-Royce SMR — Project Documentation and Risk Assessment Summary 2023

    Rolls-Royce SMR Ltd, project documentation and regulatory submissions to ONR for Generic Design Assessment (GDA) of the Rolls-Royce 470MW SMR design. GDA is the pre-licensing assessment process used in the UK for new reactor designs before a site-specific licence application. Rolls-Royce SMR entered GDA in 2022. Also referenced: NuScale Power LLC, NRC Standard Design Approval Documentation (NuScale US460 SDAA, US NRC, 2022). UK government SMR competition: Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, Small Modular Reactor Competition (DESNZ, 2023). Available: rolls-roycesmr.com; nrc.gov; gov.uk/government/collections/small-modular-reactors.

  • [I10]
    INDUSTRY Lloyd's — Nuclear Market Bulletin (NP/NL/NV Capacity and Conditions)

    Lloyd's of London, nuclear-related market bulletins issued by Lloyd's Market Association (LMA) and Lloyd's Underwriters Nuclear Risks Association (LUNRA). Lloyd's participates in nuclear insurance primarily through the NRI pool (UK) and through pool-to-pool international treaties. Specific bulletins include: LMA guidelines on nuclear exclusion clauses (NEAL 2003 and derivatives); Lloyd's nuclear risks underwriting guidance (restricted to Lloyd's market). Note: Lloyd's does not publish GWP data for nuclear at sub-class level. Nuclear is a small but technically demanding component of the Lloyd's energy book. Contact: lma.org.uk (restricted market access for specific technical bulletins).
