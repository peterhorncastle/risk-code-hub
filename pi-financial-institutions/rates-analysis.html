Four Market Cycle Eras

Era A · 2008–2012

GFC Claims Shock — Hard Market

+35–60%

Lehman, Madoff, LIBOR claims shock forces significant premium increases. Lloyd's syndicates hardened rates aggressively; some withdrew from retail IFA segment. Loss ratios peaked 120–140% for poorly underwritten books. EST

Era B · 2013–2017

RDR Market Rebuild — Soft to Stable

−5 to +5%

RDR restructures the IFA population — smaller, better-capitalised, better-trained. Premium volumes compress as smaller IFAs exit; rates soften slightly as claims frequency falls for remaining population. DB transfer claims begin emerging at tail. EST

Era C · 2018–2022

DB Transfer & MiFID II — Progressive Hardening

+15–40%

DB pension transfer claims wave drives sustained hardening. MiFID II compliance costs increase awareness of suitability exposure. LCF collapse reinforces regulatory tail risk. Sub-limits for DB advice become standard. EST

Era D · 2023–2026

Consumer Duty & ESG — Disciplined Market

+5 to +20%

Market maintains discipline ahead of anticipated Consumer Duty and ESG claims wave. Crypto sub-limits standard; ESG underwriting questions added to proposals. Rate increases moderating but not reversing. Capacity orderly. EST

Return on Equity — Bull / Base / Bear

Illustrative Lloyd's syndicate ROE for a £50m FI PI portfolio under three market scenarios (2024 underwriting year). Assumes 30% expense ratio, Lloyd's premium levy (3%), investment return 4.5%. ILLUS

Bull Scenario
18.5%

Assumptions:

  • Loss ratio: 55% (benign claims year)
  • Expense ratio: 30%
  • Combined ratio: 85%
  • Investment return: 4.5%
  • No Consumer Duty or ESG large losses
  • Continued rate adequacy +10%
Base Scenario
11.2%

Assumptions:

  • Loss ratio: 68% (moderate claims)
  • Expense ratio: 30%
  • Combined ratio: 98%
  • Investment return: 4.5%
  • 1–2 mid-size DB transfer aggregate losses
  • Rate change: flat to +5%
Bear Scenario
−4.8%

Assumptions:

  • Loss ratio: 95% (Consumer Duty wave)
  • Expense ratio: 30%
  • Combined ratio: 125%
  • Investment return: 4.5%
  • ESG claims materialise; DB transfer tail
  • Rate change: −5% (competitive pressure)
Key Profitability Drivers — FI PI: The class is highly sensitive to (1) DB pension transfer advice volume in the insured portfolio — this is the primary frequency driver; (2) the emergence timing of Consumer Duty claims — currently latent, expected to crystallise 2025–2027; (3) ESG greenwashing civil claims following regulatory enforcement (2–3 year lag from enforcement action); (4) investment return environment — FI PI policies typically have 18–36 month claim-to-payment cycles, making investment income a meaningful offset.
