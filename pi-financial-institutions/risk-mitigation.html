Module 06 — Risk Mitigation

PI E&O Financial Institutions — Risk Mitigation

Pre-loss controls, regulatory compliance standards, Consumer Duty implementation, and claims-stage guidance for FI PI insureds and their advisers.

Overview History Timeline Loss Database Underwriting Rates & ROE Risk Mitigation Global Programme References Rater

FI PI claims are fundamentally preventable through rigorous advice process documentation, regulatory compliance, and early complaints handling. The controls below reflect the industry consensus for firms seeking to minimise claims frequency and severity — and to maximise their position in coverage disputes if a claim does arise. EST

Prevention Controls

Prevent

Suitability Assessment Framework

Comprehensive client profiling covering risk appetite, investment knowledge, time horizon, financial circumstances, and sustainability preferences. MiFID II Article 25 standard for EU/EEA and post-Brexit FCA equivalent. Document the assessment process not just the outcome — the process is what survives a court challenge.

Highest effectiveness — primary defence
Prevent

Product Due Diligence & Governance

Pre-approval product governance process for any new product recommended to clients. Includes counterparty credit risk assessment, liquidity analysis, complexity evaluation, and retail suitability classification. Documented sign-off required before adviser recommends product to client base. Prevents Lehman notes / mini-bond class of claim.

High effectiveness — eliminates class risk
Prevent

DB Transfer Specialist Process

Defined benefit pension transfer advice requires: AF7-qualified adviser, written TVAS, dual sign-off for transfers above £500k CETV, record of client being told the recommendation against transfer is the default position. Ongoing FCA supervisory focus on DB transfer advice quality since British Steel case.

Critical for IFAs with DB transfer advice
Prevent

Introductions & Referral Due Diligence

Formal due diligence on all third-party introducers before accepting referred clients. Risk-based assessment of introducer's own regulatory status, incentive structure, and client source. Particular scrutiny for overseas pension transfer introducers and introducers referring to high-risk or illiquid investments.

Essential — AR and introducer claims are top claims driver
Prevent

Consumer Duty Outcome Monitoring

Post-sale monitoring of whether products continue to deliver good outcomes for clients — the ongoing obligation under FCA Consumer Duty (July 2023). Requires systematic product performance review, proactive client communication where outcomes deteriorate, and documented remediation where products underperform their marketed purpose.

Growing importance — Duty claims emerging from 2025
Prevent

ESG Methodology Documentation

For firms marketing ESG or sustainable mandates: written ESG investment policy, fund labelling review against SDR/SFDR categories, documented screening methodology, and third-party ESG data source documentation. Greenwashing claims rely on the gap between ESG marketing and actual portfolio composition.

Material for DFMs with ESG AUM >20%
Prevent

Vulnerable Client Identification

FCA Consumer Duty requires identification and appropriate treatment of vulnerable clients. Documented vulnerability identification process, enhanced suitability assessment for vulnerable clients, and advisor training on indicators of vulnerability (health, life events, resilience, capability). Elderly client advice (equity release, lifetime mortgage) highest exposure category.

FCA supervisory focus area — always document
Prevent

Conflict of Interest Management

Written conflicts policy identifying all sources of conflict (product commissions, soft dollar benefits, cross-selling incentives, related party transactions). Disclosed to clients in writing. Commission disclosure obligation under Consumer Duty and MiFID II — undisclosed commissions are a claims generator (see motor finance DCAs, PPI).

Statutory obligation — failure creates automatic PI exposure
Detect

Compliance Monitoring Programme

Risk-based file review of advice files on a sample or thematic basis. Frequency proportionate to risk: DB transfer files reviewed at 100% for first 12 months of any new adviser; other high-risk advice categories reviewed quarterly. Early detection of systemic advice failures before they become claims.

Systemic detection — prevents class action exposure
Detect

Complaints MI & Root Cause Analysis

Systematic management information on complaints by adviser, product category, and client segment. Monthly root cause analysis to identify emerging trends before they generate regulatory attention. FOS complaint patterns are a leading indicator of PI claim trends — address root causes before FCA supervision review.

Essential early warning system
Detect

Regulatory Horizon Scanning

Systematic monitoring of FCA consultation papers, policy statements, supervisory focus areas, and enforcement actions. Regulatory change typically precedes liability by 2–3 years — embedding new standards before enforcement begins reduces exposure. Consumer Duty outcome monitoring requirements are the current critical horizon.

Leads to pre-emptive process improvement
Respond

Early Complaint Resolution

FCA 8-week complaint resolution requirement. Early resolution at redress cost before FOS referral typically costs 30–50% less than FOS-adjudicated outcomes. Insured must notify FI PI insurer of any circumstances that may give rise to a claim — failure to notify promptly is a common coverage dispute trigger.

Significant cost reduction — notify insurer immediately
Respond

Insurer Notification Protocol

Establish a clear internal protocol for when complaints and circumstances must be notified to FI PI insurer. Common failures: (1) team leader resolves a complaint without notifying insurer and later larger claim arises from same fact pattern; (2) FCA investigation opened without insurer notification. Both create coverage disputes.

Coverage preservation — protocol must be written and trained
Recover

Run-Off PI Programme

FCA requires minimum 6-year run-off PI cover post-cessation of regulated activities. Many claims arise on advice given 3–5 years before the claim is made (discovery period on retirement advice, inheritance tax advice, long-term structured products). Run-off premium investment is a PI cost that cannot be avoided.

Regulatory requirement — plan for run-off costs

Adviser Guidance — Before & During a Claim

Before a Claim Arises — Pre-Loss Actions

  1. Document everything at the point of advice. The suitability letter, TVAS report, and client agreement are the primary defence documents. Recreating documentation after a complaint is discovered is potentially fraudulent and always counterproductive.
  2. Maintain a complaints register. All complaints — even verbal ones quickly resolved — should be logged with date, subject matter, and resolution. The FCA's 8-week rule applies from first written complaint; log verbal complaints with a written acknowledgement.
  3. Review product warnings proactively. When market conditions change (rate rises, market falls, credit events), review active product recommendations and contact clients where products have materially underperformed their described risk profile. Consumer Duty requires proactive action.
  4. Notify PI insurer of circumstances, not just claims. Any complaint that could develop into a claim, FCA inquiry, or FOS referral should be notified as a circumstance. Failure to notify circumstances promptly is a leading cause of coverage disputes at claim stage.
  5. Engage compliance adviser for high-risk advice. DB transfers, overseas pension transfers, and high-risk investment recommendations to retail clients should always be reviewed by an internal or external compliance adviser before advice is issued.

During a Claim or Investigation — Claims-Stage Actions

  1. Notify PI insurer immediately. As soon as a formal complaint, claim letter, FCA investigation notice, or FOS referral is received — contact the FI PI insurer without delay. Coverage preservation requires immediate notification. Do not attempt to resolve without insurer involvement.
  2. Preserve all records. Implement an immediate document preservation hold. Do not delete, amend, or 'improve' advice records after a complaint is received. Evidence of document manipulation destroys the defence and potentially coverage.
  3. Do not admit liability. Even where the advice was clearly poor, admission of liability without insurer involvement prejudices the insurer's subrogation rights and may invalidate coverage. Express sympathy to the client but do not admit wrongdoing.
  4. Co-operate with FCA investigations. FCA investigation co-operation is both a regulatory obligation and a coverage requirement. Appoint legal counsel with regulatory investigation experience. FI PI policies cover defence costs from first day of investigation — use them.
  5. Segregate complaints from the same root cause. Where multiple client complaints arise from the same advice failure or product recommendation, treat them as a potential aggregate claim. Notify the insurer of all related complaints simultaneously; aggregate deductibles and sub-limits may apply.
  6. Consider early settlement. FI PI insurers typically prefer early economic settlement to protracted litigation. Where claim merit is clear, early settlement is typically cheaper for all parties. Discuss settlement authority with insurer before responding to any claimant or FOS.
Coverage Forfeiture Risk: The three most common causes of FI PI coverage disputes are (1) late notification — complaints that become claims without being notified as circumstances; (2) retroactive date issues — advice given before the retroactive date on the current policy; and (3) dishonesty exclusion arguments — insurer contends adviser knew the advice was inappropriate. Advisers should treat all three as compliance matters, not just insurance matters. EST
