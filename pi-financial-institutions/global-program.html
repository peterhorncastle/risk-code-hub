International financial institutions operating across multiple jurisdictions require a programme structure that delivers simultaneous local coverage compliance (admitted requirements), global limit adequacy, and consistent policy wording. The complexity is compounded by varying regulatory minimum PI requirements across jurisdictions. EST

FI PI Global Programme Placement Workflow

  1. 1
    Risk Information Gathering (6–8 weeks pre-renewal) Obtain completed proposal form covering all entities, jurisdictions, revenue by advice category, AUM by geography, claims history (5 years), regulatory authorisations in each jurisdiction, and run-off cover requirements. Identify DB transfer, ESG, crypto exposures specifically.
  2. 2
    Master Policy Placement — Lloyd's / London Market Place master policy with lead underwriter. Establish coverage terms, retroactive date, deductible structure, sub-limits, and applicable endorsements (US nexus, crypto, ESG). Lloyd's lead's stamp provides the basis for all local policies.
  3. 3
    Jurisdiction Regulatory Compliance Review Map all operating jurisdictions to admitted/non-admitted requirements. Identify where local policy paper, fronting, or surplus lines access is required. Confirm minimum limits meet all local regulatory requirements — FCA, MiFID II, ASIC, MAS, SFC.
  4. 4
    Local Admitted Policies — Fronting Arrangement Where fronting required (India, Brazil, EU post-Brexit where member state requires local paper), arrange fronting agreement between Lloyd's and local insurer. Confirm follow-form language and DIC/DIL (Difference in Conditions / Difference in Limits) structure.
  5. 5
    Excess Layer Placement Place excess layers with consortium of Lloyd's syndicates and company market insurers. Brief each excess underwriter on master terms, sub-limits, and exclusions. Confirm follow-form language — excess policies must follow master terms exactly for consistent global coverage.
  6. 6
    Facultative Reinsurance (Cat Layer) For programmes above £25m total limit: approach specialist FI PI fac RI market for top-and-drop layer. FI PI fac RI capacity is limited — early market approach required (10–12 weeks pre-inception). Fac RI pricing driven by specific programme loss history and emerging ESG/Consumer Duty perception.
  7. 7
    Claims Co-ordination Protocol Establish multi-jurisdiction claims protocol before inception. Define lead insurer's rights, notification requirements in each jurisdiction, discovery rule variations (UK: actual discovery; US: occurrence vs claims-made), and choice of law. FI PI claims often span multiple jurisdictions — protocol prevents coverage disputes at claims stage.
  8. 8
    Policy Documentation & Confirmation of Cover Issue master policy, local policy certificates, and schedule of admitted compliance confirmation to client compliance team. Provide separate confirmation by jurisdiction for regulatory submission where required. Maintain programme diagram showing layers, deductibles, and sub-limits.

Illustrative Case Study — Pan-European IFA Network Programme Rebuild

Illustrative Case Study

Nordic Wealth Advisers Group — UK / Sweden / Germany / Netherlands FI PI Programme

Nordic Wealth is a pan-European IFA network (UK: FCA-authorised, Sweden: FI-authorised, Germany: BaFin-authorised, Netherlands: AFM-authorised) with 220 advisers, £1.8bn AUA, and significant DB pension transfer activity in the UK subsidiary (£95m CETV pa). Following two DB transfer claims in 2022 totalling £3.2m, incumbent insurer declined renewal. ILLUS

Programme Rebuild Challenges

  • UK subsidiary — DB transfer sub-limit required; prior claims must be disclosed
  • German entity — BaFin requires locally admitted policy paper (GDV form)
  • Swedish entity — Finansinspektionen requires MiFID II minimum limits in SEK
  • Netherlands — AFM accepts Lloyd's direct; MiFID II minimum limit required
  • Prior insurer declined — market concerned about DB transfer volume
  • ESG mandate AUM at 18% of total — emerging underwriting question

Programme Solution

  • Master policy: Lloyd's lead, £3m any-one-claim, £6m aggregate
  • UK DB transfer sub-limit: £1.5m any-one-claim / £4m aggregate (new DB transfer compliance framework submitted)
  • Germany: GDV-form local policy via Munich fronting insurer; follow-form Lloyd's
  • Sweden: Lloyd's direct (FI-accepted); €1.25m minimum confirmed in SEK equivalent
  • Netherlands: Lloyd's direct; €1.25m minimum confirmed
  • ESG mandate endorsement: £500k sub-limit; ESG methodology documentation submitted
  • Premium: £310k pa (prior: £185k) — +68% reflecting DB transfer history and market change
  • Deductible: £25k per claim (prior: £10k); aggregate deductible cap: £150k

Key outcome: DB transfer compliance framework (including dual sign-off, AF7 qualification confirmation for all 14 DB-qualified advisers, and monthly compliance file review) was the critical enabler for Lloyd's market acceptance. Without this, the risk would have been unplaceable following the prior claims. ILLUS

DIC/DIL — Difference in Conditions / Difference in Limits: When a fronted local policy is narrower than the master Lloyd's policy (due to local regulatory form requirements or local wording constraints), a DIC/DIL endorsement on the master Lloyd's policy fills the gap. This ensures the insured does not suffer a coverage gap between what the local policy provides and what the master intends to cover. DIC/DIL is standard practice for global FI PI programmes with admitted fronts in India, Brazil, or other restricted jurisdictions. EST
