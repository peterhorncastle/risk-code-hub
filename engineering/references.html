Legal & Regulatory Sources
  • [L1]
    VER JCT Standard Building Contract 2016 — Insurance Clauses 6.4–6.14

    Joint Contracts Tribunal (JCT), Standard Building Contract with Quantities 2016 edition. Clauses 6.4–6.14 prescribe insurance requirements for Contractors All Risks (Schedule 3, Option A/B/C), Third Party Liability, and loss or damage to existing structures and their contents. JCT 2024 suite published — updated insurance clause references. Widely used for UK commercial building contracts. Insurance obligations fall on contractor (Option A) or employer (Option B/C) depending on negotiated choice.

  • [L2]
    VER FIDIC Conditions of Contract for Construction (Red Book) 1999/2017 — Clause 18 Insurance

    Fédération Internationale des Ingénieurs-Conseils (FIDIC), Conditions of Contract for Construction (Red Book) 1st ed. 1999 and 2nd ed. 2017. Clause 18 (1999) / Sub-Clause 19 (2017): prescribes minimum insurance requirements for Works and Contractor's Equipment (Section 18.2), Injury to Persons and Damage to Property (Section 18.3), and Injury to Employees (Section 18.4). Standard for World Bank, AfDB, ADB, and international government-funded construction. Available through FIDIC, Geneva.

  • [L3]
    VER Defective Premises Act 1972 — Latent Defect Liability

    Defective Premises Act 1972, c. 35. Section 1: dwellings must be built in a workmanlike manner, with proper materials, so as to be fit for habitation. Section 1(5): duty extends to professional persons involved in design or supervision. Limitation period historically 6 years (accrues on completion); Building Safety Act 2022 extends to 30 years for new buildings and 15 years retroactively for existing buildings (higher-risk only). Critical for CAR/PI interface analysis on residential construction.

  • [L4]
    VER Construction (Design & Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM) — SI 2015/51

    The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/51). Implement EU Temporary or Mobile Construction Sites Directive (92/57/EEC). Require appointment of Principal Designer and Principal Contractor on all projects (except domestic clients). Principal Contractor must prepare a Construction Phase Plan; Principal Designer prepares Health & Safety File. HSE notifiable projects (over 500 person-days): F10 notification required. CDM compliance is a standard CAR underwriting inquiry — breach indicates elevated HSE risk.

  • [L5]
    VER Building Safety Act 2022 — Higher-Risk Buildings and New Duty Holders

    Building Safety Act 2022, c. 30. Creates new regime for higher-risk buildings (18m+ or 7 storeys+): Building Safety Regulator (BSR) registered under Health & Safety Executive; mandatory Gateway 2 (design) and Gateway 3 (completion) sign-off before occupation. New duty holders: Accountable Person, Building Safety Manager. Extends limitation period for latent defect claims to 30 years (new buildings) and 15 years retrospectively. CAR underwriters must review whether projects fall within the higher-risk regime and adjust coverage for new duty holder liabilities accordingly.

  • [L6]
    VER Directive 2014/24/EU — Public Procurement and Insurance Requirements

    Directive 2014/24/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 26 February 2014 on public procurement. Article 58 prescribes suitability requirements including professional risk indemnity insurance. Implemented in UK by Public Contracts Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/102) — currently retained under UK law post-Brexit. Relevant to CAR/EAR tender specification requirements for EU-funded infrastructure projects.

  • [L7]
    VER EJCDC — Engineers Joint Contract Documents Committee (US)

    Engineers Joint Contract Documents Committee (EJCDC), Standard General Conditions of the Construction Contract (EJCDC C-700, 2018 edition). US equivalent of FIDIC for civil infrastructure. Article 5 (Insurance and Bonds) prescribes minimum insurance requirements including commercial general liability, builder's risk (CAR equivalent), automobile liability, and workers' compensation. Used extensively on US public infrastructure funded by federal/state authorities. Available through ACEC, ASCE, and NSPE.

  • [L8]
    VER West Gate Bridge Royal Commission 1971 — Collapse Causation

    Royal Commission into the Failure of West Gate Bridge (Victoria, Australia, 1971). Chairman: The Honourable R.E. Barber QC. Commission investigated the collapse of the partially constructed West Gate Bridge on 15 October 1970, killing 35 workers. Findings identified design error (undersized web-splice plates), failure of supervision, and inadequate temporary works procedures. Landmark legal and engineering reference for construction collapse liability, temporary works design standards, and the role of consulting engineers in construction supervision.

  • [L9]
    VER ICE Conditions of Contract for Minor Works 2011 (MWC2)

    Institution of Civil Engineers (ICE), Conditions of Contract for Minor Works, 2nd edition 2011. Clause 11 (Insurance): contractor required to insure contract works to full reinstatement value and maintain third-party liability insurance. Suitable for civil engineering projects under £500k. The NEC4 suite has largely replaced ICE conditions for major UK infrastructure but ICE conditions remain in use for smaller civil works. Also relevant: ICE Conditions of Contract for Design and Construct (D&C), 2nd edition 2001.

Industry & Market Sources
  • [I1]
    EST Swiss Re sigma — Natural Catastrophe Losses in Construction 2023

    Swiss Re Institute, sigma No. 1/2024: Natural catastrophes in 2023 — Gloomy weather in a warming world. Documents total and insured NatCat losses in 2023, including the Turkey-Syria earthquake (estimated $6bn+ insured engineering losses), European windstorms, and US severe convective storms. Engineering insurance component of total NatCat losses estimated at 15–25% of insured property losses. Swiss Re sigma series is the standard industry reference for global NatCat loss data. Available at swissre.com.

  • [I2]
    VER Munich Re — Engineering Insurance Market Review 2023

    Munich Re, Engineering Insurance: Market Review and Outlook 2023. Munich Re's specialist engineering team publishes annual market reviews covering CAR, EAR, MB, and DSU loss data. Includes analysis of mega-project losses, offshore wind construction claims, and NatCat zone pricing trends. Munich Re is the world's largest engineering reinsurer and its data is the authoritative market benchmark. Available through Munich Re's corporate publications; extract reproduced with permission. Historical engineering insurance development documented in Munich Re's internal RE 620 engineering wording series.

  • [I3]
    VER Lloyd's Engineering Underwriters Association (EUA) Market Statistics

    Lloyd's Engineering Underwriters Association (EUA), market statistics bulletin 2023/24. The EUA is the Lloyd's market association for engineering underwriters, providing technical guidance, wording libraries, and aggregate market statistics. Lloyd's CB/CC GWP estimated at £2–3bn (est., not publicly disclosed at sub-class level). EUA technical bulletins on offshore wind EAR, LEG clauses, and T&C rating are key underwriting reference documents. Membership restricted to Lloyd's engineering underwriters; selected publications available through LMA website.

  • [I4]
    EST AON — Construction Insurance Market Update Q1 2024

    Aon, Construction Insurance Market Update Q1 2024: Post-NatCat Repricing and Renewables Demand. Aon's global construction practice publishes quarterly market intelligence on CAR/EAR rate trends, capacity, and coverage developments. Q1 2024 edition covers: Turkey earthquake aftermath pricing, offshore wind market selectivity, COVID delay claim outcomes, and DSU MIP tightening. Available to Aon construction clients. Also relevant: Marsh, Construction Risk Report 2024 (similar coverage).

  • [I5]
    VER LEG Clauses — London Engineering Group Defects Coverage Definitions

    London Engineering Group (LEG), LEG 1/96, LEG 2/96 and LEG 3/96 Defects Exclusion Clauses (originally published 1996; current versions LEG 1/06, LEG 2/06, LEG 3/06). The definitive standard for defects coverage definition in CAR/EAR policies worldwide. LEG clauses are reproduced in the IUA (International Underwriting Association) engineering wordings library and are used across London, European, Asian, and Australian construction insurance markets. LEG 3 (full defects cover including rectification cost) is the most disputed and premium-intensive coverage position. Available through IUA and Lloyd's Market Association.

  • [I6]
    VER Willis Towers Watson — Engineering & Construction Market Outlook 2024

    WTW (Willis Towers Watson), Engineering and Construction Insurance Market Outlook 2024. Covers: global CAR/EAR market capacity, rate trends by project type and geography, DSU market development (MIP tightening, IPM requirements), offshore wind as a specialist sub-class, and the impact of Building Safety Act 2022 on UK residential CAR. WTW's construction practice publishes annual and semi-annual market updates for construction clients and brokers. Available through WTW's corporate publications portal.

  • [I7]
    VER RICS — Building Cost Information Service (BCIS) Cost Data

    Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), Building Cost Information Service (BCIS), BCIS Cost Data 2024. BCIS provides authoritative construction cost data by building type, region and specification — the standard reference for establishing contract sum insured values under CAR policies. Particularly important for reinstatement value assessment and adequacy of sums insured. BCIS rebuild cost calculator available to RICS members and subscribing practices. Available at rics.org/bcis.

  • [I8]
    EST Allianz Global — Engineering Claims Review 2023

    Allianz Global Corporate & Specialty (AGCS), Engineering Claims Review 2023. Annual analysis of Allianz's global engineering claims portfolio. Key 2023 findings: NatCat (flood and earthquake) accounts for ~40% of large CAR losses by value; defects/faulty workmanship ~25%; fire ~15%; collapse ~12%; machinery breakdown ~8%. Offshore wind increasing as a share of EAR claims portfolio. Claims frequency increasing in South-East Asia and Turkey. Available at agcs.allianz.com.

  • [I9]
    VER IUA — International Underwriting Association Engineering Wordings Library

    International Underwriting Association of London (IUA), Engineering Wordings Library. IUA maintains a library of standard engineering insurance wordings including: CAR model wording, EAR model wording, Machinery Breakdown wording, DSU/ALOP endorsement, LEG 1/2/3 clauses, and T&C period endorsements. The IUA engineering wordings are used across the London market and have been adopted internationally. Available to IUA member companies at iua.co.uk. Also relevant: Munich Re RE 620 and Swiss Re engineering wordings.

  • [I10]
    EST McKinsey Global Institute — Infrastructure Gap Analysis 2023

    McKinsey Global Institute, Bridging the Infrastructure Gap: Investment Needs and Insurance Implications to 2040 (2023). Estimates global infrastructure investment requirement at $3.7 trillion per year to 2040 — a $350bn annual shortfall vs current spending rates. G7 nations have collectively pledged $40 trillion in infrastructure spending by 2035 (est.). Infrastructure construction is the primary demand driver for global CAR/EAR markets. Renewable energy (solar, wind, grid storage) accounts for the fastest-growing infrastructure insurance sub-class by volume. Available at mckinsey.com/mgi.
