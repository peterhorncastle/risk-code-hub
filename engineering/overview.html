Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

CB — Contractors All Risks (CAR)

Contractors All Risks covers physical loss of or damage to contract works (buildings under construction, civil engineering structures) during the construction period, including materials on site, temporary works and contractors' plant and equipment. Also covers third-party liability (TPL) arising out of the construction operations. Written on an all-risks basis subject to named exclusions. The principal peril drivers are NatCat (flood, storm, earthquake), fire, collapse and third-party bodily injury. CB covers civil engineering (roads, bridges, dams, tunnels) and general building works. [1]

CC — Erection All Risks (EAR) + Machinery Breakdown (MB)

Erection All Risks covers mechanical, electrical and electromechanical plant during installation, testing and commissioning — turbines, generators, processing equipment, offshore platforms, wind turbines. The CC code also encompasses Machinery Breakdown (MB) cover for operational plant post-completion, providing first-party sudden and accidental breakdown of insured machinery. The Testing & Commissioning (T&C) period is a critical sub-section of EAR, typically attracting a separate rate as defect-related losses peak during first operation of new plant. [2]

DSU / ALOP — Delay in Start-Up

Delay in Start-Up (DSU) — also marketed as Advanced Loss of Profits (ALOP) — covers the financial consequences of a delay to project completion caused by an insured physical damage event under the underlying CAR/EAR policy. DSU pays the principal's lost revenue or additional finance costs for the period between the planned and actual completion date. DSU is a material coverage for revenue-generating infrastructure (power stations, toll roads, offshore oil platforms). Maximum indemnity period (MIP) and indemnity basis are key underwriting parameters. [3]

Coverage Taxonomy

Contract Works

Physical loss or damage to the permanent and temporary works during the construction period. The broadest section — covers the project itself from first breaking of ground to practical completion. NatCat perils (flood, storm, earthquake) are the primary drivers of large losses. Reinstatement value basis. [4]

Plant & Equipment

Contractors' own plant, machinery and equipment — tower cranes, excavators, concrete pumps, scaffolding. Typically written at market value or agreed value; mobile plant faces accumulation risk across multiple sites. Subject to maintenance and roadworthiness warranties.

Third Party Liability (TPL)

Liability to third parties for bodily injury or property damage arising from construction operations. The most litigated section. Covers members of the public, adjacent property owners, and utility owners. Collapses, falling objects, vibration damage and traffic management failures are primary TPL triggers. Indemnity to principal clauses are standard. [5]

Delay in Start-Up (DSU/ALOP)

Pays the principal's financial loss (revenue, finance costs, fixed costs) during the delay period caused by a physical damage event. Only triggered by an insured loss under the underlying CAR/EAR policy. Maximum Indemnity Period (MIP) typically 12–36 months. Critical for power generation, toll roads, airports. [3]

Machinery Breakdown (MB)

Post-completion first-party cover for sudden and accidental breakdown of operational plant — boilers, turbines, generators, pressure vessels, electrical switchgear. Covers repair/replacement costs but not wear and tear, gradual deterioration or inherent vice. Typically written standalone or combined with EAR (CC code). [6]

Advanced Loss of Profits (ALOP — Post-Completion)

Business interruption coverage triggered by Machinery Breakdown post-completion. Covers revenue loss during machinery repair periods. Indemnity period and daily indemnity amount are key parameters. Particularly relevant for power generation (per-MW per-day basis) and process plant.

LEG Clause Explainer — Defects Coverage Definitions:
The London Engineering Group (LEG) developed three standard clauses to define how defects in design, materials or workmanship are treated under CAR/EAR policies. LEG clauses are among the most negotiated coverage issues in construction insurance.

LEG 1 (Exclusion 006): All costs of making good defects in design, materials or workmanship are excluded — the most restrictive position. The insurer pays for damage to surrounding works but not the defective item itself, and only if the damage can be separated from the defect.

LEG 2 (Exclusion 007): Covers sudden and accidental physical loss or damage to the surrounding works caused by a defect, but excludes the cost of correcting the defect itself. The standard market compromise position.

LEG 3 (Exclusion 008): The broadest coverage — pays the full cost of rectification including the defective portion, subject to exclusion of pre-existing defects known to the insured. Demanded by major contractors and EPC principals; commands significant additional premium. [I5]
NatCat Key Driver: Natural catastrophe perils — flood, earthquake, windstorm — are the primary drivers of large CAR losses. Geolocation in high NatCat zones (Turkey, Japan, South-East Asia, US Gulf Coast) is the single most important rating variable for large civil engineering projects. The 2023 Kahramanmaras earthquake in Turkey generated an estimated $6bn+ in insured engineering losses across ongoing construction projects and operational plant. [I1]
