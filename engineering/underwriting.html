Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Contract Type & Project Value (PML Basis)

Whether the policy covers CAR (civil/building) or EAR (mechanical/electrical) fundamentally drives the rate. Project value (contract sum or erection value) on a Probable Maximum Loss (PML) basis drives the premium base. EAR carries higher T&C risk than pure civil CAR. Offshore and subsea projects attract the highest rates. [I2]

Rate on line: 0.10–0.60% for civil CAR; 0.20–1.50% for EAR T&C-heavy
High Impact

Project Duration & Construction Period

Extended programme duration increases cumulative NatCat season exposure, extends TPL exposure, and raises the probability of a large loss event. Multi-year programmes (3–7 years) are risk-multiplied compared to 12-month builds. Annual project values or day-rate structures may apply for large programmes. [I6]

Duration loading: +15–30% for 3-year vs 1-year programme at equivalent contract value
High Impact

Geolocation & NatCat Zone Exposure

The single largest rate variable for large projects. Earthquake zones (Japan, Turkey, Central America, Western USA), flood plains (South-East Asia, river valleys), wind/typhoon tracks (China, Philippines, Japan, Caribbean, Gulf Coast USA) and avalanche zones all attract significant NatCat loading. Lloyd's risk codes by postcode/geocode are applied. [I1]

NatCat loading: 1.5–5.0× base rate for highest-hazard earthquake/typhoon zones
High Impact

Principal & Contractor Track Record

Contractor experience on similar project type, prior claims history (3–5 year CAR claims record), financial strength (solvency risk for completion guarantee), HSE record, and quality of management systems (ISO 9001, CDM compliance). Repeat programmes with blue-chip contractors attract preferential rates. [L4]

Track record credit: −15 to −25% for repeat blue-chip contractor with clean 5-year CAR history
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

LEG Clause Selected

The choice of LEG 1, 2 or 3 materially affects the premium. LEG 3 (full defects cover including cost of rectification) commands 20–50% additional premium over LEG 2. The LEG clause also signals the contractor's view of design risk — a demand for LEG 3 implies design complexity and innovation risk. [I5]

LEG 3 loading vs LEG 2: +20 to +50% on contract works premium
Medium Impact

Testing & Commissioning (T&C) Period

The T&C section of EAR policies is separately rated. The commissioning period — when plant is first operated under load — carries the highest defect-related loss probability. T&C duration (typically 3–12 months) and the complexity of the plant being commissioned (power generation, offshore, process plant) drive T&C rates. [I9]

T&C rate: typically 0.20–0.80% of erection value for 6-month T&C period on complex plant
Medium Impact

Subcontractor Prequalification

Main contractor's subcontractor selection and oversight processes. Poorly prequalified subcontractors are a primary source of quality deficiencies and temporary works failures. Underwriters increasingly require evidence of subcontractor prequalification registers and audit processes, particularly for specialist civil and mechanical works. [L4]

Subcontractor management quality: moderate positive or negative rating adjustment
Medium Impact

DSU/ALOP — Indemnity Period & Basis

DSU/ALOP is rated on the Maximum Indemnity Period (MIP), the estimated daily financial loss to the principal (revenue, finance costs), and the likelihood of a delay caused by an insured event. Power generation (£/MWh per day) and toll road DSU are most complex. DSU typically adds 25–60% to the base CAR/EAR premium. [I6]

DSU loading: typically 25–60% on top of base CAR/EAR GWP depending on MIP and loss basis
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Site Security & Perimeter Protection

Secured perimeter fencing, CCTV, on-site security staff and materials storage management reduce opportunistic theft of plant, materials and equipment. Important for urban sites with high materials exposure. Typical CAR theft from site loss: £5k–£500k range.

Lower Impact

Maintenance Period Duration

The maintenance period (typically 12 months post-practical completion) during which the contractor remains responsible for defects. Longer maintenance periods (24–36 months, common in EPC contracts) extend the policy period and carry additional IBNR for latent defect discovery during maintenance.

Lower Impact

Performance Bond & Collateral

Whether a performance bond (typically 10% of contract value) is in place gives underwriters comfort on contractor completion risk. Bonds do not reduce insured loss but reduce completion risk exposure and signal creditor confidence in the contractor.

Lower Impact

Professional Indemnity Interface

How the CAR programme coordinates with the designer's PI cover (typically 10% professional fee/limit) affects the analysis of defect losses at the boundary. CAR underwriters increasingly require evidence of the design team's PI cover and the indemnity to principal clause in the engineering appointment. [L1]

Pure delay without physical damageCAR/EAR requires physical damage trigger for DSU activationNo standard market cover for pure delay risk (e.g. COVID-type delay)
DSU Coverage Gap — Physical Damage Trigger: A common misconception among construction clients is that DSU/ALOP cover pays for all project delays, including supplier delays, permitting delays, or force majeure events such as COVID-19. In fact, DSU responds only where a delay is caused by a physical loss or damage event insured under the underlying CAR/EAR policy. Pure time-related delay risk remains uninsured under standard construction programmes. Underwriters should ensure DSU MIP, indemnity basis and notification obligations are clearly communicated at inception. [I6]
