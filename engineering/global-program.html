Master vs Project-Specific Policy

Master Annual Programme (MAP)

  • Single annual policy covering all projects started during the programme year, up to a declared project value threshold
  • Premium based on annual turnover or anticipated project values — adjustable at year end
  • Suitable for contractors with regular, homogeneous project profile (e.g. residential housebuilder, infrastructure O&M contractor)
  • Individual project limits apply; mega-projects exceeding threshold require separate notification/endorsement
  • Simplified administration but potentially inadequate limits for large one-off projects
  • DSU typically not available on MAP basis — requires project-specific attachment

Project-Specific Policy

  • Single policy for a named project, tailored to the specific contract sum, duration, location and risk profile
  • Allows bespoke coverage: LEG 3, DSU/ALOP, specific T&C section, vessel schedule (offshore), NatCat zone loading calibrated to site geocode
  • Required for major infrastructure (£50m+), mega-projects, offshore, nuclear, and projects in high NatCat zones
  • Higher acquisition cost but more accurate pricing and coverage alignment
  • Enables London market lead with full control of coverage terms and conditions
  • DSU/ALOP, Subcontractors' All Risks (SAR) and endorsements all available at project level
Jurisdiction Profiles — 6 Key Markets

United Kingdom

JCT (Joint Contracts Tribunal) standard building contracts and NEC4 contracts dominate. ICE conditions for major civil works. Employers' Liability (EL) insurance compulsory under Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — written separately from CAR/TPL. Building Safety Act 2022 creates new duty holders and 30-year latent defect liability for higher-risk buildings (18m+). Construction (Design & Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM) impose mandatory Principal Designer and Principal Contractor roles. [L4][L5]

European Union

CE marking requirements for construction products (CPR Regulation EU 305/2011). Eurocodes (EN 1990 series) govern structural design standards. Local admitted CAR/EAR required in France (French Construction Decennial — Dommages Ouvrage — is a mandatory 10-year latent defect cover for buildings, separate from CAR). Germany requires Bauherrenhaftpflicht (developer TPL) and local engineering insurance placement for publicly funded projects. Public procurement Directive 2014/24/EU mandates insurance requirements in tender specifications. [L6]

United States

AIA (American Institute of Architects) standard contracts dominate commercial building. EJCDC (Engineers Joint Contract Documents Committee) for civil infrastructure. Builder's Risk (US equivalent of CAR contract works cover) and Commercial General Liability (CGL equivalent of TPL) are the standard products. Surety bonds (bid bond, performance bond, payment bond) are contractually required on federal/public projects under the Miller Act. Admitted insurance required in most states; Lloyd's S&L placement common for complex/large risks. Strict project close-out and PC certificate notice obligations. [L7]

Middle East

FIDIC contracts (Red, Silver, Gold Book) are the dominant standard for major infrastructure — standard on World Bank, Islamic Development Bank and Abu Dhabi/Dubai government-funded projects. Clause 18 (Insurance) of FIDIC 1999/2017 prescribes minimum CAR/EAR/TPL insurance requirements. Local insurance regulators (SAMA Saudi Arabia, CBUAE UAE) increasingly require local admitted placement. QA/QC requirements stringent on Vision 2030 Saudi Arabia mega-projects. Accumulation of construction exposure in UAE (Expo 2020 legacy, NEOM Saudi) driven significant market activity. [L2]

Asia-Pacific

Diverse market: Japan, South Korea, Australia (well-developed, admitted required); South-East Asia and Pacific Islands (emerging, significant NatCat exposure). Typhoon (Japan, Philippines, China), earthquake (Japan, New Zealand, Taiwan) and flood (Bangladesh, Vietnam, Thailand) are primary NatCat perils. Australia requires local admitted placement; Suncorp, QBE and IAG are domestic leaders with Lloyd's often reinsuring. Chinese domestic market has PICC and People's Insurance as dominant CAR writers; international programmes for multinational EPC contractors may have London DIC components.

Africa

Infrastructure investment boom driven by China Belt & Road Initiative, World Bank infrastructure financing, and African Development Bank projects generates significant CAR/EAR exposure. Fronting requirements in many jurisdictions (Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa) require local admitted policy — fronting insurer reinsures 90%+ to London. Currency risk (premium and claims in local currency vs USD/GBP programme) is a significant programme design challenge. Political Risk interface (civil unrest, government action, currency inconvertibility) for remote infrastructure projects. Lloyd's international wrap critical to provide coverage depth unavailable from local markets.

FIDIC Contract Types — Insurance Relevance
Red Book

Conditions of Contract for Construction (1999/2017)

Traditional civil engineering — employer designs, contractor builds. Clause 18 requires CAR (works), TPL, and employer/contractor liability insurance. Contractor bears construction risk. Standard for World Bank infrastructure. Risk allocation favours employer.

Yellow Book

Conditions of Contract for Plant & Design-Build (1999/2017)

Contractor designs and builds — EAR relevance higher as contractor takes design risk. Clause 18 insurance requirements broadly similar to Red Book but contractor's design risk increases PI interface with EAR LEG clause selection. T&C testing obligations specified.

Silver Book

EPC / Turnkey (1999/2017)

Contractor takes full engineering, procurement and construction risk — turn-key delivery. Dominant in power generation, process plant. Contractor bears greater design risk — LEG 3 most appropriate. DSU/ALOP critical for principal protecting revenue. Risk-heavy for contractor; requires robust EAR programme.

Gold Book

Design-Build-Operate (2008)

Contractor designs, builds and operates for a concession period. Insurance transitions from CAR (construction) to property/MB (operational) at practical completion. DSU transitions to business interruption post-completion. Complex programme design: construction insurer and operational insurer must coordinate on handover.
