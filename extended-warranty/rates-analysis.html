Market Rate Environment (2000–2026)

Extended warranty is not a commodity market with a single pricing cycle — it is a portfolio of sub-classes (electronics, white goods, motor, home) each driven by distinct loss trends, regulatory interventions, and technology cycles. The market does not follow the classic Lloyd's hard/soft cycle to the same degree as short-tail property or liability lines, but significant repricing events occur following mass defect events, regulatory interventions, and inflationary periods. IBNR and Reserve Development Characteristics

Extended warranty is a relatively short-tail class compared to liability lines. Consumer electronics claims are typically reported and settled within 6–18 months of the warranty event. Motor MBI has a slightly longer tail — complex powertrain repairs may involve authorised dealer backlogs — but is still substantially settled within 24 months of the event year. EST

The key reserving risk is mass defect IBNR — where a product line is failing at rates significantly above priced expectations but claims have not yet been fully reported. Gaming console and white goods mass defects have historically generated IBNR development over 3–5 years as the full affected population works through failures at different rates across the bathtub curve. Underwriters writing multi-year programme contracts should carry explicit IBNR margins of 10–20% of annual claims for mature programmes.

Obsolescence reserves are a unique feature of extended warranty. Unlike most insurance lines, the cost of a future claim may exceed the current cost if: (a) the product is discontinued and parts become scarce; (b) supply chain disruption inflates parts costs; or (c) technological change means replacement units cost more than the original product (e.g. OLED television replacing LCD). Explicit obsolescence reserving of 2–5% of claims is considered best practice for programmes with 3+ year terms. EST
