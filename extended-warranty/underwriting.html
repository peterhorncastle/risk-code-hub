The Extended Warranty Underwriting Model

Extended warranty insurance is underwritten on a frequency–severity (F×S) model rather than a large-loss model. The risk profile is characterised by high claim frequency, low-to-moderate severity, and predictable aggregate loss patterns — more similar to motor or household insurance than to specialty liability. EST

The underwriter's core task is to assess: (1) the likelihood that a covered product will fail during the warranty period; (2) the cost of repair or replacement when it does; and (3) the programme's structure — whether the insurer bears first-loss risk or sits above a retention held by the programme administrator.

Key Rating Factors

High Impact

Product Category & Sub-Category

Loss ratios vary dramatically: mobile device accidental damage 60–80%; white goods M&EB 25–40%; HVAC systems 35–55%; gaming consoles 20–50% depending on generation. Category is the primary determinant of expected loss ratio. EST

High Impact

Product Age at Warranty Inception

Failure probability rises sharply with product age (bathtub curve). New products: low failure rate in the early "infant mortality" period. 3–5 year products: entering the wear-out phase — significantly higher claim frequency. Key variable for MBI motor products. EST

High Impact

Manufacturer / Brand Quality Rating

Historical failure rates by OEM brand are tracked by major insurers. Premium brands (Apple, Bosch, Miele) typically have lower failure rates than budget or mid-range brands. Underwriters apply a brand quality loading (+/− 10–25%) to base rates. EST

High Impact

Warranty Term & Coverage Scope

Longer terms expose the insurer to more of the wear-out curve. Accidental damage cover significantly increases frequency vs M&EB-only. Cover for cosmetic damage, data recovery, or screen replacement requires separate loading. Term pricing is typically non-linear — a 3-year term is priced at more than 3× the 1-year rate.

Medium Impact

Programme Retention / Attachment Point

Where the insurer sits above a retention held by the administrator, the layer's loss ratio and pure IBNER development differ from first-loss programmes. Stop-loss structures above an 80% or 90% loss ratio are common for large administrators. The attachment point materially affects expected burning cost. EST

Medium Impact

Parts & Labour Inflation Assumptions

Repair costs are driven by parts availability, technician labour rates, and logistics. Post-COVID inflation in parts and labour significantly increased claims costs. Underwriters now model inflation escalation explicitly — typically CPI + 2–4% for consumer electronics components. EST

Medium Impact

Distribution Channel

Point-of-sale retail capture produces adverse selection (consumers who feel anxious about the product or are not sophisticated) but also includes lower-risk consumers buying impulsively. Direct mail, online subscription, and renewal markets have different risk profiles. Claims frequency typically highest on direct-to-consumer channels. EST

Medium Impact

Geographic Market (Regulatory & Legal)

Statutory consumer rights vary significantly — UK/EU 2-year guarantee narrows coverage gap; US statutory protection lower; Australia Consumer Law medium-term. Fraud rates differ by geography: US accidental damage fraud rates significantly higher than UK.

Lower Impact

Service Network Quality (TPA / Repair Network)

Claims costs depend heavily on whether the insurer controls repair network or reimburses any repairer. Authorised repair networks (Apple Authorised Resellers, Bosch Service Points) reduce cost vs open networks. TPA claims handling efficiency affects IBNR development speed.

Lower Impact

Obsolescence & Parts Availability

Products more than 3 years old face increasing risk of parts unavailability, forcing replacement at current market prices (often higher than original purchase price). This is a longer-term programme risk that requires an explicit obsolescence reserve for multi-year programmes.

Lower Impact

Excess / Deductible per Claim

Consumer excess (e.g. £100 screen replacement excess) significantly reduces frequency of minor accidental damage claims. Home warranty service call fees ($100–$125 per visit) manage HVAC claim frequency. Excess design is a key programme structure lever.

High Impact

Fraud Rate by Segment

Accidental damage claims on smartphones have fraud rates estimated at 5–15% of claims in some US markets. Staged damage, multiple claims, false purchase dates, and organised rings are common. Fraud loading is applied to AD cover — typically +8–15% on gross premium in high-fraud geographies. EST

Premium Formula

// Extended Warranty — Insurance Premium Calculation (programme basis)
Gross Premium = (Expected Claims Cost) ÷ (1 − Expense Ratio − Target Profit Margin)

Expected Claims Cost = Premium Volume × Expected Loss Ratio
Expected Loss Ratio = Base Loss Ratio × Brand Factor × Age Factor × Term Factor × Geography Factor × Fraud Loading

// Typical programme expense structure:
Retail Commission: 20–40% of consumer premium
TPA / Admin: 10–15%
Insurer Acquisition: 3–5%
Claims Handling: 5–8%
// Target combined ratio: 85–95% (insurer layer)
Target Underwriting Profit: 5–15%

⚠️ EV Battery Underwriting — Proceed with Caution

Electric vehicle battery extended warranty is an emerging and poorly-priced class. Key underwriting concerns:

  • Catastrophic failure vs degradation: Statutory warranties cover failure; extended warranties must define whether "capacity below X%" is a trigger. Litigation risk on boundary.
  • Parts availability: Battery packs for vehicles >5 years old may be unavailable or cost more than the vehicle's market value.
  • Technology obsolescence: Battery technology is evolving rapidly — solid-state batteries (2027–2030?) may make current chemistry obsolete before policies expire.
  • No credible long-run loss history: Mass-market EV fleet is less than 10 years old. Loss development beyond Year 5 is essentially unknown. GAP
  • Underwriting recommendation: Limit terms to 3 years; require Battery State of Health (SoH) diagnostic at inception (>80%); impose per-battery sub-limits; use stop-loss above 85% LR.
