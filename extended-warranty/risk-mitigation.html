Risk Mitigation — Overview

Extended warranty risk management operates across four distinct layers: underwriting controls (what you write and how you price it), fraud prevention (preventing illegitimate claims), programme oversight (monitoring TPA and administrator performance), and emerging risk management (EV batteries, new technology categories). Unlike property or liability lines, the insurer is typically one step removed from the consumer — making TPA quality the single most important ongoing risk management lever. EST

Underwriting Controls

Rate Credit −10–20%

Product Quality Audit (Pre-binding)

Independent technical assessment of the product being warranted — failure rate data, reliability test results, known defect history, manufacturer quality management system (ISO 9001 or equivalent). Critical for new product launches where no claims history exists. VER

Rate Credit −10–15%

Inception Diagnostic / Inspection

For motor MBI and EV warranty: diagnostic check at policy inception to confirm vehicle health. Battery State of Health (SoH) for EVs — minimum 80% required. Pre-existing condition exclusions supported by documented condition report. EST

Rate Credit −8–12%

TPA Pre-approval and Governance

Administrator must be approved by the insurer prior to programme launch. Governance requirements: minimum net worth, claims handling service standards (72-hour response SLA), complaints handling procedure, financial reporting to insurer monthly. VER

Rate Credit −5–8%

Consumer Excess / Deductible

Per-claim excess reduces moral hazard and eliminates very small claims. Screen repair excess of £100 reduces AD claim frequency by 15–25%. Home warranty service call fee ($100–$125) effectively filters spurious HVAC calls. Excess design is a key pricing lever. EST

Rate Credit −5%

Repair Network Authorisation

Use of manufacturer-authorised repair networks (Apple Authorised Service, Bosch Service) reduces claim cost variance and quality risk. Open networks allow any repairer — higher cost variance and fraud risk. Rate differential: authorised network -5% vs open network.

Structural Control

Stop-Loss / Attachment Structure

For large administrators, writing a stop-loss above 80–90% combined loss ratio rather than first-loss eliminates small programme volatility while retaining the insurer's ability to participate in attractive programmes. Critical for motor MBI where single-claim severity can distort individual programme results.

Structural Control

Aggregate Annual Limit

Cap on total annual claims payments prevents mass defect events (gaming console, white goods) from generating unbounded losses. Aggregate limit at 150% of expected annual claims is typical for consumer electronics programmes.

Rate Credit −3–5%

Replacement Cap (Market Value)

Replacement obligation capped at the current market value of the product, not the original purchase price. Critical where product values depreciate rapidly (smartphones, laptops). Prevents insurers from being forced to pay £800 for a £200 used phone replacement.

Structural Control

New Product Launch Monitoring

First 6 months of a new product generation are monitored at enhanced frequency for emerging defect patterns. Trigger: if claims frequency exceeds 3× expected in any quarter, programme is reviewed and may be suspended. Gaming console history justifies this approach.

Fraud Prevention Framework

Extended warranty fraud — particularly on mobile device accidental damage cover — represents a material loss driver. US market estimates suggest 5–15% of AD claims have a fraudulent element. EST UK fraud rates are lower (estimated 3–8%) due to better data sharing through Insurance Fraud Bureau (IFB) and Claims and Underwriting Exchange (CUE).

🔍 Fraud Detection Checklist — Accidental Damage Claims (Smartphones)

Purchase validation: Serial number / IMEI check against purchase date — is the policy inception consistent with genuine ownership?
Multiple claim check: Query claims history database — has this consumer claimed on multiple devices in the last 12 months?
Damage consistency: Does the stated damage mode (drop damage, liquid ingress) match the physical evidence? Are screen cracks consistent with drop damage or applied pressure?
Time pattern: Claim submitted within 30 days of policy inception — flag for enhanced review (25% of fraud occurs in first month).
Repair shop validation: Is the repair shop authorised? High-frequency claims from specific repair shops — indicator of organised fraud ring.
Device network check: Is the device still active on the same network as registered at policy inception? Sudden network change may indicate device resale.
Geographic consistency: Does the claimed location of damage make sense given the account holder's profile?

Obsolescence Risk Management

🔧 Managing Parts Availability and Obsolescence

Obsolescence is a unique and underappreciated risk in extended warranty. Key controls:

  • Replacement cap at current market value: Never commit to replacing a 5-year-old product at original purchase price. Current market value replacement eliminates the "can't find parts, must pay premium" scenario.
  • Parts stocking requirements: For high-volume programmes (major appliance brands), require OEM commitment to parts availability for the warranty term. EU Right to Repair (2024) makes this a legal obligation for covered categories.
  • Like-for-like equivalence clause: If exact replacement is unavailable, allow replacement with a comparable specification product. Prevents disputes about replacement model selection.
  • 3+ year term obsolescence loading: Programme pricing for terms exceeding 3 years should include an explicit obsolescence reserve — recommended 2–5% of projected claims volume.
  • Annual rate reviews: Multi-year programme agreements should include annual rate review triggers linked to parts price indices and OEM pricing announcements. EST

EV Battery Warranty — Risk Protocol

⚡ EV Battery Underwriting Protocol (2024)

Electric vehicle battery warranty requires enhanced risk controls given the novelty of the class and severity of battery replacement costs ($10,000–$20,000+). Recommended protocol:

  • Inception SoH diagnostic mandatory: Battery State of Health ≥80% required. Diagnostic performed by OEM-authorised service centre within 30 days of policy inception. Report retained by insurer.
  • Coverage trigger definition: Cover catastrophic failure (battery unable to charge, thermal event, BMS fault) only. Explicitly exclude degradation below threshold unless separately agreed and priced. Draft clear policy language distinguishing failure from degradation.
  • Maximum term 3 years: Beyond 3 years, battery technology evolution and parts availability are insufficiently predictable. 5-year terms should be declined until the class matures.
  • Per-battery sub-limit: Maximum replacement cost sub-limit — e.g. $20,000 per battery event — prevents exposure to future battery pack cost escalation on premium EV models.
  • Stop-loss protection: For any EV battery book above £10m NEP, purchase aggregate stop-loss protection above 100% LR from specialist market. Reinsurers are beginning to quote this class. EST
  • Annual portfolio review: EV battery book to be reviewed annually — claim frequency emerging data, OEM recall events, NHTSA/DVSA enforcement actions, and battery technology developments all create material repricing triggers.

Top Actions for Extended Warranty Insureds (Administrators / TPAs)

✅ Seven Actions to Improve EW Programme Insurability & Pricing

Provide monthly loss development data — early warning systems demonstrating claims visibility are the most impactful underwriting credibility signal
Invest in fraud prevention technology — IMEI databases, claims analytics, SIU capability. Demonstrably lower fraud rates justify 5–10% premium reduction
Use authorised repair networks wherever possible — cost control and quality consistency reduce loss ratio variance significantly
Conduct product quality due diligence before launching new product warranty programmes — OEM reliability data reduces adverse selection risk
Maintain FCA/regulatory authorisation and Consumer Duty compliance documentation — regulatory risk is the fastest-growing reason for programme suspension
Build obsolescence management into programme design — replacement market value caps and like-for-like clauses prevent tail cost escalation
Maintain adequate financial reserves — insurers require evidence of net worth ≥5× annual expected claims; undercapitalised administrators face capacity withdrawal
