Rating Factors — High Impact
High Impact

Production Budget (Key Determinant)

The total budget is the primary rating factor — it drives the size of all sub-limits (cast, negative, equipment) and determines the scale of potential abandonment loss. Larger budgets mean larger cast salaries to insure, more equipment on set, more complex logistics. Rate expressed as a percentage of budget varies inversely: micro-budget productions pay higher rates proportionally than studio tentpoles. EST

Rate range: 0.25%–1.5% of production budget depending on class and risk profile
High Impact

Key Cast Medical Status

All cast members insured for amounts above $250k must undergo a full pre-production medical examination by an approved physician. Medical reports reviewed by specialist cast underwriters. Prior illness, surgery, substance use history and current health status assessed. Insurable conditions lead to premium loading; uninsurable pre-existing conditions are excluded. VER

Medical loading: +10% to +100%+ depending on risk profile; pre-existing exclusions standard
High Impact

Production Location

Location is a critical factor: domestic (UK/US studio) vs international location, infrastructure quality, political stability, medical facilities, and climate all directly affect production risk. Remote or developing-country locations attract surcharges for evacuation risk, inadequate medical facilities, and weather exposure. High political-risk zones require specific political risk assessment. VER

Location loading: +15%–50% for developing-country international locations; +25–80% for high-risk zones
High Impact

Schedule Length & Tightness

Longer schedules mean more exposure time for cast illness, equipment loss and location incidents. Tight schedules with no schedule buffer ("float") dramatically increase the probability that any delay causes a compensable loss — there is no contingency to absorb short stoppages. Underwriters review production schedules in detail and rate on schedule duration and buffer days. VER

Loading for zero schedule buffer: +15–25%; credit for 10%+ buffer days: −5 to −10%
Rating Factors — Medium Impact
Medium Impact

Genre & Stunt Content

Action, martial arts and stunt-heavy productions attract surcharges of 15–30% on the production package — particularly on third-party liability and cast covers. Period drama attracts weather and costume exposure but lower stunt risk. Documentary and reality TV are lower-risk genres. Animation/VFX-heavy productions shift risk from physical to post-production.

Action/stunt surcharge: +15–30%; documentary credit: −10 to −20% vs feature film baseline
Medium Impact

Cast Age Profile

Older insured actors carry higher mortality and morbidity risk. Age is the primary demographic factor in cast underwriting — all other factors being equal, a 70-year-old lead actor carries materially higher cast risk than a 35-year-old. Age loading is applied on a sliding scale above 55. Specific senior cast may require individual medical review even at lower insured values.

Age loading 55–65: +10–25%; 65–75: +25–60%; 75+: individually rated / may decline
Medium Impact

Completion Bond Status

Bonded productions (with an approved completion guarantee company) have more intensive oversight: bond companies review scripts, budgets, schedules, and daily production reports. This oversight reduces the frequency of preventable production shutdowns. Underwriters apply a moderate credit for bonded productions as the bond company effectively acts as a co-risk manager. EST

Completion bond credit: −5 to −12% on production package rate
Medium Impact

Pandemic / COVID Endorsement

Since 2020, communicable disease (including COVID-19) is a universal exclusion from cast insurance without a specific paid endorsement. Where pandemic cover is sought, it is rated separately and subject to strict conditions (testing protocols, bubble arrangements, vaccination status). Most productions accept the exclusion and rely on contractual protections and government schemes where available.

Pandemic endorsement (where available): +5–15% additional premium; most productions accept exclusion
Rating Factors — Lower Impact
Lower Impact

Director / Producer Experience

Experienced directors and producers with track records of on-budget, on-schedule delivery receive modest credit. First-time features or debut directors on large-budget productions receive loading. Studio productions (with professional line producers and production managers) are lower risk than independent productions with inexperienced production management.

Lower Impact

Studio vs Independent

Major studio productions (Warner Bros, Disney, Universal, Netflix originals) benefit from institutional risk management: professional safety departments, experienced production management, established vendor relationships, and completion bond oversight. Independent productions rely more heavily on insurance as their primary risk management tool.

Lower Impact

Digital vs Film Negative

Digital production is now near-universal. Digital footage is recoverable from cloud backup systems if protocols are followed — but hardware failure, accidental erasure and cyberattack can destroy irreplaceable footage. Underwriters review data backup protocols; productions with offsite cloud backup receive modest credit over single-copy digital-only storage.

Lower Impact

VFX / Post-Production Intensity

Productions with extensive VFX (visual effects) carry post-production risk: VFX vendor insolvency, technology failure, missed delivery dates for completed VFX sequences. While not directly covered under the production package, VFX-heavy productions may seek separate post-production insurance. Underwriters note VFX concentration risk when reviewing large franchise productions.

Premium Rating Formula
// Film Production Package (PF) — indicative rating formula
Base Premium = Production Budget × Base Rate %

Cast Premium = Sum of (individual cast salary insured × medical risk factor)
                    × age loading × stunt loading

Adjusted Package Premium = Base Premium
  × Genre Factor // 0.8× (documentary) → 1.3× (action/stunt)
  × Location Factor // 1.0× (UK/US studio) → 1.5× (remote/high-risk)
  × Schedule Buffer Factor // 1.25× (zero buffer) → 0.9× (10%+ buffer)
  × Bond Credit // −5 to −12% for completion-bonded productions
  × Experience Credit // −5 to −10% for experienced production teams

Total GWP = Adjusted Package Premium + Cast Premium + E&O Premium

Cast Insurance Coverage Gap — COVID Exclusion: Since March 2020, communicable disease is universally excluded from cast insurance. If a lead actor contracts COVID-19 and cannot work, causing production shutdown, the primary driver of loss is not covered under a standard policy. Productions must rely on contractual protections (e.g., force majeure clauses), government schemes where available, or specifically purchased pandemic riders (now rare and expensive). Any production with cast age 55+ in a post-COVID environment should confirm the precise scope of the communicable disease exclusion and consider whether any government backstop is available. EST
