Prevention Controls (6)
PREVENT

Cast Pre-Production Medical Examination

Mandatory full physical examination for all cast members insured at $250k or above. Conducted by an approved physician using insurer-standard protocols. Results reviewed by specialist cast underwriting team. Pre-existing conditions identified are either excluded, accepted with loading, or render the individual uninsurable at the required limit. Repeat medicals for productions exceeding 6 months. [I5]

PREVENT

Stunt Safety Protocols (Post-Twilight Zone / Post-Rust)

BECTU (UK) and IATSE (USA) certified stunt coordinators required for all stunt work. Pre-production stunt planning with rehearsal requirements before camera. All stunt performers hold professional certification. Stunt plan submitted to insurer for approval before policy binding. Production must comply with the applicable stunt safety bulletin relevant to jurisdiction. No stunt coverage without certified coordinator on set. [I3]

PREVENT

Weapons / Armoury Protocol (Post-Rust)

Mandatory post-Rust protocol: licensed armourer designated on set at all times when any prop weapon is present. No live ammunition permitted on set under any circumstances. All cast and crew trained in safe weapons handling before first weapons use. Chain-of-custody log for all prop weapons from transport to storage. Daily inspection log signed by designated armourer. Insurance notification within 24 hours of any weapons incident. [I3] [I4]

PREVENT

Location Scout Safety Assessment

Pre-production safety assessment of all shooting locations: structural integrity (for sets and historic buildings), proximity to water/heights, access for emergency services, local medical infrastructure quality, political stability assessment for international locations. Production risk manager sign-off required before location approval. Locations in conflict-adjacent zones require separate political risk assessment and insurer notification. [L1]

PREVENT

COVID / Communicable Disease Testing Protocol

Even where pandemic exclusion is in force, many international co-productions require ongoing testing protocols for access to certain territories (Australia, New Zealand, parts of Asia) and as a condition of cast/crew travel. Proactive testing reduces the likelihood of large-scale cluster outbreaks halting production. Testing protocol documentation supports any future coverage dispute resolution. [I9]

PREVENT

Weather & Environmental Monitoring

Outdoor productions and international location shoots require dedicated weather monitoring service (meteorological contract) and pre-agreed weather day protocols. Clear decision-making criteria for weather-related shoot suspension protect cast and crew safety and also prevent compounding schedule delay from poor weather management. Weather logs feed into any delay/abandonment claims calculation.

Detection Controls (4)
DETECT

Daily Production Report (DPR)

The daily production report tracks actual vs planned schedule (scenes completed vs budgeted), daily spend vs budget, crew attendance, and any incidents. The DPR is the production equivalent of a daily management account — it is the primary tool for detecting early schedule slippage or cost overrun before it becomes a claim. Insurers and bond companies review DPRs for bonded productions. [I5]

DETECT

On-Set Incident Reporting

All on-set incidents — regardless of apparent severity — must be reported to the production risk manager within 24 hours. Near-misses, minor injuries, equipment failures and property damage all trigger incident reports. Early notification to insurers of potential claims is a policy condition; late notification is grounds for coverage reduction. Rust demonstrated the consequence of lax incident reporting culture. [L2]

DETECT

Cast Health Monitoring

Cast health monitoring is ongoing, not limited to pre-production medicals. Productions with senior cast (55+), or cast with disclosed health conditions, require periodic check-ins with a designated on-set medical professional. Any health concern triggering a production delay must be reported to the cast insurer within the notification period specified in the policy — typically 48–72 hours of first knowledge. [1]

DETECT

Location Security Monitoring

For productions in politically sensitive or remote international locations: ongoing monitoring of the security environment by a designated security advisor. Clear evacuation protocols agreed pre-production. Insurance notification triggers if the security environment deteriorates materially after policy inception. Kidnap & ransom coverage considered for high-risk-zone locations as a standalone ancillary product alongside the production package.

Post-Rust On-Set Weapons Protocol — 8-Point Compliance Checklist

  • Licensed armourer designated — a named, certified armourer is on set at all times when prop weapons are present; armourer's certification verified and copied to production file
  • No live ammunition on set — ever — absolute prohibition on live rounds anywhere on or near the production set; all ammunition (including blank rounds) controlled exclusively by armourer
  • Cast and crew training — all cast handling weapons, and all crew in the vicinity of weapon use, complete on-set weapons safety briefing before first use; attendance log maintained
  • Daily weapons inspection log — armourer conducts daily inspection and function test of all prop weapons; signed log retained for the duration of production and 3 years post-completion
  • Chain of custody log — every prop weapon's movement (transport, storage, set placement, return) documented in chain-of-custody log; log reviewed by production risk manager weekly
  • No weapons drawn at anyone (post-Rust rule) — prop weapons are not pointed at any person at any time, even during rehearsal or camera test, without specific director approval and armourer supervision
  • Safety committee review — productions involving significant weapon use submit to a pre-production safety committee review including armourer, stunt coordinator, production manager and director
  • Insurance notification within 24 hours — any weapons-related incident, near-miss, or policy trigger is notified to the production insurer within 24 hours; failure to notify within policy period is a condition of coverage [I3] [L1]
AI / Likeness Insurance — Emerging Risk: The SAG-AFTRA 2023 Collective Bargaining Agreement for the first time regulates the use of AI-generated digital likenesses of actors in productions. [L5] Productions that use AI to recreate, age, de-age, or replace a performer's appearance must comply with consent and compensation requirements. New insurance product development is underway in the Lloyd's market addressing:
  • E&O coverage for AI likeness rights infringement claims (failure to obtain consent, breach of AI use provisions)
  • Technology E&O for failure of AI generation systems during production (virtual production LED volume failure, AI de-ageing system failure mid-shoot)
  • "Likeness insurance" concept: covering loss if a performer withdraws consent post-production requiring reshooting AI-generated scenes
As of May 2026, no standardised Lloyd's AI/likeness endorsement exists — underwriters are developing bespoke wordings on a case-by-case basis. Productions using significant AI-generated performer content should seek specific coverage confirmation in writing.
COVID / Communicable Disease — Ongoing Consideration (Post-Pandemic): Even though the acute COVID-19 pandemic period has passed, communicable disease remains a relevant production risk consideration:
  • International co-productions shooting in Asia-Pacific territories may face territory-specific testing/quarantine requirements
  • Cast insurance policies now universally contain communicable disease exclusions — review the exact wording; some are broader than COVID-only
  • Any future pandemic or significant outbreak would create an identical coverage gap to 2020 — production budgets should include contingency for schedule disruption from infectious illness even where not insured
  • Government production facility backstops (UK PIF) are not guaranteed to repeat — do not assume government support will be available for future pandemic events [I9]
7 Top Actions for Insureds
01

Commission Pre-Production Medical Reviews Early

Pre-production medicals for all insured cast should be completed at least 6 weeks before filming to allow time for medical loading negotiation or cast substitution if uninsurable.

02

Appoint Certified Armourer Before Scripting Weapons

For any production involving firearms, designate a certified armourer before production design begins. Build armoury protocol into the production safety plan from day one — not as an afterthought.

03

Confirm E&O Coverage Before Distribution Agreements

Distributors require valid E&O before signing delivery agreements. Commission chain-of-title review, music clearance audit, and name/likeness clearance at script stage — not post-production.

04

Build Schedule Buffer Into the Production Plan

Include at least 8–10% of shooting days as contingency buffer. Zero-buffer schedules transform any delay into an insurance claim; adequate buffer absorbs minor incidents without triggering policy response.

05

Establish Digital Asset Backup Protocol From Day One

Implement 3-2-1 backup rule for all digital footage: three copies, two different media types, one offsite/cloud. Daily backup logs are a policy condition for digital asset coverage. Never rely on a single storage copy.

06

Report All Incidents Within Policy Notification Period

Late notification is the most common grounds for coverage reduction in production insurance. Report all incidents — including near-misses and minor injuries — to the insurer within the 24–48 hour notification period specified in the policy.

07

Specifically Address AI Use in Policy Submission

If your production uses AI-generated content, deepfake de-ageing, or AI voice cloning, disclose this in the policy submission and seek written confirmation of E&O coverage for AI-related IP claims. Do not assume standard E&O wordings cover AI-specific risks.
