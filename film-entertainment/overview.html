Lloyd's Risk Code Coverage

PF — Film Production Insurance / Entertainment

Lloyd's PF code covers all aspects of film, TV, streaming and entertainment production insurance. The core product is the Film Production Package — a multi-peril policy covering cast, negative/digital assets, props/sets/wardrobe, equipment, third-party liability and errors & omissions. Written for productions from micro-budget indie films to $300m+ studio tentpoles, documentary, reality TV, theatre, music video and video game production. [I5]

Cast Insurance — The Key Peril

Cast insurance is the most expensive element of the production package. If a lead actor is incapacitated through death, injury or illness, a $200m film may need to be abandoned, postponed or reshot — triggering enormous loss. All insured cast must complete a full pre-production medical examination. Cast coverage is subject to medical underwriting and exclusion of pre-existing conditions. [I5]

Errors & Omissions (E&O) — Mandatory for Distribution

E&O insurance covers legal costs defending claims of copyright infringement, defamation, invasion of privacy, right of publicity, and title clearance failures. Distributors (Netflix, Amazon, studios, broadcasters) require a valid E&O policy before taking delivery of a completed film or series. Typical minimum: $1m per claim / $3m aggregate. E&O is usually written separately from the production package. [L3]

Coverage Taxonomy — Film Production Package

Cast Insurance

Death, accidental injury or illness of any insured cast member or key crew causing abandonment, postponement or interruption of production. Mandatory pre-production medical exam for all insured individuals. Sub-limits apply per person. Loading for age, health history and production role. [1]

Negative / Digital Asset

Loss, destruction or damage to original footage — whether film negative, exposed footage, digital files on storage media, or cloud/server-hosted dailies. Covers reshooting costs and re-recording expenses. Increasingly important as productions shoot digitally and manage terabytes of irreplaceable footage. [2]

Props, Sets & Wardrobe

Physical production assets — purpose-built sets, period costumes, specialised props and wardrobe items. Covers loss, damage or destruction. Location shooting adds exposure: sets and wardrobe transported internationally are at risk of customs seizure, weather damage and theft.

Equipment

Cameras, lighting rigs, grip equipment, sound recording gear — whether owned or hired in. Equipment hire is standard in film: broken or stolen hired cameras trigger both replacement cost and daily hire charges. Specialist lens and camera systems may require agreed value cover. [3]

Third-Party Liability

On-set accidents, location property damage, public liability for production activities in public places. Key exposure: prop weapon incidents (post-Rust), stunt accidents, and location damage during filming. Galvanised by the Rust 2021 shooting incident which resulted in criminal charges and civil litigation. [I3]

Errors & Omissions (E&O)

Covers legal defence costs and damages for claims of copyright infringement, defamation, invasion of privacy, right of publicity, title clearance failure. Essential — all major distributors mandate E&O before delivery. Often written separately with higher limits to satisfy all distribution agreements globally. [L3]

Completion Bond vs Production Insurance — Critical Distinction: A completion bond (or completion guarantee) is not insurance — it is a guarantee from a bond company that the film will be delivered on time and on budget. If the producer defaults, the bond company takes over the production. Banks and financiers require completion bonds before providing production financing. The bond company is a sophisticated party with step-in rights; they review scripts, budgets and schedules intensively. Production insurance (PF) covers specific perils (cast illness, equipment loss, liability) but does not guarantee delivery. The two products work alongside each other: a bonded film will also carry a production package, but the bond company may have subrogation rights against the production insurer. [I5]
Post-Rust On-Set Weapons Protocol (2021): The fatal shooting of cinematographer Halyna Hutchins on the set of Rust in October 2021 — the first on-set firearms death in nearly 30 years — fundamentally changed weapons protocols industry-wide. A licensed armourer must be designated on set at all times when prop weapons are present. No live ammunition is ever permitted on set. Daily weapons inspection logs and chain-of-custody records are now standard. Third-party liability underwriters require confirmation of protocol compliance for any production involving prop weapons.
