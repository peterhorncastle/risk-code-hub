International Production Programme Structure — 4 Layers
Layer 1 — Excess of Loss Reinsurance
XL catastrophe reinsurance protecting the Lloyd's PF book against accumulation of large cast claims (e.g., multiple key cast ill during same global pandemic-type event) and single large liability events. Sits above each syndicate's net retention. Pandemic-related losses explicitly excluded from reinsurance protection since 2020.
Layer 2 — Lloyd's International Master (Production Package)
The core product: Lloyd's global production package covering cast insurance, negative/digital asset, props/sets/wardrobe, equipment (owned and hired in), third-party property damage & general liability, and (optionally) additional living expenses for key personnel. Written by Lloyd's syndicate as master policy; responds globally subject to local admitted insurance requirements. [I5]
Layer 3 — Local Workers' Compensation / Employers' Liability (Where Shooting)
In each filming territory, local statutory requirements for workers' compensation or employers' liability must be satisfied by locally admitted insurance. US productions require state workers' comp (each state separately regulated). UK productions require EL under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. Australian productions require state-based workers' compensation. Local policies coordinate with the master programme. [L1] [L8]
Layer 4 — Errors & Omissions (Typically Written Separately, Per-Film)
E&O is generally written separately from the production package — typically by a specialist E&O market (Lloyd's, Hiscox, Chubb) on a per-film or per-series basis. E&O follows the life of the content through distribution, not just through production. Distributor/platform E&O requirements dictate minimum limits (typically $1m–$3m per claim; $3m–$5m aggregate for major platforms). [L3]
Co-Production Treaties & Tax Incentives
Official Co-Production Treaties: The UK has bilateral co-production treaties with over 30 countries, enabling qualifying productions to access both territories' film tax credits simultaneously. Key treaty partners include: France, Germany, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, South Africa, India, Jamaica, and all EU MEDIA/Creative Europe member states. A treaty co-production must meet qualifying criteria (creative contribution, expenditure thresholds, key creative personnel nationality). Treaty status unlocks local tax incentives — and requires local insurance compliance in all partner territories. [I6]

Jurisdiction Profiles — 6 Key Film Markets

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

The primary Lloyd's PF market. BFI Film Tax Relief provides up to 25% rebate for qualifying UK productions. BECTU and Equity crew; UK HSE on-set safety enforcement under the Health & Safety at Work Act 1974. [L1] Post-Rust armoury guidance issued by BECTU and PGGB in 2022. UK Production Debt Facility provides financing backstop post-COVID. Strong infrastructure: Pinewood, Shepperton, Warner Bros Leavesden studios.

BFI Tax Relief 25%BECTU/EquityHSE Enforcement

🇺🇸 United States

The world's largest production market. SAG-AFTRA regulates cast; IATSE regulates crew. State film tax credits vary widely — Georgia, New Mexico, New York and California offer competing incentives. E&O is mandatory for all US distribution agreements. Workers' comp is state-regulated — each state has its own scheme (California SCIF; New York State Fund). OSHA enforces on-set safety. [L6] The 2023 SAG-AFTRA CBA AI provisions are binding on all signatory productions. [L5]

🇦🇺 Australia

Growing production destination favoured for landscape diversity, skilled English-speaking crews and competitive currency. Screen Australia administers the Producer Offset (16.5%). MEAA (Media, Entertainment & Arts Alliance) represents cast and crew. State-based workers' compensation (each state has its own scheme). Strong infrastructure in Sydney (Fox Studios), Melbourne and Queensland. Popular with US runaway production.

Producer Offset 16.5%MEAAState WC

🇨🇦 Canada

Second-largest destination for US "runaway production" after Australia. Vancouver and Toronto compete aggressively for US studio productions. Federal Production Tax Credit (25%) plus provincial credits create stacking potential of up to 45%+ in some provinces. Telefilm Canada supports qualifying Canadian content. IATSE Canada crews; provincial Workers' Compensation Boards (WCB). Canadian Content (CanCon) rules apply for domestic tax credits. [I7]

Federal + Provincial CreditsWCBVancouver/Toronto

🇨🇿 Eastern Europe (Czech Republic / Hungary / Romania)

Lower production costs, high crew quality, versatile locations and EU co-production treaty access make Eastern Europe a major destination for European and US productions. Barrandov Studios (Prague) and Origo Studios (Budapest) offer world-class facilities. Cash rebates (Czech: 20%; Hungary: 25%; Romania: 35%) are competitive. Infrastructure improving. EU social insurance requirements apply for local crew. [I6]

Cash Rebates 20–35%EU Co-production TreatiesLower Cost Base

🇿🇦 South Africa

Cape Town and Johannesburg offer diverse locations (desert, coastline, urban, wildlife), English-speaking crews, and the DTI Rebate (25% on qualifying South African spend). Strong infrastructure developing. Political stability improving. Security is a consideration — productions require location security assessment. Workmen's Compensation Fund registration required for South African crew. Emerging as a preferred destination for US and UK productions avoiding higher-cost European locations. [I7]

DTI Rebate 25%English-Speaking CrewsDiverse Locations
Tax Credit Loss — Emerging Coverage: An emerging question for the PF market: if a production fails (abandonment or financial collapse) and the anticipated tax credit/incentive rebate is not received as a result, can the producer recover the lost tax relief under any insurance policy? Standard production packages do not cover loss of anticipated tax credits. A specialist tax credit insurance product is available in some markets (primarily for large productions with confirmed qualifying spend), but it is not widely available. Productions financing with tax credits as a significant source of equity should seek advice from a specialist tax credit insurer. [I5]
